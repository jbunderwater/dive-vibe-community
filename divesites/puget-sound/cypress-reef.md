# Cypress Reef

A rocky reef near Cypress Island offering rich bull kelp and cold-water invertebrate communities.

## Overview

Cypress Reef lies in the waters off Cypress Island, one of the less-visited islands in the San Juan archipelago — a DNR-managed island with no permanent residents, meaning marine life pressure is low. The reef is dominated by dense bull kelp canopy in summer, which creates a dynamic three-dimensional habitat for fish and invertebrates. Water temperature holds at 7–11°C year-round; a dry suit is standard.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Cypress Island, San Juan Islands, Puget Sound, Washington
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 8–14 metres
- **Water Temperature**: 7–11°C
- **Current**: Moderate tidal
- **Best Time**: June to October

## Dive Profile

The kelp canopy creates a snorkelling-accessible entry environment at 1–3 metres before divers descend through the canopy to the reef below. Work from the reef crest at 5–8 metres down to the deeper edge at 18 metres. The kelp forest is most impressive in July and August when fronds are at maximum length. Allow adequate bottom time for a thorough exploration of the rocky terrain before ascending.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only. Anchor clear of the kelp canopy or use the boat's engine to hold position. Entry by back-roll; avoid entanglement in kelp fronds during descent by descending along the anchor line. On ascent, surface in a clear area away from the densest kelp.

## Tips

- Navigating through the kelp canopy is part of the experience — relax and let the fronds part around you, and you will not become entangled
- Visit in late summer (August–September) when kelp is at peak growth and fish school density is high
- Cypress Island's protected waters mean sea conditions are generally calm — good for less experienced intermediate divers

## Safety

Kelp entanglement is a minor but real risk — if tangled, stop, and slowly back out rather than thrashing. A dive knife or cutting tool should be accessible. Cold water requires proper exposure protection. Deploy an SMB well clear of the kelp before ascending.
