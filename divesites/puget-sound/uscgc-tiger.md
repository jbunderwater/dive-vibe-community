## USCGC Tiger

USCGC Tiger is a historic wreck dive in Puget Sound, North America.

## Overview

The USCGC Tiger is a 125-foot former Coast Guard cutter with a WWII history — she was patrolling off Barber's Point on December 7, 1941, earning a Battle Star for Pearl Harbor — later decommissioned and eventually used as a floating hull in Tacoma before finding her final resting place in Puget Sound at 18 meters. The wreck now provides structure in an otherwise open-water environment and has attracted lingcod, cabezon, rockfish, and the occasional wolf eel to its encrusted hull, with visibility in Puget Sound typically ranging from 5-15 meters depending on conditions and season. Cold water between 7-12°C year-round means a drysuit is strongly recommended, and tidal current timing is essential for a comfortable dive.

## Wreck History

**Correction (re-audit, 2026-08-26):** earlier text here identified the USCGC *Tiger* as a "Treasury-class" cutter. That is incorrect and has been fixed. Two independent sources (Wikipedia and a pacificwrecks.com record) confirm she was an **Active-class** patrol boat — Treasury-class cutters were a larger, later, unrelated USCG class. The USCGC *Tiger* (WSC-152) was 125 feet (38 m) long and displaced 232 tons. On December 7, 1941, while patrolling off Barber's Point, Honolulu, the *Tiger* was present during the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor and earned a single Battle Star for this action. She spent the remainder of World War II escorting convoys in and out of Honolulu Harbor alongside her sister ship *Reliance*.

The *Tiger* was decommissioned on November 12, 1947 and sold on June 14, 1948. She was renamed *Polar Merchant* and was ultimately used as a stripped floating hull in Tacoma, Washington. According to Wikipedia, she was "completely stripped and used as a floating hull in Tacoma, Washington" as of 2018 — a floating hulk, not a sunken or scuttled wreck at that point.

**Note (unresolved, re-audited 2026-08-26):** No source — dive-specific or otherwise — documents the *Tiger*/*Polar Merchant* actually sinking, being scuttled, or coming to rest at these south Puget Sound coordinates. She may still have been an intact floating hull as late as 2018, well after this OSM wreck node was presumably surveyed. ## Marine Life

Wrecks in the South Puget Sound / Tacoma area attract lingcod, cabezon, copper rockfish, and giant Pacific octopus. Plumose anemones (white and orange) colonize steel surfaces. Wolf eels may occupy larger hull cavities. Nudibranchs are seasonally abundant on all encrusted surfaces.

## Dive Profile

Depth 18 metres. Single-tank dive with comfortable bottom time. Drysuit mandatory in 7–12°C water. South Puget Sound tidal exchanges can be significant; plan around slack water. Visibility typically 5–12 metres.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from Tacoma-area launches. No shore entry available.

## Safety Considerations

Drysuit mandatory. Tidal current timing essential. Steel hull poses entanglement and penetration risks — do not enter enclosed spaces without appropriate wreck training and equipment. SMB required.
