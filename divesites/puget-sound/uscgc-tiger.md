## USCGC Tiger

USCGC Tiger is a former Coast Guard cutter with a documented WWII Pearl Harbor history whose hull now serves — per the best available source — as a breakwater at Tyee Marina near Tacoma, not a conventional submerged wreck dive.

## Overview

**Important correction (re-audited 2026-08-27):** Earlier research for this page could not confirm the vessel's post-military fate beyond "used as a floating hull in Tacoma." A direct, dated maritime-history source (Pacific Wrecks) now fills in that gap — and the answer changes how this site should be understood. In the 1980s or 1990s, the ex-*Tiger* hull was towed to Tyee Marina on Commencement Bay, stripped down to the gunnel, filled with foam, and put to work as a breakwater at the marina entrance, where it remains today. That is consistent with this OSM node's own tags, which mark the wreck as `hull_showing` and visible at both high and low tide — a showing, foam-filled hull is what you would expect from a breakwater, not a site sitting fully submerged at 18 metres. Treat the 18-metre depth figure on this page as unconfirmed rather than a settled fact; this may be a shoreline curiosity to view rather than a scuba dive.

## Wreck History

USCGC *Tiger* (WSC-152) was an Active-class patrol boat built by American Brown Boveri Electric Corp in Camden, New Jersey: laid down February 1, 1927, launched April 18, 1927, and commissioned May 3, 1927. She displaced 232 tons and measured 125' x 23.6' x 7.6'. Both Wikipedia and Pacific Wrecks (a dedicated maritime-history archive, cross-checked against each other for this audit) agree on these figures.

On December 7, 1941, while patrolling off Barber's Point, Oahu, *Tiger* was present during the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor and earned a single Battle Star for the action; Pacific Wrecks notes she detected an underwater contact that may have been a Japanese midget submarine before the sounding faded. Later that December she helped survivors from two torpedoed vessels: a lifeboat from *SS Lahaina* (thirty survivors, aided December 20) and one from *SS Prusa* (fourteen survivors, found December 27). For the rest of the war she and sister ship *Reliance* alternated guarding Honolulu Harbor and escorting convoys.

*Tiger* was decommissioned November 12, 1947 and sold June 14, 1948, likely to Tacoma Boiler Works, and renamed *Polar Merchant*. She then worked as a tug between Puget Sound and Alaska and later as a fishing boat.

**Corrected final fate (re-audited 2026-08-27):** Per Pacific Wrecks, sometime in the 1980s or 1990s the hull was moved to Tyee Marina at the edge of Commencement Bay near Tacoma, had its superstructure removed down to the gunnel, was gutted, and was filled with foam to serve as a breakwater at the marina entrance — where it remains today. This is a materially different picture than "sunk as a dive site": it is a shoreline structure, not a submerged wreck placed for diving. The OSM tags on this node (`hull_showing`, visible at both high and low tide) are consistent with that account rather than with a fully submerged 18-metre wreck.
