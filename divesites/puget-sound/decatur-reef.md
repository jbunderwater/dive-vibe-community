# Decatur Reef

A rocky reef in central Puget Sound with consistent fish life and accessible cold-water invertebrate communities.

## Overview

Decatur Reef sits in the southern portion of Puget Sound's main basin, in an area that sees regular boat traffic from the Seattle metropolitan area. The reef structure drops to 18 metres and supports a community of Pacific Northwest cold-water species that includes lingcod, multiple rockfish species, and giant Pacific octopus. The site is accessible year-round and serves as a productive destination for divers based in the greater Seattle and Tacoma areas. Visibility in this part of Puget Sound tends to be 6–10 metres, influenced by tidal mixing and occasional freshwater runoff from the surrounding watershed.

## Dive Profile

The reef slopes from shallow at the crest (6–8 metres) to the maximum depth of 18 metres at the outer edge. Most productive diving is on the mid-depth zone (10–15 metres) where invertebrate cover is densest. Plan a straightforward out-and-back navigation along the reef spine, conserving air for a comfortable safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only. Anchor on the up-current side of the reef. Back-roll or giant stride entry. Navigate the reef using compass and natural features. Deploy an SMB before ascending and surface upwind of the vessel. Puget Sound has heavy vessel traffic — never surface without an SMB deployed.
