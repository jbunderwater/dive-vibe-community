## Manta Reef Tofo

Manta Reef is Tofo's best-known dive site, a submerged pinnacle system with two cleaning stations — but a two-decade research record now shows manta encounters here have declined sharply, and the site is better understood today as a rich reef-fish "aquarium" dive than a guaranteed manta site.

## Overview

Manta Reef sits approximately 15–16 km from Tofo, reached in 25–35 minutes by boat (this replaces an earlier, incorrect "6 km / 10 minutes" figure — current listings from Tofo Scuba and Liquid Dive Adventures both place the site much further offshore). The site comprises pinnacle formations and rocky reef sections with a plateau at roughly 19–26 metres depth, bordered in places by 5–10 metre walls. Two cleaning stations operate on the reef, serviced by cleaner wrasse.

**Manta sightings — corrected:** Earlier versions of this page cited an "80% success rate" for manta encounters, calling this "one of the highest-probability manta encounters on the African coast." That claim does not hold up against current sources. Marine Megafauna Foundation's two-decade monitoring study at Tofo (cited by PADI's own reporting) documents an 88% decline in manta sightings during the first decade of observation and up to a 99% decline in the second decade, driven primarily by overfishing (with climate-driven shifts in plankton distribution and tourism pressure as secondary factors). Tofo Scuba's own current site description puts manta sightings at approximately 15% of dives — no higher than other deep sites in the area — and now markets Manta Reef primarily for its schooling fish and "aquarium"-like biodiversity rather than for reliable manta encounters. Divers should not book this dive expecting a near-guaranteed manta sighting.

Loggerhead turtles, giant moray eels, and large stationary schools of bigeye and snapper (concentrated along the north wall) are resident on the reef structure. Potato groupers are also present. Whale sharks are documented in the Tofo area generally and are occasionally seen in the water column near the reef during the November–March plankton season, but this is an opportunistic regional encounter, not a site-specific guarantee.

The dive is classified Advanced by local operators due to depth and the possibility of current requiring drift techniques and negative entries.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tofo, Inhambane Province, Mozambique
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 26 meters
