## Taunton

A 228-foot Danish cargo steamer built in 1902 and wrecked in November 1920, its engine and boilers still standing upright in just 6 meters of water near St. George's — one of Bermuda's most photogenic shallow wrecks.

## Overview

The Taunton was a 228-foot (69.5 m) steel-hulled cargo steamer built in 1902 in Copenhagen by Burmeister & Wain. Sources disagree on her nationality at the time of loss: several Bermuda tourism and dive-shop sites describe her simply as a "Danish steamer" (reflecting her Danish-built origin), while the Bermuda 100 Project states she was chartered to the United Fruit Company and sailed under the Norwegian flag. On November 24, 1920, while carrying coal from Norfolk, Virginia to St. George's, Bermuda, she ran aground on the North East Breaker after encountering fog at the entrance to the island's northern reef passage. The ship lies broken up on the reef in about 6 meters of water, but her engine and steam boilers remain standing nearly upright, reaching toward the surface — their profile visible as shadows from above on a calm day. The Taunton is frequently cited as one of Bermuda's most photogenic shallow wrecks, with the upright machinery creating dramatic visual compositions against the sandy reef bottom. Its very shallow depth makes it equally accessible to divers and snorkelers. The ship's bell was recovered by Teddy Tucker and reportedly used as a prop in the 1977 film *The Deep*; it is now displayed at the Gibbs Hill Lighthouse Museum.

No site-specific marine life reports were found for the Taunton in dive operator listings or research sources — available coverage focuses on the wreck's history and its shallow, upright machinery. Bermuda's typical shallow-reef fauna can be expected here, but specific species sightings at this site have not been documented in available sources.

## Dive Profile

The site is compact and the upright engine and boilers provide immediate landmarks from the moment of descent. Work around the machinery before exploring the scattered hull remains. The 6-meter depth allows indefinite bottom time, making this an excellent extended exploration site for newer divers. The entire site can be snorkeled from the surface on calm days.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Bermuda operators, particularly those based near St. George's on the East End. Giant stride or back roll entry. The near-surface machinery provides visual reference throughout the dive. An SMB is still recommended for boat pickup at this offshore location.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The upright engine and boilers are the primary photographic subjects — approach from below for dramatic upward-looking compositions
- This is an excellent orientation dive for wreck diving beginners
- The northeastern location near St. George's tends to be well-sheltered from southwest swell
- A glass-bottom boat or snorkel tour over this site is worthwhile if diving conditions are marginal

## Safety Considerations

At 6 meters, this is one of Bermuda's most benign wrecks from a depth perspective. The primary concerns are surface boat traffic at the offshore location and occasional current from tidal activity on the northeastern reef. Always carry an SMB and use it to signal the surface before ascending. Stay with the guide or group in this offshore environment.

## Photography

The Taunton's upright engine and boilers in shallow, clear water are exceptional photography subjects. Upward-looking wide-angle compositions with the machinery silhouetted against the bright surface are iconic. Natural light photography is outstanding at 6 meters. Snorkelers with waterproof cameras can photograph the machinery from above.
