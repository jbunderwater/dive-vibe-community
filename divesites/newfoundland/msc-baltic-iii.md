## MSC Baltic III

MSC Baltic III is a large containership wreck on Newfoundland's west coast, currently mid-removal and not accessible to divers.

## Overview

The MSC Baltic III is a large containership that grounded on Newfoundland's west coast in February 2025 and, as of August 2026, is the subject of an active wreck-pulling and dismantling operation. She is not, and has never been, an accessible recreational dive site: an exclusion zone has been in place since the grounding, and the site currently hosts an active industrial salvage operation with heavy equipment and personnel. Divers interested in this vessel's history should monitor official Canadian Coast Guard advisories rather than plan any visit; this entry exists in our database as a documented wreck of record, not as a dive recommendation.

Note: our data previously carried this site under the name "MSC Baltics III." Every source consulted — Canadian Coast Guard communications, CBC, gCaptain, and Maritime Executive — consistently uses the singular "MSC Baltic III," so the name has been corrected in this QA pass.

## Current Status (updated 2026-08-14)

The MSC Baltic III grounded on February 15, 2025, after losing power in severe winter weather near Wild Cove (also reported as Cedar Cove) in the Bay of Islands, Newfoundland. All 20 crew members were rescued by Royal Canadian Air Force helicopter. Resolve Marine was appointed in spring 2026 to lead the removal and remediation effort under contract to Mediterranean Shipping Company. By mid-2026, 471 of the vessel's 472 cargo containers had been removed (the final container is too deformed to extract by normal means and will be dealt with during dismantling). On July 31, 2026, crews began actively pulling the wreck toward shore — a deliberately slow, gradual process — with the accommodation block subsequently being dismantled section by section and shipped to shore for recycling. Bulk fuel has been removed from the vessel, though residual fuel and periodic tar-ball/oily-debris finds are still reported during daily shoreline monitoring. The Canadian Coast Guard continues to warn the public to stay clear of the active work site. Full removal/remediation is expected to extend into 2027.

**The site is not accessible for recreational diving. An active exclusion zone and live salvage operation are in place as of August 2026; approaching the site is prohibited under Transport Canada marine notices.**

## Safety Considerations

This site is off-limits to recreational divers as of August 2026. An active exclusion zone enforced by the Canadian Coast Guard, combined with an ongoing heavy-salvage operation (wreck pulling, dismantling, barge traffic), makes this an unambiguous no-go area. Monitor Canadian Coast Guard and Transport Canada advisories for any future update on exclusion-zone status once the removal is complete.
