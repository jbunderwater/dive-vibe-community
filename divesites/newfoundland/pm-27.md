## PM-27

PM-27 is a historic wreck dive in Newfoundland, North America.

## Overview

PM-27 (also recorded as PLM-27) was an ore carrier sunk during the German U-boat attacks on Bell Island's loading piers on November 2, 1942, the same raid that claimed the SS Rose Castle. Sources disagree on her wartime nationality and ownership (see Wreck History below). The wreck rests at around 30 meters in Conception Bay alongside the other Bell Island WWII victims, forming part of the most historically significant collection of war wrecks in Canadian waters. The cold dark Atlantic conditions — temperatures hover near 2°C in winter and rarely exceed 14°C even in summer — preserve the hull remarkably well.

- **Location**: Newfoundland, North America

## Wreck History

The PLM-27 (the name derives from Paris-Lyon-Méditerranée, the French railway company associated with her original ownership) was a 5,633-gross-ton steam ore carrier. **Sources conflict on her wartime flag, owner, and casualty count, and this has not been resolved:**

- Wikipedia, Community Stories, and Scuba Diver Life describe her as a Free French vessel operating under the Free French Forces, and state that 12 of her crew were killed when a single torpedo struck her amidships and she sank in approximately 60 seconds.
- Uboat.net's merchant-shipping database records her as British-registered, owned by Gibbs & Co of Cardiff, built in 1922 by Sir Raylton Dixon & Co Ltd, Middlesbrough, and lists 7 dead of a 49-person complement (the master, 35 crew, and six gunners reaching shore).

Both figures are reported here rather than picked, since neither source set has been independently reconciled. What all sources agree on: on the night of November 2, 1942, approximately one minute after U-518 (Kapitänleutnant Friedrich-Wilhelm Wissmann) had fired on the Rose Castle, PLM-27 was struck by torpedo and sank within roughly a minute. Scuba Diver Life reports that PLM-27 had fired flares to help Rose Castle survivors just before she herself was targeted. The attack on PLM-27 and Rose Castle formed the second U-boat strike on Bell Island, part of what Parks Canada recognizes as the only direct enemy action on a working North American community during WWII.

## Marine Life

Wrecksandreefs.com specifically describes flounders resting on nearly every surface of the wreck and crabs crawling in the rubble below — this is the only marine-life detail confirmed site-specifically for PLM-27 rather than the Bell Island group generally. The intact propeller — the only one preserved among the four Bell Island wrecks — is heavily encrusted with marine growth, and a lifeboat off the side is described as covered in marine life. No specific fish beyond flounders, and no invertebrates beyond crabs and general marine growth, are confirmed for this wreck in particular; earlier claims of nudibranchs or plumose anemones specific to PLM-27 were not supported by any source found and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The PLM-27 is the shallowest of the four Bell Island wrecks, with the main deck at approximately 18 m (60 ft) and the deepest sections around 30 m (100 ft), making it accessible to recreational divers at all levels and allowing the longest bottom times of the four wrecks. The wreck split nearly in two when it sank; the torpedo hole amidships, where steel plates are peeled back, is a primary feature. The massive intact propeller with one blade missing a chunk from hitting bedrock is a highlight. The forecastle, poop deck, a complete bathtub, and a lifeboat frame reduced to its outline in marine growth are all identifiable. A derrick arrangement in each cargo hold is visible. Water temperature is approximately 7°C (45°F) throughout the descent. Visibility can be variable with a white haze reported at times; the site receives the most light of the four Bell Island wrecks, making it excellent for photography.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only. Ocean Quest Adventures (Conception Bay South, NL) operates guided trips from spring through autumn. The PLM-27 is recommended for all certification levels given its recreational depth and long bottom times. The anchor and chain from the PLM-27 are displayed at the Bell Island war memorial ashore.

## Tips and Recommendations

The PLM-27's light penetration and shallowest profile make it the most photogenic and accessible of the four Bell Island wrecks. Drysuit and thermal undergarments are mandatory despite the relatively shallow depth — Conception Bay temperatures remain cold year-round. Because this is the easiest of the four wrecks, it is often used as an introduction to the Bell Island WWII dive sites before progressing to the deeper Lord Strathcona and Rose Castle.

## Safety Considerations

Cold water (approximately 7°C / 45°F) requires full drysuit protection for comfortable diving. As with all Bell Island wrecks, this is a designated war grave; no artefact removal is permitted. The split hull creates some overhead areas; divers entering the interior should carry appropriate lighting.
