## MSC Baltics III

MSC Baltics III is a historic wreck dive in Newfoundland, North America.

## Overview

The MSC Baltic III is a large containership that grounded on the northern coast of Newfoundland in February 2025. As of mid-2026 she is the subject of an active industrial salvage operation, not a diveable site: crews have removed 471 of her 472 cargo containers, dismantled the superstructure and funnel, and began pulling the hull toward shore on July 31, 2026, splitting the vessel at the midship break in the process. Approaching the site is prohibited while salvage work is underway. This entry is retained for reference, but divers should not expect it to become an accessible wreck dive in the foreseeable future — full remediation is expected to extend into 2027, and the vessel is being actively broken apart rather than left in place.

## Current Status

The MSC Baltic III (a 33,767 dwt containership) grounded on February 15, 2025, after losing power in severe winter weather near Wild Cove in the Bay of Islands, Newfoundland. All crew members were rescued by Royal Canadian Air Force helicopter. Resolve Marine Group, contracted by Mediterranean Shipping Company, has led the removal effort since spring 2026. As of late July 2026: bulk fuel has been removed (a quantity of residual fuel remains onboard, with spill-response measures on standby), 471 of 472 cargo containers have been extracted (the last is too deformed to remove), and the superstructure and funnel are being cut apart and staged for recycling. Hatch covers have been removed and a water treatment system installed and tested. Pulling operations to bring the forward section ashore after splitting the hull at the midship break began July 31, 2026. The goal is to complete the bulk of the work before winter weather sets in, with final remediation potentially continuing into 2027.

## Safety Considerations

This site is off-limits to recreational divers. The Canadian Coast Guard advises the public to remain clear given the scale of dismantling work now underway at the site. Monitor Canadian Coast Guard and Transport Canada advisories for updates before considering any visit.
