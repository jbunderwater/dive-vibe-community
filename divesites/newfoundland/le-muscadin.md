## Le Muscadin

Le Muscadin is an intentionally beached wooden hulk at the entrance to Miquelon village — a shore-viewable historic wreck rather than a conventional underwater dive site.

## Overview

Le Muscadin is a wooden vessel, built in 1967 at Bonavista, Newfoundland (reportedly the last wooden boat built at the Bonavista workshops), that ended its working life as a sand barge before being intentionally run aground at the entrance to the village of Miquelon around 2011. She sits largely on land and in the intertidal zone rather than fully submerged, and has been left to deteriorate in place since a planned museum project for the hulk did not go ahead. This entry previously (and incorrectly) described Le Muscadin as a 25-meter underwater wreck dive with tropical-style macro life — that description does not match any available source and has been removed in this QA pass. As it stands, Le Muscadin is best treated as a shore-accessible historic curiosity rather than a scuba destination.

## Wreck History

Le Muscadin was originally launched as the *Prudence Ann*, a fishing vessel built in 1967 at the Bonavista workshops in Newfoundland — reportedly the last wooden vessel built there. She was later purchased by a Miquelon resident, Joseph Poirier, who renamed her Muscadin after a family nickname, and subsequently sold to the Allen-Mahé company, which used her as a materials transport and sand barge. Around 2011, she was intentionally run aground at the entrance to Miquelon village. Ownership passed to Miquelon's Syndicat d'Initiative with plans for a museographic display, but the Syndicat is no longer active and the hulk remains, slowly deteriorating, at the village's edge on the road to Langlade.

## Entry and Exit

Viewable/accessible from shore at the entrance to Miquelon village, on the road to Langlade. This is not an established scuba dive site — no dive operator or diveable-depth information was found in any source consulted. Divers or visitors interested in Saint-Pierre and Miquelon's actual dive sites should contact the Club Nautique Saint-Pierrais directly.

## Safety Considerations

As an aging, deteriorating wooden hulk largely out of the water, hazards are more consistent with visiting a shoreline ruin than a dive site: unstable/rotting timber, rusted fittings, and tidal conditions around the base of the hulk. This entry should not be treated as a recreational scuba dive without further site-specific confirmation.
