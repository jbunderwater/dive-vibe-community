## Tuwanek

Tuwanek Point is a shore dive on Sechelt Inlet on the Sunshine Coast, with two small rocky islets offshore, granite walls and a reputation for wolf eels and giant Pacific octopus. Sources describe it as dived by day and night.

## Overview

DAN describes a shore dive with a "healthy surface swim" to two small islands, with smooth sloping granite walls, boot sponges, feather stars, sea cucumbers and anemones. Vancouver Scuba Diving School describes the north island as one of the three Lamb Islets, with a rocky drop-off on its western side and boulders on the bottom, and the southern island as the deeper dive. Gear-up is on the beach; the school describes a few steps to the water, with parking for three vehicles and a picnic table.

ScubaBoard posters in 2007 describe the good diving as on the back side of both islands. The north (right) island is a sloping rock bottom to about 80 ft (24 m) with more life and easier buoyancy, and the south (left) island is a vertical wall reported to as deep as 130 ft (40 m), though the poster only followed it to about 100 ft. This depth comes from a single forum post.

ScubaBoard posters from 2010 and 2012 describe it as one of the least current-sensitive sites they know, with no real need to plan around current.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tuwanek Point, Sechelt Inlet, Sunshine Coast, British Columbia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Current**: Minimal to none, per three ScubaBoard posters
- **Best Time**: A winter dive report described visibility of at least 50 ft

## Marine Life

Vancouver Scuba Diving School lists wolf eels, octopus and seals around the north island. ScubaBoard posters report a resident wolf eel, small octopuses and an occasional seal in 2007, and a pair of wolf eels on the north islet, giant Pacific octopus and a juvenile wolf eel on the south islet in 2010. DAN reports boot sponges, feather stars, sea cucumbers, anemones, gobies, moon jellies and a buffalo sculpin guarding egg clutches, and notes the divers missed a wolf eel and an octopus others had seen the week before. X-Ray Mag describes large, abundant octopus, wolf eels, seals and sea lions, large Puget Sound king crabs and carpets of anemones and sponges.

## Dive Profile

DAN and ScubaBoard describe a surface swim or a bottom-contour route (one 2007 poster describes descending to about 40 ft near the float dock and following the contour on the left for about 15 minutes to a small cove) to reach the islets. The north island's slope suits slower buoyancy practice; the south island's wall is the deeper option.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the beach, with the length of the walk to the water depending on the tide (a ScubaBoard poster notes 5 ft versus 50 ft from the street). Return to the beach to exit.

## Tips and Recommendations

- X-Ray Mag recommends diving it by day and again at night, as the marine life differs.
- Check Porpoise Bay or Point Atkinson tide corrections to plan the walk (ScubaBoard).

## Safety Considerations

The south island's wall drops quickly to depths of 30 m and more in cold water, so watch depth carefully. Dive with a buddy and use a surface marker buoy for the surface swim.

## Photography

DAN and X-Ray Mag describe boot sponges, feather stars and anemones as macro and wide-angle subjects, with wolf eels and octopus as the headline subjects.
