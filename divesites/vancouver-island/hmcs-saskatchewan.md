## HMCS Saskatchewan

HMCS Saskatchewan is a former Royal Canadian Navy Mackenzie-class destroyer escort, sunk as an artificial reef off Snake Island near Nanaimo on 14 June 1997. She sits upright and is dived by boat from Nanaimo.

## Overview

HMCS Saskatchewan (DDE 262) was laid down in 1959, launched on 1 February 1961 and commissioned on 16 February 1963. She was paid off in 1994, bought by the Artificial Reef Society of British Columbia (ARSBC) and scuttled off Snake Island on 14 June 1997. The ARSBC lists her as its fifth reef project. One dive-travel article gives the sinking as October 1997, but Wikipedia, the Canadian navy history page, ScubaBC and the ARSBC project list all give June 1997.

Sources agree on a length of 366 ft (about 111-112 m). Displacement is given as 2,880 tonnes (2,830 long tons) full load on Wikipedia and about 2,900 tons by ScubaBC.

Reported depths vary by source and tide. ScubaBC, X-Ray Mag and Divers Alert Network put the seabed at about 130 ft (40 m). Nanaimo Dive Outfitters gives 24-40 m overall and 30-36 m for the dive profile. Reported structure depths include the radar platform at about 45 ft (14 m), the bridge at about 65 ft (20 m), the forward guns at about 100 ft (30 m) and the fore and aft decks at about 85-95 ft (26-29 m). Nanaimo Dive Outfitters describes a shared mid-ship buoy as the usual start point and a diver passage along the ship it calls "Burma Road".

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Snake Island near Nanaimo, British Columbia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Current**: Described as mild to slight by Divers Alert Network and X-Ray Mag
- **Best Time**: Nanaimo Dive Outfitters reports visibility is usually best September through March, with spring and summer clarity reduced by algae blooms and herring spawn

## Marine Life

Reports specific to this wreck:

- Plumose anemones cover the hull and bow guns (Nanaimo Dive Outfitters, Scuba Diver Life, Divers Alert Network).
- Lingcod and rockfish; Scuba Diver Life names tiger, China and copper rockfish (Nanaimo Dive Outfitters, ScubaBC, Divers Alert Network).
- Nudibranchs (Nanaimo Dive Outfitters, ScubaBC).
- Schools of shiner perch and baitfish around the bridge and upper structure (Nanaimo Dive Outfitters).
- Cabezon, cloud sponges and feather stars (ScubaBC; X-Ray Mag also mentions cabezon and cloud sponges on the larger Nanaimo wrecks).
- Crabs, shrimp and tunicates (Divers Alert Network).

## Dive Profile

Charters run from Brechin Boat Launch in Nanaimo, with a 15-20 minute ride to the site according to Nanaimo Dive Outfitters. Dives typically start at the mid-ship buoy, from which divers can work toward the bow guns and bridge or toward the stern. Operators recommend Advanced Open Water or equivalent, recent experience around 30 m, and nitrox for longer bottom times.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by charter boat from Nanaimo. Descent is made on an ascent line or mooring on the wreck; one trip report describes a line moored at the bow. Divers should follow the skipper's briefing for ascent and pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Book with a Nanaimo charter operator that knows the site and its mooring.
- Carry a primary light and a backup if exploring beyond the open exterior.
- Maintain good buoyancy; operators note silt and delicate growth inside the ship.
- Cold water: Nanaimo Dive Outfitters and trip reports describe water around 8-10 C, so appropriate thermal protection is needed.

## Safety Considerations

This is an advanced site because of depth and cold water. Penetrating any wreck carries risk, and entering the interior requires wreck-diving training, proper lights, redundant gas planning and a guideline; ScubaBC describes the ship as prepared for penetration by certified wreck divers, and a trip report describes a primary light failing inside the ship. Dive within your training, plan gas for the 30 m range and use a surface marker buoy.

## Photography

Plumose anemones on the guns and hull, and the open bridge and upper structure, are the main subjects described by operators and trip reports.
