## Dodd Narrows

Dodd Narrows is a narrow tidal channel between Mudge Island and Vancouver Island near Nanaimo, dived as a boat-supported drift around slack water. Sources agree it is a high-current dive for experienced divers.

## Overview

Element Escapes describes boat access only, with live boat drops the most common method, in a channel about 55 m (180 ft) wide at its narrowest and with currents up to 9 knots. DAN also reports currents peaking over 9 knots, with about 25 minutes around slack water before the current picks up; its author's group drifted roughly half a mile with the boat following the bubbles. DAN places the wall starting around 70 ft with the canyon floor about 20 ft deeper, and a buttress at 35 ft that offers shelter from the current. Element Escapes gives an approximate depth of 27 m (90 ft). PNW Diving calls it a high-current boat dive, advanced because of the current and heavy boat traffic.

Season advice differs. DAN reports heavy summer boat traffic and dives it in fall, while PNW Diving says it is generally diveable only on the mildest days in early spring and that spring herring spawn can make visibility very poor.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Dodd Narrows, between Mudge Island and Vancouver Island, near Nanaimo
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters
- **Current**: Up to 9 knots (Element Escapes, DAN)
- **Best Time**: Sources disagree: fall (DAN) or mild early-spring days (PNW Diving); sea lions are present in winter (Element Escapes)

## Marine Life

Element Escapes reports schools of rockfish, sea stars, mosshead warbonnets and colourful anemones and other invertebrates covering vertical walls on the Mudge Island side, and California and Steller sea lions hunting salmon in the current in winter. DAN reports frilly and Tealia anemones, barnacles, sculpin, purple ochre sea stars, yellow sponges, quillback rockfish, scallops, surfperches and bull kelp. PNW Diving reports nudibranchs and anemones, with painted anemones standing out.

## Dive Profile

DAN describes entering near slack water, then drifting along the wall with the current while the boat follows the bubbles. Element Escapes notes the live drop as the usual approach.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only. Use a surface marker buoy for pickup. Element Escapes warns of strong currents, boat traffic, kelp and orcas, and notes there is no parking, washroom or garbage receptacle at the access point.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Element Escapes strongly advises diving with an experienced guide or on a charter.
- Check current tables and dive only at slack.
- Plan around boat traffic, which both DAN and PNW Diving describe as heavy in the warmer months.

## Safety Considerations

This is an advanced drift dive: currents of up to 9 knots, boat traffic and cold water. Dive with an experienced operator, stay with the group and carry a surface marker buoy.

## Photography

Wall invertebrates (anemones, sponges, sea stars) are the main subjects reported by Element Escapes, DAN and PNW Diving.
