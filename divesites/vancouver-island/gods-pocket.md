## God's Pocket

God's Pocket Marine Provincial Park lies off the northern tip of Vancouver Island near Port Hardy and is known for current-swept walls, pinnacles and kelp in and around Browning Pass. It is dived by boat, mostly around slack water.

## Overview

BC Parks places the park on the north side of Goletas Channel, about 10 km northwest of Port Hardy, at the entrance to Queen Charlotte Strait. Dive Magazine gives 20 km, and God's Pocket Resort gives about 10 nautical miles by boat from Port Hardy, so reported distances range from roughly 10 to 20 km. The park includes Hurst, Bell, Boyle and Crane Islands and several smaller islets, and is reached by boat or floatplane. BC Parks says most of the park's scuba diving takes place in nearby Browning Pass and that divers should be experienced and well equipped.

God's Pocket Resort, on Hurst Island, describes Browning Pass diving as walls, pinnacles, chutes, beaches and kelp forests, with 31 named sites whose locations are shared on the boat. It names Seven Tree, Fantasy Island, Hoodi Nudi Bay, Ruth's Rocks and Buttertart. Large tides (up to 18 ft) make the pass run fast; exposed sites are dived at slack in 45-60 minute dives, and some dives drift with the current.

The resort requires 100 logged dives including 50 cold-water drysuit dives, and a recognised diver medical within three months of the trip.

## Site Information

- **Location**: God's Pocket Marine Provincial Park, near Port Hardy, northern Vancouver Island
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Dive Magazine reports 6-25 m depending on season
- **Current**: Strong tidal currents; dives are timed to slack water
- **Best Time**: God's Pocket Resort runs mid-March to end of May and mid-August to end of October, reporting mid-March and late October as often clearest and late May and late August as often calmest

## Marine Life

God's Pocket Resort reports red soft corals, bright yellow sponges, giant white plumose anemones (metridiums) and kelp forests; giant acorn barnacles; giant Pacific octopus; wolf eels, including four resident at Fantasy Island; white-lined dirona nudibranchs; glove sponges; and decorated warbonnets and grunt sculpins. It also reports humpback whales at the surface in some spring and fall weeks. Dive Magazine names plumose anemones, Puget Sound king crabs, sunflower sea stars, rockfish and giant Pacific octopus in the park, and BC Parks notes whales and bald eagles are common. These are park-wide reports rather than being tied to one dive site.

## Dive Profile

God's Pocket Resort sets "lines off" times around slack water, which shifts daily, and guests plan their own dives after a briefing, with no in-water guide. Some sites are drift dives and the resort does not use reef hooks.

## Entry and Exit

God's Pocket Resort runs dives from a 40-foot aluminum dive boat with a dive elevator and diver recall system. Dive days depend on weather and the captain decides whether to go out.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Water temperature is reported at 7-9 C by God's Pocket Resort, 10.5 C by Dive Magazine and 44-54 F (about 7-12 C) by Divers Alert Network; a drysuit is expected.
- The resort requires a dive computer and surface marker buoy, dive-accident insurance with evacuation cover, and your own exposure protection and regulators.
- DAN describes summer plankton blooms reducing visibility and winter as too stormy.

## Safety Considerations

This is an advanced destination because of tidal current, cold water and remoteness. Follow slack-water timing from the operator, carry a surface marker buoy and dive within your training. The park has no facilities or potable water (BC Parks).

## Photography

God's Pocket Resort and DAN describe colourful walls of soft corals, sponges and anemones, with nudibranchs and other invertebrates for macro work.
