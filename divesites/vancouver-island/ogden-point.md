## Ogden Point

Ogden Point Breakwater in Victoria is a shore dive along an 800 m granite and concrete breakwater, with depth increasing from a shallow sandy bay to deeper sections at the outer end. Visitor in Victoria calls it the city's most accessible shore dive.

## Overview

Element Escapes and Zentacle both describe the breakwater as about 800 m long, built of stacked granite blocks and concrete. Zentacle describes five flags along the wall: from the beach to Flag 1 the bottom shallows slowly to 8 m; Flag 2 is at 8-17 m; Flag 3 at 17-20 m; Flag 4 at 22-25 m; and Flag 5 at 28-35 m over mud and sand with tumbled granite blocks. Element Escapes gives an approximate maximum of 24 m (80 ft), with the second area reaching about 25 m and reef balls at 10-15 m. A ScubaBoard poster described about 60 fsw in the outer section. This page records 35 m as the upper reported figure, noting that sources range from about 24 m to 35 m. Zentacle's listing of 15 m is inconsistent with its own flag description.

Element Escapes describes granite and concrete "stairs" from the surface to the seafloor, five painted dive flags marking underwater plaques, and ample parking (paid or street) with no public washrooms. It describes currents as moderate to strong, especially at depth, with the third area past the second bend having strong currents and larger waves.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Ogden Point Breakwater, Victoria, British Columbia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong, especially at depth (Element Escapes)
- **Best Time**: Waters around Victoria are generally clearest in fall and winter (Visitor in Victoria)

## Marine Life

Element Escapes lists rockfish, giant Pacific octopus, lingcod, greenlings, crustaceans, anemones, nudibranchs and wolf eels. Zentacle lists several rockfish (canary, tiger, black, yellowtail, Puget Sound, quillback), octopus, harbour seals, dogfish, ratfish, wolf eels, lingcod, greenlings, perch, flounder, grunt sculpins, warbonnets, king crab, swimming scallops, nudibranchs and metridium and plumose anemones. ScubaBoard posters report wolf eels, octopus, rockfish, convict fish, large lingcod, scallops, crabs and nudibranchs. X-Ray Mag reports rockfish, kelp greenlings, nudibranchs, perch and sea stars, with octopus and wolf eels denning in the blocks and large reef balls added as habitat.

## Dive Profile

Dives follow the breakwater out from the beach, with the shallow sandy bay to Flag 1, the mid-sections to Flag 4, and the deepest outer section at Flag 5. A ScubaBoard poster recommends walking the top of the breakwater and diving back toward shore along the wall. Element Escapes advises novices to dive the second area with a guide.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the beach, the breakwater or a dive boat (Zentacle). Entry from the breakwater needs care because of barnacles and concrete blocks hidden by bull kelp. Entry and exit points can be anywhere along the breakwater.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Element Escapes notes hazards including slippery barnacles and blocks, fishing tackle from permitted line fishing, and boat traffic.
- ScubaBoard posters report water of about 7-8 C and describe 7 mm wetsuit or drysuit use.
- Dive the sections that match your experience: the shallow bay for training, the outer sections for experienced divers.

## Safety Considerations

Depth and current rise toward the outer end of the breakwater, so the site is rated Intermediate overall. Plan around slack water, watch for fishing line and boat traffic, and use a surface marker buoy.

## Photography

Element Escapes and ScubaBoard describe anemones, nudibranchs, crabs and fish among the blocks; X-Ray Mag notes added reef balls as habitat.
