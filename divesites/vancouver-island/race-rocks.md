## Race Rocks

Race Rocks is an ecological reserve at the eastern end of the Strait of Juan de Fuca, about 17 km southwest of Victoria, with fast tidal flow, kelp, sea lion haul-outs and dives made from boats around slack water.

## Overview

BritishColumbia.com describes the reserve as one of the most challenging dive sites in the Strait of Juan de Fuca, about 17 km southwest of Victoria, with tidal flow up to 7 knots and currents often above 5-6 knots. Slack tides occur every 6.5 hours and form the diving window. Visitor in Victoria reports currents up to 8 knots and a boat-accessed dive for more experienced divers. The same BritishColumbia.com page reports that the marine area extends to 120 ft (37 m), that divers may search for debris from over 100 ships wrecked at Race Rocks, and that spring and fall are the best seasons for visibility.

The reserve was established by the Province of British Columbia in 1980 and declared a candidate Marine Protected Area in 1998 (BritishColumbia.com). Racerocks.ca refers to a Rockfish Conservation Area and a Marine Protected Area advisory board for the reserve. Tours and dive boats go from Pedder Bay and Victoria. The island is closed to the public and landing requires a permit (RV West).

## Site Information

- **Location**: Race Rocks Ecological Reserve, about 17 km southwest of Victoria
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 37 meters
- **Current**: Typically 5-7 knots (BritishColumbia.com), up to 8 knots (Visitor in Victoria)
- **Best Time**: Spring and fall for visibility (BritishColumbia.com); a dive-industry article also calls these the best seasons

## Marine Life

BritishColumbia.com lists sponges, anemones, hydroids, soft corals, barnacles, tunicates, urchins, sea stars and kelp, sculpin, rockfish and lingcod, and harbour seals, Steller and California sea lions and northern elephant seals in the reserve. X-Ray Mag describes pink and white soft coral, finger sponges, nudibranchs, coralline algae, kelp and brooding anemones on kelp stalks. Racerocks.ca records Steller and California sea lions hauling out around the main rock, along with harbour seals and northern elephant seals. These are reserve-wide reports rather than from a single dive location.

## Dive Profile

X-Ray Mag describes a fast, high-energy dive best explored during slack current. Operators time dives to slack, which recurs about every 6.5 hours.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only, typically from Pedder Bay or Victoria. Arrange entry and pickup with the operator and carry a surface marker buoy.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Book with an operator that regularly dives the reserve; BritishColumbia.com describes it as one of the most challenging sites in the strait.
- Water temperature near Victoria is 8-12 C (Visitor in Victoria), so a drysuit or thick wetsuit is needed.
- Check current reserve rules and any Rockfish Conservation Area restrictions with your operator.

## Safety Considerations

This is an advanced current dive with currents often over 5-6 knots outside slack. Dive only at slack, with an experienced operator, and carry a surface marker buoy.

## Photography

X-Ray Mag highlights soft corals, finger sponges, nudibranchs and anemones on kelp stalks.
