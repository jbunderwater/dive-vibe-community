## Grotta della Pastizza

Grotta della Pastizza is a cave dive site in Ustica, Europe.

## Overview

Grotta della Pastizza is an articulated system of interconnected caves near Ustica's harbour, named for the "Pastizzu," a pyramid-shaped rock that marks the entrance. The cave has considerable differences in height between its cavities and contains two chambers that emerge above sea level; divers can surface inside one of them and remove their masks beside a statue of the island's patron saint, San Bartolomeo. Light effects inside the cave are described by multiple operators as particularly striking.

**Marine life (re-verified 2026-07-08):** Dive operator listings for the site describe pipefish, spider crabs, brown groupers, amberjacks, jacks, slipper lobsters, sponges, stony corals, and nudibranchs by day. Night dives — for which the site is specifically recommended — turn up conger eels, moray eels, shrimps, cuttlefish, octopus, sea hares, cowries (Cypraea), further nudibranchs, and the night anemone Alicia mirabilis. Two distinct external areas exist around the entrance rock: one with stones and seagrass, the other a drop-off wall.

Sources disagree on maximum depth (19-22 metres depending on source) and the previously published "83-metre" cave length could not be reconfirmed this round, so it has been removed rather than repeated.

## Site Information
