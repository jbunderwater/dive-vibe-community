## Azores

Atlantic paradise with blue sharks, mobula rays, and pristine volcanic formations.

## Description

Azores is a notable diving destination in the Europe region, offering diverse dive sites with depths ranging from 18 to 60 meters. Water temperatures average 16-22°C (61-72°F) year-round, peaking at 23-25°C (73-77°F) in August and dropping to 16-17°C (61-63°F) in winter; visibility typically reaches 10-40 meters (30-130 feet). The diving season runs May to October, with the best conditions during May to October.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Boat Diving**: Boat-accessible sites reached through local dive operators
- **Wreck Diving**: Historic shipwrecks preserved in the region's waters
- **Night Diving**: After-dark diving reveals nocturnal marine species and different reef behaviors

### Accessibility

- **Getting There**: Azores is accessible via international and regional flights to nearby airports. Check with airlines for current routes and connections.
- **Dive Operators**: Professional dive operators offer equipment rental, guided dives, certification courses, and boat trips to offshore sites.
- **Accommodation**: Options range from dedicated dive resorts to budget-friendly guesthouses, with many properties located near popular dive sites.
- **Transportation**: Local transportation and dive operator transfers are the primary means of reaching dive sites.
- **Facilities**: Dive sites vary in available amenities; operator-run sites typically provide comprehensive facilities while remote sites may have limited infrastructure.

### Marine Life & Environment

- **Water Conditions**: Water temperatures range from 16-22°C (61-72°F) on average, peaking around 23-25°C in August and dropping to 16-17°C in winter, with visibility of 10-40 meters (30-130 feet). Currents are generally light to moderate, though open-Atlantic seamounts (Princess Alice Bank, Dom João de Castro Bank) see moderate to strong currents. Gulf-Stream influence keeps the Azores well short of true cold-water diving destinations (e.g. Norway, Svalbard, Greenland, the Great Lakes) — this project does not apply the cold-water minimum-Intermediate-difficulty rule to Azores as a whole. Individual sites are still rated on their own depth/current/access profile per the standard difficulty rubric.
- **Marine Biodiversity**: The waters support diverse marine ecosystems including groupers, moray eels, octopus, barracuda, sea bream, amberjack, nudibranchs, seahorses, posidonia seagrass, red coral.
- **Conservation**: Local conservation efforts help protect marine habitats and ensure sustainable diving practices.

## Additional Information

- **Best Time to Visit**: May to October. Outside the main season, conditions may be less favorable.
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR)
- **Language**: Portuguese
- **Safety**: Always dive within certification limits. Be aware of cold water (north), currents, boat traffic. Verify the location of the nearest hyperbaric chamber before diving.
