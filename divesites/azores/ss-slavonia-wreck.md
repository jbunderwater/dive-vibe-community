# SS Slavonia Wreck

A 155-metre Cunard liner that ran aground off Flores in 1909 — widely cited as the first ship to transmit an SOS distress signal.

## Overview

The RMS Slavonia (originally SS Yamuna) was built in 1902 by Sir J. Laing & Sons at Sunderland. Originally laid down as the *Yamuna* for the British India Steam Navigation Company, she was purchased by Cunard Line before completion and renamed. At 10,606 GRT and 155 metres (510 ft) in length, she carried up to 71 first-class, 74 second-class, and 1,954 steerage passengers.

On 10 June 1909, she ran aground in foggy conditions at Ponta dos Fenais (Baixa Rasa), Flores, while en route from New York to the Mediterranean. All aboard were rescued without loss of life: *Prinzess Irene* took off 110 cabin-class passengers and *Batavia* took off 300 steerage-class passengers, with the remaining crew (225 total) leaving the ship later that day — for a rescued total of 635. One secondary source instead gives 410 as the number of passengers aboard; the two figures are not directly reconcilable from available sources and may reflect different accounting (e.g., passengers only vs. passengers plus crew). She was declared a total loss and abandoned; insured for £90,000. Cargo recovered included 400 bags of coffee, 1,000 copper ingots, 200 casks of oil, and 25 pieces of agricultural machinery, shipped onward to Liverpool aboard the *Letty*.

The SOS claim: multiple sources, including a dedicated history of early SOS use, credit the *Slavonia* with transmitting the first maritime SOS distress call, sent by the wireless operator at the Radio-Flores station on her behalf. Wikipedia flags this specific claim as needing a stronger citation, and the exact timing of the transmission is not precisely documented. A competing claim for RMS Republic's January 1909 distress call is not equivalent, since Republic used the earlier Marconi "CQD" signal rather than SOS. The Slavonia claim is widely repeated and not contradicted by an earlier confirmed SOS use, but it remains without a definitive primary-source citation.

The wreck lies at approximately 18 metres depth, less than 50 metres from the coast at Lajedo, Flores. It was designated an Underwater Archaeological Park in 2015. The wreck is substantially broken up after more than a century of wave action.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Flores is remote; most visitors arrive by inter-island ferry or small plane
- The wreck is heavily broken up after 115+ years; remains scattered at 18 m
