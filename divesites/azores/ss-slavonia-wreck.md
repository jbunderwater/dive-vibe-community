# SS Slavonia Wreck

A 155-metre Cunard liner that ran aground off Flores in 1909 — widely cited as the first ship to transmit an SOS distress signal.

## Overview

The RMS Slavonia (originally SS Yamuna) was built by Sir J. Laing & Sons at Sunderland; sources differ slightly on the build year, with Wikipedia giving 1902 and GG Archives giving a 1903 launch. Originally laid down as the *Yamuna* for the British India Steam Navigation Company, she was purchased by Cunard Line before completion and renamed. Length is consistently reported at 155 metres (510 ft); gross tonnage is given as 10,605–10,606 GRT after an early refit (Wikipedia notes an initial 8,831 GRT before the refit). She carried up to 71 first-class, 74 second-class, and 1,954 steerage passengers in her refitted configuration.

On 10 June 1909, she ran aground in foggy conditions at Ponta dos Fenais, Flores, while en route from New York to Trieste (Mediterranean service). All passengers and crew were rescued without loss of life by the ships *Prinzess Irene* and *Batavia*; sources give differing totals aboard — a dedicated Azores travel source cites 410, while Wikipedia records 110 cabin passengers, 300 steerage passengers, and 225 crew (approximately 635 total). The ship subsequently broke apart in later storms; she was declared a total loss around 15–16 June. Some cargo was recovered: 400 bags of coffee, 1,000 copper ingots, and 200 casks of oil.

The SOS claim: multiple sources state the *Slavonia* sent the first maritime SOS signal in history. Wikipedia notes this claim requires a stronger source; the signal's use predated widespread adoption of SOS (CQD remained the Marconi standard in 1909). The claim is historically significant and widely cited but not yet conclusively verified by a primary source.

The wreck lies at approximately 18 metres depth, less than 50 metres from the coast at Lajedo, Flores — figures independently confirmed by a dedicated Azores dive/travel source in addition to general historical accounts. It was designated an Underwater Archaeological Park in 2015. The wreck is substantially broken up after more than a century of wave action.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Historically notable: widely cited as the first ship to send an SOS signal (1909) — though this claim remains subject to historical debate
- 155-metre Cunard liner, 10,606 GRT — significant maritime heritage site
- Underwater Archaeological Park since 2015 — protected status
- Flores is remote; most visitors arrive by inter-island ferry or small plane
- The wreck is heavily broken up after 115+ years; remains scattered at 18 m
