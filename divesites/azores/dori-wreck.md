# Dori Wreck

A WWII Liberty ship that took part in Operation Overlord — now a 134-metre wreck resting 800 metres off Ponta Delgada, considered the best dive on São Miguel.

## Overview

The Dori Wreck is considered by many local operators the best dive site on São Miguel. Correcting an earlier misidentification in this database: the wreck is **not** the Liberty ship *Thomas Donaldson* (which was torpedoed in 1945 near the Kola Inlet, Russia, and remains there — divers recovered artifacts from that separate wreck as recently as 2016). Wikipedia's "List of Liberty ships (E)" and the Maritime Administration (MARAD) vessel-history database instead identify the Dori as the former **SS Edwin L. Drake**, an EC2-S-C1 Liberty ship built by Bethlehem Fairfield Shipyard, Baltimore — keel laid 26 June 1943, launched 31 July 1943, delivered 10 August 1943. She measured 441.5 ft (134.4–134.5 m) in length with a gross tonnage of 7,176 GRT.

During WWII she sailed in convoy service that included stops at Omaha Beach, Normandy, consistent with local operator accounts that she "took part in Operation Overlord along the coast of Normandy." After the war she passed through several owners and names — Wikipedia records intermediate names including *Praxiteles* before she became the Liberian-flagged cargo ship *Dori*. On 16 January 1964, while en route from Emden, West Germany to New Orleans with a cargo of steel coils, she sprang a leak off Ponta Delgada; she was beached but subsequently sank, coming to rest approximately 800 metres off the São Miguel coast. Now an Underwater Archaeological Park and no-take marine reserve, the wreck rests on a sandy seabed. The stern top sits at 9 metres — accessible to snorkellers — with the bow at 20–21 metres.

## Site Information

- **Location**: 800 m off Ponta Delgada, São Miguel, Azores
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 meters
- **Current**: Weak
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Clouds of small fish surround the stern; Moroccan white seabreams (*Diplodus cervinus*) gather around the boilers. Moray eels, octopuses, nudibranchs, and groupers inhabit the structure. Schools of Almaco jacks (*Seriola rivoliana*), yellowmouth barracudas (*Sphyraena viridensis*), and axillary sea breams are observed in the water column. The no-take reserve status supports denser populations than surrounding unprotected reef.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Considered the best dive on São Miguel by multiple local operators
- WWII veteran (as SS Edwin L. Drake): built 1943, sailed convoy routes including Omaha Beach during Operation Overlord
- Only 5 minutes by boat from Ponta Delgada — convenient day-dive access
- Underwater Archaeological Park and no-take marine reserve
- Stern at 9 m is excellent for snorkelling; bow at 20–21 m for deeper exploration
- Suitable for night dives
- Wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment — always carries risk regardless of experience level
