# Dori Wreck

A WWII-built Liberty ship — later identified as SS Edwin L. Drake — resting as a 134-metre wreck 800 metres off Ponta Delgada, widely cited by local operators as the best dive on São Miguel.

## Overview

The Dori is considered by many local operators the best dive site on São Miguel. Correcting an earlier misattribution in this database: the wreck is not the WWII casualty "SS Thomas Donaldson" (that vessel was torpedoed by U-968 in March 1945 and remains a separate, still-existing wreck on the seabed near Kildin Island, Russia — it was never raised or repaired). Independent research (Wikipedia's List of Liberty Ships, scubaweather.com, and a dedicated historical-documentary paper by Portuguese maritime archaeologist Alexandre Monteiro) instead identifies the Dori as the former **SS Edwin L. Drake**, a Liberty EC2-S-C1 class ship built in 1943 by the Bethlehem-Fairfield Shipyard, Baltimore (7,176 GRT, approximately 134 metres long). During WWII she called at ports including Norfolk, Gibraltar, Molotovsk, Kola, and Omaha Beach, France — consistent with wartime Atlantic convoy and Normandy supply-run service, though the specific "Operation Overlord" framing repeated by Azores dive-tourism sites lacks a primary citation and should be treated as likely but not fully confirmed.

After the war she passed through several owners and names — *Seadrake* (1951), *Phoenix* (1954), *Anassa* (1957, Panama flag), *Praxiteles* (1960) — before being renamed *Dori* under Liberian registry in 1962. On 16 January 1964, while en route (reportedly from Emden, West Germany, to New Orleans) carrying a cargo of steel coils, she developed a leak and was beached; she subsequently sank approximately 800 metres off the Ponta Delgada coast. The wreck now sits within a Marine Cultural Reserve / Underwater Archaeological Park and no-take area on a sandy seabed. The stern top sits around 9 metres — accessible to snorkellers — with the bow at 20–22 metres (dive.visitazores.com gives a 21m maximum depth; PADI's listing gives 22m).

## Site Information

- **Location**: 800 m off Ponta Delgada, São Miguel, Azores
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 meters
- **Current**: Weak
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Dive.visitazores.com confirms clouds of small fish and shoals of pelagic fish around the wreck, with a Moroccan/white seabream (*Diplodus* sp.) aggregation around the boilers — sources give conflicting species-level identifications for this seabream, so it is described here at genus level rather than a specific binomial pending a clearer source. PADI's site listing separately tags the location for shark, whale, dolphin, and turtle sightings; these read as PADI's general regional dive-finder categories rather than confirmed individual sightings at this specific wreck, so treat them as indicative rather than guaranteed. Earlier entries for this site listed moray eels, octopuses, nudibranchs, groupers, Almaco jacks, yellowmouth barracuda, and axillary sea bream — these could not be re-confirmed against a site-specific source in this pass and have been removed pending better sourcing. The no-take reserve status is reported to support denser fish populations than unprotected surrounding areas.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Considered the best dive on São Miguel by multiple local operators
- Correct ship identity is SS Edwin L. Drake (built 1943, Bethlehem-Fairfield Shipyard) — not "Thomas Donaldson," a different WWII-loss ship still resting near Russia
- Only 5 minutes by boat from Ponta Delgada — convenient day-dive access
- Marine Cultural Reserve / Underwater Archaeological Park and no-take area
- Stern at ~9 m is excellent for snorkelling; bow at 20–22 m for deeper exploration
- Suitable for night dives
- Wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment; interior exploration is never risk-free
