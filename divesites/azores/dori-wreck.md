# Dori Wreck

A WWII Liberty ship that took part in Operation Overlord — now a wreck resting 800 metres off Ponta Delgada, considered the best dive on São Miguel.

## Overview

The Dori Wreck is considered by many local operators the best dive site on São Miguel. She was a Liberty EC2-S-C1 class steam merchant, 7,176 GRT, built by Bethlehem-Fairfield Shipyards, Baltimore, and delivered in 1943 (specialist sources give a delivery date of 10 August 1943; a secondary account gives September 1943). Sources report her length as approximately 130–134 metres. She was launched and christened *SS Edwin L. Drake*, named for oil pioneer Edwin Laurentine Drake.

Specialist wreck-history research (an academic historical-documentary review of the vessel, and a dedicated Azores dive-site history page) independently and consistently identifies the ship's WWII service as the *Edwin L. Drake*: she sailed in convoy UGS 16 from Hampton Roads to Port Said in August–September 1943, her crew was credited with assists in downing a German Focke-Wulf aircraft, and she was one of the few ships of her type to take part in Operation Overlord off Normandy. **Note:** some Azores tourism pages identify the wreck's WWII-era ship as "SS Thomas Donaldson," a different Liberty ship torpedoed by U-968 in March 1945 during convoy JW-65. That identification is not supported by the specialist sources above — the U.S. Maritime Administration's official vessel-history record for Thomas Donaldson shows her as a separate total loss in 1945 with no documented connection to a later career or to Dori. This appears to be a mix-up between two different WWII Liberty ships and the "Thomas Donaldson" identity has been removed from this page pending better sourcing.

After the war, the ship changed hands and names several times before being renamed *Dori* by the early 1960s, operating as a Liberian-flagged cargo vessel. On 16 January 1964, while en route from Emden to New Orleans carrying a cargo of steel coils, she developed leaks and was beached/sank approximately 800 metres off the Ponta Delgada coast. Now an Underwater Archaeological Park and no-take marine reserve, the wreck rests on a sandy seabed. The stern top sits at 9 metres — accessible to snorkellers — with the bow at 20–21 metres.

## Site Information

- **Location**: 800 m off Ponta Delgada, São Miguel, Azores
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 meters
- **Current**: Weak
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Clouds of small fish surround the stern; Moroccan white seabreams (*Diplodus sargus cadenati*) gather around the boilers. Moray eels, octopuses, nudibranchs, and groupers inhabit the structure. Schools of Almaco jacks (*Seriola rivoliana*), yellowmouth barracudas (*Sphyraena viridensis*), and axillary sea breams are observed in the water column. The no-take reserve status supports denser populations than surrounding unprotected reef.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Considered the best dive on São Miguel by multiple local operators
- WWII veteran: sailed in convoy UGS 16 and took part in Operation Overlord as the *Edwin L. Drake* before later becoming *Dori*
- Only 5 minutes by boat from Ponta Delgada — convenient day-dive access
- Underwater Archaeological Park and no-take marine reserve
- Stern at 9 m is excellent for snorkelling; bow at 21 m for deeper exploration
- Suitable for night dives
- Wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment
