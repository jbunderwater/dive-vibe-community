# Dori Wreck

A WWII-built Liberty ship, later renamed several times, that sank in 1964 after a cargo hold leak — now a 134-metre wreck resting 800 metres off Ponta Delgada, considered one of the best dives on São Miguel.

## Overview

**Correction (2026-07-12):** Earlier versions of this entry identified the wreck as the SS Thomas Donaldson, torpedoed by U-968 in March 1945 and supposedly raised and renamed Dori. That is a documented mix-up with a different ship: the real Thomas Donaldson sank near Kildin Island in the Barents Sea and was never raised — Russian Northern Fleet divers were still salvaging her cargo (including a Sherman tank, 2014–2015) at that wreck site decades later, which is incompatible with a vessel later sailing as a commercial ship in the Atlantic. The two ships share only a coincidental resemblance (both Liberty-class, similar tonnage, both called at the Kola/Molotovsk area during the war), which is the likely source of the error.

The vessel that became the *Dori* was built as **SS Edwin L. Drake**, an EC2-S-C1 Liberty ship delivered from the Bethlehem-Fairfield Shipyard, Baltimore, in September 1943 (7,176 GRT, 441.5 ft / approximately 134.6 metres long, per the U.S. government's MARAD Vessel History Database). Detailed WWII voyage records (Allegany County Historical Society) show she made extensive Atlantic and Pacific convoy runs — UK ports, Gibraltar, Naples, Salerno, the Soviet ports of Molotovsk and Kola, and later Okinawa and Tokyo — and specifically list a call at **Omaha Beach, France**, consistent with dive.visitazores.com's claim that the ship later known as Dori took part in the Normandy campaign (Operation Overlord/Operation Neptune).

After the war she changed hands and names several times: renamed *Seadrake* (1952), *Phoenix* (1954), and *Anassa* under Liberian flag (1957), before becoming *Dori* in 1962. On 16 January 1964, while en route to New Orleans carrying a cargo of steel coils, she developed a leak and was beached/sank approximately 800 metres off the Ponta Delgada coast. Now protected as a Marine Cultural Reserve and no-take zone (PADI), the wreck rests on a sandy seabed. The stern top sits at 9 metres — accessible to snorkellers — with the bow between 20 and 22 metres depending on the source consulted.

## Site Information

- **Location**: 800 m off Ponta Delgada, São Miguel, Azores
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 meters
- **Current**: Weak
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Clouds of small fish surround the stern; Moroccan white seabreams (*Diplodus cervinus*) gather around the boilers. Moray eels, octopuses, nudibranchs, and groupers inhabit the structure. Schools of Almaco jacks (*Seriola rivoliana*), yellowmouth barracudas (*Sphyraena viridensis*), and axillary sea breams are observed in the water column. The no-take reserve status supports denser populations than surrounding unprotected reef.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Considered one of the best dives on São Miguel by multiple local operators
- WWII-built Liberty ship (as Edwin L. Drake, 1943) that record shows called at Omaha Beach during the Normandy campaign — not the torpedoed Thomas Donaldson previously (incorrectly) credited here
- Renamed four times (Seadrake, Phoenix, Anassa, Dori) before sinking in 1964 from a cargo-hold leak, not war action
- Only 5 minutes by boat from Ponta Delgada — convenient day-dive access
- Protected as a Marine Cultural Reserve and no-take zone
- Stern at 9 m is excellent for snorkelling; bow at 20–22 m (sources vary) for deeper exploration
- Suitable for night dives
- Wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment; it is not without risk despite the wreck's accessible depth
