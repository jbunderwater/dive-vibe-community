## Tanduk Rusa

Tanduk Rusa ("Deer Antler" in Indonesian) is an advanced dive site at the northern tip of Gili Banta (Banta Island), an uninhabited ancient volcanic island off the northwest coast of Sumbawa. Multiple sources place it just outside the official Komodo National Park boundary rather than inside it — one dive-shop review states plainly that Banta "lies a few miles from the Komodo National Park" and "is not included within the park boundaries," even though it sits immediately adjacent to the park and is commonly marketed alongside it. Sources confirm Tanduk Rusa functions as a manta ray cleaning station.

**Naming note**: Sources are inconsistent about site names in this corner of Gili Banta. A French-language trip report and a ScubaBoard thread both describe "Tanduk Rusa" and "Roller Coaster" as alternate names for the same location (the ScubaBoard thread also lists "Highway to Heaven" as a third name), while a dive-site database describes essentially the same promontory/current profile under the name "Tanjung Rasa a.k.a. Highway to Heaven." This database currently lists Tanduk Rusa and Rollercoaster as two separate sites with different coordinates and depths — that may be correct (two distinct nearby spots sharing a nickname), or it may reflect a duplicate entry from the same physical site being scraped under two names. This should be checked against GPS data before further edits.

## Overview

Tanduk Rusa sits at the northern part of Gili Banta and features an underwater shallow promontory at approximately 12 metres depth that functions as a manta ray cleaning station — mantas hover here to allow cleaner wrasses to remove parasites and dead skin. This is independently confirmed by a French dive trip report (which describes the exact 12 m promontory and cleaning-station behavior) and a ScubaBoard trip report referencing manta sightings "in the shallows" at Tanduk Rusa. The site is protected in a slight indent from the fierce currents that sweep around Banta's outer points, giving divers a chance to observe mantas in relatively stable conditions. The wider Gili Banta area is known for grey reef sharks, whitetip sharks, blacktip sharks, giant trevally, big-eyed jacks, and tuna feeding on the plankton-rich currents flowing through the Sape Strait/Flores Sea. Depths on the slopes reach 40 m. Advanced certification is required given the strong currents typical of the area and the need for negative entries. Access is typically by liveaboard or day trip from dive operators based in east Sumbawa (e.g., Kalimaya Dive Resort, roughly 40 minutes by speedboat) — this specific operator's own dive-site page could not be independently verified during this QA pass (it returned no readable content), so it is not counted as one of the confirming sources below.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern tip of Gili Banta, just outside the Komodo National Park boundary per most sources (though frequently marketed as part of the "Komodo diving" region)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Currents**: Strong; vertical and horizontal
- **Best Season**: April–November

## Marine Life

Manta rays and cleaner wrasses are confirmed specifically at this promontory. Grey reef sharks, whitetip sharks, blacktip sharks, giant trevally, big-eyed jacks, and yellowfin tuna are reported for the wider Gili Banta area rather than this exact spot — treat those as regional context rather than site-specific confirmations.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the cleaning station at ~12 m and hover to observe mantas. Slopes descend to 40 m. Currents can be powerful — plan negative descent and use reef hooks if necessary.
