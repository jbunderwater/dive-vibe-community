## Tikno Reef

Tikno Reef is a muck and macro dive site at Sangeang Island in east Sumbawa, one of three classic dives (alongside Bubble Reef and Black Magic) offered in the Sangeang volcanic diving area. The site suits divers seeking deeper muck diving than the nearby shallower sites.

## Overview

Tikno Reef features a sloping black sand bottom starting around 10 metres and dropping gradually to about 30 metres, with a sheer drop beyond that to 40 metres or more in places. Black coral is abundant, clustered off the main slope, and sea whips are a defining feature — most of the whips host gobies that spend their entire lives on these structures. Orangutan crabs hiding in anemones and bubble corals, hawkfish perched among gorgonians, ornate ghost pipefish, hairy squat lobsters, and nudibranchs are among the targets for macro photographers, alongside garden eels and clusters of Spanish dancer egg ribbons on the sandy sections. Barrel sponges and firefish have also been logged here. The volcanic setting gives the reef an unusual character — coral formations and sponges growing out of dark substrate with high contrast for photography. Currents are generally light. Access by liveaboard or day trips from Kalimaya Dive Resort. Best season April to November.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Sangeang Island, east Sumbawa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Visibility**: ~25 metres
- **Currents**: Light to occasional
- **Best Season**: April–November

## Marine Life

Sea whip gobies, orangutan crabs, hawkfish, ornate ghost pipefish, hairy squat lobsters, nudibranchs, garden eels, Spanish dancer egg ribbons, firefish, black coral, gorgonians, barrel sponges.

## Photography Tips

High contrast between black volcanic substrate and colourful marine life makes for striking images. Look for sea whip gobies at close range and check bubble corals for hiding orangutan crabs. Macro lens recommended.
