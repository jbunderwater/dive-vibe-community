## Hot Rocks

Hot Rocks is a volcanic dive site on Sangeang Island, off the northeast coast of Sumbawa, named for warm sand and rocks near volcanic gas vents.

## Overview

Master Liveaboards places the site on the northern side of Sangeang; Michael McFadyen's log and starfish.ch place it off the northeast. The dive starts around 5-8 m on black sand where gas bubbles rise from the seabed; the sand and some rocks near the vents are very warm, and Master Liveaboards says some rocks are too hot to touch. Beyond the vents are gorgonian sea fans and black coral. McFadyen reached about 28 m on a slope that runs to about 30 m, with 30 m visibility, about 27 C water and some surge over the reef.

**Conditions conflict**: Master Liveaboards describes a protected reef with no current; starfish.ch says currents can be tricky; McFadyen reports surge. Liveaboard Indonesia gives a depth of 5-15 m, which conflicts with the 28-30 m reported by McFadyen.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North/northeast side of Sangeang Island, northeast Sumbawa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Visibility**: about 30 metres (McFadyen)
- **Water Temperature**: about 27 C (McFadyen)

## Marine Life

Pygmy seahorses (Master Liveaboards, McFadyen, Neptune Liveaboards, starfish.ch), ghost pipefish, orangutan crabs, frogfish, ribbon eels and mushroom coral pipefish (Master Liveaboards; Neptune Liveaboards also lists ribbon eels, ghost pipefish and frogfish). McFadyen logged several nudibranch species (including red-gilled nembrotha), leaf fish, clown anemonefish, emperor angelfish, banded sweetlips and snapper. Master Liveaboards reports bumphead parrotfish in the blue. Starfish.ch notes hawkfish in the gorgonians.

## Safety Notes

Do not touch rocks or sand near the gas vents; they can be hot. McFadyen reports the gas as probably sulphurous.
