## Rollercoaster

Rollercoaster is an advanced drift/current dive at the northwest tip of Gili Banta (Banta Island), an uninhabited volcanic island situated between east Sumbawa and Komodo National Park (Banta itself lies just outside the park's official boundary, per most sources). The site takes its name from the undulating underwater topography and the unpredictable push of vertical and horizontal currents.

**Naming note**: This database lists "Rollercoaster" and "Tanduk Rusa" as two separate sites with different coordinates, but several independent sources use these two names interchangeably for what appears to be the same location at the northern/northwest tip of Gili Banta — a ScubaBoard trip report refers to "Tanduk Rusa (Roller Coaster)," a dive-shop blog post spells it "Tondok Rasa a.k.a. Roller coaster," and a dive-site database describes the same steep-slope, current-swept promontory as "Tanjung Rasa a.k.a. Highway to Heaven." It is unclear whether Rollercoaster and Tanduk Rusa are truly distinct dive spots close to each other, or a single site duplicated in this database under two names. This should be checked against GPS/depth data before either entry is edited further.

## Overview

Rollercoaster sits at an exposed, current-swept corner of Gili Banta. It is confirmed by a dedicated dive-operator write-up as "one of the most intense drift dives in the wider Komodo region," with currents that "can change intensity multiple times during a single dive" and sections that accelerate without warning. That same source confirms whitetip and grey reef sharks in deeper water, plus giant trevally, barracuda, and tuna in the open water and channels — schooling fusiliers and occasional eagle/mobula rays are also reported. Blacktip sharks are reported for the wider Gili Banta area by other sources but are not specifically confirmed at this site. The claim of "cold upwellings from Flores" traces to a general description of Gili Banta's channel — one dive-site database notes that "cold water from the Flores basin in the north is forced through this channel" — rather than a Rollercoaster-specific measurement; it's plausible for this exposed corner but should be read as an area-wide characteristic rather than a site-exclusive one. The strong, variable currents require Advanced certification and experience with negative entries. Lanyards for camera rigs are advisable. Access is typically by day trip from east-Sumbawa dive operators or by liveaboard. Best season April to November.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northwest tip of Gili Banta, Sumbawa / Komodo border
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Currents**: Strong — vertical and horizontal; cold upwellings possible
- **Best Season**: April–November

## Marine Life

Confirmed at this site: whitetip sharks, grey reef sharks, giant trevally, barracuda, tuna, fusiliers, and occasional eagle/mobula rays. Blacktip sharks and big-eyed jacks are reported for Gili Banta generally but not confirmed specifically here — treat as regional context.

## Dive Profile

Negative entry is common given currents. Follow the undulating walls and slopes, using rock formations for shelter when needed. Depths to 30 m; pelagic action often in mid-water above the reef. Hold camera rigs securely in variable current.

## Safety Notes

Cold upwellings can surprise divers — plan for possible thermoclines and carry adequate exposure protection. Strong current requires current hook or reef hook readiness. Advanced divers only.
