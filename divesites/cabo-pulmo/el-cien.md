# El Cien

## Overview
El Cien ("One Hundred," named for its approximate depth in feet) is a deep outer reef site with a dramatic drop-off, offering Cabo Pulmo's most exposed pelagic diving. Also referred to locally as Mario's Reef. Bull sharks and nurse sharks patrol the deeper sections, while large groupers, dog snappers, and bigeye trevally aggregations move through the site. Sea turtles and, per at least one source, yellowfin tuna and sand sharks are regular visitors. The 30-meter depth and exposure to open-ocean currents — which can be strong and unpredictable — make this advanced divers only. El Cien and La Esperanza are often paired on the same boat trip and are frequently grouped together in source material as "the outer reef bars." Tiger sharks have been listed as a possible encounter at Cabo Pulmo's outer reef bars generally, but no source confirms a sighting at El Cien specifically.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur (outer reef)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong; unpredictable
- **Best Time**: October to May for peak pelagic activity

## Marine Life
Bull sharks (most reliably November–May), nurse sharks, large groupers, dog snappers, bigeye trevally, sea turtles, mobula rays (seasonal), yellowfin tuna. The drop-off edge is where most pelagic encounters occur. Tiger sharks and sand sharks are listed as possible species at Cabo Pulmo's outer reef bars in general sources; no site-specific confirmed sighting reports were found for El Cien in particular.

## Dive Profile
Advanced certification required. The reef drops to 30 meters and currents can shift without warning. Descend to the reef edge and scan the blue water for pelagic traffic. Monitor depth and air carefully — deep profiles with current reduce safe bottom time below the 45-minute park limit. Carry an SMB.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Often paired with La Esperanza on the same boat trip
- Position yourself near the drop-off edge for best pelagic viewing
- Wide-angle setup essential
- Current strength varies significantly between dives — listen to the guide's briefing carefully
