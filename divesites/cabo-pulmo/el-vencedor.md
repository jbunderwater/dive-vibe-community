# El Vencedor

## Overview
El Vencedor is the most famous dive site in Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park and the park's only wreck. No Wikipedia entry, maritime registry, or Wikidata record for the vessel could be located, so the history below rests entirely on dive-operator and dive-database accounts, cross-checked across five independent sources. Most sources (Adventures in Baja, Girls That Scuba, East Cape Adventures, Divearoo) describe the vessel as a wooden-hulled Mexican tuna fishing trawler that struck the reef and sank; two sources (Adventures in Baja, Girls That Scuba) instead describe it as a "cargo vessel." No source gives the vessel's original name's origin, its exact tonnage, or a specific sinking date — cited timeframes cluster in the early-to-mid 1980s, with no source supporting a date as early as 1978. Length estimates converge on approximately 27–30 meters (90–100 feet). Because the hull was built mostly of wood, little of the original structure survives; what remains are scattered metal components — engine, propeller shaft, gears, cooling-room machinery, and netting — spread across the sandy bottom. The site now functions as an artificial reef and is the park's primary location for bull shark encounters, with large schools of yellow snapper and grunt permanently colonising the wreckage.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: October to May for bull shark season; year-round for marine life

## Marine Life
Bull sharks (present much of the year, most reliably November through May), large schools of yellow snapper and grunt, giant moray eels, golden groupers, pufferfish (balloon fish), amberjacks and skipjacks, garden eels on surrounding sand. All of these are reported specifically at this site by at least two of the sources consulted. The scattered wreckage acts as an artificial reef concentrating marine life within the broader park ecosystem.

## Dive Profile
Descent to the wreck on sand at 14 meters. The hull is accessible for external exploration along its full length. Spend time hovering above the sand flats on approach to observe garden eels before they retreat. The 45-minute park dive limit applies. Do not attempt penetration without proper training and equipment.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Bring a torch for peering into the hull where morays shelter
- Approach garden eels slowly — they retract into the sand quickly
- During bull shark season, stay calm and maintain a low profile near the wreck
- Maximum group size of 6 divers per guide (park rule)
