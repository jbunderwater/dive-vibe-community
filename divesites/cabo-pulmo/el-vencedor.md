# El Vencedor

## Overview
El Vencedor is the most famous dive site in Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park and the park's only wreck. A wooden-hulled Mexican tuna trawler, El Vencedor struck the reef and sank to a sandy bottom around 14–15 meters. No Wikipedia entry, maritime registry, or official wreck record could be found for this vessel — everything known comes from dive-operator and dive-database accounts, which disagree on the specifics: length is variously given as 27 meters/90 feet or 100 feet, and the sinking date is cited anywhere from 1978 to the early-to-mid 1980s (roughly "35 years" before a 2021 account). No source gives a tonnage figure. Because the hull was primarily wood, little of the original structure survives; what remains are scattered metal components — engine, propeller shaft, cooling machinery, gear, and fishing net — spread across the sand. The site now functions as an artificial reef and cleaning station. It is the park's primary site for bull shark encounters, with schools of yellow snapper and graybar grunt permanently colonising the wreckage. Giant moray eels shelter among the machinery, and golden grouper, Gulf grouper, cubera snapper, and pufferfish are also reported at the site, alongside garden eels on the surrounding sand flats.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: October to May for bull shark season; year-round for marine life

## Marine Life
Bull sharks (present much of the year, most reliably November through May), large schools of yellow snapper and graybar grunt, giant moray eels, golden grouper, Gulf grouper, cubera snapper, pufferfish, garden eels on surrounding sand. During the October–November sardine migration, fish activity increases markedly. The scattered wreckage acts as an artificial reef and cleaning station, concentrating marine life within the broader park ecosystem.

## Dive Profile
Descent to the wreck on sand at 14 meters. The hull is accessible for external exploration along its full length. Spend time hovering above the sand flats on approach to observe garden eels before they retreat. The 45-minute park dive limit applies. Do not attempt penetration without proper training and equipment.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Bring a torch for peering into the hull where morays shelter
- Approach garden eels slowly — they retract into the sand quickly
- During bull shark season, stay calm and maintain a low profile near the wreck
- Maximum group size of 6 divers per guide (park rule)
