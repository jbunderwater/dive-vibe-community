# El Vencedor

## Overview
El Vencedor is the most famous dive site in Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park and the park's only wreck. A wooden-hulled fishing vessel struck the reef and sank to a sandy bottom around 14 meters. Sources conflict on several basic facts about the vessel and should be read as unresolved rather than settled: most operators describe it as a tuna trawler roughly 27–30 meters (90–100 feet) long that sank in 1978 after running into the reef, but at least one operator instead describes a "small cargo ship" that sank roughly 35 years before a 2021 account (implying the mid-1980s), and another describes a much smaller "40-foot fishing vessel" resting at around 12 meters. No source provides a tonnage figure, a confirmed vessel name origin, or an authoritative build date, and none should be assumed. What can be said with reasonable confidence from multiple independent accounts: the hull was built primarily of wood and has largely collapsed, so what remains today is a debris field of metal components — engine, propeller shaft, gearing, and other machinery — rather than an intact ship, and it now functions as an artificial reef. It is the park's primary site for bull shark encounters, with resident schools of yellow snapper and graybar grunt around the wreckage. Giant moray eels (including green and jeweled morays, per diver trip reports) shelter among the machinery, and thousands of garden eels populate the surrounding sand flats.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: October to May for bull shark season; year-round for marine life

## Marine Life
Bull sharks (present much of the year, most reliably November through May), large schools of yellow snapper and graybar grunt, dogtooth snapper, giant moray eels (green and jeweled morays reported by divers), golden groupers, pufferfish, amberjacks, skipjacks, and garden eels on the surrounding sand. During the October–November sardine migration, fish activity increases markedly. The debris field acts as an artificial reef concentrating marine life within the broader park ecosystem.

## Dive Profile
Descent to the wreck on sand at roughly 14 meters (sources place the debris field between about 12 and 15 meters). The site is a scattered debris field rather than an intact hull, so exploration means moving between machinery components on the sand rather than following a single ship outline. Spend time hovering above the sand flats on approach to observe garden eels before they retreat. The 45-minute park dive limit applies. Do not attempt penetration of any remaining structure without proper training and equipment — collapsed wooden wreckage carries entanglement and entrapment risk.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Bring a torch for peering into machinery and gaps where morays shelter
- Approach garden eels slowly — they retract into the sand quickly
- During bull shark season, stay calm and maintain a low profile near the wreck
- Maximum group size of 6 divers per guide (park rule)
