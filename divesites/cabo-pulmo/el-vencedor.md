# El Vencedor

## Overview
El Vencedor is the most famous dive site in Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park and the park's only wreck. A wooden-hulled Mexican fishing vessel, El Vencedor struck the reef and sank to a sandy bottom at 14 meters. Sources disagree on several core facts. **Vessel type**: most operators (My Scuba Diving Gear Guide, Mexico's Greatest Dive Sites) describe it as a tuna trawler, while at least two others (Adventures in Baja, Cabo Adventures) describe it as a small cargo vessel — the exact classification is not settled. **Length**: most sources place it at roughly 27–30 meters (90–100 feet); one operator (Costa Coral Mexico) cites a much shorter 12-meter (40-foot) hull, an outlier not corroborated elsewhere. **Build date and tonnage**: not documented in any source consulted. **Sinking date**: cited variously as 1978, 1980, and "nearly 40 years ago" (dating an early-2020s article to the mid-1980s) — no single authoritative record confirms an exact year, though all cluster in the late 1970s to mid-1980s. **Cause**: multiple independent sources agree the vessel struck the reef and sank; this is the best-corroborated fact about the wreck. Because the hull was built mostly of wood, little of the original structure survives intact — what remains on the sand is scattered metal: the engine, propeller shaft, and machinery, per Mexico's Greatest Dive Sites. The site now functions as an artificial reef. It is the park's primary site for bull shark encounters, with schools of yellow snapper and grunts colonising the wreckage. Giant moray eels shelter among the machinery, golden groupers and pufferfish patrol the structure, and garden eels populate the surrounding sand flats.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: October to May for bull shark season; year-round for marine life

## Marine Life
Bull sharks (present much of the year, most reliably November through May), large schools of yellow snapper and grunts, dogtooth snapper, giant moray eels, golden groupers, pufferfish, amberjacks (skipjacks), garden eels on surrounding sand. The scattered wreckage acts as an artificial reef concentrating marine life within the broader park ecosystem. No source consulted specifically confirmed a seasonal sardine migration at this site versus the park generally, so that claim has been removed.

## Dive Profile
Descent to the wreck on sand at 14 meters. The hull is accessible for external exploration along its full length. Spend time hovering above the sand flats on approach to observe garden eels before they retreat. The 45-minute park dive limit applies. Do not attempt penetration without proper training and equipment.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Bring a torch for peering into the hull where morays shelter
- Approach garden eels slowly — they retract into the sand quickly
- During bull shark season, stay calm and maintain a low profile near the wreck
- Maximum group size of 6 divers per guide (park rule)
