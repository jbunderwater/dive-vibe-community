## Cabo Pulmo

Home to the only living coral reef in the Sea of Cortez, Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park is a conservation success story where roughly 30 years of protection produced a documented 463% increase in fish biomass and UNESCO World Heritage recognition.

## Description

Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park protects a 20,000-year-old coral reef system along the East Cape of Baja California Sur. After local fishermen voted to ban fishing, Mexican President Ernesto Zedillo declared the 71.11 km² area a federal marine park on June 5, 1995, and it became one of the most successful marine reserves in the world. A peer-reviewed 2011 study (Aburto-Oropeza et al., published in PLoS ONE) documented a 463% increase in total fish biomass between 1999 and 2009, with top-predator biomass increasing roughly elevenfold — figures researchers described at the time as unmatched by any other marine reserve studied. The reef system now supports massive schools of bigeye trevally (the famous "jack tornado"), seasonal bull sharks, resident sea lion colonies, transiting whale sharks, and mobula rays. Twelve dive sites range from shallow sea lion encounters to 30-meter deep reef drop-offs with pelagic shark action. All diving is boat-based from the small village of Cabo Pulmo, with rides of 5-15 minutes to every site in the park.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Wreck Diving**: El Vencedor — a wreck (most sources describe it as a tuna trawler; a couple describe it as a small cargo vessel) on sand at 14 meters, the park's most famous site and primary location for seasonal bull shark encounters. See the El Vencedor page for a full source-by-source breakdown of the conflicting vessel details.
- **Reef Bar Systems**: El Bajo, Los Morros, and Meros form the park's central reef complex, home to the iconic jack tornado. Cabo Pulmo's reef hosts around 11 documented coral species (per Wikipedia and Smithsonian Ocean) — some tourism sources claim "12 of the 14 coral species in the world," a figure not corroborated by scientific sources and not repeated here
- **Canyon Diving**: El Cantil — one of the largest reef systems in the park, with miniature canyons, swim-throughs, and cavern-like structures (divided into North, South, and El Cantilito sections). Las Navajas offers canyon topography used for both diving and snorkeling
- **Deep Pelagic Sites**: La Esperanza and El Cien — outer reef drop-offs to 30 meters with bull sharks, nurse sharks, tuna schools, and mobula rays (Advanced only)
- **Sea Lion Colony**: La Lobera — playful California sea lions interact with divers at 8 meters near Los Frailes
- **Cave & Rock Formations**: Las Casitas — boulder and rock formations with caves, crevices, and swim-throughs, hard and soft coral cover, and garden eels on the surrounding sand
- **Macro Diving**: Los Chopitos — shallow reef with invertebrates and small fish for patient photographers

### Accessibility

- **Getting There**: Fly to San Jose del Cabo International Airport (SJD). Cabo Pulmo village is a 2-hour drive east along the East Cape road. The final stretch is unpaved. No public transportation — rental car or pre-arranged shuttle required
- **Dive Operators**: A handful of small operators based in Cabo Pulmo village, including Adventures in Baja and Costa Coral Mexico. Book in advance during peak season (October-May)
- **Accommodation**: Limited to small eco-lodges, vacation rentals, and casitas in Cabo Pulmo village. No large hotels or resorts. Stock up on supplies in San Jose del Cabo or Los Barriles before arriving
- **Facilities**: Very limited infrastructure. No hyperbaric chamber on site — nearest is in Cabo San Lucas (2+ hours). No ATMs in the village. Bring cash and essentials. Cell service is unreliable
- **Transportation**: All dive sites are accessed by small pangas (open boats) launching from the village beach, 5-15 minutes to any site

### Marine Life & Environment

- **Water Conditions**: Water temperature ranges from 20°C (68°F) in winter to 28°C (82°F) in summer. Visibility typically 10-25 meters. Mild currents at inshore sites; moderate to strong currents at outer reef sites (La Esperanza, El Cien). Thermoclines possible at depth
- **Signature Species**: Bigeye trevally (jack tornado — the park's iconic spectacle), bull sharks (seasonal, primarily at El Vencedor, La Esperanza, El Cien), California sea lions (resident colony at La Lobera), whale sharks (transiting, seasonal), mobula rays, humpback whales (December-March, songs audible underwater), grouper aggregations, green sea turtles, giant moray eels
- **Conservation**: The reef's recovery since 1995 is one of the most documented marine conservation successes worldwide. The 463% total fish biomass increase measured between 1999 and 2009 (Aburto-Oropeza et al. 2011, PLoS ONE) demonstrated that full marine protection can restore even heavily depleted ecosystems. UNESCO World Heritage site as part of the Islands and Protected Areas of the Gulf of California (inscribed 2005); also a Ramsar wetland of international importance (designated 2008)

### Park Rules

- **45-minute dive limit** enforced on all dives
- **Maximum 6 divers per guide** — no exceptions
- **No touching** marine life or reef structures
- **No gloves** allowed while diving (to discourage touching)
- **No fishing** of any kind within park boundaries
- **Park entrance fee** required (paid through dive operators)
- **No anchoring** on the reef — boats use moorings only

## Additional Information

- **Best Time to Visit**: October to May for peak marine life including bull sharks, jack tornados, mobula rays, and whale sharks. Summer (June-September) brings warmest water but reduced pelagic activity and occasional hurricanes
- **Currency**: Mexican Peso (MXN). Bring cash — no ATMs in Cabo Pulmo village
- **Language**: Spanish; English spoken by dive operators
- **Safety**: No hyperbaric chamber in Cabo Pulmo — nearest in Cabo San Lucas (2+ hours). The remote location means medical evacuation is slow. Dive conservatively. Advanced sites (La Esperanza, El Cien) have unpredictable currents and should only be attempted by experienced divers
