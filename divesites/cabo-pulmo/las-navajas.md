# Las Navajas

## Overview
Las Navajas is a rock-and-canyon formation in Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, used as both a snorkeling stop and a dive site — it appears on Cabo Pulmo Beach Resort's snorkel tour route alongside Mermaid Beach and the Sea Lion Colony. Independent sources describe the underwater terrain as deep canyons and rock formations, with parts of the site shallow enough for snorkeling. Multiple general accounts note that rays and sharks are seen here more often than at some other park sites, though none of the sources consulted give site-specific depth figures, dive profiles, or confirmed species lists beyond that general description. One travel-guide source mentions hammerhead sharks at the site, but this claim could not be corroborated by a second independent source, so it has not been included below. Given the limited site-specific detail available, this description leans on general park characteristics for anything not directly confirmed for Las Navajas.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life
Sources agree rays and sharks are encountered more frequently at Las Navajas than at some nearby sites, but none of the sources consulted confirm specific species for this site. Regional Cabo Pulmo fauna (reef sharks, rays, tropical reef fish, and seasonally bull sharks and jack schools) may be present, but no site-specific sighting reports beyond the general "rays and sharks" characterization were found. No source confirmed hammerhead sharks at this specific site despite one travel guide mentioning them for the area.

## Dive Profile
Canyon topography from shallow sections (used for snorkeling) down to approximately 15 meters. Divers navigate through the rock formations and canyon walls. Site-specific dive-profile detail (entry point, typical route, turnaround depth) was not found in the sources consulted, so the profile described here is a general characterization rather than an operator-confirmed plan. The 45-minute park dive limit applies.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Good site for combining a dive and snorkel on the same visit — it is also used as a standalone snorkel stop
- Relatively sheltered — a reasonable option when conditions are rough at outer sites
- Ask your operator for the current day's specific route, since published site-specific detail for this location is limited compared to other park sites
