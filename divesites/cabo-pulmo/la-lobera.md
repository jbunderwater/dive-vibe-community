# La Lobera

## Overview
La Lobera ("The Sea Lion Place") sits just before Los Frailes bay at the southern end of the park and is home to a resident California sea lion colony — also referred to by some operators as the Los Frailes / "Frailes Rock" sea lion colony, describing the same rocks at the southern tip of Punta Frailes. The playful pinnipeds are the main attraction — they regularly approach divers, spinning and darting through the water column in close proximity. The site descends from the sea lion rocks at around 5 meters to a sandy bottom at approximately 18 meters maximum, giving divers and snorkellers alike room to explore. Tropical fish — including surgeonfish and Moorish idols — populate the rocky substrate, and spotted eagle rays are listed as a possible seasonal sighting. Sources disagree on the colony's seasonal pattern: one prior source described the sea lions as present year-round except roughly August–October (relocating during hurricane season), while other current sources describe them as most reliably seen January through October. This description flags that disagreement rather than picking one version — confirm current sightings with a local operator before planning a trip around the colony.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, near Los Frailes (southern end)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: Sources disagree on the colony's off-season — see Overview; May–June for newborn sea lion interactions

## Marine Life
Resident California sea lion colony (interactive with divers), surgeonfish, Moorish idols. Spotted eagle rays are listed as a possible seasonal sighting at this site. The sea lions are the star attraction. Humpback whales are a seasonal presence in Cabo Pulmo waters generally (December–March) and their songs can sometimes be heard underwater, but no source specifically confirmed whale encounters at La Lobera versus the broader park area — the previously listed damselfish, parrotfish, mackerel, jacks, and barracuda have been removed because no current source confirms them for this specific site.

## Dive Profile
The active zone is shallow — most of the dive is spent in 3–8 meters of water near the sea lion colony rocks. Sandy areas extend to approximately 18 meters for those who want to explore beyond the colony. Let the sea lions come to you. The 45-minute park dive limit applies, though air consumption at this depth is minimal. May–June brings newborn sea lions that attempt to interact while mothers herd them away.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Do not chase or touch the sea lions — they will approach on their own
- Wide-angle lens recommended for sea lion portraits and interaction shots
- Also excellent as a snorkeling site
- Often scheduled as the final dive of the day due to its shallow, relaxing profile
