# El Bajo

## Overview
El Bajo (also known as "El Bajo de los Meros") is a long reef bar running roughly 800 metres north-south with sand channels on both sides. One of the most biologically rich sites in the Sea of Cortez, it hosts multiple coral species making it the northern anchor of the park's reef system. Large grouper schools patrol the reef top, garden eels populate the sandy margins, and sea turtles cruise through regularly. Seasonal visitors include mobula rays, and jacks are a confirmed presence on the reef — Los Morros, immediately to the north, is the site most consistently confirmed by sources for the park's dense "jack tornado" bigeye trevally aggregations, and boats often work both sites on the same trip, so it should not be assumed El Bajo delivers that spectacle as reliably as Los Morros. Nudibranchs and frogfish reward patient macro divers along the coral heads.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, Baja California Sur
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-25 meters
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: Year-round; seasonal mobula ray sightings peak October-May

## Marine Life
Large grouper schools (including leopard groupers), garden eels, sea turtles, seasonal mobula rays, nudibranchs, frogfish, porkfish, grunts, diverse hard corals, and jacks (Dive O'Clock confirms jacks specifically at El Bajo). Reef conditions and diverse structure also attract angelfish, pufferfish, and multiple eel species. The park's iconic dense bigeye trevally "jack tornado" formations are most consistently reported at neighboring Los Morros rather than confirmed as a regular El Bajo feature.

## Dive Profile
Depths range from 9 to 18 meters along the reef bar. Most divers follow the bar north-south, exploring coral formations on the top and checking the sand channels on either side. The 45-minute park dive limit applies. Currents are generally mild but can pick up near the canyon edge.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Carry a macro lens for nudibranchs and frogfish on the coral heads
- Check the sand channels on both sides of the bar for garden eels and resting turtles
- Often combined with nearby Los Morros or Meros on the same boat trip
- For the best odds at the jack tornado spectacle specifically, ask about diving Los Morros on the same trip
