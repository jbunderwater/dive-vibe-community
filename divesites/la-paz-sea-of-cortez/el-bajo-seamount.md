## El Bajo Seamount

El Bajo Seamount (also called El Bajo Marisla or Marisla Seamount) is a legendary advanced dive site approximately 13 km north-northeast of La Paz, renowned for schooling scalloped hammerhead sharks.

## Overview

El Bajo is a group of three isolated underwater pinnacles within Espiritu Santo National Park. According to Wikipedia and PADI dive records, the three peaks rise to between 52 feet (central peak, 16 m), 69 feet (southern peak, 21 m), and 83 feet (northern peak, 25 m) from the surface, with walls dropping far deeper into open ocean. The formation is named after the dive-cruise vessel Marisla II (formerly the USCG Cutter Columbine) — the first documented sport dive on El Bajo was made in 1957 by Richard M. Adcock, and Maria Luisa and Richard Adcock ran dive-cruise trips to the site from 1968–2009. Approximately 90 minutes by boat from La Paz, El Bajo has been recognized since the 1970s as one of the world's premier sites for schooling hammerhead sharks.

Marine biologist Dr. Peter Klimley first documented schooling scalloped hammerhead sharks at El Bajo in the late 1970s (SeaWatch, TOSEA). His research found the sharks navigate using the magnetic fields generated by lava flows around the seamount, following them "like driving a car on a highway" between El Bajo and feeding grounds up to 10 miles offshore before returning by morning. Two independent sources describe the composition of the schools differently: SeaWatch describes the large aggregations as predominantly juvenile males drawn in from the open ocean to feed and visit cleaning stations, while TOSEA's account describes gatherings as predominantly females engaged in dominance contests. Both accounts agree the sharks use El Bajo as a seamount meeting point along a migration corridor. SeaWatch also reports that hammerhead numbers at El Bajo declined substantially beginning in the mid-1990s from their 1980s peak — a trend echoed by other regional conservation sources — so current sightings can range from small groups to the historic hundreds, and are not guaranteed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Espiritu Santo National Park, approximately 8 miles (13 km) north-northeast of La Paz
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

El Bajo is best known for schooling scalloped hammerhead sharks (see Overview for school composition and population-trend caveats). Confirmed sightings at this specific site, per PADI, Cortez Club, and Underwater Photography Guide, include giant Pacific manta rays, whale sharks (seasonal), amberjacks, tuna, wahoo, octopus, a resident colony of 50+ Panamic green moray eels, dolphins, and sea turtles. Hammerhead sightings are most reliable in cooler months (September–December) per multiple operator sources.

## Dive Profile

Three submerged pinnacles top out between 52 and 83 feet (16–25 m). Divers typically work the upper sections of the peaks where hammerheads congregate, then follow the walls deeper. Most productive dives take place below 30 meters. Strong, variable currents are normal; blue-water conditions and open-ocean surge are common. Drift diving technique is often used.

## Entry and Exit

All access is by boat from La Paz — typical crossing time is approximately 90 minutes. Local operators including the Cortez Club, Cantamar Dive, and La Paz Divers run day trips to El Bajo. The site is within Espiritu Santo National Park; a park fee applies.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Advanced Open Water certification minimum; Rescue Diver or higher strongly recommended given depth and current.
- Blue-water seamount diving skills and strong buoyancy control are essential.
- Arrive early — morning dives typically have calmer conditions and better hammerhead activity.
- Wide-angle lens is optimal for pelagic photography.
- Bring a surface marker buoy (SMB); open-ocean conditions make boat pick-up critical.

## Safety Considerations

Strong and unpredictable currents are normal at this open-ocean site. Divers must be comfortable in blue water with limited visual reference. Depth can lead to nitrogen narcosis below 30 m. Always dive with a local guide who knows current patterns. Surface conditions can be choppy on the 90-minute crossing; seasickness precaution is advisable.
