# HMS Maori

Malta's shallowest warship wreck — a Tribal-class destroyer famous for helping sink the Bismarck, bombed in Grand Harbour in 1942.

## Overview

HMS Maori was a 115-metre Tribal-class destroyer launched in 1937, famous for her role in the sinking of the German battleship Bismarck in May 1941. She was hit by a single German bomb while moored in Valletta's Grand Harbour on 12 February 1942 and sank. In July 1945 the wreck was raised to clear the harbour; while the salvaged hull was under tow for scuttling, the aft section was lost in deeper water and only the bow was recovered and re-scuttled at its current position in St Elmo Bay, near the entrance to Marsamxett Harbour. What divers see today is therefore only the bow section — roughly 42 metres of the original 115-metre ship — lying broken on a sandy bottom at a maximum depth of 16 metres. Visibility at this harbour site is typically poor and a DSMB is required due to harbour boat traffic.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Fort St. Elmo, Grand Harbour, Valletta
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 16 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 5–15 meters (often poor)
- **Current**: Light; harbour boat traffic
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Bream schools, goatfish, and occasional seahorse. The harbour environment limits diversity compared to open-water sites.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Malta's shallowest warship wreck — accessible to Open Water divers
- HMS Maori helped sink the Bismarck — significant naval history
- This is the BOW SECTION only (~42m of the original 115m destroyer) — the stern was lost separately during the 1945 salvage tow
- Visibility is typically poor — this is a harbour site
- DSMB mandatory due to harbour boat traffic
