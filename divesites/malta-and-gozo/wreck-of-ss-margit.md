# Wreck of SS Margit

A WWII air-raid casualty in Kalkara Creek — a 100-metre steam passenger ship broken up by wartime demolition charges, now one of the few WWII wrecks in Malta within Advanced Open Water depth range.

## Overview

SS Margit (also known by the name MV Odile) was a steam passenger ship of approximately 100 metres in length. She was sunk in an air raid on 19 April 1941 in Kalkara Creek, on the eastern side of Grand Harbour, Valletta, where she had lain crewless for roughly two years beforehand. In 1943, her two masts and funnel were removed with demolition charges so the berth could be used during preparations for the Allied invasion of Sicily.

The wreck lies parallel to the shore in the middle of Kalkara Creek on a silty bottom. She is considerably broken up and listed to port. Sources report the depth inconsistently: MaltaDives and Techwise Malta give an average depth of 16 metres and a maximum of 24 metres, while other operators cite a resting depth of approximately 22 metres — divers should expect a working range of roughly 16–24 metres. Visibility is typically reduced by silt and by heavy maritime traffic in the creek.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Kalkara Creek, eastern side of Grand Harbour, Valletta
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 22 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Reduced — silty bottom, busy harbour traffic
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

No site-specific marine life reports were found for this wreck. The wider Malta/Gozo waters support grouper, moray eels, octopus, bream, barracuda, and Mediterranean corals, but none of these are confirmed as specific to SS Margit.

## Dive Profile

A boat dive to the silty creek bottom where the broken hull lies parallel to the shore. The vessel is heavily fragmented — her masts and funnel were deliberately removed in 1943 — so expect a debris-field style exploration rather than an intact hull. Poor visibility from harbour silt and traffic is typical; a torch and careful buoyancy help avoid further disturbing the bottom.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, within Kalkara Creek. The site sees regular maritime traffic — deploy a DSMB and maintain awareness of boat activity in the creek.

## Tips and Recommendations

- One of the few WWII wrecks in Maltese waters within Advanced Open Water (30m) depth range
- Expect poor-to-moderate visibility — this is a working harbour creek, not open water
- The wreck is heavily broken — her funnel and masts were removed by explosives in 1943, not by wartime damage alone

## Safety Considerations

Intermediate-level wreck dive. Visibility can be poor; maintain buddy contact and carry a torch. Boat traffic in Kalkara Creek is a real hazard — deploy a DSMB before ascending.
