## Little Komodo

Little Komodo is Triton Bay's most talked-about dive site — a boulder reef at the southern tip of Saruenus (Seruenus) Island where huge, soft-coral-draped rocks nearly break the surface, named for the rugged character of its submerged rock formations.

## Overview

Little Komodo sits at the southern end of Saruenus Island in Triton Bay, West Papua, Indonesia. The site is built around massive boulders whose rough, weathered profile is what gives it its name — divers and operators consistently describe it as one of the most photographed sites in the bay. Below the surface, the boulders are heavily colonized by soft corals, and the site is known for dense schools of anthias, damselfish, and fusiliers moving over the reef. Deeper sections of black coral hold sweetlips and snapper, with hunting jacks and wobbegong sharks also reported among the boulders. Currents through the area keep the reef in productive condition, and guides frequently time dives so that a favorable drift carries divers from Little Komodo toward the nearby Aquarium site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Triton Bay, West Papua, Indonesia (southern tip of Saruenus/Seruenus Island)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 8-20 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong; dive guides time entries with tidal flow
- **Water Temperature**: 27-30°C (81-86°F)
- **Best Time**: October to April (dive season)

## Marine Life

Little Komodo is reported by multiple independent sources to host dense schools of anthias, damselfish, and fusiliers around its soft-coral-covered boulders. Sweetlips and snapper shelter in the site's black coral growth, and hunting jacks and wobbegong sharks are also documented here. Macro photographers report pygmy seahorses, nudibranchs, and shrimp among the coral and rock crevices. Beyond these confirmed sightings, Triton Bay's broader reputation for soft coral biodiversity applies, but no site-specific source was found for additional species (e.g., epaulette sharks, bumphead parrotfish, or flasher wrasse) at this particular site, so they have been omitted rather than assumed.

## Dive Profile

Descend onto the top of the boulder reef at 5 to 8 meters, where soft coral density and natural light are strongest. Work along the rock faces and boulder gaps before working deeper toward the black coral zone, where sweetlips, snapper, and hunting jacks tend to concentrate. Currents can run strongly across the exposed boulder faces; guides often use this to set up a drift that carries the group toward the neighboring Aquarium site rather than fighting back to a fixed anchor point. Finish with a safety stop in the shallows, where the schooling anthias and damselfish activity is typically at its best.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by liveaboard tender or day-boat. The boat positions in the lee of the boulders for entry by giant stride or back roll; exit is via the tender's dive ladder. In stronger current, guides commonly run this as a drift dive toward Aquarium rather than returning divers to a fixed pickup point — confirm the plan with your guide before descending.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your guide about the current dive plan — a drift toward Aquarium is common when conditions allow
- Bring both wide-angle (for the boulder-and-coral scenery) and macro (for pygmy seahorses, nudibranchs, and shrimp) capability if your system allows
- Currents can build quickly here; stay close to the reef structure as a windbreak if flow increases
- Watch the black coral zone carefully for sweetlips, snapper, and hunting jacks

## Safety Considerations

Currents at Little Komodo can intensify quickly as tidal flow changes. Stay within sight of your guide and be prepared to hold position near a boulder briefly if current spikes. Maintain good buoyancy around the coral-covered rock faces to avoid contact damage. Always carry and deploy an SMB for ascent, particularly on drift-style dives toward Aquarium where you may surface away from a fixed boat position.

## Photography

Little Komodo's soft-coral-draped boulders are widely cited as some of the best wide-angle subject matter in West Papua, especially where boulders break toward the surface and can be shot with ambient light. For macro, work the crevices and coral branches for pygmy seahorses, nudibranchs, and shrimp with a 60mm or 100mm lens.
