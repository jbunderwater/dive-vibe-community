## Little Komodo

Little Komodo is one of Triton Bay's best-known dive sites: a boulder-and-ridge reef with soft coral cover on Saruenus Island, usually dived as a drift.

## Overview

Little Komodo is on Saruenus Island in Triton Bay, West Papua, Indonesia. Sources describe it in somewhat different terms. Diveplanit describes huge soft-coral-covered boulders that nearly break the surface, and Dive The World describes a drift over boulders, ridges and a channel, with a vertical stretch. A Bluewater Dive Travel trip report calls it a sloping reef around a small islet and lists it as one of three linked sites with Aquarium and Pintu Arus. Operators say that when currents allow, the dive can drift along a soft coral wall and end at Aquarium. Sources also disagree on where it sits: Divernet places Little Komodo at the northern mouth of the Iris Strait, while other sources place it on Saruenus Island.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Triton Bay, West Papua, Indonesia (Saruenus/Seruenus Island)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Not confirmed for this site
- **Current**: Variable. Operators describe a drift dive when conditions suit; a Bluewater trip report found it slack on one dive and pulling on another.
- **Best Time**: Dive-the-World recommends October to April for Triton Bay generally; the Divers Alert Network feature gives October to May as the dive season

## Marine Life

Species reported at Little Komodo itself:

- Anthias, damselfish and fusiliers over the soft-coral-covered boulders (Diveplanit; The Seven Seas)
- Sweetlips and snapper, including among black coral bushes (Diveplanit; Dive The World; The Seven Seas)
- Jacks (Diveplanit) and trevallies and mackerel along the edges (The Seven Seas)
- Wobbegong sharks (Diveplanit; The Seven Seas, which has them resting on sand)
- Rainbow runners, bumphead parrotfish, nudibranchs and pygmy seahorses, the last reported by Dive The World as shallow as about 15 m

A Bluewater Dive Travel trip report also lists a pair of clown triggerfish at a cleaning station and a broadclub cuttlefish on its Little Komodo dives, and notes that on a day with current, schooling fish, chevron barracuda, coral trout and lobetail snappers appeared. Divernet reports manta rays, giant grouper and batfish at Little Komodo, but this appears in a general account of the site and is not corroborated by a second source here. Treat it as unconfirmed.

## Dive Profile

No source gives a reliable depth-by-depth profile. Dive The World describes the dive as typically conducted as a drift, with most notable life between 10 and 20 m. Diveplanit says the drift along the soft coral wall can end at Aquarium when conditions allow. Follow your guide's briefing for the day.

## Entry and Exit

Not described in the sources consulted. Boat access only; the exit plan depends on the current, so confirm it with your guide.

## Safety Considerations

Current varies by day and tide, and sources describe both slack and pulling conditions. Drift dives can end away from the boat, so carry a surface marker buoy and stay with your guide.

## Photography

Diveplanit, Dive The World and The Seven Seas all describe soft corals covering boulders with fish schooling above them. Dive The World also lists pygmy seahorses and nudibranchs at Little Komodo, which suit macro work.
