## Namatota Channel

Namatota Channel is this database's entry for Namatota Wall (also spelled Namatote), a wall dive off the southern tip of Namatota Island, close to the bagan fishing platforms where whale sharks gather.

## Overview

Liveaboard.com describes Namatote Wall as a wall dive just off the southern tip of Namatote Island, with visibility sometimes slightly limited by shifting current and overwhelming numbers of macro species. The Seven Seas describes the southern flank of Namatota Island, with the wall dropping into deep water covered in sea fans, soft corals and black coral. Divehappy calls it "a pleasant dive ... nearby and a useful consolation if the whale sharks are a no-show", so it is separate from the bagan encounter. Earlier versions of this page included a rock-art history of Namatota Island and a claim attributed to X-Ray Mag; neither could be re-confirmed in the sources fetched on 2026-10-30, so both have been removed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Triton Bay, West Papua, Indonesia, off the southern tip of Namatota Island
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 28 meters
- **Current**: The Seven Seas reports gentle pulses of current; Liveaboard.com reports shifting current that sometimes limits visibility
- **Best Time**: Dive season October to April/May per regional sources

## Marine Life

Reported at Namatota Wall:

- Nudibranchs, gobies and shrimp (Liveaboard.com; The Seven Seas)
- Giant sea fans, soft corals and black coral thickets (The Seven Seas)
- Pipefish, leaf scorpionfish, hawkfish, groupers, anthias, fusiliers and occasional turtles (The Seven Seas)

Whale sharks are seen at the nearby bagans (Divehappy; Divers Alert Network), not on the wall.

## Dive Profile

Descend onto the wall and work along it. Sources do not give a depth profile.

## Safety Considerations

Current changes during the dive and can limit visibility. Dive with a local guide and carry a surface marker buoy.
