## Anse Noire

Anse Noire — the Black Cove — is a small volcanic black-sand beach reached by a short footpath from the neighbouring Anse Dufour, on Martinique's southwest coast.

## Overview

Anse Noire is a sheltered bay where volcanic rock outcrops frame the beach and extend underwater. It shares the same resident turtle population that makes the adjacent Anse Dufour well known, since the two coves are connected by a short coastal path and turtles range between them. No site-specific source with detailed depth or reef-structure data for Anse Noire itself was found during this audit, so depth and the general beginner-friendly shallow character have been kept as previously recorded, but marine-life claims beyond turtles have been removed pending confirmation.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Les Anses d'Arlet area, southwest Martinique
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Coral reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 8 meters

## Marine Life

Sea turtles are reported at Anse Noire as part of the same population that frequents Anse Dufour nearby. No source found for this audit names specific reef-fish or invertebrate species at Anse Noire itself; regional Caribbean reef fauna would be expected but has not been confirmed site-specifically.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry over black volcanic sand with some rock; the beach is reached via a short footpath from Anse Dufour, or its own small parking area. Move carefully over the rocky sections at the entry.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Pair with Anse Dufour for a two-site shore-diving morning
- The black volcanic sand and rock create a visually distinctive setting compared to the island's white-sand sites

## Safety Considerations

The rocky entry can be slippery — consider water shoes until fully submerged. Turtles are protected; keep a respectful distance and do not touch.
