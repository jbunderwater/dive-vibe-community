## Le Roraima (épave)

Le Roraima is the most famous of the Saint-Pierre wrecks — a 103-metre iron-hulled steamship that sank on 8 May 1902 after the catastrophic eruption of Mont Pelée destroyed the city of Saint-Pierre and the fleet anchored in its bay.

## Overview

The Roraima was a 2,712-gross-ton combination steamer, 103.63 metres long, built in 1883 at the Aitken & Mansel shipyard in Whiteinch, Glasgow, and launched on 5 June 1883. She was originally built for the Mogul Steamship Company of Glasgow and launched under the name *Ghazee*. The Quebec Steamship Company purchased her on 30 November 1899 and renamed her *Roraima* for New York-Windward Islands passenger-cargo service. On the morning of 8 May 1902, the ship had just anchored in Saint-Pierre bay when Mont Pelée's pyroclastic flow struck, tearing away her bridge superstructure and capsizing her onto her starboard side. Her cargo of potassium intensified the resulting fire, which burned for roughly three days before she finally sank. Of the 68 people aboard, 57 died; the 11 survivors included nanny Clara King and eight-year-old Marguerite Hamilton Stokes, most rescued by the French cruiser *Suchet*. Commandant Jacques Cousteau, who filmed the wreck, nicknamed it *"l'épave aux cheveux blancs"* (the white-haired wreck) - French-language sources attribute the "white hair" to virgulaires (sea pens) coating the hull, though some English write-ups describe it more loosely as hair-like algae. The wreck was rediscovered by diver Michel Météry in 1974.

**Sources disagree on the wreck's exact dimensions and depth.** Wikipedia gives the hull length as 103.63m; a Martinique dive-site directory (Tribloo) states 110m. Depth figures also vary: French Wikipedia records 40-45m, the English-language Wikipedia summary gives 40-60m, and Tribloo's detailed breakdown - which matches this site's stored depth field - puts the bow at 36m and the stern at 55m. The wreck lies roughly 300 metres from shore, a figure confirmed independently by French Wikipedia and by Tribloo.

## Dive Profile

- **Depth range**: approximately 36 m (bow) to 55 m (stern) per Tribloo's detailed figures; other sources cite a broader 40-60m range
- **Entry**: Boat dive, approximately 15 minutes from Saint-Pierre harbour
- **Typical dive**: Divers descend to the main deck and work along the hull; the engine room, boiler, a captain's bathroom with preserved blue faience tiles, remnants of the chimney base, and bollards are recognisable features
- **Visibility**: Generally good in Saint-Pierre bay; can be reduced by volcanic sediment stirring

## Marine Life

Schools of barracudas, snappers, and trumpetfish are reported around the superstructure, with small reef fish inhabiting the coral and sponge growth on the hull. The "white hair" appearance for which the wreck is best known comes from a dense covering of pale sea pens (virgulaires) rather than fish life.

## Safety Notes

- Advanced certification (CMAS 3 or PADI Advanced Open Water with deep and wreck specialties) is required by local operators
- The stern depth (55-60m per most sources) is beyond recreational no-decompression limits; bottom time must be strictly managed and is not a routine or "safe" undertaking without appropriate technical training
- Wreck penetration beyond the main deck areas is not recommended without a guide familiar with the layout
- Surge from passing boat traffic can affect visibility near the seafloor

## Photography

The Roraima offers exceptional wide-angle opportunities: the encrusted hull, bollards, and chimney base provide striking subjects. Macro photographers will find abundant invertebrates and small reef fish on the coral-covered steel.
