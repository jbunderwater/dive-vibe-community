## St. Pierre Wrecks

The bay of Saint-Pierre holds roughly a dozen documented wrecks, all lost on 8 May 1902 when the pyroclastic surge from Mount Pelée destroyed the city and capsized or burned every vessel anchored in the harbour.

## Overview

Wikipedia's inventory of the Saint-Pierre wrecks and the French underwater-archaeology database Archéonavale both document close to a dozen wrecks in the bay, including the *Roraima* (2,712-ton steamer, covered separately on this site), the *Amélie*/*Raisinier*, the *Biscaye* (ex-*Gabrielle*, wooden hull with a cargo of salt cod), the *Clémentina* (locally-built wooden coaster), the *Diamant* (steam vedette that was ferrying passengers when her boilers exploded), the *Nord-America* (Italian three-masted barque), the *Dahlia* (a WWII-era minesweeper that sank decades later, in the 1950s, in an unrelated accidental fire, not in the 1902 eruption), and the *Tamaya*, a three-masted ship whose bell - recovered in 1983 and inscribed "Tamaya 1862" - is now held at the Franck-A.-Perret volcanology museum in Saint-Pierre. The *Tamaya* rests at roughly 85 metres, beyond recreational and most technical scuba limits, and is not part of a typical recreational dive here despite lying in the same bay. Depths across the accessible wrecks in the bay range from roughly 12 metres (*Amélie*) to around 55-60 metres (*Roraima*), consistent with the depth field on this entry. Espace Plongée Martinique, a Trois-Ilets dive centre, lists several of these wrecks individually among its northern-zone sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Saint-Pierre bay, Martinique
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 60 meters (the deepest of the individually-diveable wrecks in the bay; the *Tamaya* at ~85m is beyond recreational/technical scuba range and not included in this figure)
