## Épave Amelie

The Épave Amelie (also recorded as the *Raisinier*) is a shallow, shore-accessible wreck lying in Anse Turin, just south of Saint-Pierre on Martinique's Caribbean coast — one of the most accessible wreck dives in the French Caribbean, suitable for snorkellers and beginner divers.

## Overview

The *Amélie* was a 48-metre three-masted sailing vessel, a sister ship to the *Belem*, built in 1890 at the Sevestre shipyard in Chantenay. Sources disagree on the hull material — steel (Belle Martinique) versus iron (an earlier source) — so this is noted as unresolved rather than stated as fact. After arriving at Saint-Pierre from Marseille in late April 1902, the ship developed a hull leak while anchored in the harbour; the crew fought the leak for two days before the steamer *Diamant* towed her to Anse Turin for repairs, where she sank. The subsequent Mont Pelée eruption of 8 May 1902 then covered the surrounding area in volcanic debris. The wreck now lies scattered across several pieces on grey volcanic sand.

**Depth conflict flagged:** sources report the wreck's depth variably — 5-8m (Snorkeling Report), around 12m (Wikipedia), and 4-10m (Belle Martinique) — likely reflecting that the hull is broken into several pieces lying at slightly different depths rather than one uniform depth. The depth field above uses 12m as the upper bound rather than picking a single figure.

The site is marked by four yellow buoys delimiting a protected zone. Shore access is from Anse Turin beach, located near Le Carbet on the coastal road to Saint-Pierre, just before the tunnel.

## Dive Profile

- **Depth range**: 5–8 m (15–25 ft)
- **Entry**: Shore entry from Anse Turin grey sand beach; free access
- **Duration**: Suitable for long, relaxed dives given the shallow depth
- **Conditions**: Relatively calm; morning currents can strengthen; wind-driven waves reduce visibility by stirring fine volcanic sediment

## Marine Life

Sponges, hard corals, gorgonians, worms, and anemones have colonised the fragmented hull sections. Common residents include sergeant major fish, smooth trunkfish, and dusky squirrelfish. Occasional encounters include longlure frogfish, rays, turtles, and snake eels (corrected from "sea snakes" in a prior version, which is not a species found in this region). The sandy areas surrounding the wreck host camouflaged torpedo rays; nearby seagrass beds attract juvenile fish and herbivorous species.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry directly from the beach. The *l'Abri Côtier* restaurant at the beach provides amenities. Additional services are available in nearby Le Carbet.

## Photography

The shallow depth and natural light make this an excellent site for natural-light photography throughout the day. The encrusted hull fragments surrounded by tropical reef fish offer strong macro and wide-angle opportunities without the need for flash equipment at depth.
