## Épave Amelie

The Épave Amelie (also recorded as the *Raisinier*) is a shallow, shore-accessible wreck lying in Anse Turin, just south of Saint-Pierre on Martinique's Caribbean coast. It is one of the most accessible wreck dives in the French Caribbean, suitable for snorkellers and beginner divers.

## Overview

The *Amélie* was a 48-metre iron sailing vessel built in 1890 at the Sevestre shipyard in Chantenay, a sister ship to the *Belem*. After arriving at Saint-Pierre from Marseille in late April 1902, the ship developed a hull leak while anchored in the harbour. The steamer *Diamant* towed her to Turin Cove for repairs, where she sank. The Mont Pelée eruption of 8 May 1902 then covered the surrounding area in volcanic debris. The wreck now lies scattered across several pieces on grey volcanic sand.

The site is marked by four yellow buoys delimiting a protected zone. Shore access is from Anse Turin beach, located near Le Carbet on the coastal road to Saint-Pierre, just before the tunnel.

**Depth note**: this page previously listed a 5-8m depth range. Wikipedia's Saint-Pierre wrecks inventory and two independent Martinique dive-site sources (Espace Plongée and Québec Yachting) instead converge on roughly 9-12 metres, and the depth field has been corrected accordingly.

## Dive Profile

- **Depth range**: approximately 9–12 m per Wikipedia, Espace Plongée, and Québec Yachting
- **Entry**: Shore entry from Anse Turin grey sand beach; free access
- **Duration**: Suitable for long, relaxed dives given the shallow depth
- **Conditions**: Relatively calm; morning currents can strengthen; wind-driven waves reduce visibility by stirring fine volcanic sediment

## Marine Life

Sponges, hard corals, gorgonians, and anemones have colonised the fragmented hull sections. No source consulted for this re-audit named specific fish species observed at this wreck beyond general references to reef fish colonising the structure; the previous text's list of sergeant major fish, smooth trunkfish, dusky squirrelfish, longlure frogfish, rays, turtles and sea snakes could not be re-confirmed against a site-specific source and has been removed rather than restated as fact. Divers should expect typical shallow Martinique reef-fish life on the encrusted hull sections, without a confirmed species list for this particular wreck.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry directly from the beach. Additional services are available in nearby Le Carbet.

## Photography

The shallow depth and natural light make this a good site for natural-light photography throughout the day. The encrusted hull fragments offer wide-angle opportunities without the need for flash equipment at depth.
