## Nahoon Wreck

The Nahoon is a deliberately-sunk artificial reef off Les Anses d'Arlet, southern Martinique, with an unusually documented past life as a French beacon-tender before its conversion into a three-masted sailing ship.

## Overview

Multiple dive-shop histories agree on the outline: the vessel was launched in 1911 (built 1909-1911) at Royan as the *Quinette de Richemond* (also spelled *Quinette de Rochemont*), a beacon-tender built for Phares et Balises de Gironde to maintain buoys and lights in the Gironde estuary/North Sea approaches. It was later renamed *Masoala* (1948) and then *Caraïbe* (1957), and ran aground in Pointe-à-Pitre bay, Guadeloupe, in 1966 after Hurricane Inès. Henry Wakelam purchased the grounded vessel, converted it into a three-masted ship, and renamed it *Nahoon*. It was deliberately sunk off Les Anses d'Arlet as an artificial reef, an initiative of local dive centres and the Regional Tourism Committee.

**Sources disagree on two points, which are flagged rather than resolved:**
- **Sinking year**: 1993 (Alpha Plongée, Tribloo) vs. 1994 (Espace Plongée Martinique)
- **Length**: 35m (Espace Plongée Martinique) vs. 41m (reported elsewhere)

The wreck now sits flat/upright on a sandy bottom at 35-36m and is described by dive shops as remarkably well preserved, with masts still standing.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Les Anses d'Arlet / Pointe de la Baleine, southwest Martinique
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters

## Marine Life

Dive-shop reports consistently mention a green moray eel that patrols the hull, snappers that congregate near the masts, and lobsters and crabs in the structure. Also reported: stingrays, barracuda (in schools, per one source), angelfish, porcupinefish, king mackerel (Spanish mackerel/thazards), and tube and vase sponges colonising the wreck. Claims of a "steering wheel still mobile" from an earlier draft of this page could not be confirmed by any source and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The wreck lies flat on sand at 35-36m; divers typically descend along a line in open water before reaching the structure. The masts, main deck, and open holds are described as accessible. Wreck penetration beyond open areas requires proper wreck-diving training — it is not a beginner activity, and interior spaces are never guaranteed safe.

## Safety Considerations

Advanced Open Water certification (or CMAS 2 equivalent) is the minimum required by local operators, given the 35m depth. Divers should be briefed on current conditions before descending.
