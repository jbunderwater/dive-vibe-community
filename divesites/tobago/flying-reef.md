## Flying Reef

Flying Reef is a beginner-friendly drift dive on Tobago's south/Atlantic coast, a short boat ride from Crown Point, named either for its proximity to the airport or for the gentle current that lets divers "fly" along its ruler-straight reef edge.

## Overview

Re-audited 2026-07-05: a fabrication was found and removed. The previous description placed Flying Reef "in Columbus Passage" with "the reef wall extending to the north side of Little Tobago Island" — but Columbus Passage is the channel between Trinidad and Tobago near Crown Point, nowhere near Little Tobago, and no source supports that claim. Multiple independent current sources (PADI, underseatobago.com's south-coast site listing, Scuba Diver Life, Visit Tobago) consistently describe Flying Reef as being on the south to Atlantic side of Tobago, a short boat ride from the Crown Point beaches — not Speyside. This dataset's stored coordinates (near Little Tobago/Speyside) do not match that description; this is flagged for a maintainer to review, since no source gave precise-enough coordinates to confidently correct it here. Flying Reef's Atlantic waters have a generally gentle drift of 0-2 knots, though the pace can pick up. The reef is large, running in a straight line that lets divers cover significant distance on a single drift. A highlight is a sunken ship's anchor on the reef. Visibility averages 15–25 meters. Water temperature holds at 26–28°C.

## Marine Life

Re-audited 2026-07-05: the previous marine-life list ("parrotfish, blue chromis, Creole wrasse") is replaced with what multiple independent sources (Scuba Diver Life, PADI, underseatobago.com) actually confirm for this site: stingrays, turtles, nurse sharks, schools of bigeye and glasseye snapper, parrotfish, French angelfish, and porcupinefish. A sunken ship's anchor on the reef is a notable feature where porkfish and schools of Bermudan chub gather. "Blue chromis" and "Creole wrasse" are not supported by these sources and have been dropped. "Eagle rays as defining feature" and "Caribbean reef sharks attracted by fish schools," removed in the prior pass for lack of a source, remain unconfirmed and excluded.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the reef crest and follow the drift along the straight reef edge. The sunken anchor partway along the reef is a landmark worth pausing at for porkfish and chub schools. The full reef area can be covered comfortably in a single tank thanks to the current. Safety stop in the shallow reef zone at 5 meters.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Crown Point/south-coast operators, a short ride out. Giant stride entry; SMB on ascent. A good introductory drift dive for divers new to current.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Watch the sandy margins for stingray and nurse shark encounters
- The sunken anchor partway through the dive is worth extra time — porkfish and Bermudan chub school around it
- A relaxed, beginner-appropriate introduction to drift diving thanks to the generally gentle 0-2kt current
- Confirm which coast the operator is running the dive from, since sources place this site on the south/Atlantic coast rather than Speyside

## Safety Considerations

Current is generally gentle but can increase. Deploy SMB before ascending. Fire coral is present on reef crests. Standard Caribbean dive precautions apply.

## Photography

The straight reef edge and drifting fish schools make for classic wide-angle drift-dive photography. The sunken anchor with schooling porkfish and chub is a strong signature shot. Reef face photography in the 8-12 meter range works well in good ambient light.
