## Maverick Wreck

The M.V. Maverick — originally named the Scarlet Ibis — is a former Trinidad-Tobago inter-island ferry deliberately sunk in April 1997 to create an artificial reef, now resting upright at approximately 30 metres off Crown Point with the top deck roughly 15-18 metres down.

## Overview

The vessel originally served as the MV Scarlet Ibis, one of two roll-on/roll-off ferries introduced in the 1960s to provide the inter-island shipping link between Trinidad and Tobago; it served the route for approximately 25 years before retirement. After retirement, the ferry was renamed the Maverick. The Association of Tobago Dive Operators (ATDO), together with the Tourism and Industrial Development Company (TIDCO), acquired the vessel, stripped it of environmental and safety hazards, and deliberately sank it in April 1997 off Crown Point on Tobago's southwestern coast to create an artificial reef and dive site — this was a planned scuttling, not an accident.

Sources disagree significantly on the vessel's length, and the discrepancy goes beyond a simple rounding difference: visittobago.gov.tt cites 198 feet 9 inches on one page, dive operators and PADI commonly describe it as 185 feet, and at least two other independent sources (justgottadive.com, and a separate visittobago.gov.tt page) cite 350 feet. All three figures could not be reconciled from the sources available, so they are reported here as a genuine open conflict rather than resolved to a single number. The wreck rests upright on a flat sandy seabed at approximately 30 metres (100 feet); the top deck's depth is reported variously as 15m (49ft), 16.7m (55ft), and 18m (60ft) across different sources — likely reflecting measurement point or tide rather than real disagreement, but noted for completeness. The superstructure has developed fan coral and sponge encrustation and attracts large schools of Creole wrasse, striped grunts, and mangrove snappers, along with clams and reef-building crabs. A resident green moray eel and visiting cobia are reliable encounters; individual operators have also described a long-resident large grouper and a large barracuda that regular dive guides recognize on repeat visits, though these are anecdotal rather than guaranteed sightings. The Maverick is the most frequently visited wreck in Tobago and is used for Advanced Open Water, Deep Diver, and Wreck Diver certifications. Visibility averages 15–25 metres (one source describes closer to 15m/50ft on the wreck itself). Water temperature is 26–28°C.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Crown Point, southwestern Tobago
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–25 meters

## Marine Life

Large schools of Creole wrasse, brown chromis, striped grunts, and mangrove snappers swirl around the superstructure. A resident great barracuda holds position at the wreck, and cobia make regular visits. A large green moray eel is a consistent resident. The car deck interior provides sheltered habitat for reef fish communities, and vibrant sponges, corals, clams, and reef-building crabs cover the superstructure. Individual dive operators have described long-term resident individual animals — a large grouper and a notably large barracuda — that guides recognize across repeat dives; these are single-source anecdotes rather than confirmed permanent residents, so are noted as such rather than presented as guaranteed sightings.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the top deck (reported between 15-18 metres depending on source) and explore the superstructure before proceeding to the car deck level and the sandy bottom at approximately 30 metres. The wreck's upright position makes orientation straightforward. NDL limits at 30 metres are restrictive — plan for 20–25 minutes at the deepest sections before ascending to the superstructure for remaining bottom time. Safety stop at 5 metres. Typical dive duration on the wreck is reported at 30-35 minutes.
