## Maverick Wreck

The M.V. Maverick — originally named the Scarlet Ibis — is a former Trinidad-Tobago inter-island ferry deliberately sunk in 1996 or 1997 (sources differ) to create an artificial reef, resting upright at approximately 30 metres with the top deck roughly 15-18 metres down.

## Overview

The vessel is reported as the former Scarlet Ibis, a ferry that linked Trinidad and Tobago in the 1960s and 1970s (PADI). Build date, builder and tonnage could not be confirmed in two independent sources (no Wikipedia article exists, and a ship database entry is paywalled), so they are omitted. It was deliberately scuttled as an artificial reef and dive site, not lost in an accident. The sinking year is reported as 1997 by most sources (including Discover Trinidad & Tobago) and as 1996 by PADI. Length is disputed: sources report 168 ft (Scuba Diving), 185 ft (PADI), 198 ft 9 in (Visit Tobago), and 350 ft / about 107 m (Islands.com, Deeper Blue, Just Gotta Dive). These cannot be reconciled and should not be treated as settled. The wreck rests upright on sand with the bottom at roughly 30-33 m (100 ft) and the upper deck reported at 15-18 m (about 50-60 ft). Dive operators describe it as the most frequently visited wreck in Tobago, used for Advanced Open Water, Deep Diver and Wreck Diver courses. Penetration of any part of a wreck requires proper training and equipment. Visibility and temperature figures from earlier versions were not re-confirmed in this pass and should be checked with an operator.

Sources disagree significantly on the vessel's length, and the discrepancy goes beyond a simple rounding difference: visittobago.gov.tt cites 198 feet 9 inches on one page, dive operators and PADI commonly describe it as 185 feet, and at least two other independent sources (justgottadive.com, and a separate visittobago.gov.tt page) cite 350 feet. All three figures could not be reconciled from the sources available, so they are reported here as a genuine open conflict rather than resolved to a single number. The wreck rests upright on a flat sandy seabed at approximately 30 metres (100 feet); the top deck's depth is reported variously as 15m (49ft), 16.7m (55ft), and 18m (60ft) across different sources — likely reflecting measurement point or tide rather than real disagreement, but noted for completeness. The superstructure has developed fan coral and sponge encrustation and attracts large schools of Creole wrasse, striped grunts, and mangrove snappers, along with clams and reef-building crabs. A resident green moray eel and visiting cobia are reliable encounters; individual operators have also described a long-resident large grouper and a large barracuda that regular dive guides recognize on repeat visits, though these are anecdotal rather than guaranteed sightings. The Maverick is the most frequently visited wreck in Tobago and is used for Advanced Open Water, Deep Diver, and Wreck Diver certifications. Visibility averages 15–25 metres (one source describes closer to 15m/50ft on the wreck itself). Water temperature is 26–28°C.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Crown Point, southwestern Tobago
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

PADI lists large schools of Creole wrasse, brown chromis, striped grunts and mangrove snappers, and a large green moray, with cobia visiting during migration. Discover Trinidad & Tobago reports snappers and rainbow runners under the car deck and bait fish schools on the upper deck. Earlier single-operator anecdotes about a resident grouper and barracuda, and unsourced claims about the car-deck interior, were removed.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the top deck (reported between 15-18 metres depending on source) and explore the superstructure before proceeding to the car deck level and the sandy bottom at approximately 30 metres. The wreck's upright position makes orientation straightforward. No-decompression limits at 30 metres are short; plan with your operator. Safety stop at 5 metres.
