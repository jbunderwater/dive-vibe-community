## Bounty

The Gili Islands' most accessible wreck dive — a pontoon on Gili Meno's western slope fully overgrown with soft corals and sheltering diverse marine life.

## Overview

The Bounty Wreck lies on the western or southwestern side of Gili Meno. It began life as a wooden floating pier that served Gili Meno's boat traffic, not a ship — no source consulted describes it as a vessel. It sank during a storm while abandoned in the water, though sources disagree on exactly when: Gili Air Divers and Dive Central Gili both cite 2001, Manta Dive Gili Air cites 1999, PADI cites "around 2003," and Terumbu Divers says only "early 2000s." No single year is independently confirmed, so treat any specific date with caution. The structure measures approximately 30 m long and 15 m wide, with its highest point at 8–9 m and the main deck resting at 18 m — consistent across Gili Air Divers, Dive Central Gili, and PADI — making it accessible throughout its length for Open Water level divers. Over two decades the structure has been colonised by soft and hard corals and now functions as a thriving artificial reef. On the southern side of the wreck, a pair of old bicycles draped in coral have become one of the most photographed subjects in the Gili Islands, and a large bommie nearby is home to thousands of glassfish. Frogfish, scorpionfish, ghost pipefish, damselfish, and green sea turtles are confirmed by multiple independent sources.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West/southwest side of Gili Meno, Gili Islands / Lombok
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–25 m
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Scorpionfish and frogfish camouflaged against the coral-encrusted surface — confirmed by three independent sources (PADI, Gili Air Divers, Gili Divers). Green sea turtles are the most consistently reported species, confirmed by four sources. Ghost pipefish and peacock mantis shrimp among the soft coral growth, and sweetlips over the structure — confirmed by Gili Air Divers. Damselfish and a large bommie of thousands of glassfish confirmed by both PADI and Gili Air Divers. Drummerfish (convict surgeonfish) confirmed by Gili Divers. Whitetip reef sharks confirmed by Manta Dive Gili Air, along with angelfish (Emperor, Semi-Circle, Blue Girdled, Masked, Lemon Peel varieties), schooling fusiliers, butterflyfish, and several anemonefish varieties.

Pygmy seahorses appear only in Gili Divers' general site-index listing for Bounty, not on any of the dedicated wreck-specific pages (PADI, Gili Air Divers, Manta Dive Gili Air) — treat as a single-source, tentative sighting rather than a confirmed regular resident. Moray eels, which appeared in an earlier version of this page, are not confirmed by any source checked and have been removed. One source (PADI) also mentions "sharks, whales, and dolphins" in a generic list alongside the confirmed species — the whale and dolphin claims are not corroborated anywhere else and are implausible for an 18 m wreck dive, so they are excluded here.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the deeper tip of the pontoon at 18 m and work your way up to the shallowest section at 8 m. Search the coral growth methodically for frogfish and scorpionfish. The entire wreck can be circled multiple times given the generous air at this depth. Complete the safety stop above the shallow section.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Gili Meno or Gili Trawangan operators. Mooring on the wreck. Entry via back roll. Minimal current on most days. Ascent from the wreck structure for the safety stop.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Frogfish are cryptic; look for irregular shapes on the encrusted surfaces
- Scorpionfish are extremely well-camouflaged on the hull — never rest hands or knees on the wreck surface
- A torch reveals colour on the soft corals that is lost without artificial light
- Combine with Meno Wall for a comprehensive Gili Meno diving day

## Safety Considerations

Avoid contact with the coral-encrusted structure — both to protect the coral and because well-camouflaged scorpionfish can be hard to see before you touch down. Do not attempt interior penetration without wreck diving training. Always carry an SMB.

## Photography

Frogfish portraits in their cryptic positions on the wreck suit macro. Scorpionfish camouflaged on encrusted hull. Wide-angle captures the complete pontoon structure with accompanying fish life. The coral-covered hull makes stunning close-focus wide-angle subjects.
