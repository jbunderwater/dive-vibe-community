# Carpenter's Reef

A coral boulder site on Antigua's exposed southeastern coast — also known as Carpenter's Rock and Carpenter's Hole — with Caribbean reef sharks, spotted eagle rays, and nurse sharks, dived primarily as a drift in calmer months.

## Overview

Carpenter's Reef (also known locally as Carpenter's Rock or Carpenter's Hole) sits in the passage between Nanton Point and Stoney Cove in Antigua's Windward Bay area. The site consists of large, coral-covered boulders rather than a continuous reef wall, with the most dramatic diving in the form of a drift when tidal current is running. DiveCarib, which operates the site regularly, reports a depth range of 20–60ft (6–18m) with Caribbean reef sharks, spotted eagle rays, nurse sharks, green moray eels, Bermuda chub, and Atlantic blue tang. Toplist.info independently confirms the site (also called Carpenter's Hole), reporting a maximum depth of 24.4m/80ft with an average around 13.7m/45ft, soft corals and sponges, and visibility as low as 5–10m — poorer than most of Antigua's leeward sites. Scuba Travel describes the terrain as an "underwater peninsular" with cliff-like formations dropping from about 5m at the top to 20m on the deeper side, corroborating the boulder/ridge character from an independent angle. Invasive lionfish are present, consistent with their spread across Caribbean reef sites generally. The site is fully exposed to the Atlantic Ocean and requires calm conditions; toplist.info recommends the same mid-December through mid-May window as DiveCarib.

## Marine Life

The following marine life has been confirmed at Carpenter's Reef by DiveCarib: Caribbean reef sharks, spotted eagle rays, nurse sharks, green moray eels, Bermuda chub, and Atlantic blue tang. Invasive lionfish are also reported. General Caribbean reef fish inhabit the boulder complex per toplist.info's "abundant marine life" note, though that source does not itemise species. Previous descriptions mentioning horse-eye jacks, bar jacks, hawksbill turtles, and black coral at this site were unconfirmed regional generalisations that have been removed.

## Dive Profile

When current is running: entry at the upstream end of the boulder zone, drift along the coral-covered boulders at 10–18 metres, ascend gradually and exit at the downstream pickup point. When current is slack: explore the shallower boulder tops and base for eels and nurse sharks. Total dive: 35–50 minutes depending on current speed.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive. The operator assesses current conditions and times the dive accordingly. Entry by backward roll, descending quickly to working depth. When drifting, ascent occurs at the downstream pickup point with DSMB deployment. No mooring — the drift dive pickup is coordinated with the chase boat.

## Tips and Recommendations

Carpenter's Reef is not dived very often precisely because the exposed position requires calm conditions. The mid-December to mid-May window gives the best chance of good conditions. Visibility here tends to run lower (5–10m per toplist.info) than Antigua's leeward sites, so expect a moodier, more intimate dive rather than the long-range visibility of Cades Reef. DSMB skills are essential before diving here. Ask the operator whether conditions favour a drift or a static dive on the day.

## Safety Considerations

Exposed to the full Atlantic; only dive in appropriate conditions assessed by the operator. DSMB deployment is mandatory — never ascend without deploying. The exposed position means wave action can make entry and exit difficult. Lionfish are present — avoid contact. Moray eels under boulder overhangs can be large; maintain distance.
