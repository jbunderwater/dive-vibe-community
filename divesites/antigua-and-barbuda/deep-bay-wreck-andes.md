# Deep Bay Wreck (Andes)

The barque Andes lies in shallow water in Deep Bay, Antigua, where she sank in 1905 after a cargo fire.

## Overview

The Andes was a three-masted barque that sank in Deep Bay in 1905 while on a voyage from Trinidad. Sources agree on the year, the pitch cargo and the fire: Yacht Warriors and Antigua Marine Guide give the departure from Trinidad in 1905, Ancient Origins says she was bound for Chile with a cargo of tar when she caught fire and sank, and a sailing blog (mailasail.com) says the crew anchored in Deep Bay and the rush of air when the hatches were opened turned a smouldering fire into an uncontrollable one. A 2016 Historical & Archaeological Society newsletter gives the same account in more detail; its specific figures (exact dates, cargo quantity, captain, tonnage) appear in that source alone and are not repeated here.

Build date and place are not stated in any of the sources fetched. A South Australian passenger-ship database lists an iron barque named Andes built in Sunderland in 1874 (866 gross tons), but nothing links that record to the Deep Bay wreck, and the newsletter describes the Deep Bay vessel as steel, so the build details are left unresolved. No vessel length is given in the sources reviewed.

The wreck lies due south of Shipstern Point on the way into Deep Bay. Reported depth is about 25 feet (Yacht Warriors), under 30 feet (Antigua and Barbuda tourism), about 30 feet or 9 metres (Ancient Origins, The Moorings) and about 10 metres (mailasail.com). It is protected by the Marine Areas Act of 1972, and visitors are asked to leave everything in place. Sources describe it as suitable for snorkelling as well as diving.

Wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment and is not recommended on the basis of this page.

## Marine Life

Yacht Warriors reports the wreck is encrusted with coral and home to fish and sea turtles; mailasail.com reports three lionfish seen on the wreck; The Moorings lists parrotfish, damselfish and angelfish, with lobsters, arrow crabs and turtles possible. Antigua Marine Guide describes a varied and abundant population of fish and coral.

## Access and Conditions

The wreck is described as lying not far offshore in Deep Bay (HAS newsletter). The sources reviewed do not describe the entry point or route; ask a local operator. Deep Bay is also an anchorage, so use a surface marker and watch for boat traffic.
