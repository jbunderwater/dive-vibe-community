# Barbuda Reef

A general entry for diving around Barbuda, Antigua's less-visited sister island, where reefs and numerous wrecks are reported but dive infrastructure is minimal.

## Overview

Antigua and Barbuda's tourism site says Barbuda is encircled by reefs containing an enormous number of wrecks, most yet to be explored. Barbudaful.net says there is no diving centre on the island, that tanks can be hired or filled at the fisheries building and other local outlets, that hundreds of wrecks from different periods lie around the coast, that they are mapped and protected by the Marine Areas Act, and that currents at some wreck sites can be dangerous, so local advice and permission from the Council should be sought.

A 2015 ScubaBoard thread names Spanish Point as a Barbuda dive area at about 60 feet (roughly 18 metres), with rows of ridges separated by sandy channels, and warns the site is exposed and can be rough. A 2015 blog post also describes long ridges of reef off Barbuda's southeastern tip reaching out to about 21 metres. This database entry is a general area marker with low-confidence coordinates, not a specific dive site.

## Marine Life

No site-specific marine-life reports were found in the sources reviewed; the regional Caribbean fauna of Antigua and Barbuda applies, but no species are attributed to this site here.

## Access and Conditions

Barbuda diving is arranged with local help or Antigua-based operators; ScubaBoard participants suggest arranging equipment through an Antigua dive shop. Sites can be exposed and rough, so check weather and local advice.
