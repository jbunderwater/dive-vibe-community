# Andes Wreck

**Note: This entry and the Deep Bay Wreck (Andes) entry both refer to the same vessel at the same location.** The authoritative entry is Deep Bay Wreck (Andes); see that page for full details.

## Overview

The Andes was a three-masted steel barque built in England in 1874, under the command of Captain Rees Griffiths. She sailed from Trinidad on 5 June 1905 carrying 1,330 barrels of pitch destined for road paving in Chile. The poorly stowed cargo generated friction that heated the pitch and produced smoke from the ship's hollow steel masts; Griffiths made for Antigua, where the harbour master directed the burning ship away from St John's Harbour to Deep Bay. When the hatches were opened there, the influx of air caused the smouldering cargo to flare up, and the Andes sank on 9 June 1905. Sources disagree on the exact year: most (including the Historical & Archaeological Society of Antigua & Barbuda, Antigua Marine Guide, Yacht Warriors, and Ancient Origins) place the sinking in 1905, while one Alamy stock-photo caption dates it to 1904 — this database follows the 1905 consensus. The vessel's tonnage is reported as 866 tons in one historical source, but that figure has not been corroborated elsewhere, so it is omitted here pending a second source. The wreck lies in approximately 7.5–9 metres (25–30 feet) of water in Deep Bay, accessible from shore. She is protected under Antigua's Marine Areas Act of 1972. The wooden elements have long since decayed, but iron structural elements remain visible, heavily encrusted with coral and marine growth.

The wreck is also listed as Deep Bay Wreck (Andes) in this database — both entries describe the same site. See the Deep Bay Wreck (Andes) page for a full dive description.
