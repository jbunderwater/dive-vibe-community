## SAS Transvaal Wreck

The SAS Transvaal is a WWII-era Loch-class frigate, scuttled as an artificial reef in Smitswinkel Bay, False Bay, and now the northernmost of a five-wreck dive trail.

## Overview

The SAS Transvaal was laid down as HMS Loch Ard for the Royal Navy on 20 January 1944 by Harland & Wolff in Belfast and launched on 2 August 1944, but was transferred to the South African Navy before completion. Commissioned as HMSAS Transvaal (pennant F602) on 14 May 1945, she was a Loch-class frigate of 93.6m length, with a standard displacement of 1,435 long tons (rising to 2,260 long tons at deep load — sources describe her tonnage differently depending on which load condition is being cited, and one dive-guide source rounds this to "about 2,000 tonnes displacement," which is consistent with those figures). During her service she took part in repatriating South African troops from Egypt (1945-46), escorted the royal yacht during King George VI's 1947 tour of South Africa, and assisted with the annexation of the Prince Edward Islands in 1947-48. A propeller shaft failure in December 1963 led to her decommissioning on 14 August 1964; she was sold for scrap in 1977 and scuttled by explosive charges in Smitswinkel Bay on 3 August 1978 to form an artificial reef, the northernmost of five wrecks on this dive trail within what is now the Table Mountain National Park Marine Protected Area (protected since 2004, permit required).

She lies upright on a flat sand bottom at about 34m, with her main deck around 29m. The hull is actively deteriorating: the bow section has broken off and the forward superstructure has largely collapsed, so this is considered an advanced, permit-required dive and penetration should only be attempted by divers with specific wreck-diving training — the collapsing structure adds real risk, and it should never be treated as routine. Visibility is variable: sources describe typical conditions of 6-8m, occasionally over 10m, and rarely above 20m on exceptional days.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Smitswinkel Bay, False Bay, Western Cape, South Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 34 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 6-8m typical, occasionally 10m+, rarely 20m+
- **Permit**: Required — Table Mountain National Park Marine Protected Area

## Marine Life

The wreck is heavily encrusted with invertebrate life, including mauve sea cucumbers, sponges, and sea fans, plus barred fingerfin — a fish community that dive guides note is seldom reported anywhere else in the region except on the Smitswinkel wrecks specifically. Broader descriptions of "soft corals, sponges, sea fans, anemones, nudibranchs and starfish" are given for the five-wreck Smitswinkel Bay site as a group rather than for the Transvaal individually in every source.
