## Shark Alley Gansbaai

Shark Alley is the shallow channel between Dyer Island and Geyser Rock near Gansbaai — one of the most reliable places in the world to see great white sharks from a surface cage.

## Overview

Shark Alley is the shallow channel between Dyer Island and Geyser Rock, a few kilometres offshore from Gansbaai on South Africa's Western Cape coast. Geyser Rock hosts a Cape fur seal colony of around 60,000 animals, whose pups are preferred prey for the adult great white sharks that patrol the channel — making this one of the most reliable great white encounter sites anywhere. This is surface cage diving: divers are lowered in a cage just below the waterline from the boat, so scuba certification is not required. Winter months (May-August) see the highest concentration of shark activity as seal pups start entering the water. Reclassified from "beach" to "drift" for this QA pass, since every source describes it as a channel between two islands rather than a shore-entry beach site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Channel between Dyer Island and Geyser Rock, near Gansbaai, Western Cape, South Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 8 meters
- **Best Time**: May-August for peak shark activity

## Marine Life

Great white sharks ranging from about 1.5m to over 5m are the draw, patrolling the channel to hunt Cape fur seals from the Geyser Rock colony. Dyer Island itself also hosts a declining African penguin colony (roughly 5,000 birds as of a 2015 count).
