## Raggie Cave Aliwal

Raggie Cave Aliwal is a cave dive site in South Africa, Africa.

## Overview

Raggie Cave (also called Raggies Cave) is described by PADI and Aliwal Dive Centre as the most popular dive site on Aliwal Shoal — a large overhang/cavern on the edge of the reef, at a depth PADI lists as 16m (one operator guide lists it at up to 18m) where ragged-tooth sharks (sand tigers, "raggies") gather in numbers during their winter-spring migration, roughly July through November. Divers kneel on the sandy viewing area at the cave entrance and watch the sharks; entry into the cave itself is not permitted while sharks are present. Aliwal Dive Centre notes resident potato bass and several types of moray eel around the surrounding overhangs. The site sits within the Aliwal Shoal Marine Protected Area off Umkomaas.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Aliwal Shoal Marine Protected Area, off Umkomaas, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 16 meters
- **Best Time**: July-November for ragged-tooth sharks

## Marine Life

Ragged-tooth sharks are the headline species, present in the cave and its viewing area during the winter-spring migration. Resident potato bass and multiple moray eel species are reported around the surrounding overhangs by Aliwal Dive Centre.
