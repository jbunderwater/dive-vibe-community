# Aliwal Shoal

A rocky reef, the remains of an ancient sand dune, about 5 kilometres off the KwaZulu-Natal coast, known for ragged-tooth shark aggregations and two shipwrecks.

## Overview

Aliwal Shoal is a rocky reef about 5 km off the KwaZulu-Natal coast, formed from an ancient sand dune. Wikipedia gives the average depth of the Crown at about 12.5 m and of the Ridge at about 19.5 m, with the shallowest part, at the north end of the Crown, reaching within 6 m of the surface. PADI lists dive sites on the shoal from about 12 m to 27 m (Cathedral is listed at 27 m, Raggies Cave at 16 m, Chunnel Cave at 14 m, North Sands at 16 m), with wrecks at 32 m and 27 m and technical diving extending deeper. Dive styles listed include wreck, drift and reef diving. The shoal sits inside a Marine Protected Area that was expanded in 2016.

Wikipedia and PADI report ragged-tooth (grey nurse) sharks congregating between July and November; some operators cite June as the start of the season. The shoal is named after the sailing vessel Aliwal, which nearly foundered on it in 1849.

## Marine Life

Sources agree on ragged-tooth sharks (Carcharias taurus) at the shoal in the July-November window, and on hard and soft corals. PADI also lists whales, dolphins and turtles as common sightings at the shoal, without attributing them to particular dive sites. No other species are attributed here because no site-specific source was found.

## Named Sites on the Shoal

See the separate pages for [Raggie Cave](raggie-cave-aliwal.md) and the [MV Produce wreck](mv-produce-wreck.md). The SS Nebo, a steamship that sank in 1884, is the shoal's other wreck; it is not yet a separate entry in this database.

## Access

This page does not describe launch procedures, visibility, water temperature or current strength, because the sources checked did not give consistent, citable figures. Check with a local operator for current conditions. Dive operators at Umkomaas run boat trips to the shoal.
