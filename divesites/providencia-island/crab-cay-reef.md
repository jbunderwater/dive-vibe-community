## Crab Cay Reef

Crab Cay Reef surrounds a small rocky cay in the Old Providence McBean Lagoon National Natural Park, offering shallow crystal-clear diving accessible to all levels.

## Overview

Crab Cay (Cayo Cangrejo) is a small rocky cay — Wikipedia gives its area as 0.02 km2 (about 20,000 square metres) — surrounded by sandbanks, sitting within the Old Providence McBean Lagoon National Natural Park and the wider UNESCO Seaflower Biosphere Reserve. (A previous version of this page stated the cay was 500 square metres; that figure could not be traced to any source and has been corrected against Wikipedia.) The reef tops at around 12 metres and is noted for crystal-clear water and strong fish density resulting from the park's protection. The site is accessible as a snorkel or introductory dive and is commonly visited on boat tours around the island. Reviewers on TripAdvisor report turtles, triggerfish, needlefish, thousands of small fish sheltering under the wooden pier, and occasional small sharks.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Providencia Island, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters

## Marine Life

Sea turtles, triggerfish, needlefish, and dense schools of juvenile fish are commonly reported at Crab Cay, including large numbers of small fish sheltering under the wooden pier. An occasional small shark has been sighted near the pier. The dense fish populations reflect the site's protection within the national park.
