## El Planchon

El Planchon is Providencia's own wreck dive — a capsized WWII-era tanker on the outer reef — and is a different vessel from the similarly-named "Blue Diamond" wreck that several databases place off San Andrés island.

## Overview

**Renamed from "Blue Diamond" on 2026-08-25 after wreck-history research.** The prior entry carried a location conflict: PADI, Scuba Diving Magazine, Zentacle, DiveAdvisor, and colombiacorners.com all independently place the "Blue Diamond" (Barco Hundido) wreck off the west coast of San Andrés island, roughly 90km from Providencia and well outside this destination's bounds — none of them place it at Providencia. A fresh re-check of myscubadivinggearguide.com, the source the prior entry leaned on for a Providencia "Blue Diamond," found that page does not use that name at all; it names Providencia's wreck "Planchon."

Providencia's actual, well-documented wreck goes by El Planchón (sometimes "Planchon" or "El Planchon"). It is confirmed under this name, at this location, by six independent sources: PADI's Spanish-language site listing (coordinates 13.37167, -81.41796, within Providencia's bounds), Joe's Scuba Shack, Waterpixels, Hotel Deep Blue, and the two local operator directories Felipe Diving Shop and The Dive Shop Old Providence. It is a cargo/petroleum tanker from the WWII era, lying capsized (upside down) on a coral terrace. Local guides quoted by Waterpixels and corroborated by Hotel Deep Blue describe it as a vessel that resupplied German submarines and was deliberately scuttled by its own crew near the end of the war — this is oral history relayed by local dive guides, not a claim traceable to a maritime archive or registry, and no source could independently confirm a specific ship name (beyond the site's own "El Planchón" designation, which may simply be a descriptive term), build date, or tonnage. Those details are omitted here rather than invented. One source gave a single, unconfirmed length figure (~30m) that could not be cross-checked against a second independent source, so it has also been omitted.

Reported depth varies by source: Joe's Scuba Shack cites 15m (50ft); The Dive Shop Old Providence gives 18–24m. The wreck sits on a coral terrace, densely colonized by coral and sponge growth, and forms what one source describes as "an artificial cliff." Penetration of the interior is explicitly prohibited/not recommended by every source that addresses it — this is not a site for wreck penetration.

Note: a separate PADI listing named "El Planchón" exists off Isla Fuerte (near Cartagena, ~9.36°N/-76.18°W) with an unrelated "sunk in 1984" cargo-barge history. That is a different wreck at a different location sharing a generic name ("planchón" is a common Spanish term for a flat-bottomed cargo barge/tanker) — not a source of genuine conflict about this Providencia site once the coordinates are checked.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Providencia Island, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 24 meters

## Marine Life

Multiple sources agree on parrotfish, jacks/angelfish, moray eels, lobsters, and occasional reef sharks around the wreck structure; The Dive Shop Old Providence adds basket stars on the hull. Waterpixels separately reports triggerfish and groupers, with reef sharks described as regular presences. The surrounding reef supports the Caribbean reef fish typical of the Seaflower Biosphere Reserve.
