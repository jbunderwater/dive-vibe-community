## Blue Diamond

This entry is recorded as "Blue Diamond," a shallow wreck near Providencia — but a 2026-08-23 deep-research re-audit found no source, in over a dozen re-checked, that actually documents a wreck of this name at Providencia. The name is very likely a mislabeling of "El Planchón," the one wreck consistently attested at this destination.

## Overview

**Identity conflict (re-confirmed 2026-08-23):** PADI's "Blue Diamond" / Barco Hundido dive-site page gives exact coordinates of 12.53547°N, -81.73594°W — this is San Andrés island, roughly 50 km from Providencia, and PADI describes it as a distinct, separately-documented wreck. No source checked in this audit places a wreck called "Blue Diamond" at Providencia (13.3°N).

By contrast, five independent sources consistently document a single Providencia-area wreck under a different name, "El Planchón" (also "Planchon"): Joe's Scuba Shack describes a WWII-era submarine supply ship at 15 m, hull side up, mostly intact; PADI's Spanish-language "El Planchón" listing gives coordinates of 13.37167°N, -81.41796°W (within Providencia's waters) and calls it a WWII-era cargo tanker lying upside down; Travel and Diving lists "Planchón de Providencia" as a shipwreck site frequented by sharks, moray eels, and barracuda; The Dive Shop Old Providence lists "El Planchón" at 18–24 m, describing it as the wreck of a WWII cargo oil tanker; and Felipe Diving Center — the shop run by the diver credited with naming most of Providencia's sites — lists "Planchón" at 50 ft (15 m) and specifically calls it a German WWII oil tanker wreck. None of these five sources uses the name "Blue Diamond."

Given this, the "Blue Diamond" name recorded for this entry is not confirmed by any independent source, and is most likely either (a) a mislabeling that borrowed the name of the separate, well-documented San Andrés wreck, or (b) a purely local nickname not attested in any source checked. This entry has **not** been renamed to "El Planchón," because no source explicitly ties these specific coordinates to that name — that would require guessing rather than confirming. Because the vessel's actual name cannot be confirmed, its build date, exact length, tonnage, and precise sinking cause cannot be responsibly cross-checked or reported here; conflicting or invented specifics are not included.

What multiple sources do agree on for a Providencia-area wreck consistent with this location: a WWII-era cargo/oil tanker, resting upside-down or on its side, at a depth in the 12–24 m range depending on source, colonised by coral and sponges, and accessible to Open Water-level divers.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Providencia Island, Caribbean (name/identity unconfirmed — see note above)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive

## Marine Life

Sources describing the Providencia-area wreck (under the name "El Planchón") report parrotfish, moray eels, lobsters, and gray reef sharks around the structure. Because the vessel identity for this specific entry is unconfirmed, these should be read as regional/analogous reports rather than a confirmed site-specific list for an entry named "Blue Diamond."
