## Tete's Place

Tete's Place is a very shallow coral shoal known as Providencia's "fishiest" dive site, with dense pillar coral formations and large schools of reef fish at just 7–9 metres depth.

## Overview

Tete's Place is a coralline shoal close to Providencia's shoreline at 7 to 9 metres, featuring a step-like descent over a sandy and rocky bottom with sections of seagrass and algae. The site is notable for its living stands of pillar coral — a species now considered functionally extinct in Florida but thriving here, confirmed independently by Scuba Diving Magazine and colombia-travels.com in addition to the local operator description — along with octocorals and a high density of schooling fish. Waterpixels specifically documents mixed schools of bluestriped and French grunts, snappers, sweetlips, juvenile fish, puffer fish, and nurse sharks at this site. The Dive Shop Old Providence separately reports stingrays and, occasionally, bottlenose dolphins here — the dolphin sighting is a single-source report and should be treated as occasional rather than expected. Scuba Diving Magazine and colombia-travels.com both describe spectacular night dives at this site, with bioluminescent ostracods lighting up the water column. The shallow, calm conditions make it equally suitable for snorkelers and entry-level divers. It sits within the UNESCO Seaflower Biosphere Reserve.

**Re-audit note (2026-08-24):** the previous version of this page named "peacock flounder" and "yellow-spotted stingray" specifically; neither could be re-confirmed at this site by any source and has been removed. Only "stingrays" generally is confirmed, via The Dive Shop Old Providence.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Providencia Island, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 9 meters

## Marine Life

Dense stands of pillar coral and octocorals support large schools of bluestriped and French grunts, snappers, sweetlips, and squirrelfish, with moray eels occupying the reef crevices. Puffer fish, nurse sharks, and stingrays are also reported here, and bottlenose dolphins are occasionally sighted (single-source report). Night dives reveal bioluminescent ostracods in the water column. The site is described by local operators as the "fishiest" dive on Providencia for sheer fish density.
