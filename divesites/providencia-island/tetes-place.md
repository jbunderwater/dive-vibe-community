## Tete's Place

Tete's Place is a very shallow coral shoal known as Providencia's "fishiest" dive site, with dense pillar coral formations and large schools of reef fish at just 7–9 metres depth.

## Overview

Tete's Place is a coralline shoal close to Providencia's shoreline at 7 to 9 metres, featuring a step-like descent over a sandy and rocky bottom with sections of seagrass and algae. The site is notable for its living stands of pillar coral — a species now considered functionally extinct in Florida but thriving here — confirmed independently by Felipe Diving Center's own listing ("corales de pilar"), along with octocorals and a high density of schooling fish. The shallow, calm conditions make it equally suitable for snorkelers and entry-level divers. It sits within the UNESCO Seaflower Biosphere Reserve.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Providencia Island, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 9 meters

## Marine Life

Dense stands of pillar coral and octocorals support large schools of bluestriped and French grunts (photo-documented at this site by Waterpixels Travel), snappers, and squirrelfish, with moray eels and stingrays occupying the reef crevices and sandy sections (The Dive Shop Old Providence). The Dive Shop Old Providence also reports occasional bottlenose dolphin sightings — a single-source claim not corroborated elsewhere, noted as occasional rather than routine. A previously-listed "peacock flounder" sighting and "yellow-spotted" qualifier on the stingrays could not be traced to any re-pulled source and have been removed. The site is described by local operators as the "fishiest" dive on Providencia for sheer fish density.
