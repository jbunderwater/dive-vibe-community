## Bongoyo Reef

Bongoyo Island is a real, well-documented uninhabited island roughly 2.5 km north of Dar es Salaam (Wikipedia), in the Dar es Salaam Marine Reserve. It is reached via a roughly 30-minute boat ride from the Slipway complex on the Msasani Peninsula. The island and its reef are covered by three independent domains (Wikipedia, Tanzania Tourism, Seaunseen).

## Overview

The habitat ranges from shallow, gently sloping coral reef to sandy expanses, seagrass beds, and a shark lagoon (Tanzania Tourism). Bongoyo also holds the largest coconut crab specimens recorded in the country (Wikipedia — Dar es Salaam Marine Reserve). A Tanzania-based diver/photographer site (Seaunseen) documents several genuinely distinct named features here: a wreck listed as "MV Moto" (only Seaunseen was found documenting it, so no history, dimensions or depth are given here, per the two-source rule for wreck claims), plus "Bongoyo Inner Reef," "Bongoyo Patches," and "Pipefish City" as separate, named sites. Our "Bongoyo Reef" entry most likely corresponds to general reef diving around the island rather than one specific named feature — this ambiguity is noted rather than resolved by invention.

## Marine Life

Confirmed by Tanzania Tourism: butterflyfish, groupers, sweetlips, sea urchins, giant clams, octopuses, starfish, hermit crabs, and sea cucumbers, with hawksbill and green sea turtles nesting on the island's beaches and reported as frequently seen by divers over the reefs. These species are listed by Tanzania Tourism for the Bongoyo/Mbudya reserve area rather than for one dive spot. Claims of seahorses, ghost pipefish, and reef sharks from the previous version could not be independently confirmed and have been removed rather than repeated as fact.

## Entry and Exit

Day boats depart from the Slipway complex on the Msasani Peninsula, roughly 30 minutes to the island (Wikipedia, Tanzania Tourism). A small conservation/reserve fee applies.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your operator whether "Bongoyo Reef" refers to the general island reef, "Bongoyo Inner Reef," or "Bongoyo Patches" — these are documented as distinct sites nearby
- A wreck named "Moto" is listed by one source nearby; ask your operator for current details. Wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment
- Bongoyo's coconut crabs are a feature of the island itself, not the dive
