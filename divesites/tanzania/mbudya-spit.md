## Mbudya Spit

Mbudya Island, part of the Dar es Salaam Marine Reserve, is a real and well-documented island roughly 20 minutes by boat from Kunduchi on the mainland. Independent travel reporting describes the island itself as "essentially a comma-shaped sandbar" with a reef beginning roughly 50 meters offshore where the sand drops away — a shape consistent with a "spit" feature, though this site's recorded coordinates sit slightly southeast of the island's charted center rather than confirmed at a specific "northern tip," so the exact match to a named "spit" feature is not certain.

## Overview

Mbudya Island (Wikipedia-confirmed coordinates 6°39'23"S 39°15'0"E) is one of four islands (with Bongoyo, Pangavini, and Fungu Yasini) making up the northern Dar es Salaam Marine Reserve, reachable by roughly 15-20 minute boat ride from Kunduchi or the Msasani Slipway. The island hosts the endemic isopod *Metacirolana mbudya*, found nowhere else, and endangered coconut crabs. Independent travel reporting (The EastAfrican) describes the island's shape and notes the reef begins roughly 50 meters from shore where the sandy bottom drops off. No source, however, names or describes a dive site called "Mbudya Spit" specifically, so depth, visibility, and marine-life claims below are stated cautiously.
