## Maziwe Reef

Maziwe Reef surrounds Maziwe Island (also spelled Maziwi), a small sand island roughly 8 kilometers southeast of Pangani on Tanzania's northern coast. The island and surrounding reef form the Maziwe Marine Reserve, established in 1975 to protect one of East Africa's most important sea turtle breeding sites, and diving/snorkeling here is centered on turtle encounters and reef life rather than dramatic topography.

## Overview

Maziwe Island is a low, unvegetated sand cay surrounded by coral reef. Historical records describe the island as once forested, but it was denuded by human activity (tree-cutting) by the 1980s and is now largely a sandbank, most visible at low tide. The reserve is documented as hosting close to 400 species of fish and around 35 genera of hard and soft corals, sponges, and algae. Multiple sources describe it as among the best diving and snorkeling destinations on Tanzania's mainland coast, though none of the sources checked gave precise dive depth or visibility figures for the surrounding reef — this listing's 15m maximum depth and Beginner rating should be treated as a general characterization rather than a precisely sourced figure.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Maziwe Marine Reserve, ~8km offshore Pangani, northern Tanzania
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Coral reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Current**: Not independently confirmed
- **Best Time**: Year-round access; calmer seas reported June–September and December–February

## Marine Life

Green sea turtles are well documented at Maziwe, both for reef encounters and — historically — for nesting on the island itself (alongside olive ridley turtles; historical records cite roughly 200 nests per season from both species when the island still had vegetation). Sources do not give a reliable per-dive turtle count, so no specific number is stated here. Beyond turtles, sources describe general reef fish diversity (nearly 400 species reported) and coral cover, but do not name specific additional species as confirmed sightings at this site, so none are listed here.

## Dive Profile

Not independently confirmed in detail. General reef diving around the island's fringing coral, at depths consistent with a shallow sand-cay reef system, is the expected profile.

## Entry and Exit

Boat departure from Pangani or nearby beaches; sources describe the reserve as open and visitable year-round for swimming, snorkeling, and diving, with a reserve entry fee. Exact boat travel times were not independently confirmed for this update.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Maziwe is an active marine reserve and turtle nesting site — avoid touching or crowding turtles
- The island itself is a low sandbank that is most visible at low tide; check timing with your operator
- Snorkeling is a viable alternative to diving here given the shallow depths reported

## Safety Considerations

No unusual site-specific hazards were identified in sources checked. General precautions for shallow reef diving apply: monitor for boat traffic, and be mindful of the reserve's conservation status when interacting with marine life.

## Photography

Turtle encounters are the primary photographic draw, supported by multiple independent sources. No further site-specific photography guidance was confirmed.
