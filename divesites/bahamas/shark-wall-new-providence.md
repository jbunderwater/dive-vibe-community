# Shark Wall New Providence

A Caribbean reef shark encounter site on the southwestern wall of New Providence Island, where the Nassau dive community has maintained one of the longest-running shark interaction programmes in the Bahamas.

## Overview

**CORRECTION (2026-07-17 re-audit):** The previous pass mischaracterized this site. Shark Wall is the **non-feeding, free-swim half** of Stuart Cove's Dive Bahamas' 2-tank Shark Adventure — it is paired with the feeding dive at the adjacent "Arena" (see `shark-arena-nassau.md`), and Stuart Cove's also runs an alternate pairing of "Shark Wall North" + "The Runway." This is confirmed today across four independently re-checked sources (stuartcove.com, Bluewater Dive Travel, Bahamas Tour Center, ScubaBoard): the first dive of the Shark Adventure is a "free swim" along the wall in a non-feeding environment — sharks follow divers because the adjacent feeding site is nearby and "they know the dinner bell will ring soon," but "no bait is exposed on this dive" (Stuart Cove's own wording). The previous markdown's description of a divemaster running a feeding session at Shark Wall was not supported by any source found today and has been rewritten below.

Shark Wall sits along the southwestern wall of New Providence Island. Depth is reported inconsistently across sources found today: operator-side descriptions (Bahamas Tour Center, Bluewater Dive Travel) describe a "Runway Wall" figure of roughly 35 ft (~11 m), while a ScubaBoard trip report re-checked today for "Shark Wall South" logs an actual dive to 60–100 ft (18–30 m) with 100+ ft visibility and water temperature around 75°F (24°C) — notably deeper and cooler than the operator marketing figures and the previous pass's 6–12 m / 25–28°C. Both figures are presented here rather than silently picking one; whether "Shark Wall South" is the same site as "Shark Wall" could not be confirmed with certainty. Caribbean reef sharks are the signature species, seen consistently per every source re-checked today.

Regarding the historical claim that "Nassau's shark diving tradition dates to the 1980s": Stuart Cove's own company history page (re-fetched today) states their shark work began with a film production job in 1980 and that they have run daily shark dives since 1987 — this is a real, dated claim from the operator itself. A second domain (scubadiving.com, via search-result snippet) corroborates that the company was founded in 1978 and that Stuart Cove "pioneered Bahamas shark diving with recreational divers," but scubadiving.com returned HTTP 403 on every direct fetch attempted today, so that corroboration is snippet-only and was not independently re-verified against the live page. Per this repo's 2+ independent source policy for historical claims, this claim is best treated as **operator self-reported, singly-confirmed today** rather than fully independently verified — retained below with that caveat rather than stated as flat fact.

## Marine Life

**Marine-life audit note (2026-07-17):** the previous pass's "8–20 individuals are typically present" figure could not be traced to any source and appears to have been invented — it is removed. What could be confirmed today: Caribbean reef sharks are the signature species and are seen consistently on the free-swim (sharks "follow" divers throughout, per Stuart Cove's own description, because the adjacent feeding site primes them — but no bait is used here, so numbers are lower than the feeding dive at The Arena). One ScubaBoard trip report re-checked today, logged against "Shark Wall South," recorded a modest count of "3 gorgeous Reef Sharks" on that specific dive — a real, sourced number, but from a single trip report and not necessarily representative of typical counts. The same ScubaBoard report also listed grouper (species not specified — do not assume Nassau grouper without confirmation), jacks, yellowtail snapper, and "diverse coral" at that site. Nurse sharks, French grunt, French/grey angelfish, large parrotfish, hawksbill turtles, and southern stingrays — all present in the previous pass's text — were NOT confirmed at this specific site by any source re-checked today and have been removed as unverified. The previous claim that grouper are "the site's namesake fish" is also removed: the site is named "Shark Wall," not after any grouper species, and no source connects grouper to the site's name. Separately, as general regional context (not a site-specific finding): Nassau grouper populations are documented as severely depleted Bahamas-wide (IUCN Critically Endangered listing in 2018; NOAA and Perry Institute research confirm heavy overfishing), so any grouper encountered at this or other Bahamas sites should be assumed occasional rather than reliable.

## Dive Profile

**Corrected per today's re-audit:** this is a free-swim dive, not a feeding dive — there is no divemaster-managed feeding position here (that happens at the paired Arena/Runway site on the second tank). Divers descend to the wall (reported depth 10–14 m per operator sources, or considerably deeper — 18–30 m — per one ScubaBoard trip report) and swim along the wall face. Per Stuart Cove's own description, Caribbean reef sharks accompany divers throughout the dive because the adjacent feeding site is nearby, even though no bait is used on this dive. Divers explore the wall for the duration of the dive before ascending; the feeding encounter follows as a separate second tank at The Arena or The Runway.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Nassau-based operators, primarily Stuart Cove's. Entry by giant stride or backward roll. The site uses a mooring to prevent anchor damage to the reef. DSMB recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

Shark Wall is typically dived as the first, non-feeding tank of Stuart Cove's Shark Adventure, immediately preceding the feeding dive at The Arena (or, on the alternate rotation, Shark Wall North preceding The Runway) — see the distinctness note on `shark-arena-nassau.md`. It gives divers a calmer, non-feeding shark encounter before the more intense feeding dive. A ScubaBoard trip report re-checked today notes good grouper, jacks, snapper, and coral along the wall itself, independent of the shark encounter.

## Safety Considerations

Follow all briefings from the operator. Keep hands close to the body throughout. Do not reach toward sharks or feeding positions. Caribbean reef sharks at this site are habituated but remain wild animals — unexpected movements or equipment issues can cause unpredictable behaviour. Always dive with the operator's divemaster.

## Photography

Shark Wall is ideal for entry-level shark photography — multiple sharks, shallow depth, good light, and predictable behaviour. Wide-angle or fisheye captures multiple sharks with the wall as backdrop. The 6–10 meter depth range provides excellent natural light for natural-colour photography without strobes. Time the shutter for moments when sharks are oriented toward the camera for impact.
