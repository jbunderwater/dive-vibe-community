# Shark Wall New Providence

A Caribbean reef shark encounter site on the southwestern wall of New Providence Island, where the Nassau dive community has maintained one of the longest-running shark interaction programmes in the Bahamas.

## Overview

**Correction (2026-07-13 re-audit):** This entry previously described Shark Wall as a feeding dive and typed it as a reef. Stuart Cove's own program description (stuartcove.com/shark-diving/shark-adventure/) is explicit that "Shark Wall" is the *non-feeding* half of its two-tank Shark Adventure — a "free swim" where no bait is exposed and sharks follow the group naturally; feeding happens on the separate Arena/Runway sand-site dive. Stuart Cove's also lists "Shark Wall" as "a coral wall along the Tongue of the Ocean" at 45 ft (~14 m) — i.e., a wall, not a flat reef ledge. Both errors are corrected below. Note also that this entry's coordinates (25.025, -77.4917) sit roughly 1 km from the separate "Stuart Cove Wall" entry in this database (25.0333, -77.4833); both appear to describe the same real site ("Shark Wall"), and should be reviewed for consolidation.

Shark Wall is one of Nassau's two primary shark-encounter dive sites, operated along the southwestern drop-off of New Providence Island by Stuart Cove's Dive Bahamas. It is a coral wall (not a flat reef) reaching a working depth of roughly 14 meters, where Caribbean reef sharks are reliably present and follow divers along the wall face in a non-feeding "free swim" — a deliberately more natural encounter than the feeding dive at the Arena. Recreational shark feeding dives at Stuart Cove's began in the early 1990s (Stuart Cove's own shark work, rooted in his film career, dates to the late 1970s), and Shark Wall has since become one of the operator's flagship sites, hosting a large share of the roughly 20,000 divers and snorkelers Stuart Cove's serves annually. Visibility averages 20–30 meters. Water temperature is 25–28°C.

## Marine Life

Caribbean reef sharks are the confirmed, source-backed species at this wall — present reliably enough that Stuart Cove's runs a daily non-feeding encounter here. No source gives a precise shark count specific to this site, so the previously listed "8–20 individuals" figure has been removed as unconfirmed. General Nassau/Stuart Cove's shark-diving descriptions also mention nurse sharks, stingrays, and turtles in the same operating area, though not confirmed at this specific wall with site-level precision. No site-specific source was found for French grunt, yellowtail snapper, French/grey angelfish, parrotfish, hawksbill turtles, or Nassau grouper at this exact location — those claims have been removed rather than presented as confirmed site records. (The earlier text also implied the site was named after Nassau grouper; the site is named for the city of Nassau, not the fish — that line has been removed as misleading.)

## Dive Profile

Descent to the wall crest at approximately 12 meters, then along the wall face to the shark-encounter depth of roughly 14 meters. Divers position along the wall and face seaward. This is run as a **non-feeding** "free swim" — no bait is exposed, and Caribbean reef sharks approach and follow the group out of habituation to the area rather than active feeding. Divers explore the wall for the remainder of their air supply before ascending.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Nassau-based operators, primarily Stuart Cove's. Entry by giant stride or backward roll. The site uses a mooring to prevent anchor damage to the reef. DSMB recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

Shark Wall is one of the most reliable and accessible shark encounters in Nassau — it is included in nearly every two-tank Shark Adventure trip, always paired with a feeding dive at the Arena or Runway. Because no bait is used here, sharks behave more naturally than at the feeding site, making this the better dive for observing normal shark behavior. Position toward the rear or side of the diver group (not directly in the lead) for the best view of sharks approaching along the wall.

## Safety Considerations

Follow all briefings from the operator. Keep hands close to the body throughout. Caribbean reef sharks at this site are habituated to divers but remain wild animals — unexpected movements or equipment issues can cause unpredictable behaviour. Always dive with the operator's divemaster.

## Photography

Shark Wall is well suited to shark photography — multiple sharks, a wall backdrop, good light at working depth, and non-feeding behavior that tends to look more natural in images than a feeding-frenzy shot. Wide-angle or fisheye captures multiple sharks with the wall as backdrop. Natural light at the ~12–14 meter depth range is generally sufficient without strobes in typical Nassau visibility.
