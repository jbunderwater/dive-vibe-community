# Shark Wall New Providence

A genuine wall dive on the southwestern drop-off of New Providence Island — the free-swim (non-feeding) half of Stuart Cove's Dive Bahamas' two-tank Shark Adventure, where Caribbean reef sharks cruise the wall face alongside divers.

## Overview

Shark Wall is a re-classification from this dataset's previous "reef" tag: Stuart Cove's own site describes the first dive of the Shark Adventure programme as taking place along "a magnificent wall," naming it "Shark Wall," and a Bahamas tourism feature on Stuart Cove's operation separately describes the operator's southern wall as starting around 35 feet (11 m) and "dropping vertically," rivalling any wall dive — this is a true vertical drop-off, not a reef ledge. On this dive, sharks swim calmly alongside divers in a non-feeding, observation-only format (the feeding dive happens separately at Shark Arena/The Arena). Nassau's shark diving tradition dates to the 1980s, when Stuart Cove and other operators began habituating sharks to encounters, developing protocols that have influenced shark diving operations worldwide. The wall's upper section carries good hard coral coverage; the wall itself continues far past recreational depths into the Tongue of the Ocean. Visibility averages 20–30 meters. Water temperature is 25–28°C.

## Marine Life

Caribbean reef sharks are the signature species on this dive, cruising the wall face in a non-feeding encounter. Beyond the sharks, sources describe a typical Nassau wall-reef community without naming precise counts or species specific to this exact site — expect grunts, snappers, angelfish, and other reef fish typical of Nassau's southwestern wall, but treat any detailed species list here as general rather than site-confirmed.

## Dive Profile

Descent to the wall crest at approximately 11 meters, then along the wall face to around 20–25 meters for the recreational portion (the wall itself drops much further). Divers position along the wall and face outward as Caribbean reef sharks approach from open water and work the wall face, with the divemaster managing the encounter. After the observation period, divers can continue along the wall for the remainder of their air supply before ascending.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Nassau-based operators, primarily Stuart Cove's. Entry by giant stride or backward roll. The site uses a mooring to prevent anchor damage to the reef. DSMB recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

Shark Wall is the most reliable and most accessible shark encounter in Nassau — it is included in nearly every two-tank Nassau dive trip alongside the Shark Arena feeding dive. Because this is a genuine wall, watch your depth and no-decompression limits as well as the sharks — it's easy to drift deeper than planned along a vertical face. Position at the outer edge of the diver group for the best photographic angles during the encounter.

## Safety Considerations

Follow all briefings from the operator. Keep hands close to the body throughout — do not reach toward sharks. Caribbean reef sharks at this site are habituated but remain wild animals — unexpected movements or equipment issues can cause unpredictable behaviour. As with any wall dive, monitor depth carefully since the wall continues far below recreational limits. Always dive with the operator's divemaster.

## Photography

Shark Wall is ideal for entry-level shark photography — multiple sharks, a dramatic wall backdrop, good light, and predictable behaviour. Wide-angle or fisheye captures multiple sharks against the wall face. The upper wall (11–20 m) provides good natural light for natural-colour photography with minimal strobe use. Time the shutter for moments when sharks are oriented toward the camera for impact.
