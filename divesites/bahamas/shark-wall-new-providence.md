# Shark Wall New Providence

A Caribbean reef shark encounter site on the southwestern wall of New Providence Island, where the Nassau dive community has maintained one of the longest-running shark interaction programmes in the Bahamas.

## Overview

Shark Wall is one of the original Nassau shark dive sites, run by Stuart Cove's Dive Bahamas along the southwestern coast of New Providence Island — and, true to its name, it is a genuine wall dive rather than a flat reef. Stuart Cove's own materials describe the site as "a magnificent wall" where divers free-swim with Caribbean reef sharks in a non-feeding encounter, with the drop-off beginning around 12 meters (40 feet) and continuing down. It is typically paired as the first dive of a two-tank trip with the shallower feeding site (The Arena or The Runway, depending on which pairing is running that day). Nassau's shark diving tradition traces to Stuart Cove, who began working with sharks on film productions around 1980, was diving with sharks daily by 1987, and introduced recreational shark encounters to the public in the early 1990s — protocols that have since influenced shark diving operations worldwide. Visibility averages 20–30 meters. Stuart Cove's own materials list average water temperature as 22–26°C (72–78°F).

## Marine Life

Caribbean reef sharks are the signature species, swimming calmly along the wall face during the free-swim portion of the dive. Nurse sharks and Caribbean reef sharks are both reported at this specific site by regional dive guides. Sea turtles and southern stingrays are seen regularly in the New Providence wall/reef complex. Nassau grouper — the Bahamas' national fish — are present in the area, though we could not confirm a site-specific population trend for Shark Wall in current sources, so we've dropped the earlier claim about historical decline from overfishing pending a site-specific citation.

## Dive Profile

Descent along the wall face beginning around 12 meters. This is the non-feeding "free swim" half of Stuart Cove's Shark Adventure — Caribbean reef sharks swim calmly alongside divers along the wall rather than being fed, with the divemaster present to manage the encounter. Divers follow the wall face for the bulk of the dive before ascending; the feeding portion of the trip happens separately at the paired sand-bottom site (The Arena or The Runway).

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Nassau-based operators, primarily Stuart Cove's. Entry by giant stride or backward roll. The site uses a mooring to prevent anchor damage to the reef structures at the wall top. DSMB recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

Shark Wall is the most reliable and most accessible shark encounter in Nassau — it is included in nearly every two-tank Nassau dive trip as the free-swim companion to the feeding dive. The wall top has good coral and interesting fish life beyond the shark encounter itself. Position at the outer edge of the diver group for the best photographic angles.

## Safety Considerations

Follow all briefings from the operator. Keep hands close to the body throughout. Do not reach toward sharks or feeding positions. Caribbean reef sharks at this site are habituated but remain wild animals — unexpected movements or equipment issues can cause unpredictable behaviour. Always dive with the operator's divemaster.

## Photography

Shark Wall is ideal for entry-level shark photography — multiple sharks, moderate depth, good light, and predictable behaviour. Wide-angle or fisheye captures multiple sharks with the wall face as backdrop. The wall top sits shallow enough (starting around 12 m) for good natural light without strobes. Time the shutter for moments when sharks are oriented toward the camera for impact.
