# Lost Blue Hole Andros

One of hundreds of blue holes surrounding Andros Island — a circular marine sinkhole accessible by boat that offers a unique Bahamian blue hole experience in the shallow waters of the Andros barrier reef system.

## Overview

Andros Island has more blue holes per square kilometre than anywhere else on Earth — sources describe over 200, most still unexplored. This entry's coordinates (24.7°N, 77.78°W) place it offshore near Fresh Creek/Andros Town, in the vicinity of Small Hope Bay Lodge's diving area — a real and plausible location for one of Andros's many blue holes. However, **the specific name "Lost Blue Hole" could not be independently confirmed for a site in Andros.** Every source we could find that documents a dive site by that exact name — including a structured dive-site database with GPS coordinates (25.03°N, 77.14°W) and multiple operator/TripAdvisor references — places "Lost Blue Hole" off Nassau/New Providence, roughly 45 km northeast of this listing's coordinates, near Rose Island. Named Andros blue holes documented in regional sources include Stargate, Cousteau's, Captain Bill's/Church's, Uncle Charlie's, Benjamin's, and Rat Cay — none called "Lost Blue Hole." It is possible this listing is one of Andros's many locally-known but undocumented blue holes that was mislabeled (in OSM or a prior curation pass) using the name of the better-known Nassau site. Readers should not assume this description matches a specific, verified named site — the depth and profile below reflect general characteristics of Andros's offshore blue holes rather than confirmed facts about this exact hole. The hole is described as dropping from the shallow shelf depth to about 30 meters, with the classic blue hole profile of narrow entrance widening into a larger chamber. Andros's blue holes are part of an extensive cave system connected to the ocean through tidal breathing — some flow in or out with the tides. Visibility in the offshore holes is generally excellent regionally: 25–35 meters in clear Bahamas water.

## Marine Life

No site-specific marine life reports were found for this location under the name "Lost Blue Hole." The description below reflects general characteristics reported for Andros offshore blue holes, not confirmed sightings at this exact site. The upper section (0–15 m) typically has a mixed reef community — parrotfish, angelfish, and blue tang from the adjacent shelf reef. Regional accounts describe a halocline (saltwater/freshwater interface) in offshore Andros blue holes around 15–25 m that creates a visual distortion, with clearer salt water below it. Spiny lobster and nurse sharks are reported at some Andros blue holes generally, but their presence at this specific location is unconfirmed.

## Dive Profile

Descent from the boat to the hole entrance at 3–5 meters, then controlled descent through the halocline at 15–20 meters to the clear saltwater below. The halocline creates a visible shimmering boundary and water temperature drop. Advanced divers explore the saltwater section to 30 meters while intermediate divers hover at the halocline for the visual experience before ascending.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Andros-based operators. Entry by giant stride from the boat. The hole entrance is typically identifiable from above as a dark blue circle against the light-coloured shelf. DSMB recommended for the open-water ascent.

## Tips and Recommendations

The halocline is the defining visual experience of a blue hole dive — the shimmering boundary between the upper murky layer and the crystal-clear saltwater below is dramatic and disorienting at first encounter. Avoid strong finning through the halocline as it disturbs the layers and reduces the visual effect. Andros operators know which holes are currently most accessible and most spectacular — follow local guidance rather than relying on general maps.

## Safety Considerations

Blue holes involve a degree of overhead environment — do not descend into the narrower lower sections without appropriate training. The halocline can cause temporary disorientation. Tidal flow in and out of some holes creates current — check with the operator on the day's tidal state. Cave diving beyond the cavern zone requires technical cave certification. Recreational divers should not penetrate further than natural daylight penetrates.

## Photography

The halocline is the primary photographic attraction of Andros blue holes — the shimmering interface layer creates a surreal visual effect that photographs dramatically with a wide-angle or fisheye lens. Looking up through the halocline from below, with the refracted surface light above, is a signature blue hole image. The circular opening photographed from below with sunlight streaming in is a classic composition.
