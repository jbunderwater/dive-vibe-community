# Dean's Blue Hole

A marine blue hole plunging 202 metres off Long Island — the world's third-deepest known ocean blue hole (after Taam Ja' and Dragon Hole), and the world record venue for competitive freediving.

## Overview

Dean's Blue Hole is a spectacular natural geological formation in a bay west of Clarence Town, Long Island, Bahamas. The sinkhole is approximately 30 metres in diameter at the surface, widening to roughly 100 metres in diameter below, then plunging to a maximum depth of 202 metres. The hole formed through limestone karst collapse — geological sources describe it as caused either by a fault allowing corrosive solution to rise, or by a buried cavern ceiling collapsing to create a sinkhole. Wikipedia now classifies Dean's Blue Hole as the world's third-deepest known ocean blue hole, after Taam Ja' Blue Hole in Mexico (found in 2024 to exceed 420 m) and Dragon Hole in the South China Sea (about 301 m). It is world-famous as the venue for competitive freediving world records: William Trubridge (New Zealand) set a Constant Weight No Fins (CNF) world record here of 92 m in April 2010, and — per freediving record sources — a further CNF record of 101 m that December, and the site hosts the annual Vertical Blue freediving competition. The hole's still water, freedom from current, and extraordinary visibility (30–40+ metres in the sinkhole itself) make it one of the most visually dramatic dive experiences in the world. Recreational scuba divers are limited to the upper 40 metres, which still represents an extraordinary dive.

## Dive Profile

Shore entry via the small beach adjacent to the hole. Descend through the narrow entrance zone to the wider chamber below 20 meters. Recreational divers typically hover at the chamber entrance at 20–25 meters to appreciate the scale before ascending. Advanced divers can explore the chamber walls to 30–35 meters — this is where the blue hole's distinctive appearance, with the pale-white limestone walls disappearing into infinite blue below, is most striking. Maximum recreational depth: 40 meters (appropriate equipment and certification required).

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the small beach at the blue hole's edge. Entry by walking in or shallow dive entry from the rock ledge. The hole has no current, making entry and exit straightforward. The calm, sheltered nature of the sinkhole makes it suitable for multiple dives per day.

## Tips and Recommendations

Freediving or snorkelling in the upper sections (0–20 m) is remarkable and requires no certification. Scuba diving to 30–40 meters requires appropriate experience and certification. The world freediving community regularly trains at Dean's Blue Hole — visitors should be aware that training sessions may be in progress and should not interfere with freedivers on the surface. Visiting at low sun angle (morning or evening) creates dramatic light shafts into the sinkhole from above.

## Safety Considerations

The extreme depth of this sinkhole creates narcosis risk for scuba divers who exceed 30–35 meters. Recreational divers must strictly observe their depth limits — the visually compelling experience of looking down into 202 meters can create a compelling urge to descend further. Always carry a freediving float/DSMB for surface visibility. The hole's calm nature is deceptive — proper training and planning are still required for deep sections. The site has also seen freediving fatalities: American freediver Nicholas Mevoli died here in November 2013 after a 72 m CNF attempt during the Vertical Blue competition, the first death at an international freediving competition — a sober reminder that extreme freediving carries real risk even for elite, experienced athletes.

## Photography

Dean's Blue Hole is one of the most photographed natural formations in the Bahamas. The classic image is looking upward from the chamber at 25–30 meters toward the circular opening above, with natural light streaming down and a freediver descending in silhouette. Wide-angle to fisheye captures the full dramatic scale. The transition from the entrance shelf to the wider chamber is best captured at the junction depth.
