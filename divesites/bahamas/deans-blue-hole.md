# Dean's Blue Hole

A marine blue hole plunging 202 metres off Long Island — the world's third-deepest known ocean blue hole (after Taam Ja' and Dragon Hole), and the world record venue for competitive freediving.

## Overview

Dean's Blue Hole is a spectacular natural geological formation in a bay west of Clarence Town, Long Island, Bahamas. The sinkhole is approximately 30 metres in diameter at the surface, widening to roughly 100 metres in diameter below, then plunging to a maximum depth of 202 metres. The hole formed through limestone karst collapse — geological sources describe it as caused either by a fault allowing corrosive solution to rise, or by a buried cavern ceiling collapsing to create a sinkhole. As of 2026, Dean's Blue Hole is confirmed as the world's third-deepest known ocean blue hole, after the Taam Ja' Blue Hole in Mexico (measured at 420+ metres in 2024, still not fully sounded) and the Dragon Hole (Longdong/Yongle Blue Hole) in the South China Sea (300.89 metres). It is world-famous as a freediving landmark: New Zealander William Trubridge set the first-ever sub-100-metre dive in the unassisted Constant Weight No Fins (CNF) discipline here, reaching 101 metres in mid-December 2010, and went on to hold the CNF world record continuously from 2007 until 2025 (progressing his own record to 102m at this site in 2016) — a 17-year reign that ended in May 2025 when Croatian freediver Petar Klovar dove to 103m in Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt. Dean's Blue Hole remains the annual venue for the elite, invite-only Vertical Blue competition, where records continue to be set: in July 2025, Alenka Artnik set a Constant Weight (CWT) world record of 123m here, and Arnaud Jerald pushed the Constant Weight Bi-Fins (CWTB) world record to 126m during the same event. Recreational scuba divers are limited to the upper 40 metres, which still represents an extraordinary dive.

## Marine Life

The upper section of the blue hole (0–30 meters) supports typical Caribbean reef fish including parrotfish, blue tang, and sergeant majors that swim in from the adjacent reef. The transition zone from the entrance shelf to the main chamber at 20 meters is the most biologically productive section. Below 30 meters the sinkhole is essentially devoid of marine life — the extreme depth and lack of light penetration create a biologically poor but visually stunning environment.

## Dive Profile

Shore entry via the small beach adjacent to the hole. Descend through the narrow entrance zone to the wider chamber below 20 meters. Recreational divers typically hover at the chamber entrance at 20–25 meters to appreciate the scale before ascending. Advanced divers can explore the chamber walls to 30–35 meters — this is where the blue hole's distinctive appearance, with the pale-white limestone walls disappearing into infinite blue below, is most striking. Maximum recreational depth: 40 meters (appropriate equipment and certification required).

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the small beach at the blue hole's edge. Entry by walking in or shallow dive entry from the rock ledge. The hole has no current, making entry and exit straightforward. The calm, sheltered nature of the sinkhole makes it suitable for multiple dives per day.

## Tips and Recommendations

Freediving or snorkelling in the upper sections (0–20 m) is remarkable and requires no certification. Scuba diving to 30–40 meters requires appropriate experience and certification. The world freediving community regularly trains at Dean's Blue Hole, and the annual Vertical Blue competition (typically held in the summer) brings elite freedivers and safety teams to the site — visitors should be aware that training sessions or competition dives may be in progress and should not interfere with freedivers on the surface. Visiting at low sun angle (morning or evening) creates dramatic light shafts into the sinkhole from above.

## Safety Considerations

The extreme depth of this sinkhole creates narcosis risk for scuba divers who exceed 30–35 meters. Recreational divers must strictly observe their depth limits — the visually compelling experience of looking down into 202 meters can create a compelling urge to descend further. Always carry a freediving float/DSMB for surface visibility. The hole's calm nature is deceptive — proper training and planning are still required for deep sections.

## Photography

Dean's Blue Hole is one of the most photographed natural formations in the Bahamas. The classic image is looking upward from the chamber at 25–30 meters toward the circular opening above, with natural light streaming down and a freediver descending in silhouette. Wide-angle to fisheye captures the full dramatic scale. The transition from the entrance shelf to the wider chamber is best captured at the junction depth.
