# Conception Wall

A dramatic wall off uninhabited Conception Island in the central Bahamas — a national park site reachable by liveaboard and by long-range day boat from Long Island, with exceptional clarity and largely undisturbed coral gardens.

## Overview

Conception Island is a protected national park (Conception Island National Park, established 1978, ~30,000 acres, managed by the Bahamas National Trust) in the central Bahamas — uninhabited, no development, no permanent population. **Correction from the 2026-05-17 pass**: the island is not liveaboard-exclusive. Stella Maris Resort on Long Island runs it as a day-boat excursion (roughly 13 miles/30 minutes by boat from the resort marina), in addition to being a stop for liveaboards such as the R/V Sea Dragon and vessels running Long Island–Conception–Rum Cay–San Salvador itineraries. Reported wall-top depths vary by source and by which mooring is used: Stella Maris cites a shallow leeside wall with tops around 14 m (45 ft); a personal dive log (geoffschultz.org) puts the top nearer 17–24 m (55–80 ft) at other moorings, dropping "into the abyss" — Exuma Sound reaches thousands of feet in this area, far beyond recreational range. Swim-throughs and tunnels on the south wall sit around 21–30 m (70–100 ft), consistent across sources. Visibility is consistently reported as excellent (30–60+ m / 100–200 ft). Water temperature ranges seasonally in the mid-to-high 20s°C.

## Marine Life

Sources agree the site holds a strong shark presence: Caribbean reef sharks, nurse sharks, and lemon sharks are reported consistently, with occasional great hammerhead and tiger shark sightings also reported (iyc.com yacht-charter site). Spotted eagle rays and southern stingrays are reported in the surrounding waters. Green turtles nest on the island itself, and turtles are reported around the wall, though no source pinned a turtle sighting to the wall dive specifically as opposed to the island's creek/nesting areas. **Source conflict on reef-fish abundance**: the current markdown previously described "large parrotfish, groupers, and snappers that are notably less skittish than at dived-out Nassau sites," but a first-hand dive log (geoffschultz.org) explicitly notes the *absence* of large fish (over ~24 in/60 cm) at Conception, with the author guessing they have been fished out — this directly contradicts the "abundant large, tame fish" framing. That claim has been removed pending better sourcing; hard coral cover, sponges (elephant-ear and barrel), and gorgonian sea fans are well-corroborated across sources and retained.

## Dive Profile

Descent to the wall crest at 12 meters, then a controlled descent along the wall face to the planned maximum depth. Most divers target the 18–30 meter zone where the sponge and sea fan formations are richest. The deeper 30–43 meter section is accessible to experienced divers but requires careful NDL management. Ascent along the wall face with a safety stop at the crest.

## Entry and Exit

Liveaboard access only — Conception Island has no facilities and is only reachable by sea. Entry by backward roll or giant stride. DSMB essential for the open-water ascent from a remote wall.

## Tips and Recommendations

Conception Wall is a bucket-list dive for advanced Bahamas divers. The combination of pristine condition, outstanding clarity, and very low traffic makes this site qualitatively different from Nassau's busier walls. Plan the dive conservatively — with 40+ meter depth potential, it is easy to spend too long at depth. The wall tops in 12–18 meters are among the finest hard coral reef sections in the Bahamas.

## Safety Considerations

Remote location means no emergency support nearby — dive conservatively and maintain adequate reserves. The open wall position can develop current — monitor direction and intensity throughout the dive. DSMB mandatory. Night diving in the anchorage is excellent but requires careful navigation back to the vessel.

## Photography

Conception Wall's pristine condition and extraordinary clarity make it a premier wide-angle photography site. The hard coral reef on the upper wall is among the most photogenic in the Bahamas. Deep sponge formations with divers in frame require good strobe positioning at depth. The fish are larger and less camera-shy than at heavily visited sites.
