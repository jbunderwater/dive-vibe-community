## Jeep Reef

Jeep Reef is a coral reef dive site located within the protected boundaries of the Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park, one of the Bahamas' most biologically rich marine reserves.

## Overview

The site takes its name from a coral-encrusted Jeep that lies abandoned on the reef near February Point in the Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park, now serving as an artificial reef structure thickly encrusted in coral and sponge. The Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park is a strict no-take marine reserve, and the reef itself is described as one of the healthiest and most colorful in the park. A constant current flows over the reef, which is credited with keeping the coral healthy. The site can only be dived at slack tide due to the strength of that current.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near February Point, Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park, Bahamas
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-30 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong at tidal change — dive at slack tide
- **Best Time**: November to May

## Marine Life

The abandoned Jeep itself is a favored home for lobster, sea slugs, porcupinefish, squirrelfish, and Nassau grouper. On the broader reef, divers report large schools of goatfish and snappers, goliath grouper, lionfish, turtles, triggerfish, and queen angelfish. Earlier text on this page described Caribbean reef sharks, nurse sharks, stingrays, and eagle rays here — that content was not corroborated by site-specific sources during this review and has been removed; it may have been carried over from a different Exuma site (Amberjack Reef, a few miles away, is documented as a shark site).

## Dive Profile

The reef top begins around 10 meters (35 ft) and slopes down to about 15 meters (50 ft) before dropping off into the deep blue of Exuma Sound. The coral-encrusted Jeep is the focal point, surrounded by healthy hard and soft coral formations. Buoyancy control is important to protect the reef structure and avoid stirring up silt around the Jeep.

## Entry and Exit

All diving here is by boat. Most divers visit on organised day trips from Staniel Cay or aboard liveaboards transiting the Exuma Cays. Check tidal timing before entry — the channels between the cays generate strong currents.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Time your dive for slack water to avoid the strongest tidal currents
- The park prohibits any collection of marine life — this is a strict no-take reserve
- Bring a wide-angle lens for the coral-encrusted Jeep, and a macro lens for the lobster, sea slugs, and squirrelfish sheltering in and around it

## Safety Considerations

Currents can build quickly in the Exuma Cays. Plan your dive profile around the tidal cycle and carry a surface marker buoy (SMB). Boat traffic in the cays can be significant during peak season.
