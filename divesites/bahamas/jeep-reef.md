## Jeep Reef

Jeep Reef is a coral reef dive site located within the protected boundaries of the Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park, one of the Bahamas' most biologically rich marine reserves.

## Overview

The site takes its name from a coral-encrusted Jeep resting on the reef near February Point in the Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park. The jeep is thickly encrusted in corals and sponges but is still recognizable, largely due to its rubber tires, which have resisted weathering. How the jeep came to be on the reef is not established — multiple, conflicting stories circulate locally and no source confirms a definitive origin, so no backstory should be treated as fact. The reef sits in a channel between Exuma Bank and Exuma Sound, which generates frequent current that feeds the reef and draws fish to the site. The Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park's no-take protections mean fish populations here are dense. The site is best dived at or near slack tide due to the current running through the channel.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Exuma Cays Land and Sea Park, Bahamas (near February Point)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-30 meters
- **Current**: Frequent, moderate to strong at tidal change — dive at slack tide
- **Best Time**: November to May

## Marine Life

Sources describing this specific site report lobsters, sea slugs, porcupinefish, squirrelfish, Nassau grouper, and bluehead wrasse living in and around the coral-encrusted Jeep. Schooling southern sennet (a barracuda-family species), trumpetfish, needlefish, French grunts, and blue tangs are also reported here, along with occasional small nurse sharks. Broader Caribbean reef species (larger sharks, rays, additional grouper species) are plausible in this protected park but are not confirmed for this specific site by any source found, so they are not listed here.

## Dive Profile

The reef top begins around 10 meters and slopes to roughly 15 meters before the terrain drops toward Exuma Sound. Begin the dive shallower and work deeper. The coral-encrusted Jeep is the focal point, surrounded by healthy hard and soft coral formations. Buoyancy control is important to protect the reef structure and avoid stirring up silt around the Jeep.

## Entry and Exit

All diving here is by boat. Most divers visit on organised day trips from Staniel Cay or aboard liveaboards transiting the Exuma Cays. Check tidal timing before entry — the channels between the cays generate strong currents.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Time your dive for slack water to avoid the strongest tidal currents
- The park prohibits any collection of marine life — this is a strict no-take reserve
- Bring a macro or general-purpose lens — the reef's smaller residents (sea slugs, squirrelfish, blennies-family fish) and the encrusted Jeep are the main photographic draw at this site

## Safety Considerations

Currents can build quickly in the Exuma Cays. Plan your dive profile around the tidal cycle and carry a surface marker buoy (SMB). Boat traffic in the cays can be significant during peak season.
