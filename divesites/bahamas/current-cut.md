## Current Cut

Current Cut is one of the most exhilarating drift dives in the Bahamas — a narrow tidal channel between North Eleuthera and Current Island where the ocean tide funnels through at up to 10 knots, sweeping divers through in under 15 minutes.

## Overview

The cut is a narrow channel just over 1 kilometre long separating the western tip of North Eleuthera from Current Island. As the tide changes, ocean water is forced through this restricted passage creating one of the fastest current dives in the Caribbean, with sources reporting flow of roughly 6-10 knots. Reported maximum depth ranges from about 10 to 18 meters depending on the source; this listing uses 18 meters (60 ft), the figure most commonly cited by Eleuthera-focused sources. The bottom is hard rock studded with sea fans, sponges, and hardy coral growth that can withstand the constant current. The experience is one of free-flying through clear water — divers describe it as a natural rocket ride. Despite its brevity (the dive is over in 10-12 minutes), the intensity of the experience makes it memorable. A gentler shore-accessible dive is reportedly possible on the northern shore side of the cut, though visibility and conditions there are less documented.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Eleuthera / Current Island channel, Eleuthera, Bahamas
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Current**: 6-10 knots at peak tidal flow; timing is tide-dependent and set by local operators
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-25 meters (incoming tide preferred for clearest water)
- **Best Time**: December to May (regional Eleuthera dive season)

## Marine Life

The resident pod of stingrays at Current Cut is one of the site's defining features and is reported consistently across sources. Blacktip and Caribbean reef sharks, eagle rays, lobster, parrotfish, and queen angelfish are reported using the rock formations and crevices as shelter from the flow, feeding on prey swept through by the current. Barracuda are reported stationing themselves in the flow.

## Dive Profile

The incoming tide provides the best visibility and the preferred dive direction. Operators time entry to a specific window around the tidal cycle — sources describe this as tide-dependent rather than a fixed number of minutes, so follow your operator's briefing rather than a generic schedule. Divers are dropped at the up-current end of the channel and carried through to the exit point where the boat is waiting. Buoyancy control must be excellent — sharp buoyancy skills are needed to stay comfortable in 10-knot flow without crashing into the bottom or flying toward the surface.

## Entry and Exit

Drift dive by boat. The boat drops divers at one end and moves to the other end to collect them as they surface. This is a one-way dive — there is no swimming back against the current. Work exclusively with local operators who know the tidal timing precisely.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This dive requires sharp buoyancy skills and prior drift diving experience — beginner divers should not attempt it
- Incoming tide is strongly preferred over outgoing for visibility and current direction
- The dive is fast — have your camera ready from the moment you hit the water
- A second, gentler dive on the northern shore of Current Island pairs well with this as a two-tank trip

## Safety Considerations

The current, reported at up to 10 knots at peak flow, is unswimmable — once in, you must complete the drift. If you surface early, deploy your SMB immediately so the boat can locate you. Do not fight the current under any circumstances. Confirm tide timing with your operator before any entry. This site is for Advanced certified divers with drift diving experience only; note that at least one source (Zentacle) rates the dive "Intermediate," but the majority of sources reviewed recommend Advanced Open Water certification as a minimum, which this listing follows.
