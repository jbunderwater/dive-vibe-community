## Le Faon Wreck

Le Faon is a large cargo ship wreck lying on its port side near Moucha Island in the Gulf of Tadjoura, one of Djibouti's most popular dive sites.

## Overview

The wreck known locally as Le Faon was built in 1959 at the Howaldtswerke shipyard in Kiel, Germany, and operated under several names during its working life: MV Priamos (Hamburg banana carrier), MV Santa Trinidad, MV Phaon, and finally MV Orchard Reefer. The build year and shipyard are corroborated by two independent sources — Lukas Spieker's Underway Blog and Shipping Today & Yesterday magazine's history of the F. Laeisz "Flying P" fleet, which confirms the Priamos was one of eight sister banana reefers (Parnass, Pelion, Perikles, Portunus, Parthenon, Piraeus, Priamos, Pentelikon) built for Laeisz and the Afrikanische Frucht-Compagnie, Hamburg, and operated as a banana carrier until 1969.

**Sinking date and cause — sources conflict.** Lukas Spieker's Underway Blog (citing shipyard/incident records) states that on 22 November 1974, while sailing from Jeddah to Singapore under the name MV Orchard Reefer, a fire broke out in the engine room after the ship had passed Aden, and that the 27-man crew — a Norwegian master, an Indonesian radio operator, and 25 Filipino crew members — abandoned ship and were picked up by the motor tanker Dora before the vessel was towed to Djibouti and capsized near Moucha Island. Atlas & Boots, an independent firsthand dive account, instead reports the vessel sank in 1975 after an explosion in the engine room. Both the exact year (1974 vs. 1975) and the precise cause (fire vs. explosion) are disputed between these two sources, and this description does not pick one over the other. The specific rescue narrative (the tanker Dora, crew nationalities and count) comes from the Spieker account only and has not been independently corroborated, so it should be read as reported rather than confirmed.

The wreck lies on its port side in 10–28 metres of water approximately 9 km from Djibouti City, making it easily accessible as a day dive from the capital. Sources disagree on the ship's length, reporting figures between 120 metres and 135 metres; 126 metres is corroborated by two independent sources (Spieker's blog and the Shipping Today & Yesterday Laeisz fleet history) and is used here as the best-supported figure. A gross tonnage of 3,027 GT is reported by the Shipping Today & Yesterday article, but this figure has not been found in any other source consulted, so it is noted here without being stated as settled fact. The hull is now heavily colonised by hard and soft corals. There is a prominent crack in the hull behind the bridge where divers can pass from what was the deck side to the keel side — wreck penetration requires appropriate training and equipment and is never without risk. Schools of batfish, glassfish, and snappers congregate throughout the structure, and moray eels are frequently seen in the coral-encrusted superstructure, per Atlas & Boots' 2018 firsthand dive account.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Moucha Island, Gulf of Tadjoura, approximately 9 km from Djibouti City
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 28 meters
