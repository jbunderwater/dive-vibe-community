## Iles des Sept Frères

The Îles des Sept Frères (Seven Brothers Islands) are a remote volcanic archipelago in the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait offering Djibouti's most demanding and spectacular diving, accessible only by liveaboard.

## Overview

The archipelago — known also as the Sawabi Islands — consists of six volcanic islands strung in an east-west line across the southern entrance to the Red Sea, within the Obock sub-prefecture of Djibouti. Strong Indian Ocean currents sweeping through the strait support extraordinary concentrations of pelagic life. Dive-the-world.com describes the sites as featuring "colourful reef fish, schools of jacks and barracuda" on steep walls and drop-offs. Scuba Diver Life and Divezone confirm the islands offer some of the most biodiverse diving in the Red Sea, with manta rays, whale sharks, pilot whales, and reef sharks recorded.

A notable dive site in this area is the wreck of the Italian submarine Torricelli (commonly called "Evangelista Torricelli" by dive operators), a Brin-class vessel — confirmed as Brin-class, 72.47 metres long, with a displacement of 1,000 long tons surfaced / 1,245 long tons submerged, by both the Wikipedia Brin-class submarine article and naval-encyclopedia.com. Note: there were several Italian Navy submarines that carried variants of this name; the vessel lost near the Seven Brothers area in 1940 is specifically the Brin-class boat launched 26 March 1939, not the earlier Archimede-class Torricelli of 1934 (which was transferred to Spain in 1937 and renamed General Mola, and is unrelated to this wreck) — dive-tourism sites that describe an "Archimede-class" or "120 metres long" submarine at this location are conflating the two vessels.

The submarine was scuttled in the Red Sea on 23 June 1940, after an engagement north of Perim Island with British destroyers HMS Kandahar, HMS Khartoum, and HMS Kingston, and the sloop HMS Shoreham. Commander Salvatore Pelosi chose to fight on the surface rather than submerge; after her deck gun action and a torpedo salvo that missed, the crew abandoned ship and the submarine sank at approximately 06:24. (An earlier version of this description omitted HMS Khartoum from the engagement; it has been added following re-verification against the Brin-class submarine Wikipedia article and naval-encyclopedia.com, both of which list all three destroyers.) The submarine sits upright at approximately 25 metres and is surrounded by white gorgonians, according to ZuBlu and Divezone diving sources. U.S. Navy casualty records (history.navy.mil) previously supported coordinates of approximately 12°34'N, 43°16'E for the loss, within the Bab-el-Mandeb area near the Seven Brothers Islands, but that page could not be re-fetched during this audit (returned a server error) — the coordinate claim is retained but flagged as unable to be re-verified this pass.

A separate wreck, La Dame Blanche, is located north-east of Ras Siyan near the Seven Brothers area. It is an unidentified cargo vessel (approximately 130 metres) lying at 25–30 metres and densely covered with white gorgonians — which gives it its name "White Lady." Sources (Divingaway, Scuba Diver Life) note it remains unidentified, so no sinking date or vessel history is included here.

Best diving season is reported as February through May when currents are more predictable.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, northern Djibouti, Obock sub-prefecture
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
