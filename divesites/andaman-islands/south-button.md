## South Button

South Button is a protected shallow coral reef within South Button Island National Park — India's smallest national park — that receives regular visits from manta and devil rays.

## Overview

South Button Island is part of South Button Island National Park, situated within the broader Rani Jhansi Marine National Park framework southwest of Havelock Island, approximately 24 kilometres away. The island and its surrounding reef are uninhabited and day-trip only, accessible December to April with forest department permits arranged by tour operators. Sources describe a shallow-water coral reef beginning as shallow as 1.8 metres (6 feet) below the surface, suitable for both snorkelling and scuba diving; none of the three current sources describe a wall, drop-off, or a specific maximum recreational depth for the site, so 12 metres is used here as a conservative estimate for a shallow fringing reef rather than an asserted figure. Visibility often exceeds 20 metres. The protected status of the national park contributes to the site's relatively undisturbed reef condition.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Button Island National Park, Andaman Islands (~24 km from Havelock Island)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters (sources describe a shallow reef starting around 2m; none give a precise maximum)
- **Typical Visibility**: 20+ meters
- **Current**: Variable
- **Best Time**: December to April (park access season)

## Marine Life

Confirmed sightings at this site include manta rays and devil rays (reported by all three sources), snappers, sweetlips, groupers, angelfish, and butterflyfish (andamanislands.com and Wikipedia), and barracuda and surgeonfish (touristinindia.com). Sea turtles are documented in the surrounding waters. Dugongs are listed by Wikipedia as part of the national park's marine fauna, though this is a park-wide description rather than a diver-specific sighting report at this exact site. A previous version of this page cited a "western wall" and "leopard shark" sightings; neither claim could be re-confirmed against any current source and both have been removed as unsupported.

## Dive Profile

This is a shallow coral-garden dive rather than a deep or wall profile: expect most of the reef in the 2-12 metre range. Visibility is consistently good, aiding navigation. Begin ascent with adequate air for a 5-metre safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only; approximately 1.5–2 hours from Havelock Island by chartered vessel. Forest department day permits are required and must be arranged in advance through licensed eco-certified operators. No overnight stays. Reef-safe sunscreen required; no touching corals.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Arrange permits well in advance — the park has limited daily visitor quotas
- The site is only accessible December to April; confirm conditions and permits before booking
- Suitable for snorkellers as well as certified divers given the shallow reef profile
- Plan for a long boat journey — bring sufficient water, food, and sun protection
- This remote site rewards patient divers: watch the blue water for passing rays

## Safety Considerations

Remote location with a long boat ride — ensure your operator has appropriate safety equipment and communication devices. Dive within certification limits. Carry an SMB.

## Photography

The ray encounters provide exceptional wide-angle opportunities. Visibility exceeding 20 metres makes this a strong site for natural-light reef photography.
