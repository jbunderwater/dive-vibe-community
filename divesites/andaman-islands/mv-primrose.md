## MV Primrose

The MV Primrose is a cargo freighter that ran aground on a reef off North Sentinel Island on 2 August 1981; the wreck cannot be dived because the island is under an exclusion zone.

## Overview

The NZ Herald describes the Primrose as a 16,000-ton freighter supplied with crew by Hong Kong's Regent Shipping Company, sailing from Bangladesh to Australia with a cargo of chicken feed. UPI (1981) calls it a Panamanian-registered freighter, and the South China Morning Post also describes a Panamanian-registered, 16,000-ton vessel. Wikipedia's North Sentinel Island article gives the grounding date as 2 August 1981. The NZ Herald and UPI describe a monsoon storm in the Bay of Bengal that lodged the ship on the coral reef, and the captain, Liu Chunglong, cabled for an urgent airdrop of weapons, citing armed islanders. Indian officials told UPI the crew was never in danger, and the SCMP reports that the islanders made no attempt to board. After several postponed attempts, the crew was winched off by helicopter (Wikipedia says the helicopter was contracted to the Oil and Natural Gas Corporation).

Sources differ on crew size: the NZ Herald gives 31 crew, the SCMP refers to 21 Hong Kong sailors, and a 1981 UPI report says 28 sailors. The NZ Herald says the wreck remains on the reef. Wikipedia reports that salvage operators were authorised to dismantle wrecks in 1991 and that the Sentinelese scavenged iron from the Primrose and another wreck; the NZ Herald says metal tools seen with the Sentinelese in 1991 are believed to have come from the wreck.

**This is not a diveable site.** Wikipedia reports that the 1956 regulation bans approaching within five kilometres of the island and that the surrounding waters out to five nautical miles (about nine kilometres) are an exclusion zone, patrolled by the Indian Navy. No diving or tourism access is permitted.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Reef off North Sentinel Island, Andaman Islands
- **Access**: Prohibited; exclusion zone patrolled by the Indian Navy
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: Not reported

## Safety and Legal Considerations

Approaching North Sentinel Island is prohibited under Indian law, with no exceptions for diving, photography, or tourism. The exclusion zone protects the Sentinelese people and their isolation.
