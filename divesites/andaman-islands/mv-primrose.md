## MV Primrose

The MV Primrose is a 16,000-ton cargo ship that ran aground on a reef off North Sentinel Island on 2 August 1981 and remains there today — but diving at this site is strictly prohibited under Indian law.

## Overview

The MV Primrose was a 16,000-ton Panamanian-registered cargo freighter operated by Hong Kong's Regent Shipping Company. On the night of 2 August 1981, while sailing from Bangladesh to Australia with a cargo of chicken feed, the vessel ran aground on a coral reef surrounding North Sentinel Island during a monsoon storm. The ship's crew is reported as 31 total by the NZ Herald and the South China Morning Post (which specifies 21 of the 31 as Hong Kong nationals); one 1981 UPI dispatch headline instead refers to "twenty-eight sailors" - this figure is an outlier against the other sources and is not resolved here, simply noted as a discrepancy. The crew, under Captain Liu Chunglong, was stranded on the hull as Sentinelese men appeared on the beach and began building boats, approaching with bows and arrows; the captain radioed urgently for an airdrop of firearms. After several days during which heavy seas prevented both rescue ships and the islanders from reaching the vessel, a helicopter contracted to the Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) winched the crew to safety. The wreck remains on the reef. In 1991, salvage operators (identified by some sources as "Mohamed Brothers") were authorised by the Indian government to dismantle the vessel; the Sentinelese confronted them with weapons. Wikipedia's North Sentinel Island article confirms the Sentinelese are known to have scavenged iron from the wreck, but does not specify what they made from it - an earlier version of this page claimed the metal was cold-forged into "arrowheads and harpoons"; that specific detail could not be re-confirmed against Wikipedia or any other current source and has been removed.

**This is not a diveable site.** The wreck is located within five kilometres of North Sentinel Island. Indian law prohibits any approach within this exclusion zone under the Andaman and Nicobar Islands Protection of Aboriginal Tribes Regulation. The Indian Navy enforces the exclusion zone. No diving or tourism access is permitted.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Reef off North Sentinel Island, Andaman Islands
- **Access**: Prohibited — exclusion zone enforced by Indian Navy
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: Not applicable (surface wreck on reef)

## Safety and Legal Considerations

Approaching within five kilometres of North Sentinel Island is a criminal offence under Indian law. No exceptions are granted for diving, photography, or tourism. The North Sentinel Island exclusion zone exists to protect the Sentinelese people, who have demonstrated hostility to outside contact and whose isolation is a matter of both legal protection and humanitarian concern.
