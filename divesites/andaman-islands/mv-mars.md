## MV Mars

MV Mars is a small wreck on a sandy bottom just off the reef northwest of Havelock Island, reported at roughly 15-16 metres depth and used as a beginner-level wreck dive.

## Overview

Dive-operator and travel sources agree that MV Mars is a small wreck off Havelock Island, lying upright on sand at about 15 metres. ScubaLov lists it as a wooden cargo vessel lost in a storm in April, located northwest of Havelock about 4 km from the island, with a depth range of 10-16 metres. Other sources describe the vessel as a small cargo ship (Andaman Tourism, Travelsetu) or a small fishing boat (an SSI dive-site listing seen in search results), and Travelsetu gives the sinking year as 2006. Reported depths range from about 15 to about 20 metres. No Wikipedia article or maritime record for the vessel was found, so the vessel's origin, size and tonnage are not documented here. A Business Standard article was also seen in search results describing an "MV Mars" of much larger size elsewhere in the Andamans; that account does not match the small wreck dived off Havelock and is not used here. ScubaLov notes a reef about 10 metres from the wreck.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northwest of Havelock Island (Swaraj Dweep), about 4 km from Havelock (ScubaLov), Andaman Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Often limited (ScubaLov)
- **Current**: Not reported by the sources consulted
- **Best Time**: Not reported by the sources consulted

## Marine Life

ScubaLov, the only source consulted that lists species for this specific wreck, reports cardinalfish, parrotfish, triggerfish, batfish, sweetlips, crocodilefish, seahorses, snappers and fusiliers. Travelsetu mentions reef sharks in a general description of the wreck, but this is not corroborated by other sources and is not treated as a confirmed sighting.

## Dive Profile

The wreck rests at about 15 metres on a sandy bottom, with the nearby reef available to explore. ScubaLov suggests drifting over to the reef. Begin ascent with sufficient reserve for a safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by boat from Havelock dive operators. Deploy an SMB on ascent for boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Listed as suitable for beginners
- Do not attempt penetration of the wreck without proper training and equipment
- Include the nearby reef in the dive plan

## Safety Considerations

Dive within your certification limits. Visibility can be limited at wreck sites. Always dive with a buddy and carry an SMB.

## Photography

ScubaLov lists crocodilefish and seahorses among the species reported on this wreck, which would suit macro work.
