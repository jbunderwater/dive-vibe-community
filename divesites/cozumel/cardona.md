# Cardona

A low-profile, hole-and-crevasse reef on Cozumel's central leeward coast, popular for beginner and introductory dives — though whether it is best reached from shore or by boat is not firmly settled by available sources (see Overview).

## Overview

Cardona is a low-profile, broad, flat reef of holes and crevasses on Cozumel's central leeward coast, popular as a relaxed beginner site. Its name is popularly linked to Mexican film director René Cardona, whose 1957-released film "Un Mundo Nuevo" (shot in Cozumel's waters around 1956) is widely credited with putting the island on the diving map; one source states this is "probably why we now have a Cardona Reef," though the naming connection itself is local lore rather than a documented fact, and the film was shot the year before its 1957 Mexico City release, not "in 1957" as sometimes stated. Depth reports vary by source — one dive-site guide lists 20–35 feet (about 6–11 meters), while this database lists a maximum of 14 meters — so treat the deeper figure as an upper bound rather than a typical dive. Current is described as mild. **Note on access**: this entry is currently tagged as a shore dive, but source material on entry type was thin and partly conflicting (one description places the reef roughly 200 meters offshore and calls it boat-accessible); this should be treated as unconfirmed pending further research rather than a settled fact.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Central leeward coast, Cozumel, Quintana Roo, Mexico (shore access)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 meters (30–80 feet; can be reduced after storms)
- **Current**: Minimal — sheltered inshore position
- **Best Time**: Year-round; less dependent on weather than offshore sites

## Marine Life

The one species confirmed specifically at Cardona by source material is the nurse shark: divers can spot sleeping nurse sharks by looking through the reef's many holes and beneath overhanging ledges. General reef fish and anemones are also reported at the site. Earlier text in this file named additional species (spiny lobster, splendid toadfish, spotted moray eels, elkhorn coral, sea turtles) — these are commonly seen elsewhere in Cozumel and plausible here given the habitat, but no source tied to this specific site confirmed them during this audit, so they have been removed rather than presented as verified.

## Dive Profile

The reef is low-profile and extends to a maximum depth in the 6–14 meter range depending on source. Navigation follows the scattered coral heads in a circuit pattern. Mild current means out-and-back or circular routes are both practical, and generous bottom times are comfortable at this depth range.

## Entry and Exit

This entry lists Cardona as a shore dive, but as noted above, source material on entry logistics was thin and one description suggests the reef may in fact be reached by boat from roughly 200 meters offshore. Divers planning to dive Cardona should confirm current access arrangements with a local operator rather than relying on this description. Specific procedural detail previously in this section (a named access point, wrist-fin technique, etc.) was not sourced and has been removed rather than presented as fact.

## Tips

- Certification students benefit from the mild, low-current conditions for skill practice
- Look through reef holes and beneath overhanging ledges for resting nurse sharks
- Confirm with your operator in advance whether this dive runs from shore or by boat

## Safety

Given the uncertainty over entry type noted above, divers should follow whatever briefing their operator gives for this site rather than assuming shore-dive protocols apply. Carry a surface marker buoy and be aware of boat traffic when surfacing. Recompression facilities are in San Miguel.

## Photography

Mild current at Cardona is favorable for stable macro work. Nurse sharks resting in the reef's holes and overhangs are a reliable subject when spotted. Other photography notes in this section (peacock flounder, trunkfish/cowfish portraits, night-diving subjects) described general Caribbean reef subjects rather than confirmed Cardona sightings and have been removed pending site-specific confirmation.
