# El Islote

An Advanced-rated southeast Cozumel reef site near the Punta Celarain lighthouse — a large patch coral island formation with strong currents and depths to 37 meters, outside the usual leeward dive corridor.

## Overview

El Islote ("The Islet") sits on Cozumel's southeastern tip, past the Punta Celarain lighthouse, beyond the main concentration of leeward dive sites. It is a large patch coral island — broad ridges of pristine hard and soft coral separated by sandy channels (living-underwater.com) — accessed only when conditions allow dive boats to round the southern tip safely (letstraveltomexico.com). Local Cozumel dive shop 60 Feet Under lists it on its dive-site table with Strong/Very Strong current and an Advanced/Deep experience rating (60feetunder.com). The remote location and Advanced difficulty rating mean far fewer boats visit than at leeward sites, and the coral and marine life reflect the reduced pressure. Coral cover includes sea fans and staghorn coral (letstraveltomexico.com).

## Site Information

- **Location**: Southeast Cozumel, near Punta Celarain lighthouse, Quintana Roo, Mexico
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 37 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Good when conditions allow
- **Current**: Strong — primary reason for Advanced rating
- **Best Time**: Calm-weather windows only; boats can only round the southern tip when seas are mild enough (letstraveltomexico.com)

## Marine Life

Turtles, sharks, and spotted eagle rays are documented at El Islote by multiple independent sources (colemanconcierge.com, letstraveltomexico.com), a result of its remote, rarely-dived location. Parrotfish are reported on the reef itself, with sharks and rays more typically seen over the adjacent sand flats (colemanconcierge.com). No other species are confirmed site-specifically for El Islote.

## Dive Profile

The dive follows the broad coral ridges with the current dictating pace and direction. 60feetunder.com's dive table gives an average working depth of 15m (50ft) with a max of 37m (120ft) — most of the dive is likely spent well shallower than the max, with deeper excursions along ridges and channels. Plan conservatively and follow your operator's briefing given the range reported across sources. The sandy channels between ridges provide navigation structure. SMB deployment is essential before ascending due to the remote location and surface current.

## Entry and Exit

Long boat trip from San Miguel — among the longest transits for Cozumel dive sites due to the southeast location (approximately 45–60 minutes), and the trip only runs when wind and swell are mild enough for boats to round the southern tip (letstraveltomexico.com). Drift dive protocol with SMB ascent applies. The captain must assess current conditions before entry.

## Tips

- Confirm current conditions with your operator before booking; this site is dived selectively and only in favorable weather
- Advanced certification and current-diving experience are genuine prerequisites
- The pristine condition of the coral, consequence of lower diver traffic, rewards those who make the effort

## Safety

Strong currents and the remote location combine to make this one of Cozumel's more demanding sites. Emergency response from San Miguel takes significantly longer than at leeward sites. All divers must carry SMBs and audible signalling devices. Do not attempt this site without a guide familiar with southeast Cozumel current patterns.
