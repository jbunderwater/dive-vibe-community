# Maracaibo Reef

A deep, current-swept wall at Cozumel's southern extreme — one of the island's most challenging and visually spectacular dives.

## Overview

Maracaibo is the southernmost dive site in Cozumel, near the Punta Celerain Lighthouse. It is accessed only during calm sea conditions due to its exposed position and large swells on approach. The wall here is sheer and dramatic, with the edge reported between 90 and 100 feet (27–30 meters) depending on the source, dropping into a sheer cliff that extends well beyond recreational limits (down to "well over 140 feet" per padi.com). The site's signature geological feature is a large, nearly perfectly formed arch — described by reefsofcozumel.com as "about 30 feet across" — reached by descending through a deep fissure; divers enter the arch from below and swim upward through it. Sources converge on an arch depth of approximately 115–120 feet (35–37 meters). The site sees relatively few dive boats given its remote, weather-dependent access. Current is almost always present and entry involves negotiating open ocean swells.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Deep south Cozumel, Quintana Roo, Mexico
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 25–40 meters (80–130 feet)
- **Current**: Strong — open exposure, typically north-to-south drift
- **Best Time**: November to March when southeast swells are minimal

## Marine Life

Reef sharks and spotted eagle rays are consistently reported along the wall; PADI's site listing names both reef shark and hammerhead shark as sightings here, and a first-person account (HuffPost) confirms turtles, a stingray, and current-borne encounters during a dive. Hawksbill and loggerhead turtles are specifically documented (padi.com). Nurse sharks and black groupers are noted as occasional encounters (reefsofcozumel.com). Common reef fish include damselfish, barracuda, grouper, creole wrasse, grunts, snapper, bar jacks, hamlets, and hawkfish, with parrotfish, octopus, and green morays as occasional sightings (reefsofcozumel.com). Note: hammerhead shark sightings are documented by PADI as a listed species but described elsewhere as an occasional/lucky encounter rather than a reliable one — don't expect them on every dive.

## Dive Profile

The dive follows the wall crest, with the edge reported at roughly 90–100 feet (27–30m) depending on the source, before the divemaster leads down through a fissure to the site's signature arch. Sources converge on an arch depth of approximately 115–120 feet (35–37m): reefsofcozumel.com describes descending ~30 feet through a fissure from the ~90ft wall edge to reach it, PADI states the arch "lies 115 ft deep," and a first-person HuffPost account puts its bottom at 120 feet. Divers enter the arch from below and swim upward through it. Time at the arch must be brief given the depth and NDL constraints; Pro Dive International notes the site is typically planned for about 20 minutes before returning to the reef top at 80ft to begin ascent. The current carries divers throughout. Ascend with an SMB well before surfacing.

## Entry and Exit

Only accessible on calm days — if there is swell at the southern tip, Maracaibo will be skipped. The boat ride from San Miguel is approximately 45–60 minutes. Entry and exit are entirely ocean-dependent. Experienced operators know the site well and will only run the trip in suitable conditions.

## Tips

- Only attempt Maracaibo with substantial advanced diving experience and current-diving confidence
- Budget enough air for a proper deep safety stop — plan to surface with at least 50 bar
- The wall crest at 10–15 meters rewards a slow drift on the ascent; don't rush back to the surface
- Autumn through early spring offers the most reliable access and calmest conditions
- Check current certification and insurance — this is a remote site far from quick emergency assistance

## Safety

Maracaibo demands excellent buoyancy control to avoid unplanned depth excursions along the vertical wall. The narcosis potential at 35–40 meters requires honest self-assessment; if you find yourself confused by narcosis, ascend immediately. Due to its remote location, emergency response time is significantly longer than at central Cozumel sites. Do not dive here without a current safety card for Divers Alert Network (DAN) or equivalent.

## Photography

The wide open visibility reported by sources creates good conditions for wide-angle shooting of the wall and arch formation, which benefits from a fisheye or ultra-wide lens given its scale ("about 30 feet across" per reefsofcozumel.com). Reef shark and eagle ray encounters reward patience and a readiness to shoot quickly. No site-specific lighting or visibility-by-depth data beyond general Cozumel conditions was found in sources.
