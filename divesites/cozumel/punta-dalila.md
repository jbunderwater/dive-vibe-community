# Punta Dalila

A shallow, approachable reef in Cozumel's southern dive zone — ideal for new divers and snorkellers seeking excellent marine life encounters within easy depth limits.

## Overview

Punta Dalila is a shallow reef in the southern section of Cozumel's leeward coast. Sources put its depth in a roughly 9–18 meter (30–60 ft) range, with most agreeing on a maximum in the 15–18 meter band, consistent with this entry's recorded 15-meter maximum. Multiple sources describe it as a flat, sandy-bottomed reef with seagrass and coral patches rather than a wall — it is not a wall dive. Current is generally described as calm to mild, though at least one operator review noted the current can run swift on some days, so conditions vary. Several sources rate it as suited to intermediate divers as well as beginners rather than a pure beginner-only site; this entry currently records it as Beginner, which is broadly consistent but slightly understates the range some operators describe.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Southern leeward coast, Cozumel, Quintana Roo, Mexico
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20–35 meters (65–115 feet)
- **Current**: Generally calm to mild; at least one source notes it can run swift on some days
- **Best Time**: Year-round; excellent in summer months when calmer conditions prevail

## Marine Life

Multiple independent sources (Coleman Concierge, Blue Note Scuba, Zentacle, and aggregated operator reviews) consistently confirm turtles, nurse sharks, and stingrays at Punta Dalila, with eagle rays reported seasonally. The endemic splendid toadfish and flamingo tongue cowries are also reported on the shallow reef top by aggregated dive-guide sources. The earlier claims of French angelfish, queen angelfish, spotted drums, butterflyfish species, cleaner wrasse stations, green/spotted morays, and Caribbean reef squid could not be confirmed as specific to this site in any source checked during this audit and have been removed — they may be present (these are common Cozumel reef species generally) but no source ties them specifically to Punta Dalila.

## Dive Profile

Start at the shallowest portion of the reef crest (3–5 meters) and work through the brain and star coral formations toward deeper terrain at 10–15 meters. The manageable depth means a 60+ minute bottom time is achievable. Extend the dive time in the 5–10 meter zone on the return — the density of life in the brightest part of the reef is outstanding. Safety stop is comfortably performed in natural light in the shallows.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from San Miguel; southern location adds 25–35 minutes to the boat ride. Mooring buoys used within the national park. The gentle conditions make entry and exit uncomplicated even for new divers.

## Tips

- Spend the majority of the dive at 5–10 meters where the reef is densest and the light most vivid
- This site works well as the first southern dive for newer certified divers before progressing to Paso El Cedral or La Francesa
- Look for the endemic splendid toadfish and flamingo tongue cowries on the shallow reef top
- Ask your operator about current conditions before the dive — reports vary from calm to swift depending on the day
- Combine with a snorkelling segment from the boat on calm days — the shallows are outstanding from the surface too

## Safety

Beginners should still carry and deploy an SMB as standard practice on all Cozumel drift dives, even gentle ones. The current at Punta Dalila is mild, but divers should understand the basics of drift protocol before their first attempt. Fire coral occurs on some coral structures. Recompression facilities are in San Miguel, approximately 30–40 minutes by boat.

## Photography

The bright, shallow, clear water at Punta Dalila is excellent for natural-light photography without strobes. Turtle and nurse shark encounters, and the endemic splendid toadfish in reef holes, are the site's most reliably reported photographic subjects based on current sources.
