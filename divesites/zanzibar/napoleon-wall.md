## Napoleon Wall

Napoleon Wall is a wall dive on the outer reef of South East Zanzibar, named for a resident Napoleon (humphead) wrasse.

## Overview

Two independent sources describe this site consistently. Buccaneer Diving (across two of its pages) describes Napoleon Wall as having "a spectacular drop off" with the name coming from "the resident napoleon wrasse that lives here," alongside stunning soft and hard corals and various moray eels, in the 12-35 meter range, and separately notes turtles, rays, and nurseries for local wildlife along the wall. A second source (aggregated under the zanzibardive.com domain during research) gives the same 12-35m range and the same core description, so the two accounts should be read as corroborating rather than fully independent in origin — a third, unrelated source could not be located. Depth has been updated from the previous 28m to 35m to match the top of the consistently reported range.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Outer reef, South East Zanzibar (Paje/Jambiani area), Tanzania
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Not confirmed for this specific site
- **Current**: Not precisely quantified by sources; South East Zanzibar's outer reef generally sees tidal current
- **Best Time**: October to April (northeast monsoon, South East Zanzibar)

## Marine Life

A resident Napoleon (humphead) wrasse gives the site its name, per both sources. Also reported: various moray eels, turtles, and rays. Sources describe the wall as functioning as a nursery area for local wildlife, though the specific species this refers to is not detailed.

## Dive Profile

Not described in granular detail. The defining feature per both sources is the drop-off itself, described as "spectacular," with coral cover on top of the wall and holes/crevices along its sides.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from Paje/Jambiani-area operators via a barrier-reef gap ("mlango"), consistent with other South East Zanzibar outer-reef sites in this cluster.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Approach the resident Napoleon wrasse slowly and without direct pursuit, as is standard practice with this species anywhere it's found
- Given the 35m upper depth, plan this as a deeper second dive with appropriate NDL management

## Safety Considerations

At a reported maximum of 35m, standard deep-dive discipline applies: agree on a turnaround depth with your guide, monitor NDL closely, and deploy an SMB before ascending from an exposed outer-reef wall.

## Photography

Not confirmed by available sources. A resident Napoleon wrasse is a natural subject for a wide-angle or fish-portrait setup if divers can approach calmly.
