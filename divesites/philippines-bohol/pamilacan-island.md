# Pamilacan Island

Pamilacan Island offers multiple dive sites — Dakit-Dakit, South Pamilacan, Spanish Tower, Sanctuary, and North — with a mix of coral walls, sandy slopes, and drift conditions. Operators disagree on how demanding the individual sites are (see below), but SM Divers' overall guidance and the site's remote, current-exposed character support an Advanced Open Water recommendation as a whole.

## Overview

Pamilacan Island is located approximately 18 kilometres southeast of Panglao Island in the Bohol Sea, requiring a 45-60 minute boat ride from Alona Beach. Part of the Bohol Marine Triangle (BMT) conservation zone, the island offers five distinct dive sites with different characters and difficulty levels. The island is exposed to significant currents on its east and south sides, making drift diving experience essential for the more exposed sites.

The five dive sites span a range, though sources do not fully agree on how difficult each one is. Sanctuary (5-20m) is consistently described as calm and beginner-friendly. South Pamilacan (5-30m) is rated for all levels. Dakit-Dakit (10-25m) is rated inconsistently: SM Divers calls it suitable for beginners and experienced divers alike with "leisurely drift dives," while Valm Divers rates it Intermediate-Advanced with "mild to strong current" — divers should get a current briefing before this dive rather than assume either rating. Spanish Tower (10-35m) is similarly disputed: SM Divers recommends advanced divers for its stronger currents, while Alona Divers describes it as suitable for "beginners to experienced divers." Confirmed marine life across the island includes green sea turtles, fusiliers, snappers, jackfish, barracuda, trevally, nudibranchs, frogfish, and pygmy seahorses. Manta rays, eagle rays, and seasonal whale sharks are listed by SM Divers as occasional visitors at Spanish Tower, but Alona Divers' description of the same sites does not mention any of these species — treat sightings as possible but unconfirmed, not a reason to expect megafauna encounters.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Pamilacan Island, ~18 km SE of Panglao, Bohol, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 26-30 degrees C
- **Current**: Moderate to strong at exposed sites (Dakit-Dakit, Spanish Tower); mild at Sanctuary
- **Best Time**: March to June

## Marine Life

Confirmed across Pamilacan's dive sites: green sea turtles (year-round), fusiliers, snappers, jackfish (Dakit-Dakit), barracuda, trevally, nudibranchs (including the "Pikachu" nudibranch at Spanish Tower), frogfish, pygmy seahorses, pipefish, and garden eels. Stingrays and cuttlefish are seen at the North site. Manta rays, eagle rays, and seasonal whale sharks are listed by SM Divers at Spanish Tower, but this is not corroborated by Alona Divers' listing of the same site — treat as possible, not guaranteed.

## Dive Profile

Dakit-Dakit (east side, 10-25m): Jackfish schools on the deeper reef; current strength is disputed between sources (leisurely drift per SM Divers, mild-to-strong per Valm Divers) — assess with your guide on the day. Spanish Tower (10-35m): Named for a colonial-era watchtower; coral walls with drop-offs; SM Divers rates currents stronger here than at the other sites. Sanctuary (5-20m): Protected coral garden; calm; best for beginners and macro. South Pamilacan (5-30m): Sloping reef with mixed coral. North (10m+): Sandy patches and drop-offs; garden eels and cuttlefish.

## Entry and Exit

Access by dive boat from Panglao Island (Alona Beach area), approximately 45-60 minutes. Full-day trip with 2-3 dives. Entry by giant stride. SMB and drift diving skills strongly recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Advanced Open Water certification is strongly recommended across multiple operators — currents at Dakit-Dakit and Spanish Tower can be strong
- Start with Sanctuary or South Pamilacan to assess conditions before attempting exposed sites
- SMB deployment is essential at drift sites — surface conditions may differ from depth
- The long boat ride makes this a full-day commitment; bring food and sunscreen

## Safety Considerations

Moderate to strong currents at exposed sites (Dakit-Dakit, Spanish Tower) can intensify without warning. Drift diving experience and SMB proficiency are essential. The remote location means extended boat time back to shore. Follow the dive guide's current assessment before each dive.
