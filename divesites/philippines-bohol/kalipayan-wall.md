# Kalipayan Wall

The house reef of Alona Beach, also known as the "Happy Wall" (Kalipayan means happy in Visayan). A wall starting at 3 meters, with a beginner-friendly upper section to about 20 meters and a deeper outer section reaching 30-32 meters that operators rate Intermediate. Soft corals, batfish, lionfish, nudibranchs, ghost pipefish, and dragon seamoth are found in the adjacent sandy area. Reachable by a short swim or a 4-5 minute banca ride from Alona Beach.

## Overview

Kalipayan Wall is the house reef of Alona Beach, positioned between Alona Reef and Danao Wall. The wall starts at approximately 3 meters and drops to around 20 meters on its upper section, decorated with soft and colourful coral heads and small gorgonian sea fans. An outer portion extends deeper, to 30-32 meters by operator listings.

The conditions are typically calm with little to no current, and visibility can reach 25 meters. Operators are split on an overall difficulty rating: Alona Bohol Divers Club rates the site "Intermediate to advanced," citing the 32-meter max depth, while other sources describe the shallow upper wall as accessible to less experienced divers. Because the site's own listed max depth (30-32m) falls in the Intermediate range under this guide's difficulty rubric, it is tagged Intermediate overall — divers who stay on the upper wall (3-20m) will find calmer, easier conditions than the outer section suggests. The sandy area adjacent to the wall is known habitat for robust ghost pipefish and dragon seamoth — prized macro finds. Batfish are a near-constant presence along the wall. Sardine clouds have been observed here according to Valm Divers.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Alona Beach, Panglao Island, Bohol, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 32 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Up to 25 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 26-30 degrees C
- **Current**: Normally calm, minimal current
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

Confirmed by multiple local operators: batfish (near-constant presence along the wall), lionfish (crevices and overhangs), nudibranchs, robust ghost pipefish (sandy area adjacent to wall), dragon seamoth, seahorses, and soft corals. Sardine clouds reported by Valm Divers. Pegasusfish (dragon seamoth) noted by Tropical Divers. Stonefish have been reported at this site but could not be confirmed across three independent sources.

## Dive Profile

Enter from banca or swim from the beach. Descend to the wall top at 3 meters. Follow the wall face, checking coral heads for nudibranchs and crevices for lionfish. The batfish group is usually found mid-wall. Explore the adjacent sandy area for ghost pipefish and dragon seamoth — both are well camouflaged. Return along the wall to the shallows. The calm conditions and shallow depth allow extended bottom times. An excellent night dive site.

## Entry and Exit

Access by banca from Alona Beach (4-5 minutes) or by swimming directly from the beach. Entry by giant stride from banca or wade-in from shore.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your guide to point out current ghost pipefish locations — they move and are well camouflaged
- The batfish group is photogenic and cooperative — approach slowly
- Excellent night dive due to easy access and calm conditions
- Perfect for a relaxed morning or final dive of the day

## Safety Considerations

Generally a sheltered, easy-current site, but the outer deeper section reaches 30-32 meters and is rated Intermediate to advanced by at least one operator — watch depth carefully and don't drift past your training limits on the outer wall. Do not rest on the sand or touch the bottom — cryptic species may be present. Watch for sea urchins on the wall.
