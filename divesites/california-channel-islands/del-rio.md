## Del Rio

Del Rio is a historic wreck dive in California Channel Islands, North America.

## Overview

The Del Rio is a steam schooner that wrecked off Anacapa Island, her remains resting in a shallow kelp-fringed rocky area at around 12 meters with sections of hull, machinery, and boiler scattered across the reef. Sea lions and harbor seals are common around Anacapa Island generally, and divers at Frenchy's Cove often report inquisitive California sea lions in the water, though no source confirms a resident rookery specifically at this cove (established sea lion haul-outs on Anacapa are best documented at Cathedral Cove and Underwater Arch). Visibility here can be exceptional on calm summer days, reaching 20 meters or more in the clear Channel Islands water.

## Wreck History

The Del Rio was a commercial fishing vessel that had previously served in mine-sweeping operations during World War II. On October 10, 1952, the Del Rio caught fire off Anacapa Island. The fishing vessels Sea Ranger and Western Monarch responded to the distress; Sea Ranger rescued the crew of 11 and stood by the burning hulk after offloading its fish cargo. The U.S. Coast Guard vessel 83366 arrived on scene and monitored the hulk to determine whether it would become a hazard to navigation. The Del Rio drifted toward the island and eventually sank at Frenchy's Cove on the west end of Anacapa Island, where she lies today on the rocky reef. The wreck falls within Channel Islands National Park and Channel Islands National Marine Sanctuary; collecting anything from the site is strictly prohibited.

## Marine Life

Frenchy's Cove on West Anacapa Island is sheltered from the prevailing northwest swell. California sea lions and harbor seals are documented as resident and breeding on Anacapa Island as a whole, and are commonly encountered by divers around the island's coves. Garibaldi are typical of the kelp-fringed shallows here. No source consulted names species specifically observed on the Del Rio's hull sections themselves — kelp bass, sheephead, and lingcod are regional expectations for this type of reef habitat rather than confirmed site-specific sightings.

## Dive Profile

The wreck rests in approximately 12 meters (40 ft) at Frenchy's Cove. The site is one of the shallower and more sheltered wreck dives in the northern Channel Islands, though the hull has deteriorated significantly over seven decades. Scattered hull sections, machinery components, and engine parts are distributed across the rocky bottom.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive — Anacapa Island lies 11–14 miles off the Ventura/Oxnard coast. Day-trip charters operate regularly from Ventura Harbor to Anacapa, making this the most accessible of the Channel Islands wreck dives. Day trips typically include 3 dives with a surface interval at Frenchy's Cove. M/V Peace (Ventura) and Spectre (Santa Barbara Aquatics) both service Anacapa Island.

## Tips and Recommendations

Frenchy's Cove is one of the most accessible Anacapa dive sites. The sheltered cove makes it a reliable dive even when conditions elsewhere are rough. The resident California sea lions are playful and frequently approach divers in the water.

## Safety Considerations

Water temperature at Anacapa ranges from approximately 12°C (54°F) in winter to 18°C (65°F) in summer. A 7 mm wetsuit with hood and gloves is recommended; conditions are generally mild relative to the northern islands. The site is protected — disturbing or removing any artifacts is a federal offense.
