## Equator

Equator is a historic wreck dive in California Channel Islands, North America.

## Overview

The Equator is a 93-foot commercial fishing vessel built in Tacoma in 1949 that struck a rock and sank at Middle Anacapa Island the same year she was built. Being a nearly-new vessel, she was a prime salvage target: per California Diving News, explosives were used to open the hull and recover her valuable, nearly-new diesel engine. The heavily salvaged debris field now sits in shallow water around 8–12 meters, where bronze and iron hardware is occasionally exposed among shifting sand and kelp. California sea lions and harbor seals are documented generally around Anacapa Island; no source consulted during this QA pass confirmed specific species sightings at the Equator wreck itself.

## Wreck History

The Equator was a fishing vessel just under 93 feet (28 m) in length, built in Tacoma, Washington in 1949. She plied the Pacific Coast fishing trade for only a short time before wrecking on the rocks of Middle Anacapa Island the same year she was built — 1949. The tuna seiner struck a rock approximately 150 yards from shore near East Fish Camp anchorage on the seaward side of Middle Anacapa Island and quickly sank in approximately 9 meters (25–30 ft) of water. Being nearly new, she was a prime salvage target: per California Diving News, explosives were used to open the hull to retrieve valuable hardware, including her expensive, nearly-new diesel engine. (A previous QA pass on this file removed this detail as unconfirmed; it has been restored after being independently corroborated against California Diving News during this re-audit.) The site is now thoroughly salvaged; few remains are found, scattered between roughly 8–12 meters, and the site is in a constant state of change as sand periodically buries and re-exposes the debris field. The wreck falls within Channel Islands National Park and Channel Islands National Marine Sanctuary, where it is a protected archaeological site; removing or disturbing anything from the wreck carries federal penalties including fines and potential jail time.

## Marine Life

California Diving News describes marine life around Anacapa Island generally — including angel sharks, bat rays, moray eels, sea lions, and harbor seals — but does not tie these species specifically to the Equator wreck. No source consulted during this QA pass confirmed marine life observations at the wreck site itself; earlier claims that garibaldi and sheephead "patrol" this specific site have been removed as unconfirmed. The site's location on the seaward side of the island exposes it to nutrient-rich upwelling typical of Anacapa's reefs generally.

## Dive Profile

The wreck is in a constant state of change: sand periodically sweeps in and buries portions of the debris field, then shifts away to expose hardware again. At times a light kelp growth makes the site difficult to spot from above. Identifiable elements include metal hardware and hull plating scattered between approximately 8–12 meters (25–40 ft). The site is shallow enough for snorkelers in very calm conditions.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive — Anacapa Island lies 11–14 miles from the Ventura/Oxnard coast. Day-charter vessels from Ventura Harbor reach the island in approximately 1–1.5 hours. The East Fish Camp anchorage is the reference point for this site. M/V Peace (Ventura) and Spectre (Santa Barbara Aquatics, Santa Barbara) both run regular trips to Anacapa.

## Tips and Recommendations

Ask your captain or divemaster specifically about the Equator when visiting Anacapa — it is a smaller, less-visited site than the Winfield Scott and requires local knowledge of current sand cover to locate the best portions. Summer and early fall offer the clearest water and most stable conditions.

## Safety Considerations

Water temperature at Anacapa ranges from approximately 12°C in winter to 18°C in summer. A 7 mm wetsuit with hood and gloves is recommended. The seaward (south) exposure of this site means it is more susceptible to swell than Frenchy's Cove on the island's north side. Dive only in settled conditions.
