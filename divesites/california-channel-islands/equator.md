## Equator

Equator is a historic wreck dive in California Channel Islands, North America.

## Overview

The Equator is a 93-foot commercial fishing vessel (a tuna seiner) built in Tacoma in 1949 that wrecked on the rocks at Middle Anacapa Island the same year. Salvagers used explosives shortly afterward to open the hull and retrieve valuable equipment including the nearly-new diesel engine, so the debris field is heavily picked-over and sits in shallow water around 8–12 meters, where bronze and iron hardware is occasionally exposed among the kelp. California Diving News specifically documents garibaldi, small cream-colored ghost gobies, feather duster and Christmas tree worms, colorful anemones, nudibranchs, and sea hares at this site.

## Wreck History

The Equator was a fishing vessel just under 93 feet (28 m) in length, built in Tacoma, Washington in 1949. She plied the Pacific Coast fishing trade for only a short time before wrecking on the rocks of Middle Anacapa Island the same year she was built — 1949. The tuna seiner struck a rock approximately 150 yards from shore near East Fish Camp anchorage on the seaward side of Middle Anacapa Island and quickly sank in approximately 9 meters (30 ft) of water. Being a nearly new and valuable vessel, she was a prime salvage target: per California Diving News, salvagers used explosives almost immediately to open up the hull and retrieve the most valuable hardware, including her nearly-new diesel motor. Few remains are now found, scattered between roughly 8–12 meters. The wreck falls within Channel Islands National Park and Channel Islands National Marine Sanctuary, where it is a protected archaeological site; removing or disturbing anything from the wreck carries federal penalties including fines and potential jail time.

## Marine Life

California Diving News specifically documents this site's marine life: bright orange garibaldi and subdued, cream-colored ghost gobies are the notable fish, while feather duster worms, Christmas tree worms, and a variety of colorful anemones dot the reef. Nudibranchs and sea hares are also found among the invertebrates. A previous version of this description also claimed sea lions and sheephead at the site; sea lions are present around Anacapa generally but are not specifically documented at the Equator itself by the sources consulted, and sheephead were not confirmed here — both have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The wreck is in a constant state of change: sand periodically sweeps in and buries portions of the debris field, then shifts away to expose hardware again. At times a light kelp growth makes the site difficult to spot from above. Identifiable elements include metal hardware and hull plating scattered between approximately 8–12 meters (25–40 ft). The site is shallow enough for snorkelers in very calm conditions.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive — Anacapa Island lies 11–14 miles from the Ventura/Oxnard coast. Day-charter vessels from Ventura Harbor reach the island in approximately 1–1.5 hours. The East Fish Camp anchorage is the reference point for this site. M/V Peace (Ventura) and Spectre (Santa Barbara Aquatics, Santa Barbara) both run regular trips to Anacapa.

## Tips and Recommendations

Ask your captain or divemaster specifically about the Equator when visiting Anacapa — it is a smaller, less-visited site than the Winfield Scott and requires local knowledge of current sand cover to locate the best portions. Summer and early fall offer the clearest water and most stable conditions.

## Safety Considerations

Water temperature at Anacapa ranges from approximately 12°C in winter to 18°C in summer. A 7 mm wetsuit with hood and gloves is recommended. The seaward (south) exposure of this site means it is more susceptible to swell than Frenchy's Cove on the island's north side. Dive only in settled conditions.
