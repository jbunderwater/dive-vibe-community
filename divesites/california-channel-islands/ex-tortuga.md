## Ex-Tortuga

Ex-Tortuga is the wreck of a U.S. Navy dock landing ship that grounded on San Miguel Island in 1987.

## Overview

The Ex-Tortuga (ex-USS Tortuga, LSD-26) was a 457-foot Casa Grande-class dock landing ship that ran aground at Cardwell Point on the southeast corner of San Miguel Island on December 15, 1987, after breaking her tow in a storm while being moved for use as a Tomahawk missile target. Sources disagree on what happened next: Wikipedia states the ship was scrapped where she lay over a period of more than eight months, while seaforces.org and Islapedia both independently state the dismantled hull was raised, towed offshore, and deliberately scuttled to become a reef on August 21, 1988. Either way, remains and debris from this large naval vessel rest at the southeast corner of San Miguel Island — the most remote and exposed of the Channel Islands — in waters swept by strong currents and cold Pacific upwelling.

## Wreck History

The USS Tortuga (LSD-26) was a Casa Grande-class dock landing ship of the United States Navy, laid down in 1944 and launched in January 1945, measuring 457 feet 9 inches (139.5 m) in length and displacing 4,490 long tons. After decommissioning she was used as a target vessel for weapons testing based out of Port Hueneme, California. On December 15, 1987, while under tow toward an area west of San Nicolas Island for a Tomahawk missile exercise, she broke free during a storm with winds to 50 knots and seas over 20 feet, and ran aground at Cardwell Point at the southeast corner of San Miguel Island. The impact broke a roughly 100-foot section of the stern free from the 355-foot fore section.

**Sources conflict on the wreck's final disposition.** Wikipedia states the ship was scrapped on site, a process that took more than eight months to complete. seaforces.org's ship history states plainly: "Final Disposition: scuttled, August 21, 1988 after grounding on San Miguel Island while being used as a target, the ship was partially dismantled, raised and towed offshore and scuttled on this date." Islapedia similarly notes the vessel "was scrapped where she grounded... [and] was towed off Cardwell Point to become a reef." Taken together, the independent sources describing a deliberate tow-and-scuttle appear to reflect the more specific account, but this has not been reconciled with Wikipedia's "scrapped on site" framing, so both versions are presented here rather than asserting one as fact. The site lies within Channel Islands National Park, Channel Islands National Marine Sanctuary, and the San Miguel Island State Ecological Reserve.

## Marine Life

San Miguel Island is known for some of the richest marine life in the Channel Islands, driven by cold Pacific upwelling. The surrounding waters support lingcod, various rockfish species, wolf eels, and California spiny lobster. Any hard structure in this area accumulates invertebrate growth rapidly. Site-specific marine life reports for the Ex-Tortuga wreck debris have not been found in sources consulted.

## Dive Profile

The wreck lies at the southeast corner of San Miguel Island (Cardwell Point). The reported depth is approximately 20 meters, though the original ship was scrapped in place and the nature of the remaining debris field is not well documented in publicly available dive sources. San Miguel Island is subject to some of the strongest currents in the Channel Islands.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only. San Miguel Island is the most remote of the northern Channel Islands, lying approximately 64 miles from Ventura Harbor and 46 miles from Santa Barbara. Overnight liveaboard charters (such as M/V Truth via Channel Islands Expeditions and M/V Peace) are the practical access option. Conditions at San Miguel are frequently poor; this site can only be dived in calm weather windows.

## Tips and Recommendations

San Miguel Island diving is best in September through November when northwest winds typically ease. Drysuit is strongly recommended. Consult your liveaboard captain about current conditions at Cardwell Point before planning a dive on this site.

## Safety Considerations

San Miguel Island is subject to strong winds, heavy swell, and powerful currents. Water temperature is typically 10–13°C (50–55°F) year-round — drysuit diving is recommended. The remote location means full diver self-sufficiency is essential. This is an advanced dive.
