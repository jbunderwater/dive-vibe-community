## Alor Archipelago

Indonesia's rising macro and big-current destination where Rhinopias hunting meets dramatic wall diving in the Pantar Strait, with far fewer crowds than Lembeh or Raja Ampat.

## Description

The Alor Archipelago sits at the eastern end of the Lesser Sunda Islands, in the heart of the Coral Triangle. Diving here divides into two distinct characters: Kalabahi Bay offers world-class muck diving on black volcanic sand, while the Pantar Strait delivers powerful drift dives along dramatic walls and pinnacles.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Walls and Overhangs**: The Cathedral and Babylon, with coral-covered walls, a swim-through and overhangs
- **Muck Diving**: Mucky Mosque (Kalabahi Bay) and Beang Abang (southern Pantar) on black sand, with Rhinopias, frogfish and ghost pipefish reported at Mucky Mosque
- **Pinnacles and Current**: Kal's Dream (seamount) and Current Alley (strong-current ridge in the Pantar Strait)
- **Reef Diving**: Clown Valley (dense anemone fields) and Mike's Delight (boulder slope with sea snakes)

### Accessibility

- **Dive Operators**: Operators reviewed include Nautika Dive Alor and Alor Tanapi Dive Resort; liveaboards such as those offered by Emperor Divers also visit Alor.
- **Access**: Dive sites are reached by boat. Check current flight and transfer options with your operator.

### Marine Life & Environment

- **Water Conditions**: Starfish.ch reports water around 27 C, dropping to about 23 C at some southern sites; The Occasional Traveller reported about 24 C at Bama. Strong currents run through the Pantar Strait.
- **Marine Life**: Site-specific sightings are listed on each site page. No destination-wide species list is given here.

## Additional Information

- **Currency**: Indonesian Rupiah (IDR)
- **Language**: Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia), with local Alorese and Pantar languages spoken in villages
- **Safety**: Strong currents in the Pantar Strait make many sites suitable only for experienced divers. Dive with a guide and carry a surface marker buoy.
