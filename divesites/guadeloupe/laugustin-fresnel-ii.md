## L'Augustin Fresnel II

L'Augustin Fresnel II is a 53-metre Canadian lighthouse tender deliberately scuttled in 2003 within the Cousteau Reserve — the most recently added and best-preserved of the three reserve wrecks, now resting at 31–33 metres depth.

## Overview

The Augustin Fresnel II was a lighthouse/buoy tender built by Yarrows Ltd. at Esquimalt, British Columbia, Canada, in the late 1940s. It served the French Lighthouse and Beacon Service in the Mediterranean (chiefly off Marseille and Corsica) before being reconditioned in Bordeaux and transferred to Pointe-à-Pitre in 1990 to maintain navigational markings for the Antilles-Guyane region. It was decommissioned in 1994 after a propulsion failure. After decontamination and preparation, it was deliberately scuttled on 9 July 2003 within the Cousteau Reserve as an artificial reef.

The wreck was originally placed at approximately 30 metres depth. Cyclone Dean (2007) has been reported by some curated notes to have subsequently shifted the vessel deeper, though this specific claim was not independently re-confirmed this pass. Current depth is reported as 31–33 metres (to the propeller) on a sandy bottom. Sources differ on both dimensions and depth: heures-saines.com gives 53m × 9m; a dedicated French Wikipedia article (fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Fresnel_(épave)) instead gives 51.3m × 9.8m beam. Depth: heures-saines.com states 33m to the propeller; Wikipedia states 28-31m. These are recorded as source disagreements rather than resolved to a single figure.

**Re-audit finding (2026-08-05):** this is the only one of the three Cousteau Reserve wrecks with a dedicated Wikipedia article, providing independent cross-verification of the builder (Yarrows Ltd., Esquimalt, BC — new detail not previously in this file), the 1994 propulsion-failure retirement, and the July 9, 2003 scuttling date. All core facts (scuttling date, cause of retirement, general service history) are now confirmed by both heures-saines.com and Wikipedia independently.

Interior spaces including cabins, galley, and dining areas are accessible for exploration.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Cousteau Reserve, south of Malendure, Basse-Terre, Guadeloupe
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters
- **Vessel Length**: 51.3–53 metres (sources vary; see Overview)
- **Vessel Tonnage**: 660 tonnes
- **Scuttled**: 9 July 2003

## Marine Life

Confirmed at this wreck by Les Heures Saines: barracuda, jacks (carangues), spiny lobsters (langoustines), and moray eels. Groupers and hawksbill turtles are reported by Tribloo and other operators. No additional species are attributed without site-specific confirmation.

## Safety

Advanced certification mandatory. Penetration of interior spaces (cabins, galley) is possible — enter only with a torch and appropriate wreck training. The vessel has been in the water since 2003 and structural integrity should be assessed before penetrating deeper interior sections. Gas management at 30–33 m is critical; Nitrox is available from Bouillante operators and extends bottom time. Safety stop at 5 metres mandatory.
