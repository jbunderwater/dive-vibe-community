## Épave de Caret

Épave de Caret is a sunken sand-dredging vessel west of Îlet Caret in the Grand Cul-de-Sac Marin, deliberately sunk to create fish/coral habitat — the exact date is contested by sources (see below) and is not asserted here.

## Overview

The wreck is a former sand-dredging vessel that was intentionally sunk within the Grand Cul-de-Sac Marin, a 15,000-hectare protected marine reserve, to create substrate for marine growth and serve as a fish concentration device. The wreck is identified on excursion routes from Sainte-Rose to Îlet Caret.

The hull is mostly shallow — the shallowest sections are at 2–3 metres and visible from the surface in calm conditions (gwadaplans.com describes it as nearly surfacing). The deepest recorded dive point is approximately 18 metres. GwadaPlans describes it as a "ship's hold grounded in the shallows that serves as a nursery for fish."

Penetration of the interior is not permitted — the hull is structurally unstable and fire corals are present on interior surfaces.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West of Îlet Caret, Grand Cul-de-Sac Marin, northern Guadeloupe
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters

## Marine Life

GwadaPlans describes the wreck as a fish nursery habitat, and icigo.com's excursion page confirms visitors observe "multicoloured exotic fish, starfish, shells" while exploring the wreck. The surrounding Grand Cul-de-Sac Marin reef system supports diverse reef life, but no specific species by name have been confirmed in published sources at this particular wreck. Regional marine life applies; no further site-specific species reports found.

## Safety

No penetration — hull is unstable and fire corals are present inside. The wreck is shallow and visible from the surface; most visitors approach as a snorkel site on excursions.
