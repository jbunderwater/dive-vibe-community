## Bligh Reef

Bligh Reef is a reef off Bligh Island in Prince William Sound, best known as the place where the tanker Exxon Valdez ran aground on March 24, 1989.

## Overview

Bligh Reef lies off Bligh Island in Prince William Sound at about 60°50'N, 146°53'W. Wikipedia places the nearest town, Tatitlek, about seven miles to the northeast, while the Exxon Valdez oil spill article says the reef is about 6 miles west of Tatitlek. It is named after William Bligh, who served as master on James Cook's third voyage, and is also called Bligh Island Reef. Wikipedia lists three groundings: SS Olympia in 1910, the Exxon Valdez in 1989, and the tug Pathfinder in December 2009. Alaska Public Media reported that Pathfinder tore a hole in its keel on the reef and that two fuel tanks lost pressure, with a slick spotted at first light. Wikipedia also reports that the reef is a halibut fishing ground and shrimp harvesting area, and that federal law (33 U.S.C. 2733) required an automated navigation light there after the 1989 spill.

At 12:04 a.m. on March 24, 1989 the Exxon Valdez struck the reef. The Exxon Valdez Oil Spill Trustee Council gives her length as 987 feet, her cargo as 53,094,510 gallons of North Slope crude, and says eight of eleven cargo tanks were punctured, spilling about 10.8 million gallons. Wikipedia gives about 10.8 million US gallons as well. The Trustee Council says the captain reported he would likely divert from the traffic lanes to avoid ice from Columbia Glacier and that the ship went farther east than reported.

No source describing recreational diving at Bligh Reef itself was found. The 30 meter depth, difficulty rating and boat access in the data file are not confirmed by a dive source. Nearby Port Fidalgo is covered by Divers Alert Network and Divernet as a Prince William Sound dive area, and DAN reports summer visibility of 10 to 40 feet there.

No dive charter serving this reef was found. Prince William Sound access is generally from Valdez or Whittier; any visit needs a seaworthy private or custom charter.

## Safety Considerations

Remote open-water site in Prince William Sound with no documented dive infrastructure, and a location with commercial vessel traffic and a history of groundings. Dry suit, buddy diving and an SMB are essential.
