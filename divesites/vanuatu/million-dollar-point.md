## Million Dollar Point

Million Dollar Point is one of the world's more unusual dive sites — a deliberately created dump of US military equipment pushed into the sea as WWII wound down, forming a sprawling artificial reef of recognizable military hardware on a gently sloping, shore-accessible site near Luganville, Espiritu Santo.

## Overview

Espiritu Santo hosted one of the largest US supply bases in the Pacific theater during WWII. When the war ended, the US offered to sell the surplus equipment to French and British colonial authorities at a steep discount — reportedly as low as six to ten cents on the dollar — but the offer was declined. Rather than ship the material home or hand it over for free, the US military drove and bulldozed vehicles, cranes, forklifts, and crates of supplies into the sea at this site over the course of about two days. The Naval Advance Base at Espiritu Santo was formally abandoned in February 1946, and sources place the dumping around that time, several months after the war's end rather than immediately in August 1945. No source found gives a reliable dollar figure for the equipment dumped at this specific site — the name reflects a general sense of "millions of dollars" of hardware rather than a documented appraisal. (A commonly cited $4 billion figure refers to total US military surplus across the entire Pacific theater, not this site alone.)

Decades later, the equipment forms an artificial reef structure encrusted with corals and sponges. The slope begins at 3 meters and extends to roughly 40 meters at the deepest accessible point, with the main concentration of equipment in the shallower and mid-depth zones. Steering wheels, bulldozer treads, and engine blocks remain recognizable under the coral growth.

## What to Expect

Shore entry is easy — a short walk down the beach to the water's edge, with the first pieces of equipment visible within meters of shore. The shallow sections are suitable for snorkelers as well as divers, with the equipment concentration increasing somewhat with depth.

The site is popular for photography: recognizable man-made objects combined with coral encrustation make for distinctive wide-angle compositions. Corals have grown over much of the machinery, and schools of reef fish move through the wreckage. Beyond that general description, no site-specific marine life reports (particular species confirmed at this site) were found — the earlier list of hawksbill turtles, barracuda, and other named species could not be traced to a site-specific source, so it has been removed rather than repeated as fact.

## Practical Information

- **Depth Range**: 3–40 meters
- **Visibility**: Not consistently documented across sources
- **Currents**: Generally mild; sheltered shore location near Luganville
- **Entry**: Shore entry — easy access suitable for all levels
- **Best For**: A wide range of divers; iconic historical site; good for photography

## Marine Life Highlights

Coral and sponge growth covers much of the dumped equipment, and reef fish are commonly seen moving through the wreckage. No source-confirmed list of specific species sighted at Million Dollar Point was found; the destination's reefs generally support typical Vanuatu tropical marine life, but that should not be read as a site-specific sighting list.
