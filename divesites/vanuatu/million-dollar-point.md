## Million Dollar Point

Million Dollar Point is a site on the shore near Luganville, Espiritu Santo, where surplus US military vehicles and machinery were pushed into the sea at the end of WWII.

## Overview

Espiritu Santo hosted a large US supply base in WWII. Wikipedia reports that the US tried to sell much of the gear to the French for 6 cents on the dollar, and that the French hoped a refusal would leave everything to them for free; the Naval Advance Base was abandoned in February 1946. Wikipedia says the Seabees built a ramp into the sea near Luganville Airfield, while McFadyen describes vehicles lined up on shore and driven into the channel. We Are The Mighty gives sale offers as low as 6 to 10 cents on the dollar. No source found documents a dollar value for the equipment at this specific site; the "$4 billion" figure quoted by We Are The Mighty refers to Pacific-wide surplus.

Reported equipment includes bulldozers, tracked cranes and excavators, graders, forklifts, tractors, trucks, trailers, Willys jeeps and Coke bottles (McFadyen, Aore Adventures, Diveplanit). McFadyen also mentions the wrecks of the Dedele and El Retiro near the site.

## What to Expect

McFadyen describes a shore dive: gear up on the grass, cross a narrow sand beach, swim out about 20 m, then descend and head west. Equipment appears within a minute, and he recommends flat seas. Diveplanit lists shore entry from Million Dollar Beach.

Depth reports conflict: Diveplanit gives about 15-28 m (15-25 m in its description), while McFadyen reports equipment on sand at about 35 m. Visibility is described as good, around 20 m on average per McFadyen, and Diveplanit notes it is clear unless there has been recent rain.

## Practical Information

- **Depth Range**: sources report roughly 15-35 meters
- **Visibility**: about 20 meters on average (McFadyen)
- **Entry**: Shore entry, short swim from the beach
- **Best For**: Divers interested in WWII history and wreckage turning to reef

## Marine Life Highlights

Diveplanit names hogfish, anthias, anemones, an electric ("disco") clam and soft corals growing on the wreckage; Aore Adventures notes corals on jeeps and schools of fish moving through the machinery.
