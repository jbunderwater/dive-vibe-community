## SS President Coolidge

The SS President Coolidge is a luxury liner converted to a WWII troopship that struck two American mines off Espiritu Santo on 26 October 1942. She now rests on her port side, with the bow at about 21 m and the stern at about 73 m.

## Overview

The Coolidge was built by Newport News Shipbuilding (laid down 1930, launched February 1931, delivered 1 October 1931). Wikipedia and Pacific Wrecks both give her tonnage as 21,936. Lengths differ by measurement convention: 615 ft (187.5 m) between perpendiculars and 653 ft (199 m) overall per Wikipedia, 615 ft per Pacific Wrecks, and "nearly 200 m" per Diveplanit.

On 26 October 1942 she struck two US Navy mines while entering the harbour at Espiritu Santo. Her captain tried to beach her, but a coral reef stopped her; she listed heavily and slid into the channel. About 5,340 men got ashore. Two people died: fireman Robert Reid, killed by the first blast, and Captain Elwood Euart, who went back aboard to help trapped men in the infirmary. Vanuatu has banned salvage of artifacts from the wreck since 1983.

Depth sources: bow around 21 m (about 20 m per Diveplanit), stern about 73 m (about 70 m per Diveplanit); Aore Adventures gives the engine room at about 150 ft (46 m). Diveplanit notes that all dives involve decompression stops.

## What to Expect

- **The Lady**: A porcelain relief known as "The Lady and the Unicorn", moved from the first-class smoking room to a wall at the far end of the dining room. Diveplanit gives about 39 m; Pacific Wrecks gives 147.6 ft (about 45 m).
- **Cargo Hold No. 2**: A barber's chair sits in an alcove near the hold opening.
- **B Deck surgery**: Cargo Hold No. 5 is the entrance to the doctor's surgery.
- **Engine room**: Reached through a hole cut in the hull by salvagers, about two-thirds along the ship.
- **Cargo and fittings**: Wikipedia lists guns, cannons, jeeps, trucks, helmets, chandeliers and a mosaic tile fountain; Diveplanit notes rifles on the hull near portholes and ammunition in the holds.

Diveplanit describes it as a shore dive, west of Million Dollar Beach just outside Luganville, with practically no current. Penetrating the wreck requires proper training, equipment and a guide familiar with the structure.

## Practical Information

- **Depth Range**: about 21-73 meters (bow to stern)
- **Currents**: Practically none, per Diveplanit
- **Entry**: Shore entry near Luganville
- **Best For**: Wreck divers with deep and decompression experience; technical training required for the deepest sections

## Marine Life Highlights

Diveplanit and Aore Adventures report moray eels (a resident giant moray nicknamed "Nessie" in one of two large winches near the bow), lionfish, triggerfish, nudibranchs and groupers; Diveplanit also reports flashlight fish in Cargo Hold No. 2 and an anemone garden with anemonefish at the 5 m decompression stop. Wikipedia notes coral growth across the wreck.
