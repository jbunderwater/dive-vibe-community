## Overview

The SS Tui Tuate was built as the tug Empire Shirley, launched in 1945 by Alexander Hall & Co. of Aberdeen for Britain's Ministry of War Transport. She was sold in 1947 to the Union Steamship Company of New Zealand and renamed Tapuhi.

During the TEV Wahine disaster at Wellington on 10 April 1968, Tapuhi tried to tow the stricken ferry about 11:00, but the line broke after about 10 minutes (Wikipedia, McFadyen). McFadyen says she rescued 174 people from the water; another account puts it at 178; Wikipedia's Wahine article does not credit her with rescues.

McFadyen reports she was sold to Fijian owners and renamed Tui Tawate in 1973 and Tui Tuate in 1974; the Aberdeen City Museum record lists both names. In 1977 she carried bunker oil salvaged from the SS President Coolidge to the SS Arcadia (McFadyen). She was bought in 1986 by a Wellington owner who planned a floating restaurant, was not seaworthy enough for the tow, and was abandoned near Luganville (McFadyen; the Aberdeen record gives abandonment in Espiritu Santo in 1987). McFadyen gives 16 May 1990 as the date she was sunk in the Segond Channel; the Aberdeen record does not mention a scuttling.

McFadyen reports the wreck upright, facing south-west, at about 44 m (deck about 41 m; one source gives 45 m), off Coral Quays Resort near Luganville. He found the funnel missing and the upper bridge intact, and reports fewer than 10 people had dived it by October 1998. On his June 2007 dive a strong tidal current ran to the bottom, with visibility of at least 30 m. He describes the dive as suited only to very experienced deep divers.
