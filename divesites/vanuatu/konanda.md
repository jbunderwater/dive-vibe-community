## Konanda

The Konanda is a former island trader sunk as a dive site off Ifira Island in Port Vila Harbour, Efate. She sits upright on sand at about 26 m.

## Overview

McFadyen reports she was launched on 20 January 1955 at the D & J Boot yard on the Rhine, Netherlands, carried raw sugar in Queensland and ran a ferry service between Nelson and Wellington, New Zealand, from 1961. Cyclone Uma wrecked her in February 1987 (McFadyen; Big Blue Vanuatu also cites damage by Cyclone Uma), and Iririki Island Resort bought her to be stripped and sunk. She was scuttled off Ifira Island by Paul Gaddes; sources differ on the date: November 1987 (McFadyen) and October 1987 (Wannadive).

Length also varies: 51 m (153 ft) per McFadyen and 45 m per Big Blue Vanuatu and Wannadive.

## What to Expect

The wreck sits upright on sand at about 26 m, with a mooring on the top of the mast at about 12 m (McFadyen); Wannadive says the derricks and rigging reach to within 10 m of the surface. McFadyen describes routes from the front hold to the second hold and up a hatch, through a large starboard cabin, and into the stern, with an engine room entrance described as tight. Wannadive and Big Blue Vanuatu say the wreck was prepared for penetration of the holds and cabins and describe it as suitable for a first wreck dive; penetration still requires proper training and equipment.

## Practical Information

- **Depth Range**: about 12-26 meters (mast to seabed)
- **Visibility**: about 20 m (McFadyen); 10-30 m (Wannadive)
- **Entry**: Boat dive, a short ride (under 10 minutes per Wannadive)
- **Best For**: Divers seeking an introduction to wreck diving

## Marine Life Highlights

McFadyen and Big Blue Vanuatu name no species. Wannadive cites a 2002 dive log mentioning large lionfish inside the wreck.
