## Konanda

The Konanda is a former coastal trader deliberately sunk near Ifira Island in Port Vila Harbour, Efate, after being damaged in a cyclone — a compact, well-prepared wreck that operators regard as one of the more approachable wreck dives in Vanuatu.

## Overview

Correction from earlier research: the Konanda lies off Efate near Port Vila, not off Espiritu Santo, and her loss has no WWII connection — both details in an earlier version of this page were incorrect. The vessel was damaged in Cyclone Uma (February 1987) and declared a total loss. She was subsequently purchased by Iririki Island Resort and deliberately scuttled by commercial diver Paul Gaddes to create a dive site — sources differ on whether this happened in October or November 1987. Reported lengths for the ship also vary: two sources give approximately 45 meters, while a more detailed vessel survey gives 51 meters (153 feet); she is described as a Dutch-built coaster originally designed as a ferry.

The wreck sits flat on a sandy bottom at about 26 meters, with her derricks and rigging reaching to within about 10–12 meters of the surface — a mooring line typically runs to the mast. Her compact size allows much of the ship to be covered in a single dive, and the interior — holds, cabins, engine room, and bridge — was deliberately prepared for safe penetration.

## What to Expect

Operators describe the Konanda as an ideal introduction to wreck diving: the structure was cleared and prepared specifically for penetration by less-experienced wreck divers, with holds and cabins accessible without technical skills. The compact hull can be circumnavigated on a single dive, from the bow to the stern superstructure.

## Practical Information

- **Depth Range**: approximately 10–26 meters (mast/rigging to seabed)
- **Visibility**: Around 20 meters reported by one source
- **Currents**: Not consistently documented across sources
- **Entry**: Boat dive from Port Vila operators
- **Best For**: Divers looking for an accessible introduction to wreck penetration
