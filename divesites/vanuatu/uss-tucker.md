## USS Tucker

The USS Tucker (DD-374) was a US Navy Mahan-class destroyer lost to a mine in the Segond Channel approach to Espiritu Santo in August 1942. The wreck lies in about 18 m of water.

## Overview

Tucker was commissioned on 23 July 1936 and measured about 341 ft (104 m) overall (Wikipedia, Evolution Underwater, Diveplanit). She struck a US-laid mine while entering the channel to Espiritu Santo. Sources disagree on dates: the mine strike is given as 1 August (Destroyer History Foundation), 3 August (Evolution Underwater) and 4 August 1942 (Wikipedia, Diveplanit), and the sinking as 4 August (Destroyer History Foundation, Pacific Wrecks) or 5 August (Wikipedia, Diveplanit). Three men were killed in the blast; Pacific Wrecks, the Destroyer History Foundation and Evolution Underwater add that three more were listed as missing. Wikipedia reports the bow was scuttled by a diving party, and that the Navy used the site for diver training before private salvagers and sport divers stripped it.

Location accounts differ: Evolution Underwater places the wreck northeast of Malo Island, about 40 minutes by boat from its base; Diveplanit places it where the Bruat Channel meets the Segond Channel, off the west point of Aore Island.

## What to Expect

Evolution Underwater and Diveplanit describe a broken wreck: a largely intact stern (upright with a list to starboard, with an entrance hatchway), a debris field of hull plates and twisted beams in the middle, and a bow lying on its port side partly buried in sand. Evolution Underwater reports two engines and prop shafts lying in the sand. Visibility is reported at 50-60 ft (15-18 m) by Evolution Underwater. Guides have used landmark line-ups rather than GPS to find the wreck. The wreck has been heavily salvaged and looted.

## Practical Information

- **Depth Range**: about 18 meters (60 ft / 10 fathoms); Diveplanit lists 17-21 m
- **Currents**: Segond Channel conditions; not documented consistently
- **Entry**: Boat dive
- **Best For**: Divers interested in a WWII destroyer wreck

## Marine Life Highlights

Evolution Underwater reports sea fans, a school of grunts and a few grouper. Diveplanit names dogface puffer, giant triggerfish, sweetlips, parrotfish, rays, six-banded and camouflaged rockcod, and large numbers of juvenile fish in enclosed spaces.
