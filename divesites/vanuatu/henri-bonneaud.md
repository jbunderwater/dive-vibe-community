## Overview

The Henri Bonneaud (also spelled Henry Bonneaud) was built in 1951 by De Hoop in Lobith, Netherlands, as River City. McFadyen and German Wikipedia give the later names as Holmbrae (1964), Paul Markson / Paulmarkson (1966) and Henri/Henry Bonneaud (1969). Both give a gross tonnage of 397 and a length of 45.85 m (McFadyen) or 45.7 m (German Wikipedia). On 19 December 1989 Kevin Green of Aquamarine Diving scuttled her near Bokissa Island, Luganville Bay, as an artificial wreck; she landed upright on her keel in 40-45 m of water. Aoredise also describes her as purposely scuttled off Bokissa Island.

McFadyen describes the wreck a few hundred metres from Bokissa's main beach. Divers drop over the starboard side and swim about 20 m off the hull for an overview, then go up the bow to the forward crew cabins, which are tight for more than two divers. Penetration continues through the forward hold, and below the bridge in the rear hold the engine room is accessible and cramped, with the large diesel engine, gauges and valves visible. Penetration requires proper training and equipment. McFadyen reports visibility of 30-45 m on his two dives and recommends a dive computer for the multi-level profile.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Bokissa Island, Espiritu Santo, Vanuatu
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 45 meters

## Marine Life

McFadyen reports gorgonians and sponges on the bow and hull, and dozens of feather stars on the propeller.
