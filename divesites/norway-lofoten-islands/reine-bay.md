## Reine Bay

Reine Bay is a curated dive area entry representing general boat diving around Reine on Moskenesøya, the southernmost inhabited island of the main Lofoten chain.

## Overview

Reine is one of Lofoten's most photographed villages, ringed by dramatic granite peaks rising directly from the fjord. The surrounding waters offer cold, clear Arctic diving in a bay sheltered by the mountainous terrain of Moskenesøya.

The best-documented dive site in the Reine area is the wreck of the MS Hadsel, a Hurtigruten-line coastal ferry (roughly 67 metres/220 feet long) that foundered in January 1958 after running aground in a strong current while en route to Bodø with passengers and mail; water flooded through a large hole in the engine room and all 20 passengers and 26 crew reached the lifeboats safely. Three independent sources (Zentacle, X-Ray Mag, and a Lofoten Planet dive-trip account) agree the wreck now lies on a sandy bottom with its top at roughly 37–40 metres and its base around 46 metres, tilted at approximately 45–50 degrees — the small variation between sources (37 vs 40 m, 45 vs 50 degrees) likely reflects measurement points rather than a real discrepancy. The wreck is described as largely intact and photogenic; we did not find a source confirming specific marine life on the wreck itself, so we don't name species here. It is a deeper Advanced-level dive and is offered as a guided excursion by Lofoten Diving from Ballstad.

The shallower bay areas around Reine also offer reef diving in kelp and rocky substrate habitat at depths up to approximately 20 metres, suitable for Intermediate divers. Water temperature is sub-10°C year-round; drysuit is required. Boat access from Reine or Ballstad.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Reine area, Moskenesøya, Lofoten Islands, Norway
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
