## MS Hamburg

MS Hamburg is a WWII wreck dive in the industrial harbour of Svolvær — one of the most historically significant wrecks in Lofoten, though sources disagree on whether it's a shore or boat dive (see Overview).

## Overview

The MS Hamburg was a German fish factory/processing ship (Fabrikschiff) operating in Svolvær harbour under German occupation in early 1941. According to the Norwegian diving federation (dykking.no), the vessel began life as the D/S Emir, launched in 1911 by the Bremer Vulcan shipyard for Deutsche Ost-Afrika Linie; this claim is only reported by one source, so it should be treated as unconfirmed rather than established fact. What multiple sources agree on: after a fire in 1938 the ship was rebuilt as a freezer/fish-processing vessel, entering service by 1939–1941 as the world's most modern fish-processing ship of its day, capable of handling roughly 175 tonnes of round fish per day (Museum Nord, dykking.no).

**Tonnage is disputed between sources.** Global Maritime History gives 9,780 tons; dykking.no and a Wikipedia-derived Tribal-class-destroyer history both cite 6,136 GRT. Both figures are reported by seemingly credible sources, so we present the range (6,136–9,780 tons) rather than picking one.

On 4 March 1941, British and Norwegian commandos raided the Lofoten Islands during Operation Claymore. HMS Tartar is credited (Global Maritime History, Wikipedia) with sinking the Hamburg along with the merchant ship Pasajes. **Sources disagree on the exact mechanism of sinking**: dykking.no notes some accounts describe artillery bombardment while others describe a boarding party using grenades and heavy weapons; a separate account (zentacle) states German crew opened the bottom vents to scuttle the ship themselves once towing it to England was judged too dangerous, and explicitly disputes the popular claim that "the British sank the ship" as propaganda. It was one of eight merchant vessels named in Wikipedia's account of the raid, which destroyed roughly 18,000 tonnes of shipping in total; several German lives were lost in the sinking.

The wreck lies on its starboard side in the harbour. **Reported depth varies by source**: zentacle and dykking.no both describe a range of roughly 5–25 metres from the shallowest structure to the seabed, while a Museum Nord summary cites a single figure of "only 18 m." We list the deepest commonly-cited figure (25 m) but note divers should expect the wreck to be workable across that whole shallow-to-25m band. The hull is largely intact — winches remain on deck, the masts are present, and cargo holds can be entered by suitably equipped divers; wreck penetration requires appropriate training and is never risk-free. A lifeboat rests on the seabed alongside (zentacle, dykking.no).

**Entry type is also disputed**: zentacle describes this as a shore dive, while dykking.no places the wreck roughly 100 metres offshore between the Skarvik shipyard and the ferry dock and recommends boat or DPV (diver propulsion scooter) access. We list this site as accessible both ways — strong swimmers may shore-enter, but boat/scooter access is recommended given the reported distance.

No source we found reports specific marine life at this wreck; divers should expect the cold-water sessile life (anemones, soft corals) and fish typical of Lofoten's inshore rock and harbour habitats, but we have no site-specific sighting reports to cite.

Water temperature is sub-10°C year-round; drysuit is essential.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Svolvær harbour, Austvågøya, Lofoten Islands, Norway
- **Entry Type**: Shore or boat entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
