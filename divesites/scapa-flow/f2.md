## F2

A WWII German escort boat paired with the YC-21 salvage barge — two wrecks close together in Gutter Sound at 15–16 metres, regularly rated one of the best non-fleet wreck diving sites in Scapa Flow.

## Overview

The F2 was built by Germaniawerft at Kiel as a Geleiteboot (escort boat), the second of a class of ten — laid down 7 August 1934, launched 2 April 1935, and commissioned 27 February 1936. She displaced 756 tonnes and measured approximately 75–80 metres in length (sources vary slightly depending on whether the pre- or post-conversion hull is measured), with oil-fired steam turbines giving her a speed of 26 knots. Her experimental high-pressure boilers proved problematic in service, making her a poor sea boat; as a result she was lengthened by four metres and converted to a torpedo recovery vessel at Wilhelmshaven in 1938–39.

Following the end of WWII, the F2 came into British hands and was moored in Gutter Sound, between Hoy and the small island of Fara, close to Lyness. She sank at her moorings in a storm on 30 December 1946. The wreck was not considered worthy of salvage until 1967–68, when she was sold for scrap to Metrec Engineering. The company used a wooden salvage barge, YC-21, in the operation; that barge foundered in a storm on 15 November 1968 while holding salvaged components from the F2, including a 20mm anti-aircraft gun. Sources give varying distances between the two wrecks — from roughly 20 metres along the connecting guide line to about 100 metres in a straight line — so the true separation is best confirmed with your skipper on the day.

The F2 herself lies on her port side (forward section) with the stern upright, with the hull broken in half aft of the bridge where salvage operations damaged the structure. Forward identifiable features include the gun, mast, and starboard anchor cable. The site is consistently cited by local operators as one of the best wreck diving sites in the Flow and serves as an ideal acclimatization dive before the deeper German fleet wrecks.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Gutter Sound, between Hoy and Fara, near Lyness, Scapa Flow, Orkney, Scotland
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 16 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 5–15 metres
- **Current**: Moderate — tidal influence in the area
- **Water Temperature**: 6–14°C (43–57°F)
- **Best Time**: May–October

## Wreck History

- **Built**: Germaniawerft, Kiel; laid down 7 August 1934, launched 2 April 1935, commissioned 27 February 1936
- **Type**: Geleiteboot (escort boat), second of class of ten
- **Displacement**: 756 tonnes
- **Length**: Approximately 75–80 metres (lengthened by 4 metres in 1938–39; sources vary)
- **Conversion**: Lengthened and converted to torpedo recovery vessel, Wilhelmshaven, 1938–39
- **Fate**: Sank at moorings in storm, 30 December 1946, Gutter Sound (between Hoy and Fara, near Lyness)
- **YC-21 barge**: Wooden salvage barge; sank 15 November 1968 during salvage of F2 components; distance from F2 reported as ~20–100 m depending on source

## Marine Life

The local dive operator's site-specific listing for the F2 and YC-21 names small shoaling fish, cod, ling, conger, and wrasse among the fish life, with scallops, queen scallops ("queenies"), crab, and the occasional lobster among the invertebrates. Broader invertebrate-encrustation claims common to the deeper fleet wrecks (anemones, dead man's fingers) are not confirmed specifically for this site by the sources consulted, so they are omitted here.

## Dive Profile

The 15–16 metre depth provides a comfortable Intermediate dive with extended no-decompression time. Begin on the F2, exploring the forward section and its identifiable features, before swimming to the YC-21 (sources give the transit as anywhere from roughly 20 to 100 metres — confirm with your skipper). The YC-21 is upright and holds salvaged F2 components including a 20mm anti-aircraft gun. A shot line or reel is recommended for the transit between wrecks. Ascend on the shot line and conduct a safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Stromness-based operators. Most Scapa Flow liveaboards and day charter operations include the F2 as part of their rotation, particularly as a first or last dive of the day. Dry suit essential. Descend directly on a shot line.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Use the F2 as a practice dive to verify dry suit buoyancy management before tackling the deeper fleet wrecks
- Camera settings, strobe placement, and buoyancy should all be tested here before the German fleet dives
- The 20mm anti-aircraft gun on the YC-21 barge is a compelling photographic subject
- Ask your skipper whether the connecting line between wrecks is in place — some operators maintain a guide line for the transit
- The F2 is worthwhile in its own right for marine life, not merely as a warm-up dive

## Safety Considerations

Dry suit proficiency is essential in Scapa Flow waters. Visibility can be poor in certain seasons — brief on navigation and buddy protocols before descending. If swimming the transit between the two wrecks, maintain buddy contact and carry a compass bearing. Hypothermia risk is real in these waters without proper thermal protection.

## Photography

The F2's shallowness makes it an excellent photography site for testing setups before the main fleet dives. The 20mm anti-aircraft gun on the YC-21 is a distinctive subject, and the shoaling fish typical of the site give a natural mid-water backdrop. The shallow depth allows more natural light than the deeper battleships on summer dives.
