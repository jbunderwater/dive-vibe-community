## Zuñiga Jetty

Zuñiga Jetty is a shallow artificial reef dive at the mouth of San Diego Bay — a rocky granite structure extending 7,500 feet from Coronado's northern tip toward Point Loma, reachable only by boat or kayak, and attracting abundant reef fish, California sea lions, and seasonal spiny lobster.

## Overview

Zuñiga Jetty (also spelled Zuniga) marks the southern boundary of the San Diego Bay entrance channel. The jetty was constructed from large granite boulders, and its ocean-facing side is the primary dive zone. The rock-to-sand interface at the base of the jetty structure draws in a classic Southern California reef fish assemblage in relatively shallow, calm water — typically 3–9 metres (10–30 feet). This is a fish-watching and lobster site rather than a dramatic wall or wreck dive; its appeal lies in accessible marine life and sea lion encounters.

**Important access correction (2026-08-28):** Earlier versions of this page described a shore entry from Coronado Beach. That was incorrect. The jetty sits at the tip of Naval Air Station North Island property, and there is no shore-based access point — divers reach it only by boat, or by kayak (paddling out after parking near the NAS North Island back gate), per spearfactor.com and a Spearboard.com diver forum thread.

**Important access restriction**: Diving inside San Diego Bay (between Ballast Point and Zuñiga Point) is illegal and divers have been ticketed. Only the ocean-facing side of the jetty is diveable. Due to constant Naval and commercial vessel traffic at San Diego Bay's entrance, a dive flag and surface tender are mandatory.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern tip of Coronado Island, at the mouth of San Diego Bay
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 10 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 15–20°C (59–68°F); 5–7mm wetsuit

## Marine Life

Sand bass and calico bass (kelp bass) are the reef fish most consistently reported here, alongside sheephead, per spearfactor.com. Halibut lie on the sandy bottom on both sides of the jetty. California spiny lobster congregate under rocks in season (October–March open season). California sea lions haul out on the outer jetty rocks and enter the water regularly — they can be territorial; do not approach or corner them.

## Dive Profile

Anchor or raft up on the ocean-facing (east) side of the jetty. The jetty slope is gradual. The most productive zone is the rock-to-sand seam in 3–9 metres, working parallel to the jetty structure. Dive on a slack-to-incoming tide for the clearest water — outgoing tide flushes murky bay water offshore. Carry a torch for examining lobster cavities and shark crevices.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry only — there is no shore access point at the jetty itself. Most charters and private boats launch from San Diego Bay or Glorietta Bay and anchor on the ocean side. Kayak divers park near the Naval Air Station North Island back gate, carry across the sand, and paddle out to the jetty; a dive flag on the kayak is required throughout.

## Safety Considerations

This site sits at the entrance to one of the US Navy's busiest ports. Boat, submarine, and Naval vessel traffic is constant and unpredictable. Fly a dive flag and have a dedicated surface tender at all times. Tidal currents at the bay entrance can be significant — stick to the slack window (±1 hour around slack tide). Sea lions can be aggressive if cornered — give them wide berth. Do not dive inside the bay (illegal between Ballast Point and Zuñiga Point).
