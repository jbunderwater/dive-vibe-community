# Viking Rock

A helmet-shaped rock at the edge of Wafer Bay with a stepped wall, powerful currents, and Galapagos sharks more reliably seen here than almost anywhere else at Cocos

## Overview
Viking Rock is a small helmet-shaped rock formation at the northern limit of Wafer Bay on Cocos Island. Below the surface, the rock drops away as a vertical wall with large stepped, staircase-like ledges cutting into the wall face, bottoming out around 39-40 meters. The site is known for powerful currents that sweep around the exposed rock, making it one of the more demanding dives at Cocos. Hammerhead sharks, whitetip reef sharks, and eagle rays are the most consistently reported species; Galapagos sharks are also seen here more often than at most other Cocos sites, and tiger sharks are occasionally encountered, though less reliably than at Manuelita or Dirty Rock. The shallower areas between 10 and 20 meters offer the best overall diving, with whitetip reef sharks, rays, and pufferfish among the volcanic rock formations.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Northern limit of Wafer Bay, Cocos Island, Costa Rica
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-25 meters
- **Current**: Strong to very strong
- **Best Time**: June to December for peak pelagic activity; best attempted in calmer current windows

## Marine Life
Hammerhead sharks, whitetip reef sharks, and eagle rays are the most consistently reported species at Viking Rock, along with Galapagos sharks, which sources note are more common here than at most other Cocos sites. Marble rays and pufferfish occupy the shallower zone at 10-20 meters, resting in wall recesses among the volcanic rock formations. Tiger sharks are occasionally seen along the deeper wall and stepped ledges, but sightings are described as unreliable compared to Manuelita or Dirty Rock — treat a tiger shark encounter here as a bonus, not an expectation. The stepped ledges at depth provide shelter for larger species and create natural observation points where divers can tuck in behind the rock to watch passing pelagics.

## Dive Profile
The best diving is concentrated in the 10-20 meter range along the wall, where the stepped ledges and volcanic rock formations hold the most life. Deeper excursions along the wall toward its 39-40 meter base bring the best chance of Galapagos shark and hammerhead encounters but consume air rapidly, especially when fighting current. The helmet shape of the rock means current splits around it, creating eddies on the lee side where divers can shelter. Work the sheltered side first, then drift along the current-exposed face if conditions allow.

## Entry and Exit
Negative entry from the panga directly onto the descent line. The exposed position at the bay entrance means surface conditions are often rougher than at sheltered Chatham Bay sites. Descent must be immediate. Exit requires ascending along the wall to 5 meters for a safety stop, deploying an SMB for panga pickup. In strong current, divers may end the dive well away from the entry point.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Focus your dive in the 10-20 meter range where the wall features and marine life are richest
- Use the stepped ledges on the lee side as shelter from current while scanning the blue for Galapagos sharks and the occasional tiger shark
- Save this site for a day when you are comfortable with Cocos current conditions — it is not a good first-day dive
- Reef hooks are useful for holding position on the wall in strong current
- Nitrox is recommended if planning deeper excursions along the wall

## Safety Considerations
Viking Rock has some of the most powerful currents at Cocos Island. The exposed position at the edge of Wafer Bay channels current directly over and around the rock. Getting swept off the wall into open water is a real risk — maintain contact with the rock structure at all times. The wall's ~40-meter base sits at the edge of recreational limits, and the combination of current, depth, and reduced visibility at depth demands strict discipline. This site is strictly for experienced current divers with excellent buoyancy control. Carry an SMB, whistle, and signaling mirror.

## Photography
The vertical wall provides dramatic depth perspective for wide-angle compositions. Galapagos sharks and hammerheads on the deeper ledges are the prize shots and require fast reactions and comfort at depth in current; a tiger shark sighting is a bonus rather than a sure thing. The 10-20 meter zone offers better light for ambient shooting of whitetip sharks and eagle rays against the wall. Strobes are essential for wall detail at any depth. Secure all camera equipment firmly for the rough entry and potential drift exit.
