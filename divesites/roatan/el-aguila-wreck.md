# El Aguila Wreck

*The El Aguila (Spanish for "The Eagle") is a cargo vessel deliberately scuttled near Sandy Bay, on the northwest coast of Roatán, in 1997 to serve as an artificial reef and dive attraction.*

## Overview

The El Aguila (Spanish for "The Eagle") is a cargo vessel deliberately scuttled near Sandy Bay, on the northwest coast of Roatán, in 1997 to serve as an artificial reef and dive attraction. Sources disagree on the vessel's length: PADI lists it as a 60-meter (200-foot) cargo ship, while Splash Inn Dive Resort, Anthony's Key Resort and DreamWrecks describe it as 75 meters (230 feet). The vessel was purchased and purposefully sunk by Anthony's Key Resort. Hurricane Mitch in 1998 broke the wreck into three distinct pieces, tilting the structure at a 45-degree angle. The wreck rests on sand at approximately 33 meters (110 feet) per most operator sources, with the mast extending upward to a shallower point (PADI separately notes a shallow section around 9 meters). After nearly three decades submerged, El Aguila is heavily encrusted with corals and sponges and is consistently rated among the top wreck dives in Honduras. No source consulted provides the vessel's original build date or tonnage, so neither is stated here.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Roatán, Bay Islands, Honduras
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters

## Marine Life

Garden eels, green moray eels, groupers, parrotfish, stingrays, snappers, barracuda, arrow crabs and frogfish are reported at the wreck by Roatan Clearwater Adventures. Splash Inn and Anthony's Key add blennies and nudibranchs on the encrusted hull. PADI notes sharks, turtles and dolphins as sightings reported in the surrounding area (not confirmed as wreck-specific).
