# Butcher Bank

*A sandy plateau and coral platform on Roatán's south side, best known among local divers as a macro and critter-spotting site rather than a pelagic destination.*

## Overview

Butcher Bank is a dive site on Roatán's south side featuring a shallow sandy plateau around 12 meters that steps down to a deeper sandy area, with a coral platform leading out toward a wall. Depths reported by local operators run to roughly 34–37 meters (111–120 feet) on the deeper sections. Visibility is typically excellent at 25–27 meters. Rather than being a pelagic or shark site, Butcher Bank is best known locally as a macro and critter destination — the shallow sand is described by operators as "a scuba nerd's paradise" for small, camouflaged finds.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South coast of Roatán, Bay Islands, Honduras
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 37 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 25–27 meters (80–90 feet)
- **Current**: Variable
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

The shallow sand supports pipefish, flounder, soles, and tube-dwelling anemones — the site's main draw for macro and critter photographers. Southern stingrays and barracuda are reported over the sand and along the coral platform. Turtle sightings are likely on the coral platform toward the wall.

*Note: An earlier version of this page described Butcher Bank as a shark and pelagic site, naming Caribbean reef sharks, whale sharks, hammerhead sharks, cubera snapper, and greater amberjack. None of these claims could be confirmed against dive-operator or dive-database sources for this specific site — those sources instead describe Butcher Bank as a sandy macro/critter site — and the shark and large-pelagic claims have been removed.*

## Dive Profile

Descend to the shallow sandy plateau at around 12 meters and work slowly across the sand, checking for camouflaged critters. Continue toward the deeper sand and the coral platform, where depths reach 34–37 meters near the wall. Take your time in the shallow sand — this is where the site's macro highlights (pipefish, flounder, soles, tube anemones) are found. Return to the shallower plateau before ascending and perform the safety stop there.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride, with a mooring or drop at the site. Standard boat operations; conditions can vary with weather.

## Tips

- Move slowly and look closely in the sand — this is a macro/critter site, not a site to expect large pelagics
- Look for southern stingrays partially buried in the sand
- Check the coral platform toward the wall for resting turtles
- Bring a macro lens if underwater photography is a priority for this dive
- Carry an SMB as standard practice for the boat pickup

## Safety

Moderate-depth dive with variable current. Monitor depth as the sand steps down toward the coral platform and wall. Maintain good buoyancy over the sand to avoid disturbing the bottom. Always carry and deploy an SMB.

## Photography

Butcher Bank rewards a macro approach. Pipefish, flounder, soles, and tube-dwelling anemones in the sand are the signature subjects. Southern stingrays partially buried in sand also photograph well. The excellent visibility (25–27 meters) supports clean backgrounds for macro work.
