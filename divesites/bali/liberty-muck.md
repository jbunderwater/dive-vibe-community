# Liberty Muck

A black sand muck dive adjacent to the famous USAT Liberty wreck in Tulamben, offering an entirely different experience from the wreck itself with outstanding critter diversity on the volcanic sand.

## Overview

Liberty Muck is the black sand area adjacent to the USAT Liberty wreck at Tulamben on Bali's northeast coast, documented in a detailed trip report by Australian diver Michael McFadyen (GPS 8°16'30.8"S 115°35'34.1"E). While most divers visiting Tulamben head straight for the Liberty wreck, this neighbouring muck site offers exceptional critter diving on the volcanic sand bottom. Entry is over a rocky beach with a short surface swim before descending; the terrain consists of sand ridges running perpendicular to the shore with gullies between them, typically dived to 24-28 meters with a zigzag route between ridges and gullies. The dark sand provides habitat for an impressive array of small, unusual marine life that thrives independently of the wreck.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tulamben, East Bali, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 28 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: ~20-25 meters (per source trip report)
- **Water Temperature**: 27-29°C
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season)

## Marine Life

Species reported at this specific site by the source trip report: mimic octopus, ornate ghost pipefish, various crab species, shrimp, nudibranchs, and garden eels (seen in the shallower 6-8 meter zone during quieter conditions). (An earlier version of this description named orangutan crabs and described mimic octopus imitating specific species -- those specific claims were not supported by the source and have been removed.)

## Dive Profile

Enter from shore and descend onto the black sand slope away from the Liberty wreck. The bottom is characterized by sand ridges running perpendicular to the shoreline. Work along these ridges, scanning carefully for critters on and between them. The best critter hunting is typically in the 12-28 meter range. Move slowly and methodically -- the most rewarding subjects are well camouflaged against the dark sand.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the beach at Tulamben, near the USAT Liberty wreck entry point. Walk into the water carrying fins and gear, and put fins on once waist-deep. Head away from the wreck toward the muck area. Exit at the same beach.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This dive is best done with a local guide who knows where the current critter residents are hiding
- Move slowly and keep your eyes on the sand -- the best subjects are often the hardest to spot
- Consider doing this as an alternative to a second Liberty wreck dive for variety
- Night dives on the muck can be exceptional, with different species active after dark

## Safety Considerations

The entry over the rocky beach requires care, especially when carrying camera equipment. Maintain careful buoyancy control over the sand to avoid silt disturbance. The maximum depth of 28 meters limits bottom time on air, so plan gas management accordingly. The site is generally sheltered with mild currents.

## Photography

Liberty Muck is outstanding for macro photography. The black volcanic sand creates clean, dark backgrounds that highlight small, colourful subjects. Mimic octopus, ghost pipefish, and nudibranchs are the primary photographic targets. A macro lens (60mm or 100mm) and a focus light are essential. The garden eel colonies also make excellent subjects if approached slowly and patiently.
