## Underwater Cave

A safe cavern dive on Menjangan Island's western wall, featuring a beautiful sandy-bottomed cave at 14 m lit by ambient light from above.

## Overview

Underwater Cave is located on the western side of Menjangan Island, where the coral wall is cut through with gullies and channels. Local operators place the cave entrance at 10–14 metres (sources vary slightly on the exact figure), where a cavern opens with a sandy floor and light filtering down from above — an easy, non-penetration cave that divers of all levels can safely explore. Note: naming for Menjangan's western/southern cave features is inconsistent across operators — some sources describe a very similar sandy, light-filled cave around 12–14 m under the names "Cave Point" or "Shark Point" rather than "Underwater Cave," so there may be overlap or confusion between these labels. The cavern walls host electric clams that flash when lit by torchlight (Menjangan Island Trip, Bali Aqua). Outside the cave, moray eels, barracuda, and schools of trevally and grouper have been reported along this stretch of wall, and white-tip reef sharks are occasionally seen resting inside the cave itself during certain seasons (Bali Aqua). Currents are reported as mild to low here by two independent sources, making it one of Menjangan's more relaxed diving environments.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–30 m typical; Menjangan Island Trip reports up to 50 m in good conditions
- **Current**: Generally mild to low (consistent across sources)
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

Electric clams line the cave walls and flash in torchlight — confirmed at this site by two independent sources (Menjangan Island Trip, Bali Aqua). Outside the cave, moray eels, barracuda, and schools of trevally and grouper have been reported along this section of wall, and white-tip reef sharks are occasionally seen resting inside the cave during certain seasons (Bali Aqua). Earlier text for this site named frogfish, anglerfish, pygmy seahorses, and tuna — no source found during this audit ties those species specifically to Underwater Cave, so they have been removed rather than presented as confirmed sightings here. Bali/Menjangan's broader reef fauna likely includes some of these nearby, but that cannot be confirmed as site-specific with current sources.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the wall from the boat and follow the reef to the cave entrance at approximately 14 m. Spend time in the cavern without penetrating beyond the light zone. Continue along the wall exploring channels and gullies between 15–25 m. Ascend gradually and complete a 3-minute safety stop at 5 m. Total dive time is typically 50–60 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Access by jukung boat from Labuhan Lalang, West Bali. Entry via back roll or giant stride. Currents on this side of Menjangan are generally mild, though conditions can still vary day to day — consult your dive guide before entry. Deploy SMB for ascent and surface pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a torch to see the cave walls clearly and to spot electric clams
- Do not penetrate beyond natural daylight — the cavern is safe as a daylight dive only
- Look for moray eels, barracuda, and grouper along the wall outside the cave
- Combine with Coral Garden or Mangrove Point for a full Menjangan day
- Ask your guide whether white-tip reef sharks have been resting in the cave recently — sightings are seasonal

## Safety Considerations

Do not penetrate the cave beyond daylight illumination — turn around when light from outside becomes insufficient. Be aware of swell surges near the cave entrance that can move divers unexpectedly. Stay neutrally buoyant to avoid disturbing the sandy floor and reducing visibility. Always dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The cave interior offers unique shots with light streaming through the entrance creating a natural halo effect. Electric clams provide dramatic close-up subjects in the dim cave walls. Outside, wide-angle shots of the gully formations and soft coral walls work well with strobe lighting.
