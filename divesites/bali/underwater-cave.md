## Underwater Cave

A safe cavern dive on Menjangan Island's northern side, featuring a sandy-bottomed cave with an entrance reported between roughly 10–15 m, lit by ambient light from above.

## Overview

Underwater Cave is located on the northern part of Menjangan Island — earlier drafts of this page placed it on the "western side," but a directly-verified operator source specifically locates it on the island's north side, and this has been corrected. Sources differ on the exact cave entrance depth, citing figures from 10 m to 15 m (one source describes a roughly 3 m × 4 m cavity at 10 m; another cites a shallower 6 m top with the cave entrance around 10–15 m); rather than pick one figure, this description treats it as an entrance in the 10–15 m range. The cave has a sandy floor with light filtering down from above — an easy, non-penetration cave that divers of all levels can safely explore. The walls are encrusted with sponges and small invertebrates, including electric ("fire") clams that flash in the dim light. Currents are generally mild here, making it one of Menjangan's more relaxed diving environments.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–25 m
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

The cave interior hosts electric ("fire") clams, colorful anthias, and damselfish adapted to low-light conditions, and juvenile whitetip reef sharks have been specifically reported sheltering in or near the cave — a more site-specific finding than the generic "reef sharks occasionally offshore" claim in earlier drafts of this page, which has been removed for lack of a supporting source. Pygmy seahorses have also been reported for this site. Claims of grouper, frogfish, and anglerfish at Underwater Cave, and of trevally/tuna in the open water beyond it, could not be confirmed by any site-specific source and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the wall from the boat and follow the reef to the cave entrance, reported at approximately 10–15 m depending on the source. Spend time in the cavern without penetrating beyond the light zone. Continue along the wall exploring channels and gullies between 15–25 m. Ascend gradually and complete a 3-minute safety stop at 5 m. Total dive time is typically 50–60 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Access by jukung boat from Labuhan Lalang, West Bali. Entry via back roll or giant stride. This side of Menjangan can have variable current; consult your dive guide for conditions on the day. Deploy SMB for ascent and surface pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a torch to see the cave walls clearly and to spot electric clams
- Do not penetrate beyond natural daylight — the cavern is safe as a daylight dive only
- Watch the cave entrance and recesses for juvenile whitetip reef sharks and colorful anthias
- Combine with Coral Garden or Mangrove Point for a full Menjangan day
- Check the sea fan colonies near the cave entrance for pygmy seahorses

## Safety Considerations

Do not penetrate the cave beyond daylight illumination — turn around when light from outside becomes insufficient. Be aware of swell surges near the cave entrance that can move divers unexpectedly. Stay neutrally buoyant to avoid disturbing the sandy floor and reducing visibility. Always dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The cave interior offers unique shots with light streaming through the entrance creating a natural halo effect. Electric clams provide dramatic close-up subjects in the dim cave walls. Outside, wide-angle shots of the gully formations and soft coral walls work well with strobe lighting.
