## Post I Jetty

A pleasant sandy slope in front of Menjangan Island's ranger station — an often-overlooked site that rewards patient divers with critters under the jetty.

## Overview

Post I Jetty sits directly in front of the West Bali National Park ranger station (Pos I) on Menjangan Island and is a gentle sandy slope with a good variety of hard and soft corals. Site-specific sources give somewhat different depth figures — one local operator describes the slope as 16–18 m before it continues into deeper water, while others describe the same slope reaching roughly 30 m — so treat 20–30 m as the working range rather than a precise number. While it lacks the dramatic vertical walls of other Menjangan sites, the shallow area beneath the jetty is genuinely special for critter hunters — ornate ghost pipefish and pipefish inhabit the structures and rubble. Because Post I sits close to the strait between Bali and Java, operators also report occasional pelagic passersby here, though these are not everyday sightings. The site works well as a final dive of the day, providing a relaxed, lower-intensity experience to complement the deeper wall dives at Pos 2 or Bat Cave earlier in the day.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 m
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

The jetty pilings host ornate ghost pipefish and pipefish — a well-documented specialty of this exact site across multiple dive operators. Blue-spotted stingrays are reported on the sandy slope. Because Post I lies near the strait between Bali and Java, one operator reports occasional sightings of reef sharks, turtles, and large fish schools passing through, though these are described as opportunistic rather than guaranteed. Beyond the confirmed ghost pipefish/pipefish critters, treat other regional Menjangan species (lionfish, batfish, cuttlefish, nudibranchs) as possible but not specifically confirmed for this site.

## Dive Profile

Descend under the jetty and spend time searching the pilings and rubble for ghost pipefish and pipefish before moving onto the sandy slope. Dive the slope between 8–20 m exploring coral heads and sandy patches. The dive is typically 45–55 minutes at a comfortable pace. Ascend slowly and complete a 3-minute safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Jukung boat from Labuhan Lalang. Entry at the jetty area. Currents are minimal at this site. Surface marker buoy recommended for boat pickup in open water.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Check jetty pilings carefully for ghost pipefish — they are masters of camouflage
- Use this as the third dive of the day when bottom time budgets are tighter
- The site is excellent for Open Water training dives due to its calm, predictable conditions
- Carry a small torch for illuminating crevices beneath the jetty
- Ask your guide about current conditions near the strait — occasional pelagic sightings have been reported here

## Safety Considerations

Currents are minimal but be aware of boat traffic around the jetty. Maintain good buoyancy near the jetty structure to avoid entanglement. Stingrays on the sandy bottom may be partially buried — shuffle feet if wading near shore.

## Photography

The jetty creates an interesting structural dive shot opportunity. Macro photographers will enjoy the ghost pipefish and pipefish on the pilings — this is one of the more reliable spots on Menjangan for them. Wide-angle shots of the jetty pilings themselves also work well in the clear water.
