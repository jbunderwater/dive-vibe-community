## Post I Jetty

A pleasant sandy slope in front of Menjangan Island's ranger station — an often-overlooked site that rewards patient divers with critters under the jetty.

## Overview

Post I (Pos I) sits directly in front of the West Bali National Park ranger station on Menjangan Island — one of only three points on the island where boats can put divers/snorkellers ashore. Sources describe it as a gentle sandy slope, sloping from roughly 16–18 m into deeper water, with coral running toward the mangroves and plenty of small fish. It is generally regarded by operators as more of a break/snorkelling and beginner-training stop than Menjangan's premier dive, in contrast to the dramatic walls at Pos II or Bat Cave. The shallow water directly beneath the jetty is notable among Menjangan dive shops for ornate ghost pipefish, and schools of batfish are reported near the drop-off. The site works well as a relaxed final dive of the day after deeper wall dives earlier on.

*Note: sources on exact maximum depth vary — one Menjangan operator (Sea Rovers) describes a 16–18 m sandy slope, while this destination's data lists a 20 m maximum depth; treat depth figures as approximate.*

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 m
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

Ornate ghost pipefish are specifically reported in the shallow water beneath the jetty (Menjangan Island Trip). Schools of batfish gather near the drop-off (Bali Fun Diving). Small reef fish are noted along the coral running toward the mangroves (Sea Rovers). Beyond these confirmed sightings, no site-specific source was found for other species — general Menjangan reef fauna likely applies, but is not attributed here without site-specific confirmation.

## Dive Profile

Descend under the jetty and spend time searching the pilings and rubble for cryptic critters before moving onto the sandy slope. Dive the slope between 8–20 m exploring coral heads and sandy patches. The dive is typically 45–55 minutes at a comfortable pace. Ascend slowly and complete a 3-minute safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Jukung boat from Labuhan Lalang. Entry at the jetty area. Currents are minimal at this site. Surface marker buoy recommended for boat pickup in open water.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Check jetty pilings carefully for ghost pipefish — they are masters of camouflage
- Use this as a relaxed break or third dive of the day when bottom time budgets are tighter
- The site's calm, shallow conditions suit Open Water training and snorkelling
- Post I is also a landing point for the ranger station and nearby Klenting Sari temples if surfacing for a break

## Safety Considerations

Currents are reported as slight to moderate. Be aware of boat traffic around the jetty and ranger station landing. Maintain good buoyancy near the jetty structure to avoid entanglement.

## Photography

The jetty creates an interesting structural dive shot opportunity. Macro photographers will enjoy the ghost pipefish on the pilings. No further site-specific photography subjects are confirmed for this location.
