## Bat Cave

Arguably the finest dive at Menjangan Island — a dramatic wall with cave entrances at 9–12 m sheltering diverse reef life.

## Overview

Bat Cave Point sits on the eastern wall of Menjangan Island, inside West Bali National Park, and is frequently cited by local operators as one of the island's best dives. Small bats inhabit the caves at the waterline, giving the site its name; the underwater cave crevasses begin at around 9–12 metres and are a haven for invertebrates and critters. The wall drops vertically, with sources reporting maximum depths anywhere from 35 to 45 metres, and is draped in gorgonian sea fans, whip corals, and large sponges. Current reports conflict: some operators describe strong, sometimes head-on currents that make for a fast drift (Bali Fun Diving), while others describe generally mild to gentle conditions (Mimpi Resort, a ScubaBoard trip report) — expect variation by tide and season. The site is often dived as the continuation of a drift from Pos 2, moving along the wall toward the cave entrances.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–30 m, with PADI reporting 50 m+ possible October–November
- **Current**: Reports conflict — described as strong by some operators and as mild/gentle by others; treat as variable and follow your dive guide's briefing
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season, best visibility)

## Marine Life

Multiple dive operator sources place schools of tuna, jacks, pompano, and fusiliers along the wall, with white-tip and black-tip reef sharks, big-eye and bluefin trevally, and redtooth triggerfish also reported here (Dive Concepts, Bali Fun Diving, Bali Aqua). Turtles, octopus, moray eels, and eagle rays have been recorded at the site (Dive Concepts, Bali Aqua). The cave crevasses and overhangs are described as a macro habitat for nudibranchs, shrimp, and pygmy seahorses (Karang Divers). Two independent sources — Dive Concepts and a ScubaBoard trip report — mention occasional whale shark sightings while drifting past Bat Cave; this is not a typical Menjangan sighting and should be treated as a rare possibility rather than an expected encounter (one other operator source, Karang Divers, explicitly ties Menjangan-wide whale shark sightings to October–November generally rather than to Bat Cave specifically).

## Dive Profile

Begin the dive at Pos 2 and drift southwest along the wall to Bat Cave Point. The wall starts at 3 m and plunges beyond recreational limits; most divers stay between 10–25 m where coral density and fish life are greatest. The cave entrances at 9–12 m are the highlight — explore them safely without penetrating beyond the light zone. Ascend slowly along the wall and conduct a 3-minute safety stop at 5 m before surfacing.

## Entry and Exit

Access by traditional jukung boat from Labuhan Lalang jetty in West Bali National Park. Entry via giant stride or back roll. Currents can run strong; follow the dive briefing on entry timing relative to the current direction. Deploy an SMB during ascent for boat pickup in open water.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Dive this site as a continuation from Pos 2 for a long, memorable drift along the wall
- Bring a torch to illuminate the cave recesses and reveal cryptic critters — nudibranchs, shrimp, and pygmy seahorses hide in the overhangs
- Ask your guide about current conditions before the dive, since reports on current strength vary by operator and tide
- Nitrox is available from most Menjangan operators and extends bottom time
- Visit in the morning for the best light angles on the wall and clearest water

## Safety Considerations

Currents can be strong and unpredictable, particularly on incoming tides. Do not penetrate the cave beyond natural light. Always dive with a buddy and carry an SMB. Down-currents are possible near the deep wall sections — maintain positive buoyancy and do not chase depth. Dive within your certification limits.

## Photography

The cave entrances provide dramatic silhouette shots with ambient light streaming from above. Wide-angle lenses suit the wall sections with large gorgonians, while macro photographers will find electric clams, nudibranchs, and pygmy seahorses inside the cave crevasses. Morning dives produce the best water column colour.
