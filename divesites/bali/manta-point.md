## Manta Point

Nusa Penida's most celebrated manta ray cleaning station — nearly year-round encounters with reef manta rays on a shallow coral plateau.

## Overview

Manta Point is located on the southwestern coast of Nusa Penida and is the most reliable place in Bali to observe reef manta rays (Manta alfredi) in their natural environment — no oceanic mantas are reported here. The site centres on shallow coral cleaning stations where manta rays circle continuously while small cleaner wrasses remove parasites from their skin. Most encounters happen at roughly 5–15 m (sources differ on the exact depth — one operator describes the station as closer to 5 m in murky water, others put it nearer 10–15 m), as the mantas rise to the cleaning stations, making this accessible even to snorkellers above. A separate rock/wall formation nearby (sometimes referenced as Batu Sepatu) extends to 30–40 m and is occasionally visited by advanced divers, but that is not the shallow cleaning-station experience most divers come for. Reported manta encounter rates vary significantly by source: some operators cite roughly 90–95% under good conditions, while others describe it more conservatively as better than 50% year-round; ScubaBoard divers note that swell can make the site inaccessible for boats on the order of 10 days a month.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Southwest coast of Nusa Penida, Bali region
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 m
- **Current**: Variable; can be strong on the exposed southern coast
- **Best Time**: April to October for most stable conditions

## Marine Life

Reef manta rays with wingspans up to 5 m are the headline attraction. Nusa Penida has one of the highest recorded rates of the melanistic (all-black) colour morph anywhere in the world — reportedly around 10% of the local population — and melanistic mantas are specifically documented at Manta Point, including a regularly-sighted individual nicknamed "Batman" for a bat-shaped marking on its underside. Blue-spotted stingrays rest on the sandy seabed, bamboo/nurse sharks cruise the reef base, turtles and eagle rays pass through, and nudibranchs, Zanzibar shrimps, and porcelain crabs inhabit the coral structure. Mola mola (oceanic sunfish) are occasionally seen during the July–October season. Whale shark encounters are reported by multiple operators at this specific site, but consistently described as extremely rare, isolated sightings rather than a predictable occurrence — this is not a whale shark site and one should not expect to see one.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the sandy seabed at around 12–15 m and approach the cleaning station slowly from a distance. Kneel or hover neutrally in the sand, remaining still while the mantas circle. Avoid rising above the mantas or chasing them. Follow the sandy plateau to the second cleaning station before ascending via the coral reef. Safety stop at 5 m for 3 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Boat only — no shore access exists. Depart from Toyapakeh (Nusa Penida) or Sanur (Bali). The boat journey can be rough crossing the Badung Strait; motion sickness medication is recommended for susceptible divers. Entry via back roll or giant stride.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Approach cleaning stations slowly and stay low — do not shadow the mantas from above
- Wear a 5–7 mm wetsuit; water temperature can drop to 15–18°C on the exposed southern side
- Manta encounters are not guaranteed; operators will advise on alternative sites if conditions prevent access
- Stay at Nusa Penida for 2–3 days to maximise opportunities and explore other sites
- Early morning departures experience less boat traffic at the cleaning stations

## Safety Considerations

The site is fully exposed to Indian Ocean swell, and surge can be powerful. Strong currents are possible. Only dive in good conditions and follow your guide's briefing — operators cancel trips to this site when weather conditions are unsafe. A 5–7 mm wetsuit is essential due to cold water upwellings. Always carry an SMB.

## Photography

Manta rays at close range on the cleaning station provide world-class wide-angle subjects. Use a rectilinear wide-angle lens for belly shots as they circle overhead. Avoid using flash on the mantas — natural light combined with sunbursts from above works beautifully at 10–15 m depth.
