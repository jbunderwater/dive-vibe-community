## Drop-off

Tulamben's dramatic volcanic wall dive — a shore-entry wall formed by a Mount Agung lava flow, a short walk from the famous USAT Liberty wreck (not part of it).

## Overview

The Tulamben Drop-off, also called the Tulamben Wall, is a vertical wall formed by lava from one of Mount Agung's eruptions (multiple sources tie the terrain to Mount Agung's 1963 eruption). The site sits at the opposite end of Tulamben Bay from the USAT Liberty wreck — several sources describe it as roughly a 10-15 minute walk away, not immediately adjacent — so it is a distinct wall dive in its own right rather than an extension of the wreck. A short surface swim from the pebble beach reaches a steep sandy slope that develops into a vertical wall, with the top around 3-5 m and the face reported by multiple independent sources to continue past 60-70 m before easing back into a slope. The wall and the sandy slope leading to it are heavily encrusted with sponges, gorgonian fans, black coral, and crinoids, and are consistently described across sources as a strong macro site precisely because it draws fewer visitors than the adjacent wreck.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tulamben, east Bali — opposite end of Tulamben Bay from the USAT Liberty wreck, roughly a 10-15 minute walk away
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry, pebble beach with a short surface swim to the wall
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner-accessible in the shallows, with deeper, more demanding terrain available; sources broadly describe it as suitable for a range of levels given adjustable depth
- **Maximum Depth**: Data field lists 30 m (a sensible recreational-limit cutoff); independent sources describe the wall face itself continuing to 60-70 m or more, so treat 30 m as the practical recreational depth rather than the full extent of the wall
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-20 m is most commonly cited, best in the morning before plankton builds up; some sources give a wider 15-30 m range
- **Current**: Multiple sources agree current is rare here, even mild current, likely due to the site's position at the sheltered end of the bay
- **Best Time**: Year-round; mornings favored for visibility

## Marine Life

Multiple independent operator sources (Aquamarine Diving, Let's Dive Tulamben, Ecodive Bali) confirm white-tip reef sharks, Napoleon wrasse, and large moray eels along the wall. Ghost pipefish and frogfish are repeatedly and independently reported both on the sandy slope leading to the wall and among the crinoids on the wall face. Leaf scorpionfish, nudibranchs, octopus, mantis shrimp, and various crab and shrimp species are commonly cited macro subjects. Triggerfish (species not consistently specified — "clown triggerfish" specifically is not confirmed by any source found and has been removed) and schooling fusiliers/trevally are present along the wall. Pelagic visitors — manta rays, mola mola, dogtooth tuna, and (per one operator, described as a rare event) whale sharks — are reported as occasional passersby in the blue water beyond the wall by at least two independent sources (Aquamarine Diving, Ecodive Bali); these should be read as occasional bonus sightings, not routine ones.

## Dive Profile

Enter from the pebble beach and make a short surface swim to the wall edge. The upper wall and shallow zone (roughly 3-5 m) are productive for a warm-up. The wall itself runs from about 15 m down past 60 m per several sources, so divers wanting to stay within recreational limits should plan to turn around well above the true base of the wall. The sandy slope at the base of the wall and leading up to it is a well-documented macro zone in its own right. Return along the wall at a shallower depth for the safety stop before swimming back to shore.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The sandy slope leading to the wall is repeatedly cited by local operators as an underrated macro spot — look for ghost pipefish, frogfish, and nudibranchs
- The site is described by operators as quieter than the adjacent USAT Liberty wreck, making it a good alternative when the wreck is crowded
- Early morning dives offer the best visibility, before plankton builds up in the water column
- Watch your depth gauge carefully — the wall continues well past recreational limits, and it is easy to descend deeper than planned while following it

## Safety Considerations

The shallow wall top makes the upper section accessible to less experienced divers, but depth increases rapidly and multiple sources describe the wall face continuing past 60 m — monitor your depth gauge closely and do not follow the wall past your training and dive-plan limits. Shore entry over loose pebbles can be slippery; water shoes or booties are recommended. Current is generally rare here per multiple sources, but confirm conditions with your guide before entry.
