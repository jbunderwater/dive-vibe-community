# Kubu Wreck

A Dutch-built patrol vessel (the Boga) intentionally sunk in 2012 as an artificial reef off Kubu village, sitting at 18–40 meters depth with a VW car, Buddha statues, and decorative props on deck.

## Overview

The Kubu Wreck (officially named the Boga) is a former Dutch-built patrol/cargo-patrol vessel used by the Indonesian government, intentionally scuttled off Kubu village on Bali's northeast coast to create an artificial reef. Multiple operator sources place the sinking in September 2012. The vessel's original build year and shipyard are not confirmed by any source found — claims of a specific 1950s construction date could not be corroborated by a second independent source, so they are omitted here.

Sources disagree meaningfully on the ship's length, ranging from approximately 40 metres (Bali Dive Cove, Evolution Diver) to 45 metres (Let's Dive Tulamben) to 50 metres (No Fear Diving, confirmed again this pass) — this description uses the commonly-cited "approximately 40–45 metres" range rather than picking a single figure. One source (Evolution Diver) reports a tonnage of roughly 150 tonnes; this figure is not independently corroborated elsewhere, so it should be treated as provisional. The wreck was reportedly sunk to a maximum depth of around 32 metres, but multiple operators (Ecodive Bali, Let's Dive Tulamben) note it has settled progressively deeper over time; current depth is commonly reported as bow ~18 metres to stern 38–40+ metres, with some recent listings (No Fear Diving) citing depths up to 45 metres. There is also a conflicting minority account (Pebble and Fins) describing the vessel as a WWII-era fishing trawler repurposed by the Dutch Navy, rather than a 1950s-era Indonesian government patrol/cargo vessel; this account is a single, less-corroborated source, while the patrol/cargo-vessel narrative is reported consistently across the majority of dive-operator sources, so it is treated as the more reliable account here, with the discrepancy flagged rather than silently resolved. No Wikipedia entry exists for this vessel — it is a lesser-known artificial-reef wreck, so this description relies on cross-checked dive-operator and maritime-tourism sources instead. The deck has been decorated with props — a (non-original) wooden steering wheel, Buddha statues, scuba tanks, and a Volkswagen car — giving the wreck a distinctive, theatrical character. The site is located near Kubu Reef, a short distance along the same stretch of coast; note that underwater bicycles/motorbikes reported in this area by at least one source (Bali AQua) appear to belong to the Kubu Reef site rather than to the wreck itself, and are not confirmed as being on the wreck.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Kubu Village, East Bali, Indonesia (400 m SE of Kubu Reef)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-25 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 27-29°C
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season)

## Marine Life

Gorgonian fans and soft corals have colonised the wreck structure in the years since sinking. Confirmed species at the site include moray eels, barracuda, batfish, lionfish, nudibranchs, octopuses, and turtles. Reef fish — surgeonfish, fusiliers, angelfish, and damselfish — shelter around the structure. The wreck prop items (Volkswagen car, Buddha statues) have become focal points for marine life and unusual photographic subjects. The surrounding sand supports the same critter diversity found along the broader Tulamben–Kubu coast.

## Dive Profile

Enter from shore and descend to the wreck. The bow at approximately 18 metres is the shallowest point and a good place to begin exploration. Work aft along the deck, observing the decorative props — Buddha statues, Volkswagen car, wooden steering wheel, and scuba tanks placed along the deck. The stern descends to 38–40 metres, so monitor depth and bottom time carefully when exploring the aft sections. Penetration of the wreck is possible but requires appropriate training, a clear exit plan, and redundant lights — never enter an overhead environment without proper wreck penetration training and equipment. Plan gas management carefully given the depth range.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry at Kubu village, the same access point used for Kubu Reef. Local porters can assist with carrying equipment from the road to the beach entry point. Enter over the rocky shoreline and navigate to the wreck site. Exit at the same location.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Start at the bow (~18 m) and work deeper to manage bottom time efficiently
- Bring a torch to illuminate the wreck interior and highlight the prop decorations
- Nitrox extends bottom time significantly at the 18–25 metre deck level
- The prop items — VW car, Buddha statues — make for unusual and entertaining photographs
- The wreck has settled deeper than originally planned; verify current depths with your operator before diving

## Safety Considerations

The stern depth of 38–40 metres makes this a deeper dive that limits bottom time on air. Plan gas management carefully and avoid spending too long at the deepest sections. Wreck penetration requires dedicated wreck penetration training, a guideline or clear exit plan, and redundant lights — never enter an overhead environment without proper equipment and training. The shore entry over rocks requires care, particularly with camera equipment.

## Photography

The Kubu Wreck offers unique photographic opportunities. The prop decorations on the deck -- statues, the old-style helm, scuba tanks, the VW car -- create distinctive compositions unlike any other wreck dive. A wide-angle lens is recommended for the wreck exterior and deck props. Bring a torch to bring out colours and illuminate details in the wreck's darker areas.
