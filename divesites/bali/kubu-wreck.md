# Kubu Wreck

A vessel known as the Boga -- most sources describe it as a Dutch-built Indonesian government patrol vessel, though one source calls it a cargo ship -- intentionally sunk in 2012 as an artificial reef off Kubu village. Reported dimensions and depths vary significantly between sources; see below for the specifics and conflicts.

## Overview

The Kubu Wreck is universally referred to as "the Boga" across every source consulted, but no source -- including a search for a dedicated Wikipedia entry, which does not exist -- confirms this was the vessel's original naval designation or class; it should be treated as the name used for the dive site rather than a verified original ship name. Most dive-operator sources (Let's Dive Tulamben, Ecodive Bali) independently describe it as a Dutch-built patrol vessel operated by the Indonesian government before it was retired; one source (No Fear Diving) instead describes it as a cargo ship, and a search-aggregated account referenced it as a "cargo-patrol vessel" purchased and donated by private owners -- this donation narrative could not be independently verified through a direct source fetch, so it is not included here. Given the majority of directly-verified sources, "Indonesian government patrol vessel, Dutch-built" is the best-supported description, but the cargo-ship characterization should be noted as a live discrepancy.

Reported length varies considerably: sources range from 35 metres to 50 metres, with most clustering around 40–45 metres. No source provided a verifiable tonnage figure from a direct fetch, so tonnage is not stated here. The vessel was intentionally sunk in 2012 to create an artificial reef and new dive attraction for the area -- this is consistently confirmed across essentially every source found (Let's Dive Tulamben, Bali Dive Cove, Ecodive Bali, Diveconcepts, Bali AQua, Go Diving Tulamben, No Fear Diving, Exotis Bali, Zero Gravity Diving). One aggregated source specifies September 2012, but that detail was not independently confirmed via a directly-fetched source, so treat the month as unconfirmed. Exactly who organized and funded the sinking is not consistently documented across sources.

The wreck sits on a sloping sandy bottom. Reported depths vary widely by source -- from as shallow as 13 metres to as deep as 45 metres for different parts of the wreck -- but the bow (shallowest point) is most commonly cited around 14–18 metres, with the stern extending to roughly 35–40 metres. At least one source (Diveconcepts) claims the wreck was originally intended to sit at a maximum of 32 metres and has gradually settled deeper over time, but this specific claim is not corroborated elsewhere, so it should be treated as unconfirmed rather than established fact. The deck has been decorated with props — a (non-original) wooden steering wheel, Buddha statues, scuba tanks, and a Volkswagen car — confirmed across multiple independent sources (Bali Dive Cove, Ecodive Bali, Let's Dive Tulamben), giving the wreck a distinctive, theatrical character. (One outlier source mentioned a "wartime jeep" instead of the VW car and another mentioned "clay amphoras" instead of the statues -- both are single-source claims that conflict with the multi-source VW car/Buddha statue description and have been excluded.) The site lies offshore from Kubu village near Kubu Reef; sources disagree on the exact distance (estimates range from roughly 60 to 300 metres offshore), so no specific figure is given here.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Kubu Village, East Bali, Indonesia (near Kubu Reef; exact offshore distance is reported inconsistently across sources, roughly 60-300 m)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-25 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 27-29°C
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season)

## Marine Life

Gorgonian fans and soft corals have colonised the wreck structure in the years since sinking. Species reported at the site by Ecodive Bali (moray eels, barracuda, batfish, lionfish, nudibranchs, octopuses) are corroborated for several species by additional independent sources -- batfish and turtles are also noted by No Fear Diving, and barracuda/whitetip reef sharks by Go Diving Tulamben. Occasional whitetip reef sharks are reported by more than one source, though sightings are inconsistent. The wreck prop items (Volkswagen car, Buddha statues) have become focal points for marine life and unusual photographic subjects.

## Dive Profile

Enter from shore and descend to the wreck. The bow is the shallowest point (commonly cited around 14-18 metres, though sources vary) and a good place to begin exploration. Work aft along the deck, observing the decorative props — Buddha statues, Volkswagen car, wooden steering wheel, and scuba tanks placed along the deck. The stern descends to roughly 35-40 metres by most accounts (some sources cite depths up to 45 metres), so monitor depth and bottom time carefully when exploring the aft sections. Penetration of the wreck is possible but requires appropriate training, a clear exit plan, and redundant lights — never enter an overhead environment without proper wreck penetration training and equipment. Plan gas management carefully given the depth range.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry at Kubu village, the same access point used for Kubu Reef. Local porters can assist with carrying equipment from the road to the beach entry point. Enter over the rocky shoreline and navigate to the wreck site. Exit at the same location.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Start at the bow (shallowest point, roughly 14-18 m depending on source) and work deeper to manage bottom time efficiently
- Bring a torch to illuminate the wreck interior and highlight the prop decorations
- Nitrox extends bottom time significantly at the shallower deck level
- The prop items — VW car, Buddha statues — make for unusual and entertaining photographs
- Depth reports for this wreck vary noticeably between operators; verify current depths with your operator before diving

## Safety Considerations

The stern depth (reported as roughly 35-40 metres by most sources, with some citing figures up to 45 metres) makes this a deeper dive that limits bottom time on air. Plan gas management carefully and avoid spending too long at the deepest sections. Wreck penetration requires dedicated wreck penetration training, a guideline or clear exit plan, and redundant lights — never enter an overhead environment without proper equipment and training. The shore entry over rocks requires care, particularly with camera equipment.

## Photography

The Kubu Wreck offers unique photographic opportunities. The prop decorations on the deck -- Buddha statues, the non-original wooden helm, the Volkswagen car -- create distinctive compositions unlike any other wreck dive. A wide-angle lens is recommended for wreck exteriors. Bring a torch to bring out colours and illuminate details in the wreck's darker areas.
