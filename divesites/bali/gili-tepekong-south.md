## Gili Tepekong South

An adrenaline dive on Tepekong's exposed south face — the site known as "The Toilet" for its powerful currents, and home to the Blue Hole chimney and resident whitetip and blacktip reef sharks.

## Overview

Gili Tepekong South is the dramatic, exposed face of the largest island in Amuk Bay off Candidasa. The Canyon — colloquially called "The Toilet" because of the swirling downcurrents it generates — is one of the most challenging dive environments in Bali, and several Bali operators decline to run it except in the calmest conditions with senior guides. Multiple sources describe a Blue Hole feature nearby: a tunnel at roughly 18–21 m that leads through to a sheer wall, with divers emerging around 28 m. The wall here drops well past 35 m and is covered in hard and soft corals, sea fans, and sponges. Schools of sweetlips, trevally, and large grouper congregate around the Canyon and "Shark Boulder" area, and whitetip and blacktip reef sharks are frequently reported.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Amuk Bay, Candidasa area, east Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 m
- **Current**: Strong to very strong; powerful down-currents possible
- **Best Time**: May to October (slack tide only)

## Marine Life

Whitetip and blacktip reef sharks are the signature species on the south face. Large schools of sweetlips, trevally, fusiliers, and grouper gather around the Canyon and Shark Boulder. Mola mola are reported on the exposed south wall during the July–October season. Turtles and barracuda are regular sightings. The walls host nudibranchs and moray eels. Pygmy seahorses have been reported on Tepekong's gorgonians, though sources don't confirm whether that sighting was on this south face specifically or elsewhere on the island.

## Dive Profile

Enter on the upcurrent side and descend to the sloping plateau, which borders a wall dropping into open water. Time the Canyon/Blue Hole entry for manageable current — dive briefings will specify conditions for the day, and some operators skip the Canyon entirely if conditions aren't calm. If diving the Blue Hole, pass through the tunnel around 18–21 m and emerge on the wall near 28 m. Ascend on the sheltered side of the island and conduct a 3-minute safety stop in calmer water.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Padangbai or Candidasa. Entry timing is critical — experienced guides will select the entry point based on current direction and strength. Only dive with operators who have recent knowledge of the site conditions. SMB deployment mandatory.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This site is for experienced advanced divers with a minimum of 50 logged dives and recent drift experience
- Only attempt The Toilet / canyon section on neap tides with slack current
- Listen carefully to the dive briefing — conditions dictate the entire dive plan
- If down-currents are present in the canyon, ascend immediately along the wall
- The Blue Hole is worth the effort — an extraordinary chimney formation with clear water

## Safety Considerations

Down-currents in the Canyon are the primary hazard — vertical surge here can pull divers up or down and requires constant equalizing. Several Bali dive operators consider the Canyon too risky to run except in calm conditions with senior guides. Never enter the canyon alone. Follow your guide's instructions precisely regarding depth and exit timing. If swept deep, maintain neutral buoyancy and swim horizontally to exit the down-current zone rather than fighting it vertically. Always carry an SMB and a safety sausage.

## Photography

The canyon walls create dramatic frame shots. Schools of sweetlips in the current produce action shots that require fast shutter speeds. Reef sharks at rest on the wall are excellent subjects with wide-angle. The Blue Hole chimney with divers backlit by the opening is a signature image of Bali diving.
