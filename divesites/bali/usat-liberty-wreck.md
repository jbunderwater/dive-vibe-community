## USAT Liberty Wreck

One of the world's most accessible wreck dives — a WWII-era US Army cargo ship torpedoed on 11 January 1942, now lying parallel to shore just 25 metres offshore at Tulamben, accessible without a boat.

## Overview

The USAT Liberty was a US Army cargo ship built as a Design 1037 vessel for the United States Shipping Board, launched on 19 June 1918 at the Federal Shipbuilding Company in Kearny, New Jersey (the first ship built at that yard) and completed later that year. She had earlier served in World War I as the Navy animal transport USS Liberty (ID-3461). On 11 January 1942, while en route from Tanjung Priok (Batavia) in the Netherlands East Indies, she was torpedoed by the Japanese submarine I-66 (renumbered I-166 in May 1942, which is why some sources refer to the attacking submarine by either name) approximately 10 nautical miles southwest of the Lombok Strait. Sources also disagree on the cargo: Wikipedia states she was carrying "railway parts and rubber," while a Japanese-submarine naval history record (combinedfleet.com) describes rubber stowed in her holds plus explosives carried as deck cargo. Both accounts agree rubber was aboard; the rest of the manifest could not be reconciled from the sources checked this pass. The US destroyer Paul Jones and the Dutch destroyer Van Ghent took the stricken vessel under tow, attempting to reach the harbour at Singaraja, but she was taking on too much water and was instead beached on the shore at Tulamben, Bali, so her cargo and fittings could be salvaged. The ship remained on the beach for over two decades until tremors from the 1963 eruption of Mount Agung caused it to slip off into the sea, where it settled on a sand slope and now lies.

Sources disagree on the ship's exact size. Wikipedia gives a length of 411 ft 6 in (approximately 125 metres) and a displacement of 13,130 tons; dive operator sources (Diving Indo, Let's Dive Tulamben) commonly cite a shorter figure of around 120 metres and a weight of roughly 6,211 tons — likely referring to gross/net registered tonnage rather than full displacement, though the sources don't specify which measure they mean. Beam is consistently reported at 55 ft (17 m) across sources. The wreck lies parallel to shore, spanning roughly 3 to 30 metres depth (Wikipedia records 25 to 100 ft / 7.6–30.5 m), with the shallowest structure a few metres below the surface and the site regularly cited as beginning about 25–40 metres out from the beach. The hull is completely encrusted in soft corals, gorgonian fans, and sponges, making this one of the world's great shore dives and one of Indonesia's most-visited dive sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tulamben, northeast Bali, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–30 metres
- **Current**: Generally mild; can increase with tide
- **Best Time**: Year-round; April–November for optimal visibility

## Marine Life

Marine life reported at this specific wreck by Tulamben dive operators includes bigeye trevally in large schools, sweetlips, angelfish, batfish, surgeonfish, sea turtles, Napoleon wrasse, great barracuda, and garden eels in the surrounding sand. Bumphead parrotfish are reported as nocturnal/dawn residents. Reef sharks are described by some operators as an occasional, not guaranteed, sighting at the wreck. As with any wreck, penetrating the interior requires appropriate wreck-diving training and equipment — it is not a routine part of a recreational dive here.
