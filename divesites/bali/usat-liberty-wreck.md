## USAT Liberty Wreck

One of the world's most accessible wreck dives — a WWII-era US Army cargo ship torpedoed on 11 January 1942, now lying parallel to shore just 25 metres offshore at Tulamben, accessible without a boat.

## Overview

The USAT Liberty was a US Army cargo ship built by the Federal Shipbuilding Company in Kearny, New Jersey, and launched on 19 June 1918 (she served in WWI as the animal transport USS Liberty before her Army career). On 11 January 1942, while carrying railway parts and rubber from Australia to the Philippines through the Lombok Strait, she was torpedoed by the Japanese submarine I-66. Two destroyers (USS Paul Jones and HNLMS Van Ghent) took her under tow toward Singaraja, but she was taking on too much water and was instead beached on the black-sand shore at Tulamben, Bali, for salvage. The ship remained on the beach until tremors from the 1963 eruption of Mount Agung caused it to slide off into the sea, where it now lies.

Sources conflict on two key dimensions. Wikipedia records the vessel's length as 411 ft 6 in (approximately 125 metres); dive operator descriptions (Let's Dive Tulamben and others) commonly give the figure as 120 metres — the true figure likely falls in that 120–125m range. Tonnage is reported even less consistently: some sources cite a gross tonnage in the 6,200–6,340 ton range, while Wikipedia's infobox lists a displacement of 13,130 tons. These are most likely different tonnage measures (gross registered tonnage vs. full displacement) rather than a genuine disagreement on ship size, but no source reconciles the two figures directly, so both are reported here rather than picking one.

The wreck lies parallel to shore roughly 25 metres out, spanning 3 to 30 metres depth, with the upper structure at 3–5 metres. The hull is completely encrusted in soft corals, gorgonian fans, and sponges, making this one of the world's great shore dives and one of Indonesia's most-visited dive sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tulamben, northeast Bali, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–30 metres
- **Current**: Generally mild; can increase with tide
- **Best Time**: Year-round; April–November for optimal visibility
