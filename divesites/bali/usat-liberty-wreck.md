## USAT Liberty Wreck

One of the world's most accessible wreck dives — a WWII-era US Army cargo ship torpedoed on 11 January 1942, now lying parallel to shore just 25 metres offshore at Tulamben, accessible without a boat.

## Overview

The USAT Liberty was a US Army cargo ship launched on 19 June 1918 at the Federal Shipbuilding Company in Kearny, New Jersey — the first vessel built at that yard. On 11 January 1942, she was torpedoed by the Japanese submarine I-66 (renumbered I-166 in May 1942, several months after this attack, which is why some later sources refer to her by the newer designation) while carrying railway parts and rubber from Australia to the Philippines, roughly 10 nautical miles southwest of the Lombok Strait. The US destroyer Paul Jones and the Dutch destroyer Van Ghent took the stricken vessel in tow toward Singaraja, but she was taking on too much water and was instead beached at Tulamben, Bali, so her cargo and fittings could be salvaged. She sat on the beach for roughly two decades until tremors from the 1963 eruption of Mount Agung caused her to slip off the beach and slide into the sea, breaking at the bow and stern, where she now lies.

Sources do not fully agree on the ship's dimensions or her exact distance from shore, and rather than pick a single figure this description reports the range. Wikipedia's infobox gives a length of 411 ft 6 in (125.43 m) and a full-load displacement of 13,130 tons; dive-operator sources (letsdivetulamben.com, diving-indo.com, PADI, stingynomads.com) commonly round the length to 120 metres, and diving-indo.com separately gives a gross tonnage of 6,211 tons — a different, smaller measure than displacement, so the two tonnage figures are not necessarily contradictory, but no single "correct" number can be confirmed. Distance from shore is reported anywhere from about 10 metres (diving-indo.com, describing the shallow snorkel-depth bow) to 25 metres (letsdivetulamben.com), 30 metres (stingynomads.com), and 40 metres (Wikipedia). What is consistent across every source: the wreck lies roughly parallel to shore, spanning about 3 to 30 metres of depth, with the shallowest structure at 3–5 metres, and the hull is thickly encrusted in soft corals, gorgonian fans, and sponges — making this one of the world's most accessible wreck dives.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tulamben, northeast Bali, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–30 metres
- **Current**: Generally mild; can increase with tide
- **Best Time**: Year-round; April–November for optimal visibility
