## USAT Liberty Wreck

One of the world's most accessible wreck dives — a WWII-era US Army cargo ship torpedoed on 11 January 1942, now lying parallel to shore just 25 metres offshore at Tulamben, accessible without a boat.

## Overview

The USAT Liberty was a US Army cargo ship launched on 19 June 1918 at the Federal Shipbuilding Company in Kearny, New Jersey. On 11 January 1942, it was torpedoed by the Japanese submarine I-66 (renumbered I-166 on 20 May 1942) while carrying railway parts and rubber from Australia to the Philippines, approximately 10 nautical miles southwest of the Lombok Strait. Two destroyers, USS Paul Jones and the Dutch HNLMS Van Ghent, took the stricken vessel in tow toward the port of Singaraja, but she was taking on too much water and was instead beached on the shore at Tulamben, Bali, so her cargo and fittings could be salvaged. The Imperial Japanese Navy's submarine service history site combinedfleet.com records that the ship capsized on the beach three days later, on 14 January 1942. She remained there until tremors from the 1963 eruption of Mount Agung caused her to slide off the beach into the sea, where she now lies broken in two.

Sources disagree on the ship's exact dimensions. Wikipedia's infobox gives a length of 411 ft 6 in (approximately 125.4 metres) and a displacement of 13,130 tons; combinedfleet.com, a reference source for Imperial Japanese Navy submarine operations, records the ship as "6,211-ton" at the time of the attack — likely a different tonnage measure (e.g. gross register tons vs. full-load displacement) rather than a contradiction, though this has not been confirmed. Tulamben dive operators, including Let's Dive Tulamben, commonly cite a rounder figure of 120 metres for the wreck's length; none of the dive-operator sources consulted state a tonnage figure. Given the spread, treat 120–125 metres as the length range and note that tonnage is not firmly settled between sources.

The wreck lies parallel to shore, with most sources placing it roughly 25–30 metres offshore, spanning 3 to 30 metres depth with the shallowest structure at 3–5 metres. The hull is completely encrusted in soft corals, gorgonian fans, and sponges, making this one of the world's great shore dives and one of Indonesia's most-visited dive sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tulamben, northeast Bali, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–30 metres
- **Current**: Generally mild; can increase with tide
- **Best Time**: Year-round; April–November for optimal visibility

## Marine Life

Species reported at the wreck by multiple independent sources (Let's Dive Tulamben and Stingy Nomads) include large schools of bigeye trevally over the hull and shallows, a resident population of bumphead parrotfish that shelters on the wreck and is most reliably seen at dusk and dawn, Napoleon wrasse, sea turtles, reef sharks, moray eels, and garden eels on the sandy areas near shore. A well-known individual great barracuda is also frequently reported by divers and dive operators. Divers should not expect every species to be present on every dive — sightings vary by dive time and conditions.

## Safety Considerations

The wreck's shallow upper sections (3–5m) are accessible to beginners, but deeper sections and any interior spaces require appropriate wreck training and equipment — penetration of the hull should only be attempted by divers with specific wreck diving certification, not as a casual extension of a normal dive.
