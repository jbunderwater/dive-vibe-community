## Manta Bay

A closer, shallower alternative to Manta Point — a sheltered bay near Broken Beach where reef mantas regularly visit.

## Overview

Manta Bay is situated near the iconic Broken Beach (Pasih Uug) on the northwest coast of Nusa Penida, making it the closer and more frequently visited of the two main manta sites on the island (vs. the farther, calmer Manta Point). It has no fixed manta cleaning stations — instead mantas are typically seen feeding and playing near the surface, around 1–6 m. Operators including Nusa Penida.org report roughly an 80% manta encounter rate here, though the bay attracts heavier snorkel-tour traffic than Manta Point and can feel crowded by mid-morning. The sandy bottom slopes from shallow water down through the low-to-mid teens.

*Note on depth: sources disagree on Manta Bay's maximum depth — Scuba Center Asia lists a 12 m maximum, another source describes a 5–12 m sandy slope continuing to roughly 30 m offshore, and this destination's data lists 18 m. Treat depth as approximate and confirm with your operator.*

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northwest Nusa Penida, near Broken Beach
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters (sources vary; see note above)
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 m
- **Current**: Generally calmer/more sheltered than Manta Point; can still be significant
- **Best Time**: April to October

## Marine Life

Reef manta rays are the primary attraction — seen near the surface (1–6 m) rather than at fixed cleaning stations, with an approximately 80% encounter rate reported by Nusa Penida.org. Scuba Center Asia's site listing additionally reports nurse sharks, nudibranchs, and turtles at this bay. No source this pass confirmed blue-spotted stingrays, clownfish, moray eels, or mola mola specifically at Manta Bay — the mola mola claim in particular is not well supported here, since mola cleaning stations documented in Nusa Penida (e.g., at nearby Crystal Bay) are in the 20–30 m range, deeper than this shallow bay typically reaches.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the sandy floor and hold position at a respectful distance from where mantas are feeding near the surface (typically 1–6 m) — there are no fixed cleaning stations here to station at, so patience and positioning matter more than at Manta Point. Allow the mantas to approach rather than chasing them. Ascend slowly and conduct a 3-minute safety stop at 5 m.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from Toyapakeh or operators based on Nusa Penida. The crossing is shorter than to Manta Point but can still be choppy. Entry via back roll from dive boat. SMB deployment for ascent is recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Arrive early to minimise overlap with snorkel tour boats that crowd the bay by midday
- Stay low and neutrally buoyant — hovering above cleaning mantas disrupts the behaviour
- Combine with nearby Crystal Bay for a contrasting two-site day
- Wear a 3–5 mm wetsuit; water is generally warmer here than at Manta Point
- Conditions are generally more manageable here on days when Manta Point is too rough

## Safety Considerations

Currents are less intense than Manta Point but can still be significant. Be aware of increased boat traffic in the bay — stay well clear of mooring lines and anchor chains. Do not approach manta rays closer than 3 m; they will leave the cleaning station if stressed. Always carry an SMB.

## Photography

Wide-angle lenses capture manta rays circling over the coral bommies. The shallower, brighter conditions at Manta Bay often produce better-lit shots than the deeper site. Capture belly patterns to contribute to manta ID research programs run by local conservation organisations.
