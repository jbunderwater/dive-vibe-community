## Menjangan - Pos 2

One of Menjangan Island's most renowned wall dives — dense gorgonian fields with resident pygmy seahorses, and, when cold-water upwelling brings them in, occasional manta rays and mola mola.

## Overview

Pos 2 (Post 2) sits on the southeast/south point of Menjangan Island and is consistently named as one of the standout wall dives at this destination across multiple independent dive-operator sources (Dive Concepts, Karang Divers, Mimpi Resort, Joe's Gone Diving). Reports on the exact top-of-wall depth vary — some cite the wall starting around 5 m, others around 12 m after a sandy slope — but sources agree the richest coral growth is concentrated in the upper and middle sections of the wall, with giant gorgonian sea fans (some cited up to roughly 3 m across), whip corals, soft corals, and sponges. Multiple sources independently describe cold-water upwelling bringing pelagic species close to the wall, including turtles, reef sharks, eagle rays, and — less predictably than at Nusa Penida's famous mola mola sites — occasional manta rays and mola mola (oceanic sunfish). The wall can connect toward Bat Cave Point for an extended drift when conditions allow.

Sources disagree on how deep the recreational portion of the wall runs: several operator pages (Dive Concepts, Karang Divers, Mimpi Resort) cite the wall descending to around 40 m, one (Divers Guide) cites a shallower 25 m limit, and one source describes the full rock face continuing to 60-70 m before the bottom. This description does not pick one figure but flags the range.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-40 m depending on source; PADI's general Menjangan-area figure is 15-30 m (up to 50 m+ in October-November)
- **Current**: Conflicting reports — some sources describe calm/gentle current typical of Menjangan generally, others describe a genuine drift dive with current that "can sometimes be strong" and direction that varies with tide
- **Best Time**: April to November (general Menjangan dive season)

## Marine Life

Large gorgonian sea fans in the middle depths of the wall are repeatedly and independently cited (Dive Concepts, Karang Divers, Intrinity Divers, Mimpi) as hosting pygmy seahorses — this is corroborated across multiple site-specific sources, not just a generic regional claim. Turtles, reef sharks, and schooling fusiliers/trevally/barracuda are commonly reported along the wall by several independent operators. Eagle rays are also independently reported at this specific site by more than one source. Manta rays and mola mola (oceanic sunfish) are reported specifically at Pos 2 by at least two independent sources (Mimpi Resort and Bali.com), who attribute these sightings to cold-water upwelling — these are described as occasional/conditional sightings, not a reliable season the way Nusa Penida's mola mola runs are, so they should not be oversold as a guarantee.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the wall from the boat, typically following a sandy slope to the wall edge before working the 15-25 m band where coral growth is richest. Scan large gorgonians for pygmy seahorses. Watch the blue water off the wall for eagle rays and other pelagics. Depending on current and operator routing, the dive may run as a fixed-point exploration or a drift toward Bat Cave Point. Ascend gradually with a safety stop around 5 m.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access only (Menjangan Island has no general shore access for visitors); typical departure points are Labuhan Lalang or Pemuteran. Entry via back roll. Given the mixed current reports above, confirm the day's conditions and current direction with your dive guide before descending. Deploy an SMB for the safety stop and boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Scan the large gorgonian sea fans methodically for pygmy seahorses — this is one of the best-corroborated highlights of the site
- Watch the open water off the wall for eagle rays, which are reported reliably at this site by local operators
- Treat manta ray and mola mola sightings as a possible bonus tied to cold upwelling, not an expectation
- Confirm current conditions with your guide before the dive, since reports on current strength at this site vary
- A possible drift connection to Bat Cave Point exists when conditions allow — ask your operator

## Safety Considerations

Reports on current strength at Pos 2 conflict — some describe it as calm, others as a genuine drift dive requiring experience, especially when current "runs." Treat it with the same caution as a current-exposed wall dive until your guide briefs you on the day's conditions. It is easy to exceed planned depth while focused on the wall face and its gorgonians — monitor your depth gauge closely, particularly given reports that the wall continues well past 30 m. Always carry an SMB.

## Photography

The gorgonian fields support both wide-angle (fan corals, wall vistas) and macro (pygmy seahorses) work. A macro lens and patience are needed to find pygmy seahorses among the gorgonian polyps; eagle rays and other pelagics in blue water reward divers ready with a wide-angle setup.
