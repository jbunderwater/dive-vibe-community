## Gili Biaha

The most remote of the three Amuk Bay islands — famous for its Shark Cave and strong currents that attract advanced divers seeking white-tip reef sharks and seasonal mola mola.

## Overview

Gili Biaha is a small crescent-shaped island approximately 4 km northeast of Gili Tepekong, making it the outermost and most remote of the three islands off Padangbai in Amuk Bay. Boat time from Padangbai is reported inconsistently across operators — from about 20 minutes to as much as 45 minutes — likely depending on the departure point and boat used, so treat this as a range rather than a fixed figure. The island is surrounded by a dense coral reef that is part rugged black wall and part slope, with sources reporting depths from roughly 10 m down to 35–40+ m. The signature feature is a cave/blowhole on the inside of the crescent, at around 10 meters, where white-tip reef sharks are consistently reported resting. The site is exposed to open ocean swells and strong current, creating conditions described as strong and unpredictable, including downcurrents; the southwest end inside the crescent is comparatively more sheltered than the more turbulent north side. Water temperature can drop to around 20°C in a thermocline during the dry season. Best visibility and conditions are generally from June to October, which also coincides with the local mola mola season, though mola mola sightings here are described as possible rather than reliable.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Amuk Bay, east of Padangbai, east Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 m
- **Current**: Strong and unpredictable; downcurrents possible
- **Best Time**: June to October

## Marine Life

White-tip reef sharks are the main attraction, consistently found resting inside the Shark Cave/blowhole at around 10 meters. Schools of glass fish fill the cave entrance. Outside the cave, lobsters (in abundance along the cracked wall), octopuses, cuttlefish, nudibranchs, pufferfish, frogfish, leaf scorpionfish, lionfish, and turtles are reported along the wall. A few times a year, a larger pod of dolphins is occasionally sighted in the area. From June to October, mola mola (oceanic sunfish) are possible during the cold-water season, though sightings are described as rare rather than reliable. Note: barracuda and tuna appeared in a prior pass of this entry but could not be independently re-confirmed for this specific site in this pass's sources — they have been removed pending confirmation rather than carried forward as fact.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the wall on the sheltered side. The Shark Cave is located at approximately 10 meters on the western face. After visiting the cave, follow the wall along the reef slope from 15–30 meters where gorgonians and larger marine life are concentrated. Allow the current to drift you along the wall. Ascend gradually and deploy an SMB well away from the island.

## Safety Considerations

This is one of Bali's most demanding dive sites. Strong, unpredictable currents and downcurrents are the primary hazards. Cold thermoclines can drop water temperature to 20°C without warning. Advanced Open Water certification and significant drift diving experience are required. Always dive with a local operator who knows the current conditions at Biaha.
