## Gili Biaha

The most remote of the three Amuk Bay islands — famous for its Shark Cave and strong currents that attract advanced divers seeking white-tip reef sharks and seasonal mola mola.

## Overview

Gili Biaha is a small crescent-shaped island approximately 4 km northeast of Gili Tepekong, making it the outermost and most remote of the three islands off Padangbai in Amuk Bay. Operators quote a wide range of boat times from Padangbai — from about 20 minutes (from a closer-departing resort) to around 45 minutes by boat, with slower traditional boats taking longer — so plan for roughly 30–45 minutes on a typical dive-shop speedboat. The island is surrounded by a dramatic black volcanic wall/terraced reef dropping to 40 meters and beyond, with a signature Shark Cave feature at around 10–12 meters where whitetip reef sharks rest. The site is exposed to open ocean swells and the Indonesian Throughflow, creating strong and unpredictable currents — operator accounts describe both up-currents on entry and down-currents along the wall. Dense coral coverage on the wall includes hard and soft corals, gorgonians, and sponges. Best visibility and conditions are from June to October, which also coincides with mola mola season.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Amuk Bay, east of Padangbai, east Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 m
- **Current**: Strong and unpredictable; downcurrents possible
- **Best Time**: June to October

## Marine Life

White-tip reef sharks are the main attraction, frequently found resting inside the Shark Cave at around 10–12 meters, along with large lobsters. A school of glass fish is reported at the cave. Outside the cave, octopuses, nudibranchs, pufferfish, turtles, and crabs patrol the wall. From June to October, mola mola (oceanic sunfish) may be encountered. One local operator (Nico Dives Cool Bali) reports that "several times a year, when lucky," a larger pod of dolphins resident to the area is sighted here — this is a genuine but occasional/rare sighting, not a reliable one, so treat dolphins as a bonus rather than an expected encounter. Generic "whales" and "tuna" sightings previously listed for this site could not be corroborated against site-specific sources and have been removed; the site shares much of its general pelagic character with neighboring Gili Tepekong and Gili Mimpang.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the wall on the sheltered side. The Shark Cave is located at approximately 10 meters on the western face. After visiting the cave, follow the wall along the reef slope from 15–30 meters where gorgonians and larger marine life are concentrated. Allow the current to drift you along the wall. Ascend gradually and deploy an SMB well away from the island.

## Safety Considerations

This is one of Bali's most demanding dive sites. Strong, unpredictable currents — including both up-currents on entry and down-currents along the wall — are the primary hazards. Cold thermoclines can drop water temperature to as low as 20°C without warning. Advanced Open Water certification and significant drift diving experience are required; this re-audit found consistent, current operator guidance supporting the Advanced rating, with no basis to downgrade it. Always dive with a local operator who knows the current conditions at Biaha.
