## Gili Biaha

The most remote of the three Amuk Bay islands — famous for its Shark Cave and strong currents that attract advanced divers seeking white-tip reef sharks and seasonal mola mola.

## Overview

Gili Biaha is a small crescent-shaped island off Padangbai in Amuk Bay, reached by boat only (roughly 45 minutes from Padangbai). The island is surrounded by a black volcanic wall — part rugged wall, part slope — with sources reporting maximum depths of 35–40 meters. Its signature feature is the Shark Cave, at approximately 10–12 meters, where white-tip reef sharks rest. Sources describe the island's southwest end as the more sheltered diving area, with more turbulent conditions on the north side. Open ocean exposure and swell create strong and unpredictable currents, including downcurrents. Dense coral coverage on the wall includes hard and soft corals, gorgonians, and sponges. Best visibility and conditions are from June to October, which also coincides with mola mola season.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Amuk Bay, east of Padangbai, east Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 m
- **Current**: Strong and unpredictable; downcurrents possible
- **Best Time**: June to October

## Marine Life

White-tip reef sharks are the main attraction, frequently found resting inside the Shark Cave at around 10–12 meters. Schools of glass fish are reported at the cave. Outside the cave, lobsters, octopuses, cuttlefish, nudibranchs, pufferfish, frogfish, leaf scorpionfish, and turtles are documented on the wall. Dolphins are occasionally sighted a few times a year. From June to October, mola mola (oceanic sunfish) may be encountered. Barracuda and tuna are also reported passing the site, consistent with sightings at neighboring Gili Tepekong and Gili Mimpang.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the wall on the sheltered side — sources indicate the southwest end of the island is more protected, while the north side sees more turbulent conditions. The Shark Cave sits at approximately 10–12 meters; sources do not consistently agree on its exact compass orientation on the island, so no specific face is stated here. After visiting the cave, follow the wall along the reef slope where gorgonians and larger marine life are concentrated. Allow the current to drift you along the wall. Ascend gradually and deploy an SMB well away from the island.

## Safety Considerations

This is one of Bali's most demanding dive sites. Strong, unpredictable currents and downcurrents are the primary hazards, with heavier swell affecting the upper sections of the reef. Advanced Open Water certification and significant drift-diving experience are required. Always dive with a local operator who knows the current conditions at Biaha, and stay close to the reef wall during descent and ascent as advised by local guides.
