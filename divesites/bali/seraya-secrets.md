# Seraya Secrets

A black sand muck diving site approximately 3 kilometres southeast of Tulamben, divided into three sub-sites that are among Bali's best locations for macro photography and critter hunting.

## Overview

Seraya Secrets is located near the village of Seraya, roughly 3 kilometres southeast of Tulamben along the northeast Bali coast, reached by car rather than directly from the beach. The site is commonly divided into three sub-sites — Top Secrets (shallow), Deep Secrets (deeper), and Noisy Reef/Noisy Secrets (named for the sounds made by resident juvenile triggerfish, in front of Villa Markisa) — though one older source instead splits it into "Left" and "Right" sections, so the exact sub-site naming varies by operator. The black volcanic sand bottom has a silty character and requires careful buoyancy control. Sources disagree on the artificial structures present: current operator listings (Let's Dive Tulamben, DiveOtion) describe a metal dome colonised with soft corals as the site's main artificial feature, while an earlier detailed dive log (Michael McFadyen) also describes a second, separate steel-frame structure built in the shape of an airplane. This description flags that discrepancy rather than asserting either way — it is possible the airplane structure was added, removed, or is simply omitted from newer listings. The maximum depth is approximately 25–35 metres depending on the sub-site explored.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Seraya, East Bali, Indonesia (approximately 3 km SE of Tulamben)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-25 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 27-29°C
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season)

## Marine Life

Seraya Secrets is one of Bali's premier critter diving destinations, and the marine life list below is corroborated across all three sources consulted this pass. Confirmed species include nudibranchs (including donut and pikachu varieties, plus several other species logged individually), harlequin shrimp, Coleman and tiger shrimp, boxer crabs, warty frogfish, ornate ghost pipefish, pygmy seahorses, blue-spotted stingrays, mimic octopus, moray eels (including yellow-edged and greyface varieties), ribbon eels, cleaner and mantis shrimp, orangutan and porcelain crabs, coral groupers, sweetlips, clownfish, emperor angelfish, and titan triggerfish. Spanish dancers — large, spectacularly coloured nudibranchs — are confirmed and are a particular highlight on night dives. The site is rated among Bali's top macro destinations.

## Dive Profile

Enter from shore and descend onto the black sand slope. The sub-sites offer different terrain, so follow your guide's briefing on which direction to take. The metal dome artificial structure is a focal point where critters concentrate around its coral-covered surfaces (see note above on a possible second, airplane-shaped structure reported by one source). Spend time carefully examining structures and the surrounding sand for camouflaged subjects. The productive depth range is from around 8 metres down to 25–35 metres depending on the sub-site. Good buoyancy is essential — stirring the silty sand reduces visibility for everyone.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry, accessed by car from Tulamben (approximately 3 kilometres). The entry point is at the beach near Seraya village. Enter carefully over the rocky shore and descend onto the sand. Exit at the same location.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This is a slow, focused critter dive -- resist the urge to cover too much ground
- A knowledgeable local guide is essential for locating the best critter spots on both sub-sites
- Night diving here is particularly rewarding, with Spanish dancers and other nocturnal species emerging after dark
- Macro photography equipment is strongly recommended

## Safety Considerations

The shore entry can be rocky, so take care when entering and exiting. Currents are generally mild but can occasionally pick up. Maintain strict buoyancy control over the sand to avoid silting out the bottom — the silty substrate is easily disturbed. Advanced certification is recommended due to the depth of the Deep Secrets sub-site (up to ~35 m) and the buoyancy demands of the site.

## Photography

Seraya Secrets is a world-class macro photography site. The black sand provides clean, high-contrast backgrounds for critter subjects. Ghost pipefish, nudibranchs, frogfish, and harlequin shrimp are the primary macro targets. The coral-covered dome structure offers interesting wide-angle compositions showing artificial reef colonisation. A dedicated macro lens (60mm or 100mm) and a good focus light are essential.
