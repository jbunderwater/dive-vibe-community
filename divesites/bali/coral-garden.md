## Coral Garden

A vibrant shallow reef on the north side of Menjangan Island, rich with hard and soft corals and suited to all experience levels.

## Overview

Coral Garden sits on the north side of Menjangan Island within West Bali National Park. Multiple dive operators describe a shallow reef/slope beginning around 3–12 m that then drops away more steeply — reported variously as a gradual drop-off, a steep slope, or a wall — toward deeper water. Reported maximum depths for the site vary widely by source (from roughly 12 m up to 40 m), so treat any single number with caution; most recreational diving here stays in the 5–20 m range. The reef is consistently described as dense with hard and soft corals, gorgonian fans, and sponges, with cleaning stations that draw in schools of larger fish. It is one of the more frequently recommended beginner and photography sites at Menjangan.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Menjangan Island, West Bali National Park, northwest Bali
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–30 m
- **Current**: Reports vary by source — generally described as gentle to mild, though one operator notes currents can be strong enough to dictate the best entry point. Confirm conditions with your boat crew before the dive.
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

Clownfish sheltering in host anemones (reported in white, green, and blue varieties) are consistently reported throughout the shallows. Titan triggerfish, bigeye trevally, and scorpionfish are the most frequently repeated sightings across dive operator descriptions. Cleaning stations here attract schools of fusiliers, snappers, and trevally. Turtles and, occasionally, blacktip and whitetip reef sharks have been reported by more than one source. Pygmy seahorses are occasionally spotted on the gorgonian fans, and nudibranchs are also reported on the reef. Note: garden eels are not a Coral Garden species — they belong to the separate Garden Eels Point / Eel Garden site nearby, and large grouper sightings could not be confirmed specifically for this site, so both have been removed from this description.

## Dive Profile

Most divers spend the bulk of the dive in the shallow 5–12 m zone, where coral cover and fish density are reportedly highest, before working out toward the deeper drop-off or slope for divers who want more depth. Beginners should stick to the shallow plateau; more experienced divers can follow the slope deeper, keeping in mind that reported depths beyond the shallow zone vary by source. Allow adequate air for a safety stop before ascending.

## Entry and Exit

Reach Menjangan Island by boat from Labuhan Lalang, northwest Bali (roughly 30–35 minutes by boat per operator reports). Entry via back roll or giant stride from the dive boat. Current strength is reported inconsistently across sources (see Site Information above), so follow your boat crew's guidance on entry point and SMB deployment for surface pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A frequently recommended site for new divers and underwater photographers
- The shallow reef in the 3–12 m zone is where most operators report the densest fish life
- Look for scorpionfish resting motionless on coral heads — they are well camouflaged
- Check the gorgonian fans for pygmy seahorses, though sightings are occasional rather than guaranteed
- Pair with another Menjangan site for a full-day itinerary, as most operators run Coral Garden alongside other nearby dives

## Safety Considerations

Titan triggerfish are present at this site and, like elsewhere in Menjangan, can become territorial around nesting sites — give them a wide berth if you notice defensive behavior. Reported current strength varies by source, so check conditions with your boat crew rather than assuming a mild current. Maintain neutral buoyancy to avoid damaging delicate coral structures. Dive within your certification limits.

## Photography

Coral Garden is regularly recommended for underwater photography thanks to its shallow, bright reef. Wide-angle shots of coral formations and fish schools work well in the shallow zone. Clownfish in the anemones make good portrait subjects, and macro shooters can look for nudibranchs and, occasionally, pygmy seahorses on the gorgonians — though the latter are not a guaranteed sighting.
