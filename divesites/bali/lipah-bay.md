# Lipah Bay

A relaxed sandy slope dive in the southern Amed area, known as a juvenile white-tip reef shark nursery, and mixing artificial reefs with natural coral patches, large bommies, and resident turtles.

## Overview

Lipah Bay is located in the southern part of the greater Amed diving area, accessible either by shore entry directly from the beach or via a short jukung (traditional outrigger boat) ride, depending on operator and tide. The site features a sandy slope with shallower coral (5-18m) giving way to a sponge garden at 25-40m, plus artificial reef structures colonized by coral through an active reef-restoration project (led by Ocean Gardener and local dive centers). The site offers relaxed diving with reliable turtle sightings, large moray eels, and abundant reef fish, and is well-suited to less experienced divers and snorkelers in its shallow sections.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Lipah Bay, Amed, East Bali, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-25 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 27-29°C
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season)

## Marine Life

Lipah Bay's reputation as a shark nursery holds up under fresh review: two independent sources this pass (Bali AQua, and a dedicated dive-site description page) confirm juvenile white-tip reef sharks are regularly found resting under large table corals in the shallow section of the reef. Note the correction to species: sources specify white-tip reef sharks specifically, not generic "reef sharks," and the earlier claim of adult reef sharks sheltering alongside the juveniles is not corroborated by any fresh source and has been removed — only the juvenile/nursery observation is supported. Sea turtles are regular sightings (species not consistently specified — "hawksbill and green" is plausible for the region but not confirmed site-specifically this pass, so is described more generally below). Large moray eels inhabit the bommies and artificial reef structures. Eagle rays and leopard morays are reported in the deeper sponge-garden zone by one source.

## Dive Profile

Enter from shore and descend onto the sandy slope. The site is a mix of terrain types -- navigate between artificial reef structures, natural coral patches, and the larger bommies. The bommies are decorated with gorgonias and barrel sponges and are worth circling for turtles and moray eels. The slope descends gradually to around 40 meters, but most of the interesting features are in the 5-25 meter range. This is a relaxed dive well suited to slow exploration.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry direct from the beach at Lipah Bay is available, or a short jukung boat ride from other points in Amed — sources describe both options in regular use, so do not assume shore-only access. Exit at the same point.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A good choice for newer divers, snorkelers, or for a relaxed second dive of the day
- Look under large table corals in the shallows for resting juvenile white-tip reef sharks — approach slowly and do not disturb the coral
- Turtles are commonly seen -- approach slowly and let them come to you
- Check around the artificial reef structures carefully for moray eels and other residents

## Safety Considerations

Lipah Bay is a straightforward dive with generally mild conditions. Currents are usually light. The sandy slope is easy to navigate, but maintain awareness of your depth as the bottom slopes gradually deeper. Standard safety practices apply -- maintain adequate air reserves and perform a safety stop.

## Photography

The resting juvenile white-tip reef sharks under the table corals and the turtles are the primary photographic attractions and are often cooperative subjects. The large moray eels in the bommies reward patient photographers. The artificial reef structures with their coral growth provide interesting compositions showing reef colonization. Wide-angle is recommended for turtles and reef-scape; macro for the invertebrates on the artificial structures.
