## Bulakan

A quiet muck-diving site near a shoreline temple in the Amed-Tulamben corridor, known among regular visitors for wunderpus and unusual nudibranchs, with a boulder-strewn entry that keeps it uncrowded.

## Overview

Bulakan is a shore-entry muck/macro site in the Amed-Tulamben corridor of northeast Bali — described by divers as the southeasternmost muck site of the Tulamben diving area, roughly 10 minutes' drive from Amed. It sits near a beachfront temple; long-time visitors note that shore facilities (dunk tanks, shelter, additional buildings) around the temple have grown over the years. **Correction from the previous version of this page**: Bulakan is a shore dive, not a boat dive, and no source found in this pass supports "Ghost Bay" as an alternate name for it — Ghost Bay (in front of Café Garam, north of Jemeluk Bay) appears in Amed dive-site listings as a separate, distinct site. The entry/exit here is notably "bouldery," and the site tends to be far less crowded than Amed and Tulamben's better-known sites, which regular visitors count among its appeal.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near a shoreline temple in the Amed-Tulamben corridor, northeast Bali (~10 minutes from Amed)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry — boulder-strewn entry/exit, described by divers as physically awkward but manageable
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters (approximate; no source in this pass gave a precise maximum depth for this specific site)
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 m
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

Confirmed by diver trip reports for this specific site: wunderpus (mimic octopus) — multiple individuals have been photographed here — and a range of nudibranchs, including Cuthona sp. ("sheep nudis"). Beyond these confirmed sightings, no site-specific source was found for garden eels, flying gurnard, blue-spotted stingrays, ghost pipefish, or cuttlefish at Bulakan specifically; the previous version of this page listed these species based on generic Amed muck-diving characteristics rather than confirmed Bulakan sightings, so they have been removed. As with other muck sites in the area, camouflaged scorpionfish and other small critters are likely but should be treated as possibilities rather than guarantees until further site-specific reports are found.

## Dive Profile

Enter from shore and work across the sandy/rubble substrate, watching for wunderpus and nudibranchs among the rubble and burrows. Cover the site methodically with a guide, who will be familiar with recent critter locations.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry near the temple; the beach here is boulder-strewn, so sturdy booties and careful footing are recommended. Exit is at the same point. Facilities near the entry point (dunk tanks, shelter) have been added over the years by whoever maintains the site.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your guide to look for wunderpus — it's one of the site's signature sightings
- Wear sturdy booties; the entry/exit is rocky and can be awkward with full kit
- Bulakan tends to be far quieter than the well-known Amed/Tulamben sites nearby
- Carry a torch to check burrows and rubble crevices for nudibranchs and other macro life

## Safety Considerations

The boulder entry/exit is the main hazard here — take it slowly, especially with camera gear. Camouflaged scorpionfish and similar species are a possibility on muck substrate generally — never place hands on the bottom. Maintain positive buoyancy and good fin control to avoid stirring up the substrate.

## Photography

Macro is the priority here — a 100mm lens with diopter suits nudibranchs, and a mid-range macro/wide setup works well for wunderpus, which can move and change shape quickly. Given the currently limited confirmed critter list, treat this as an exploratory macro dive rather than one built around a single headline subject.
