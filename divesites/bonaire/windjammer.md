# Windjammer

*Iron-hulled sailing ship Mairi Bhan, finally lost in 1912 off what is now the BOPEC terminal — lies on her starboard side in deep, technical-only water. Sources disagree on the exact cause of her sinking and on her tonnage; both are noted below rather than guessed at.*

## Overview

The "Windjammer" is the wreck of the Mairi Bhan (sometimes written Mairi Bahn), a three-masted iron-hulled sailing ship built in 1874 by Barclay, Curle & Co. in Glasgow — 239 feet (73 m) long with a 37-foot (11 m) beam. Sources disagree on her tonnage: DreamWrecks gives 378 tons, while NUMA cites a displacement of roughly 1,315 tons; neither figure has been independently confirmed against a shipping register, so both are noted here rather than one being asserted as fact. She sailed the England–India trade before being sold to an Italian shipping firm; her final cargo is consistently reported as barrels of asphalt/tar, picked up in Trinidad. Accounts of her end also conflict: one version holds that she first grounded on the reef off northern Bonaire in 1905 and was then dislodged and capsized by a hurricane in 1912; another (NUMA) describes a 1912 storm in which the asphalt cargo broke open and caught fire from a lamp, with the loss of several crew. Divers-Guide corroborates only the 1912 sinking date and the tar-barrel cargo. Given the conflicting and largely unverifiable secondary accounts, take the "ghost ship" narrative popular among Rincon fishermen as folklore rather than history. She rests on her starboard side; reported depths to the wreck range from roughly 43 to 61 meters (140–200 feet) depending on the source and which part of the structure is referenced. This is **not a recreational dive** — it sits well beyond no-decompression limits on air or standard nitrox, and access is restricted by the active oil terminal above.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northwest Bonaire, in front of the BOPEC oil terminal
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Technical
- **Maximum Depth**: 55 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–25 meters (Divers-Guide), averaging around 20 meters
- **Current**: Often strong, variable — Divers-Guide specifically warns that a northeasterly current can push divers toward the active oil terminal
- **Best Time**: Determined entirely by sea state and permit availability — not a casual dive

## Marine Life

Reports of marine life at this site are sparser than at Bonaire's recreational sites, given the depth and restricted access. NUMA describes margates (grunts) and grouper around the coral-covered hull. Other accounts mention schools of jacks and a couple of large barracuda near the structure, and Divers-Guide notes parrotfish on the shallower reef above the wreck. Macro detail is secondary here — this is a wide-angle wreck-profile dive.

## Dive Profile

The wreck lies on her starboard side. Divers-Guide places the top of the structure at approximately 48 meters, with the highest point of the rigging reaching toward 60 meters; the main hull continues deeper, and the seabed further still. The combination of strong current, depth, and the ship's exposed orientation places this firmly in technical-diving territory: a multi-gas profile with substantial decompression obligation is the norm.

## Entry and Exit

Access requires a BOPEC permit and an accredited technical operator with boat support — the wreck sits directly off an operational oil-handling terminal. Shore access is not feasible. Plan around terminal operations and sea state.

## Tips

- This is a technical dive: full trimix certification, redundant gas, decompression planning, surface support, and a guide familiar with the site are all expected
- Do not attempt on standard recreational training
- Confirm BOPEC permit status before traveling — access has been periodically restricted
- The wreck is heavily exposed to current; surface conditions can deteriorate quickly

## Safety

Depth, current, decompression obligation, and proximity to an active industrial terminal each contribute risk. Penetration of the hull is not advised without specific wreck-penetration training and dedicated equipment — the iron structure has been submerged for over a century. Always carry redundant gas and SMBs.

## Photography

The rigging and hull profile photograph dramatically against open water for divers who can capture them, particularly with another diver for scale. Strobes are essential at depth; ambient light at 55+ meters is minimal even in clear water.

## Nearby Sites

The Windjammer site sits offshore from the BOPEC area on Bonaire's northwest coast. Karpata, La Dania's Leap, and Carel's Vision are the nearest recreational-depth sites along the same coastline, but none are functional alternatives — they are wholly different dives.
