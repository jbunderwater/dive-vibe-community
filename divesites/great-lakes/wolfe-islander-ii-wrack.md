## Wolfe Islander II (Wrack)

The Wolfe Islander II is a deliberately sunk ferry wreck in Lake Ontario near Kingston, Ontario. This former Wolfe Island passenger and vehicle ferry was scuttled as a diving attraction in 1985, creating one of the most accessible and diver-friendly artificial reefs in the Kingston area. The familiar form of a mid-20th-century ferry makes this wreck approachable and interesting for divers of all experience levels.

## Overview

The Wolfe Islander II was a steel-hulled passenger and vehicle ferry, built in 1946, that served the crossing between Kingston and Wolfe Island — the same route its successor ferries serve today. Retired from service, she was deliberately scuttled near Dawson's Point off Wolfe Island on September 21, 1985 to create a recreational dive site, after hazardous materials were stripped out and interior hatches removed to allow safer swim-through access. The wreck has since become one of the most popular dives in the Kingston area. Sources describe the maximum depth differently — most put the bottom at approximately 21 metres (70 feet), with the superstructure starting around 12 metres (40 feet), though a few dive-shop accounts cite depths as great as 26 metres (85 feet); divers should treat the deeper figure as an outer bound rather than the typical profile. The vessel's familiar 20th-century form — a flat-deck car ferry with wheelhouse and passenger spaces — makes navigation intuitive and the dive engaging even for recreational divers without specific wreck experience. The Kingston area's visibility (5–10 metres) makes this a good dive in most weather conditions.

## Entry and Exit

Charter dive boats from Kingston Harbour and surrounding marinas access the Wolfe Islander II regularly. Kingston has excellent dive charter infrastructure. The site is also sometimes accessible by arrangement for club dives. Entry by giant stride. Deploy SMB on ascent.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The Wolfe Islander II complements the 19th-century wooden schooners of the Kingston area with 20th-century steel ferry character
- The ferry's large scale means repeat visits to different sections are highly worthwhile
- Drysuit recommended for cold Lake Ontario bottom temperatures
- The car deck is a dramatic diving experience — the scale of the vehicle spaces is impressive underwater
- Combine with George A. Marsh or the Frontenac for a full Kingston wreck day

## Safety Considerations

Lake Ontario boat traffic near Kingston requires careful ascent planning. Always deploy an SMB before ascending and ascend close to the vessel line or anchor line. Cold water demands thermal protection. The large scale of the wreck can cause disorientation — maintain reference to recognizable features. Dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The Wolfe Islander II's large scale and 20th-century construction provide different photographic subjects from the historic wooden wrecks nearby. The cavernous car deck, wheelhouse, and bow profile are dramatic wide-angle subjects.
