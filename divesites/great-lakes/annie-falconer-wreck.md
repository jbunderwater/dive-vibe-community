## Annie Falconer (Wreck)

The Annie Falconer is a well-known 19th-century schooner wreck resting in Lake Ontario near Kingston, Ontario, Canada. One of dozens of sailing vessels that met their end in Lake Ontario's treacherous waters, the Annie Falconer is prized by local divers for its relatively good preservation and accessible depth.

## Overview

The Annie Falconer was a two-masted wooden schooner built in Kingston, Ontario in 1867. She sank on November 12, 1904, between False Duck and Timber Islands while carrying a cargo of coal. She now rests upright on a mud bottom in approximately 24 metres of cold Lake Ontario freshwater; the stern has broken off but lies visible nearby, and deadeyes, anchors, the ship's wheel, blocks, and chain are still present on the wreck. The site was discovered by divers in August 1975. The Kingston, Ontario area is one of the most wreck-rich diving destinations in North America, with dozens of historic vessels accessible to recreational divers, and cold, dark freshwater conditions have preserved the hull well. Visibility is typically 5–10 metres.

## Entry and Exit

Charter dive boats operate from Kingston's harbour and nearby marinas. Several operators in Kingston specialize in wreck diving and offer guided dives to the Annie Falconer and neighbouring wrecks. Entry by giant stride or back roll. Deploy SMB on ascent.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The Kingston area has numerous wrecks in similar depth ranges — consider combining dives on a charter trip
- Drysuit recommended; Lake Ontario bottom water is consistently cold
- The wreck sits on a silty bottom — excellent buoyancy control helps maintain visibility
- The hull is fragile in places; avoid touching or fin-kicking wooden sections
- Heritage protection laws apply — artifacts must not be removed

## Safety Considerations

Lake Ontario is generally calmer than Lake Erie or Lake Huron but still subject to weather-driven wave action. Boat traffic around Kingston is significant — always ascend on a line or deploy an SMB early. Cold water demands proper thermal protection. Dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The upright profile of the Annie Falconer lends itself to dramatic wreck photography. Swim back to capture the entire hull in frame, or move in close for detail shots of preserved rigging and deck hardware. Natural light from above creates moody silhouette opportunities in the upper water column.
