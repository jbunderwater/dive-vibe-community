## Diamond Shoals

Diamond Shoals is a reef dive site in North Carolina, North America.

## Overview

Diamond Shoals is a series of constantly shifting sandbars extending 13 kilometers from Cape Hatteras into the Atlantic, at the heart of the notorious "Graveyard of the Atlantic" where the Gulf Stream and the Labrador Current collide. Responsible for an estimated 600 shipwrecks, the shoals offer challenging conditions with strong currents, variable visibility, and cold-water upwellings. Sand tiger sharks aggregate here in large numbers during summer, and the convergence of warm and cold currents brings an unusual mix of tropical and temperate marine species to a single site.

## Marine Life

The collision of the Gulf Stream and the cold Labrador Current at Cape Hatteras creates one of the most productive marine environments on the US East Coast. East Carolina University, NOAA, and the NC Aquariums run an acoustic telemetry program that has deployed receivers on area shipwrecks — including the Caribsea, Proteus, Tarpon, Atlas, Papoose, and Schurz — to track tagged sand tiger sharks, which show extended residency at these sites. The convergence zone draws both warm-water Gulf Stream species and cold-water temperate species into the same area.

## Dive Profile

Diamond Shoals itself is not a structured wreck dive site — it is a geographic hazard consisting of constantly shifting sandbars in 15 m or less of water. Nearby wrecks in the general area (Caribsea at 90 ft, Atlas at 90–130 ft) are the actual dive destinations. The shoals themselves offer extremely difficult conditions with strong, unpredictable currents from the converging Gulf Stream and Labrador Current. This site is listed as Advanced due to the demanding current conditions.

## Entry and Exit

Charter access from Hatteras, NC. Dive Hatteras is the primary local dive operator based in Hatteras village, running trips to the wrecks in and around the Cape Hatteras area. No shore access to offshore dive sites is possible.

## Safety Considerations

Diamond Shoals is one of the most hydrodynamically complex environments in the western North Atlantic — strong, unpredictable currents from two major ocean current systems converge here. Conditions can deteriorate rapidly. This is an area for experienced divers only, always with an experienced local charter operator who monitors real-time conditions. The shoals are responsible for an estimated 600 shipwrecks — respect the conditions that created the Graveyard of the Atlantic.
