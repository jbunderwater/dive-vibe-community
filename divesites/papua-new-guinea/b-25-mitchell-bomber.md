# B-25 Mitchell Bomber

WWII B-25D serial 41-30118, wartime-named "Elusive Lizzie" and later "Miss America" -- known to today's dive operators as "Green Dragon" -- shot down by anti-aircraft fire on 4 or 5 August 1943 (sources differ by one day) while attacking Madang Airfield. Port engine missing, with four nose guns and concretion-encrusted ammunition in place, an accessible cockpit, dorsal turret position, and open bomb bay.

## Overview

This B-25D Mitchell medium bomber, serial number 41-30118, flew and was lost under the wartime names "Elusive Lizzie" and "Miss America" according to Pacific Wrecks and Argunners Magazine; the "Green Dragon" name used by some Madang dive operators today appears to be a later, informal nickname for the dive site rather than a documented wartime identity. Piloted by Major Williston M. Cox Jr. of the 5th Air Force's 38th Bombardment Group, 405th Bombardment Squadron, the aircraft was shot down by Japanese anti-aircraft fire on 4 or 5 August 1943 (Michael McFadyen's site gives 4 August; Pacific Wrecks and Argunners Magazine give 5 August) during a low-level barge sweep and strike on Madang Airfield. The port engine was torn away by the anti-aircraft hit, and the aircraft ditched between Wangat Island (also spelled Wongat Island) and the New Guinea coast.

Of the crew of seven, flight engineer S/Sgt Raymond J. Zimmerman drowned when the aircraft went down. The rest of the crew swam to Wangat Island, where they were captured by Japanese forces and taken to a Kempeitai (military police) post at Amron for interrogation. On 31 August 1943, four of the crew -- Robert J. Koscelnak, Louis J. Rittaco, Hugh W. Anderson, and Robert L. Herry -- were executed. Major Cox was the sole member of his crew to survive as a prisoner of war; he was held until liberated in 1945. This account is corroborated independently by Pacific Wrecks and Argunners Magazine.

The wreck rests at 15-25 meters depth. The wreck is substantially intact -- the port engine is missing but the rest of the airframe is well-preserved. Four nose-mounted machine guns are still in place; the ammunition rounds are present but heavily encrusted with concretions. The cockpit is accessible and retains structural detail. The dorsal turret position is identifiable. Bombs remain in the open bomb bay. A large gorgonian sea fan has grown between the distinctive twin tail fins, adding a striking natural contrast to the wartime metal. Visibility is typically 8-10 meters, more limited than open-water sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Wangat Island, Madang Province, Papua New Guinea
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 8-10 meters
- **Current**: Mild
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

The aircraft has become an artificial reef over eight decades. The gorgonian sea fan growing between the twin tail fins is the most distinctive marine growth on the wreck. Coral and sponge encrustation covers much of the airframe. Small reef fish shelter around the fuselage, wings, and engine nacelles. The surrounding waters off Wangat Island support the typical Madang marine community.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the aircraft at 15-25 meters. The visibility is typically limited to 8-10 meters, so the wreck may not be visible from the surface -- follow a guide or descent line. Begin at the nose section where the four machine guns are still mounted, complete with ammunition belts. Move aft to the cockpit area, which can be peered into for structural details. Continue past the dorsal turret position along the fuselage to the distinctive twin tail fins, where the large gorgonian sea fan makes a dramatic photographic subject. Note the missing port engine -- the starboard engine remains in place. The depth range of 15-25 meters allows a comfortable dive duration of 30-40 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by dive boat from Madang-based operators, a short ride to Wangat Island. Entry is by giant stride. The harbour/island location provides sheltered conditions. Surface near the wreck area for boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The four nose guns with ammunition are the most historically striking feature -- examine them closely but do not touch
- The gorgonian between the twin tail fins is the signature photograph of this wreck -- position yourself for a wide-angle shot
- Visibility is limited (8-10 meters), so a torch is essential for illuminating details and bringing out colours
- Combine with the nearby Henry Leith wreck for a Wangat Island double-wreck day
- The cockpit is accessible but tight -- approach with care and maintain buoyancy to avoid stirring silt

## Safety Considerations

The ammunition in the nose guns is live ordnance -- observe from a close but safe distance and do not touch. Visibility is limited, making buddy contact and navigation important. A torch is essential. The moderate depth allows comfortable dive times without decompression concerns for most profiles. This is a WWII crash site -- treat it with appropriate respect and do not remove any artifacts.
