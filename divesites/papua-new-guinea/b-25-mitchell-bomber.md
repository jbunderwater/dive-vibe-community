# B-25 Mitchell Bomber

USAAF B-25D-5 serial 41-30118 -- officially recorded by Pacific Wrecks as "Elusive Lizzie / Miss America," and known informally to divers as the "Green Dragon" -- shot down by anti-aircraft fire on 5 August 1943 while attacking Madang Airfield. Of the crew of six, only the command pilot survived as a POW; one man drowned in the crash and four were executed. Port engine missing, with four nose guns and concretion-encrusted ammunition in place, an accessible cockpit, dorsal turret position, and open bomb bay.

## Overview

This B-25D-5 Mitchell medium bomber, serial number 41-30118, is catalogued by Pacific Wrecks under its wartime names "Elusive Lizzie" and later "Miss America." The name "Green Dragon" appears in some local dive references (including a 2013 video of the site), but it is not the aircraft's documented USAAF name -- it appears to be a dive-community nickname of unconfirmed origin, and is noted here only as such.

Piloted on its final mission by command pilot Major Williston M. Cox Jr., the aircraft took off from 17-Mile (Durand) airstrip near Port Moresby on 5 August 1943 for a barge sweep and strike against Madang Airfield. Damaged by anti-aircraft fire, it ditched between Wangat Island (also spelled Wongat Island) and the New Guinea coast. Of the six-man crew, engineer S/Sgt Raymond J. Zimmerman drowned inside the aircraft during the crash. The other five swam to Wangat Island, where they were captured by Japanese forces. On 31 August 1943, four of them -- 2nd Lt Robert J. Koscelnak, 1st Lt Louis J. Rittaco, T/Sgt Hugh W. Anderson, and pilot Capt. Robert L. Herry -- were executed at Amron. Major Cox was the only member of the crew to survive as a prisoner of war. These facts are corroborated across Pacific Wrecks, Argunners Magazine, and TracesOfWar's Amron Mission record.

The aircraft rests at 15-25 meters depth. The wreck is substantially intact -- the port engine is missing but the rest of the airframe is well-preserved. Four nose-mounted machine guns are still in place; the ammunition rounds are present but heavily encrusted with concretions. The cockpit is accessible and retains structural detail. The dorsal turret position is identifiable. Bombs remain in the open bomb bay. A large gorgonian sea fan has grown between the distinctive twin tail fins, adding a striking natural contrast to the wartime metal. Visibility is typically 8-10 meters, more limited than open-water sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Wangat Island, Madang Province, Papua New Guinea
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 8-10 meters
- **Current**: Mild
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

The aircraft has become an artificial reef over eight decades. The gorgonian sea fan growing between the twin tail fins is the most distinctive marine growth on the wreck. Coral and sponge encrustation covers much of the airframe. Small reef fish shelter around the fuselage, wings, and engine nacelles. The surrounding waters off Wangat Island support the typical Madang marine community.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the aircraft at 15-25 meters. The visibility is typically limited to 8-10 meters, so the wreck may not be visible from the surface -- follow a guide or descent line. Begin at the nose section where the four machine guns are still mounted, complete with ammunition belts. Move aft to the cockpit area, which can be peered into for structural details. Continue past the dorsal turret position along the fuselage to the distinctive twin tail fins, where the large gorgonian sea fan makes a dramatic photographic subject. Note the missing port engine -- the starboard engine remains in place. The depth range of 15-25 meters allows a comfortable dive duration of 30-40 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by dive boat from Madang-based operators, a short ride to Wangat Island. Entry is by giant stride. The harbour/island location provides sheltered conditions. Surface near the wreck area for boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The four nose guns with ammunition are the most historically striking feature -- examine them closely but do not touch
- The gorgonian between the twin tail fins is the signature photograph of this wreck -- position yourself for a wide-angle shot
- Visibility is limited (8-10 meters), so a torch is essential for illuminating details and bringing out colours
- Combine with the nearby Henry Leith wreck for a Wangat Island double-wreck day
- The cockpit is accessible but tight -- approach with care and maintain buoyancy to avoid stirring silt

## Safety Considerations

The ammunition in the nose guns is live ordnance -- observe from a close but safe distance and do not touch. Visibility is limited, making buddy contact and navigation important. A torch is essential. The moderate depth allows comfortable dive times without decompression concerns for most profiles. This is a WWII crash site -- treat it with appropriate respect and do not remove any artifacts.
