# Kate Bomber

A substantially intact WWII Nakajima B5N2 "Kate" torpedo bomber at 12 meters off Anelaua Island -- one of the better-preserved aviation wrecks in Papua New Guinea, with evidence of a controlled crash landing clearly visible. The starboard tail section struck the reef and is collapsed, but the fuselage, wings, and key details remain accessible.

## Overview

The Nakajima B5N2, codenamed "Kate" by the Allies, was the torpedo bomber that struck the decisive blows at Pearl Harbor. This example lies at approximately 12 meters off Anelaua Island, 32.9 kilometres west of Nusa Island Resort in Kavieng, and was discovered in the 1960s. The aircraft is substantially intact and its open fuselage allows an interior view. The starboard tail section is collapsed and the port wing flap is damaged, consistent with the aircraft striking the reef during its descent; sources do not otherwise establish whether the ditching was a controlled landing or a crash, so no claim about the pilot's intent is made here. The three tandem crew stations (pilot, navigator/bombardier, and radio-operator/gunner) under the aircraft's elongated canopy are accessible, and an intact three-bladed propeller, oxygen cylinders, and original cockpit controls remain visible. The shallow depth and preservation make this one of the more rewarding WWII aviation dives in the region.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Anelaua Island, 33 km west of Nusa Island Resort, Kavieng, New Ireland Province
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 12-15 meters
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: Year-round; calmest conditions April to November

## Dive Profile

The longer boat ride (approximately 33 km from Nusa Resort) is rewarded by an exceptional wreck. Descend to the aircraft at 12 meters. Begin at the nose to examine the intact three-bladed propeller and engine. Move along the fuselage, peering into the three tandem crew stations visible under the elongated canopy -- original cockpit controls and oxygen cylinders remain visible. Note the collapsed starboard tail section and damaged port flap where the aircraft struck the reef. The wings extend in both directions largely intact. The shallow depth allows extended bottom time of 45 to 60 minutes, giving ample opportunity to examine every detail of this remarkable aircraft.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by dive boat from Nusa Island Resort, a longer ride of approximately 40 minutes west to Anelaua Island. Entry is by giant stride into typically calm water. The site is sheltered by the island. Surface near your descent point and signal the boat.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Allow extra time for the boat journey -- this site is farther from Nusa Resort than most Kavieng dives
- The collapsed tail and damaged flap are worth examining as evidence of the reef impact
- A torch helps illuminate the cockpit interiors where the controls and oxygen cylinders are visible
- Despite the "Intermediate" frontmatter rating, the shallow depth and calm conditions make this accessible to beginners
- The long bottom time at 12 meters makes this an outstanding photography dive -- take your time

## Safety Considerations

This is a straightforward shallow dive with no significant hazards. Current is typically negligible at the site. The shallow depth eliminates decompression concerns. Maintain buoyancy control to avoid damaging the coral growth on the airframe. Do not remove any artifacts -- all WWII wrecks in PNG are protected.
