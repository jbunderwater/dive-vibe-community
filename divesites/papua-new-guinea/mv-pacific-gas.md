# MV Pacific Gas

A deliberately sunk former LPG carrier on the Sunken Barrier Reef off Port Moresby -- originally the MS Nanayo Maru, built in Hiroshima in 1967 -- with the bow in 18 meters and the stern in 43 meters, and marine growth that rivals the encrustation found on Chuuk Lagoon's wrecks.

## Overview

The MV Pacific Gas began life as the MS Nanayo Maru, a liquefied gas carrier built in 1967 in Hiroshima, Japan. Sold to an Australian operator and renamed Pacific Gas in 1972, she carried LPG between Australia and Papua New Guinea until she failed her annual survey in 1980 and was withdrawn from service. After sitting idle for several years, a group of local divers arranged for her to be deliberately sunk as a dive wreck: commercial diver and explosives expert Ian Short placed the charges, and the vessel went down in about 12 minutes in June 1986.

She now lies on the slope of the Sunken Barrier Reef (Horseshoe Reef) off Port Moresby, bow-first into the shallows and stern down the slope. The bow sits in about 18 meters, the wheelhouse/bridge area around 30 meters, and the stern in roughly 43 meters -- sources describe the overall depth range as 18 to 45 meters. Despite being sunk decades after the World War II wrecks of Chuuk Lagoon, the Pacific Gas has developed marine growth that several dive operators compare favorably to those far more famous wrecks.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Sunken Barrier Reef (Horseshoe Reef), off Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 45 meters
- **Bow Depth**: ~18 meters
- **Bridge/Wheelhouse Depth**: ~30 meters
- **Stern Depth**: ~43 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-20 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 26°C
- **Best Time**: April to November (dry season)

## Marine Life

The wreck supports impressive marine growth across its structure -- soft corals and Tubastrea (sun coral) cover the stern and lower sections, and the hull carries hydroids and sponges that compare favorably with the encrusted wrecks of Chuuk Lagoon. Near the bow, dive operators report ghost pipefish and leaf scorpionfish. The wheelhouse hosts snapper, sweetlips, puffer, and angelfish. One operator also reports a resident colony of flashlight fish on the wreck. Schools of jacks are commonly seen patrolling the water column around the superstructure.

## Dive Profile

This is a deep dive requiring careful gas management and planning. Most divers descend the mooring line to the bow or wheelhouse area (18-30 meters) -- the shallowest points of the wreck and the best starting position to maximize bottom time. From there, work aft along the deck toward the stern. The stern at roughly 43 meters is the deepest feature and should only be visited if gas supply and no-decompression time allow.

The engine room and lower holds are accessible for penetration, but this is an overhead environment at significant depth -- wreck penetration training, redundant gas supply, a guideline, and multiple lights are essential. Do not enter unless properly trained and equipped.

Return to the bow and wheelhouse area for the remainder of the dive, checking for ghost pipefish and leaf scorpionfish. Begin your ascent with adequate gas reserves for a safety stop -- given the depth, a minimum 5-minute stop at 5 meters is strongly recommended, and staged decompression may be required depending on bottom time.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by dive boat from Port Moresby-based operators (including Loloata Island Resort). The wreck is marked by a mooring buoy. Entry is by giant stride, with descent along the mooring line to the wreck. Surface conditions in Port Moresby harbor can include boat traffic -- maintain awareness during ascent and surface interval. Deploy an SMB during your safety stop.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Begin at the bow or wheelhouse (18-30 meters) and work deeper only as gas supply allows -- do not descend to the stern at 43 meters unless you have planned for the depth and potential deco obligation
- Check near the bow carefully for ghost pipefish and leaf scorpionfish -- they are well camouflaged against the encrusted surfaces
- The marine growth is comparable to many Chuuk Lagoon wrecks; take time to appreciate the soft coral and Tubastrea coverage on the stern and lower hull
- Nitrox is highly recommended to extend no-decompression limits in the 18-30 meter range, though it cannot be used at the deepest sections (43 meters exceeds EANx32 MOD)
- A dive torch reveals the true colors of the coral growth, which appears muted at depth under ambient light

## Safety Considerations

This is an advanced dive site. The maximum depth of roughly 43 meters at the stern severely limits bottom time on air and places divers at significant narcosis risk. Plan the dive conservatively and stick to the plan. The sloping bottom makes it easy to descend deeper than intended -- maintain constant depth awareness. Interior penetration is only for divers with wreck penetration certification, redundant lights, and ideally redundant gas. Visibility of 15-20 meters is adequate but not exceptional -- maintain buddy contact at all times. Water temperature of 26°C is comfortable but long safety stops or deco stops can still cause cooling.

## Photography

The MV Pacific Gas rewards both wide-angle and macro approaches. Wide-angle compositions of the bow and wheelhouse with diver models work well in the 15-20 meter visibility -- strobes are essential to bring out the color of the coral growth at depth. Ghost pipefish and leaf scorpionfish near the bow are macro highlights. The stern at 43 meters is dramatic but the depth limits the time available for careful composition. The dense soft coral and Tubastrea growth on the stern and lower hull provides texture and color for photographers who plan their gas for the depth.
