## M/V Transfer

M/V Transfer is a British cargo vessel that ran aground on Lighthouse Reef Atoll in 2005, now a developing artificial reef in Belize's most remote offshore atoll.

## Overview

The M/V Transfer, a British cargo vessel carrying part-containerised general cargo, ran aground on Lighthouse Reef Atoll on the night of January 6, 2005, at a spot reported as about 4.2 miles south of Sandbore Caye. 7 News Belize reporting attributes the grounding to a faulty autopilot that steered the ship into the reef, and identifies the owner as Ariane Shipping Company — this ownership detail appears in only that one source and has not been independently confirmed elsewhere. Sources describe the vessel's length as approximately 225 feet, though this figure comes from secondary reporting on the 7 News Belize article rather than a maritime registry, so treat it as approximate. Only about a third of the hull initially grounded; when Hurricane Emily passed through the region in July 2005, storm surge pushed additional sections of the wreck further onto the reef.

A Lloyd's-affiliated maritime law report (i-law.com) independently corroborates the core of this story from a different angle: it confirms the vessel ran aground on Belize's barrier reef and documents a failed 2006-era salvage attempt, in which a British insurance company paid roughly $500,000 toward removal, a local contractor (3-D Marine of Belize City) was engaged but the funds and contractor went missing, and a subsequent attempt using two Guatemalan tugs also failed to move the wreck — after which storm action pushed the hull further onto the reef, causing what the report describes as extensive coral damage. The wreck has since been slowly colonised with coral and sponge encrustation, hosting groupers, snappers, and reef fish, though no source itemizes a full species list specific to this wreck. Its remote Lighthouse Reef location means far fewer divers than mainland Belize sites, in waters with visibility regularly reaching 20-40 metres.

Penetration diving into any wreck carries risk; interior exploration should only be undertaken by properly trained and equipped divers.
