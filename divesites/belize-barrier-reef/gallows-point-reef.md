## Gallows Point Reef

Gallows Point Reef is a wall dive site off St. George's Caye, 10 miles east of Belize City, recognized as critical habitat for the critically endangered hawksbill sea turtle.

## Overview

Gallows Point Reef is a five-mile section of the Belize Barrier Reef stretching from the St. George's Caye channel south to South Gallows Point, about 10 miles east of Belize City — confirmed directly by ECOMAR's own site page. It is notable for its wall formations rather than the coral canyons typical of the northern cayes. Divers start over sandy flats at roughly 10 meters and reach the wall edge nearby; reported maximum depth varies by source and specific dive profile — PADI's single-site listing records a maximum of about 21 meters, while the outer wall face (the profile reflected in this destination's data) reaches roughly 33 meters. The wall face is draped in gorgonian sea fans and soft corals. ECOMAR has run in-water snorkel surveys at Gallow's Point since 2009 specifically to assess hawksbill sea turtle abundance, identifying it as important developmental habitat for juvenile, sub-adult, and adult hawksbills; ECOMAR describes it as the most abundant turtle species recorded at the site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off St. George's Caye, near Belize City, Belize
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters

## Marine Life

The critically endangered hawksbill sea turtle is the species most consistently recorded at this site, per ECOMAR's ongoing turtle-monitoring program, making it a recognized developmental habitat. PADI's site listing reports turtles as "most often seen," along with spotted eagle rays, snappers, angelfish, and trumpetfish. ScubaBoard divers on Sea Sports Belize trips have also reported eels and reef sharks at this site.
