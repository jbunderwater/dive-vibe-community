## Long Caye Aquarium

Long Caye Aquarium (commonly called "The Aquarium") is a wall dive off the western side of Lighthouse Reef Atoll — frequently cited by divers as the highlight of the standard Blue Hole day trip.

## Overview

Located off the western shore of Long Caye at Lighthouse Reef, The Aquarium begins as a flat table-top reef, covered in clusters of brain coral, purple sea fans, and groves of sea plumes near the mooring line. The reef edge then drops into a sheer wall — sources place the drop at approximately 11–15 metres depending on the account — that continues into very deep water; divers-guide.com records a maximum site depth of 42 metres, while PADI and most operator accounts describe recreational divers staying in the 10–18 metre range near the top of the wall, with the ledge itself continuing well beyond recreational limits. Coral ridges running perpendicular to the wall face are filled with cavities where marine life concentrates, and deep-water lace coral and black coral grow along the wall.

The site's name reflects the variety of invertebrate life reported here — shrimp among the coral crevices are frequently mentioned by dive shops — combined with consistently outstanding Lighthouse Reef visibility, typically 24–40 metres. It is a regular stop on Blue Hole day-trip itineraries, often dived as one of the day's later tanks.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Long Caye, Lighthouse Reef Atoll, Belize
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters

## Marine Life

Sources reporting on this specific site (divers-guide.com, PADI) list spotted eagle rays, sharks, green moray eels, barracuda, tarpon, grouper, parrotfish, sergeant major, pintano, queen triggerfish, spotted porcupinefish, cleaner shrimp, and turtles as sightings at The Aquarium. Schools of Bermuda chubs, sergeant majors, and creole wrasse are also commonly reported. Shark and turtle species are not identified beyond the generic terms used by these sources, so treat "reef shark" or "hawksbill" as unconfirmed for this exact site.
