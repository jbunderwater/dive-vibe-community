## Papa Whisky

Papa Whisky is an advanced wall dive on Tahiti's west coast, named after a small artificial navigational islet offshore of Faa'a airport that itself takes its name from the Papeete West (PW) aviation radio beacon in the NATO phonetic alphabet. The dive site is a natural formation near that islet — a dramatic amphitheatre where part of the reef barrier has collapsed, creating a drop-off and amphitheatre approximately 70 metres across. It is one of Tahiti's most-cited Advanced dives.

## Overview

Located 15 minutes by boat from the Punaauia/Papeete area, Papa Whisky descends from a reef starting at around 15 metres down to a maximum of 35–50 metres, where the collapsed plateau creates an open-ocean amphitheatre shape. The main dive is conducted at 25–35 m. The outer wall exposure brings oceanic current and the associated pelagic species and visibility — typically 25–30 metres. Water temperatures are 27–28°C. Due to the depth, Advanced Open Water certification or a Deep Dive specialty is required by operators.

## What to Expect

Large gorgonian sea fans — yellow ("gorgones jaunes") in particular — are a defining feature of the site, concentrated around 40 m, with the drop-off itself reaching 50 m. A dedicated dive-site guide (Scubago/DiveSSI) and Tahiti Tourisme both confirm grey reef, whitetip, blacktip and lemon sharks here, plus large-headed trevally and skipjack tuna cruising the amphitheatre; tiger sharks are reported occasionally by divers at depth but should not be treated as a reliable expectation. Note: eagle rays and turtles were listed in an earlier draft of this page but could not be independently confirmed for this specific site; they have been removed pending a source.

## Conditions

- **Best season**: April–November
- **Visibility**: 25–30 m typical
- **Current**: Moderate to strong; drift diving possible
- **Water temperature**: 27–28°C
- **Entry**: Boat dive; Advanced-only site

## Tips

This site requires Advanced Open Water certification at minimum, with deep diving experience recommended for the deeper amphitheatre sections. Shallower sections of the reef (around 15–20 m) provide a worthwhile experience for less experienced Advanced divers. The site is often paired with a second shallower dive in the same area.
