## Le Nordby

Le Nordby is a shipwreck dive site in the Raiatea lagoon, French Polynesia, and the only wreck dive in Raiatea. The *Nordby* was a three-masted Danish sailing vessel that ran aground and sank on 22 August 1900, and has since become a well-established artificial reef.

## Overview

The *Nordby* grounded on the Raiatea barrier reef on 22 August 1900, settling on the lagoon floor at depths of approximately 18–29 metres. Sources give conflicting accounts of the circumstances: some describe the vessel dragging its anchor onto the reef, while a Tahiti-focused wreck-diving article describes it grounding on a sandbank during a storm while en route from New Caledonia — the exact cause is not settled across sources. The vessel's length is also reported inconsistently: 50 m, 53 m, and 60 m have all been cited, with width given as 7–8 m; the precise figures are unconfirmed. The ship was built of steel/metal. Over 120 years of submersion, the hull has become a substantial artificial reef encrusted with corals and sponges. The wooden deck has completely disappeared, leaving the hull open and accessible. Two of the original three masts remain as stumps. The clear lagoon water provides visibility of 25–35 metres. Water temperatures average 27–28°C.

## What to Expect

The hull and mast stumps are colonised by encrusting corals, sponges, and hydroids. Surgeonfish, pennant coral fish (bannerfish), angelfish, jacks, and shrimps are consistently reported on the wreck across multiple dive-shop and dive-guide sources; a stonefish has also been noted by one operator. Nudibranchs and small loach-like fish are documented here as well. The accessible interior holds, open where the deck has decayed, allow divers to swim through the structure. The site is rated accessible to entry-level (Level 1 / Open Water) certified divers by multiple local operators, reflecting the moderate depth and calm lagoon conditions — though divers should still respect the 29 m maximum depth on the deeper sections. Note: earlier drafts of this page mentioned moray eels and stingrays at the site; neither species could be independently confirmed for the Nordby specifically and both have been removed pending a source.

## Conditions

- **Best season**: Year-round; April–November for best visibility
- **Visibility**: 25–35 m typical
- **Current**: Mild to none in lagoon setting
- **Water temperature**: 27–28°C
- **Entry**: Boat dive from Raiatea operators

## Tips

A torch greatly enhances the wreck interior — the hull's open structure allows swim-throughs to the holds. Night dives on the Nordby are recommended by local operators for different marine life encounters. The wreck's identity remained unknown for decades: until December 1996 a local journalist located the ship's name in notarial archives.
