## Rauto'Anui

Rauto'Anui is a pass drift dive in the Raiatea-Taha'a barrier reef system, French Polynesia. The Tahitian name translates roughly to "the large current" or "big channel flow," reflecting the powerful tidal circulation that defines this site.

## Overview

Rauto'Anui — locally also known as Miri Miri — is one of the productive pass dives within the shared Raiatea-Taha'a barrier reef. The pass cuts through the barrier reef and funnels tidal exchange between the ocean and the lagoon, with the strongest flows on the incoming tide. Local operators divide the Miri Miri area into several distinct dive profiles (a drift through the pass itself, a sheltered "Three Rocks" pinnacle site, and a calmer, current-free "Miri Miri Ocean" option), so conditions and depth vary by which section is dived. The channel depth reaches approximately 25 metres at the deepest point (temoanadiving.com records up to 25 m / 83 ft) before shoaling into the lagoon. Visibility on incoming oceanic water is typically 30–40 metres, with water temperatures of 27–28°C.

## What to Expect

Two independent operator sources (Te Moana Diving and Te Mara Nui) both confirm blacktip reef sharks as the consistent species here, with whitetip reef sharks also reported at the "Three Rocks" pinnacle. Schools of barracuda, jacks/trevally, and snappers are confirmed by multiple sources, along with Napoleon (humphead) wrasse. Leopard rays (not manta rays — an earlier draft's manta ray claim could not be confirmed and has been removed) are reported riding the current on the drift sections. Batfish, parrotfish, emperor angelfish and royal angelfish round out the confirmed species list. Note: earlier drafts also listed grey reef sharks, eagle rays, hawkfish, trumpetfish and moray eels for this specific pass — none of these were corroborated by the operator sources reviewed and they have been removed pending confirmation.

## Conditions

- **Best season**: April–November
- **Visibility**: 30–40 m on incoming tide
- **Current**: Strong (2–4 knots); incoming tide required
- **Water temperature**: 27–28°C
- **Entry**: Boat drift dive; timed to tidal cycle

## Tips

As with all Society Islands pass dives, timing the dive to a reliable incoming tide is non-negotiable for optimal conditions. Carry an SMB for safe boat pickup at the lagoon exit. Raiatea and Taha'a operators run this pass as part of multi-site dive programs — typically paired with a lagoon reef dive.
