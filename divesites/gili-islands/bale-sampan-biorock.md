## Bale Sampan Biorock

One of the Gili Islands' many Biorock coral restoration structures — a steel framework using low-voltage current to accelerate coral growth on the east coast of Gili Trawangan.

## Overview

Bale Sampan Biorock is one of the many Biorock structures placed in the waters around the Gili Islands since the program began in the mid-2000s (sources place the first installation and dive-shop recruitment anywhere from 2004 to 2006). This makes the Gilis one of the largest Biorock projects in the world, second only to Pemuteran, Bali — though the exact structure count varies by source, from just over 100 to around 127. The steel framework carries a low-voltage electrical current that stimulates limestone accretion — the natural substrate for hard coral — accelerating coral growth by roughly 3–5 times the normal rate and improving survival through bleaching events. No source individually documents the Bale Sampan structure's own history or colonisation separately from the wider east-coast cluster it belongs to at 15 m depth.

## Site Information

- **Location**: East coast Gili Trawangan, Gili Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef

- **Typical Visibility**: 15–20 m
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

No source names species confirmed at the Bale Sampan structure specifically. Operators describe the east-coast Biorock structures generally as hosting juvenile reef fish, scorpionfish, lionfish, damselfish, butterflyfish, triggerfish, and trumpetfish, with soft corals, sponges, tunicates, and bivalves on the frames — this regional Biorock fauna likely applies here too, but has not been confirmed at this specific frame.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the Biorock frame and circle the structure systematically at 5–15 m. Examine the coral growth on the steel framework and the fish sheltering within the structure. The dive is typically 40–50 minutes at the shallow depth. Ascend slowly and complete a standard safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Gili Trawangan. Short transit to the east coast. Entry via back roll or giant stride. Conditions are calm on the sheltered east coast. Return to the boat mooring after the dive.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your dive operator about the Biorock speciality course to learn about the technology and conservation role
- The structure demonstrates coral restoration in action — compare the growth to natural reef nearby
- Combine with a natural reef site like Turtle Point for a conservation-natural contrast day
- The shallow depth makes this ideal for underwater photography practice

## Safety Considerations

The steel frame can have sharp metal edges — do not touch the structure. Low-voltage current is safe and undetectable by humans. Maintain neutral buoyancy near the structure. Lionfish on the frame are venomous — observe from a safe distance.

## Photography

The Biorock structure provides a distinctive photographic subject showing reef restoration technology in action, with coral growth patterns contrasting against the steel geometry.
