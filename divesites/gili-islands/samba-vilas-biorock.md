## Samba Vilas Biorock

A Biorock coral restoration structure on Gili Trawangan's east coast, part of one of the world's largest Biorock reef restoration projects.

## Overview

Samba Vilas Biorock is one of the cluster of Biorock structures positioned along the east coast of Gili Trawangan, in the sheltered, calm water typical of this side of the island. The Gili Islands Biorock program began in the mid-2000s under the Gili Eco Trust (sources place the first installation and dive-shop recruitment anywhere from 2004 to 2006). No source individually documents how this particular structure's coral and fish community compares with its neighbours — the description below reflects what is generally reported for the Biorock cluster as a whole. Biorock installations are reported to increase local marine biodiversity and help stabilise substrate compared to bare sand.

## Site Information

- **Location**: East coast Gili Trawangan, Gili Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–20 m
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

No source names species confirmed at the Samba Vilas structure specifically. Operators describe the east-coast Biorock structures generally as hosting juvenile reef fish, scorpionfish, lionfish, damselfish, butterflyfish, triggerfish, and trumpetfish, with soft corals, sponges, tunicates, and bivalves on the frames — this regional Biorock fauna likely applies here too, but has not been confirmed at this specific frame.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the structure and explore its coral coverage. Examine the frame systematically for residents. Extend the dive to include other nearby Biorock structures in the east coast cluster before ascending for the safety stop. Multiple structures can be visited in a single dive.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Gili Trawangan east coast. Calm, sheltered conditions. Entry via back roll or giant stride. Straightforward return to mooring.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The east coast Biorock cluster has multiple structures that can all be visited in a single dive
- Good site for beginner divers to explore a structured and calm environment
- Night dives on the Biorocks reveal octopus, crabs, and other nocturnal species
- The conservation story is worth understanding — talk to your operator about the Gili Eco Trust
- Works well as a second dive after a deeper natural reef dive when air budget is lower

## Safety Considerations

Standard precautions for artificial structure dives — no touching the frame, maintain buoyancy, watch for lionfish. The low-voltage current is safe and harmless. Keep fins well clear of the structure to prevent damaging growing coral colonies.

## Photography

The Biorock frames covered in growing coral make visually interesting abstract subjects, and fish portraits against the coral-encrusted steel frame create distinctive backgrounds uncommon in natural reef photography.
