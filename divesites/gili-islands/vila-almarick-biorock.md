## Vila Almarick Biorock

One of the many Biorock reef-restoration structures on the east coast of Gili Trawangan — part of the island's extensive coral rehabilitation program.

## Overview

Vila Almarick Biorock is one of the many named structures in the east coast Gili Trawangan Biorock cluster (its name most likely reflects a nearby property, in keeping with how these structures are commonly labelled locally, though no source specifically confirms the naming). Like all structures in the Gili Islands Biorock network, it uses low-voltage electrical current to stimulate mineral accretion on steel frames, accelerating coral growth. Sources place the start of the Gili Islands Biorock program in the mid-2000s — accounts vary between 2004 and 2006 for the first installations — run under the Gili Eco Trust with participating local dive shops. No source individually documents this specific structure's history or marine life separately from the wider cluster; the description below reflects what is generally reported for the Biorock sites as a group. The shallow depth makes this cluster particularly accessible for beginner divers and snorkellers.

## Site Information

- **Location**: East coast Gili Trawangan, Gili Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–20 m
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

No source names species seen specifically at the Vila Almarick structure. Operators describe the Biorock structures on Gili Trawangan's east coast generally as hosting juvenile reef fish, scorpionfish, lionfish, damselfish, butterflyfish, triggerfish, and trumpetfish, with soft corals, sponges, tunicates, and bivalves growing on the frames, and stingrays occasionally seen on the surrounding sand — this regional Biorock fauna likely applies here too, but has not been confirmed at this specific frame.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the structure and explore systematically. Circle the frame examining coral density and resident fish communities. The shallow depth allows extended bottom time. Extend the dive to include other nearby east coast Biorock structures accessible within a short swim.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Gili Trawangan east coast. Sheltered conditions. Entry via back roll. Easily combined with the other east coast Biorock structures in a single dive.

## Tips and Recommendations

- An ideal site for combining multiple Biorock structures in a single dive — swim between them
- Works as a relaxed afternoon dive after more demanding morning sites
- Suitable for non-certified divers on a Discovery Scuba session in the shallows
- Night dives are possible and add a different perspective on the reef community
- The east coast cluster includes several named structures (Bale Sampan, Samba Vilas, and others) that can all be visited on one dive — none is individually distinguished by species mix, so pick based on convenience

## Safety Considerations

Standard artificial reef precautions apply — maintain buoyancy, do not touch the frame. Lionfish on the structure are venomous. Low-voltage current is safe for divers. Fin placement awareness near growing coral structures.

## Photography

Fish and coral-growth portraits against the coral-covered steel geometry are the distinctive shot at any Biorock frame, including this one. The shallow depth allows natural light photography without strobe in the upper structure sections.
