## Trawangan Dive Biorock

One of the many Biorock reef-restoration structures on Gili Trawangan's east coast — its OSM name suggests an association with the Trawangan Dive operation, though no source specifically confirms which structure they maintain.

## Overview

Trawangan Dive Biorock sits within the east-coast Biorock cluster on Gili Trawangan. Trawangan Dive is confirmed by its own site as a partner in the Gili Eco Trust's Biorock initiative and offers Biorock specialty courses, but no source identifies this particular structure as their specific installation or a "flagship" site — that framing has been removed as unconfirmed. Biorock installations in general are reported to grow coral roughly 3–5 times faster than the surrounding natural reef, with higher survival through bleaching events, and the program traces back to the mid-2000s (sources vary between 2004 and 2006).

## Site Information

- **Location**: East coast Gili Trawangan, Gili Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–20 m
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

No source names species confirmed at this structure specifically. Operators describe the east-coast Biorock structures generally as hosting juvenile reef fish, scorpionfish, lionfish, damselfish, butterflyfish, triggerfish, and trumpetfish, with soft corals, sponges, tunicates, and bivalves on the frames — this regional Biorock fauna likely applies here too, but has not been confirmed at this specific frame.

## Dive Profile

Descend from the boat to the Biorock structure. Circle it systematically while observing coral growth zones and resident species. Total dive time 40–55 minutes. Ascend for a standard 3-minute safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Gili Trawangan operators. Calm east coast conditions. Entry via back roll.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Trawangan Dive's Biorock specialty course is an established way to learn about and contribute to reef restoration in the area, whether or not it covers this exact structure
- Ask your dive guide about coral gardening opportunities during the dive
- Combine with a natural reef site for a conservation-natural contrast day

## Safety Considerations

Maintain neutral buoyancy near the coral-covered frame to avoid fin strikes. Sharp metal edges may be present on the structure. Lionfish are venomous — observe without contact. Low-voltage electrical current in the system is completely safe for divers and marine life.

## Photography

The Biorock frame makes a compelling documentary subject showing reef restoration in action. Macro on the coral growth patterns and encrusting organisms on the frame is rewarding.
