## Shark Point

One of the Gili Islands' most dramatic dives — deep ridges dropping to 50+ m on Gili Trawangan's northwest, with reliable blacktip and whitetip reef sharks and resting green and hawksbill turtles.

## Overview

Shark Point is located on the northwest side of Gili Trawangan, where the reef slope transitions to deep ridges and canyons that plunge beyond 50 m into the Lombok Strait. This is one of the most exhilarating dive sites in the Gili Islands, featuring reliable encounters with both black and whitetip reef sharks — resting under ledges and cruising in open water — along with green and hawksbill turtles resting in the shallower sections. The site is always a drift dive. The Glenn Nusa wreck — a tugboat intentionally sunk in February 2016 — lies on the sandy bottom between Shark Point and the adjacent Halik dive site at a depth of roughly 28–30 m, and is accessible as an extension of a Shark Point dive when currents allow. Most operator sources describe the vessel as 22 metres long, though one listing gives 18 metres — the exact figure is unconfirmed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northwest Gili Trawangan, Gili Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–30 m
- **Current**: Strong; always a drift dive
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Blacktip and whitetip reef sharks are the headline species, showing up either resting under ledges and in caves or cruising in open water along the canyon systems. Green and hawksbill turtles frequent the shallow reef crest at 5–12 m. Schools of jackfish, snappers, oriental sweetlips, and humphead parrotfish aggregate around the canyon heads and reef structure, and octopus are also found here.

## Dive Profile

Begin on the deep side at 25–30 m near the canyon systems, then gradually ascend through the canyons exploring fish life. Ascend via the ridge systems to the shallower area and spend time in the 5–12 m turtle zone before completing the safety stop. Current carries divers toward shore. If conditions allow and the Glenn Nusa wreck is on the dive plan, this is normally scheduled as a separate dive given the current dependency.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Gili Trawangan dive operators. The site is always a drift — enter upstream and exit wherever the current delivers you to the shallows. Coordinate closely with the boat crew on pickup point. SMB essential.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Advanced Open Water minimum; experience with strong drift diving is essential
- The 5–12 m turtle shallows are the perfect reward for an intense deep dive
- Ask your operator about combining with the nearby Glenn Nusa wreck (tugboat, sunk 2016, sitting at roughly 28–30 m; length reported as 22 m by most sources); it is a separate dive requiring current assessment
- Dive on neap tides for more manageable current speeds
- Evening dives in the shallows offer the best turtle encounters when they settle for the night

## Safety Considerations

Strong currents are constant and can accelerate suddenly. Do not fight the current — drift with it and ascend to shallower water if the current is too fast at depth. Deep ridges descend beyond recreational limits — stay aware of depth. Always carry an SMB and be prepared to surface away from the boat.

## Photography

Whitetip reef sharks resting on the ridge slopes are the signature shot. Turtle portraits in the shallow zone are the accessible highlight. The deep canyon sections suit wide-angle with divers silhouetted against the blue water.
