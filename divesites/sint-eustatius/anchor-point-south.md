# Anchor Point South

A reef slope on Statia's southern leeward coast centered on a single, striking 18th-century French anchor — physical evidence of the hundreds of merchant ships that once crowded the Golden Rock roadstead.

## Overview

Anchor Point South sits on the leeward western side of Sint Eustatius, in waters that were once among the busiest anchorages in the Atlantic world. During Statia's commercial peak in the 1770s — when the island processed more cargo per capita than anywhere in the Western Hemisphere — hundreds of vessels anchored in these waters simultaneously. Divertrek's site-specific account describes the centerpiece as a single French anchor dated to roughly 1750–1775, over 3 meters long and weighing more than a ton, with red and orange corals growing across it in contrast to the surrounding blue water. STENAPA (Sint Eustatius National Parks Foundation) manages the marine protected area and all artifacts are strictly protected under Dutch law. The reef itself features volcanic boulder structures, hard coral formations, and sandy clearings with good invertebrate diversity. Visibility averages 20–30 meters.

## Marine Life

Scubaqua, an independent local operator, lists the site-specific sightings at Anchor Point as Spanish hogfish, drumfish, lobsters, Caribbean reef sharks, and moray eels — this replaces the previous generic regional list (yellowtail snapper, blue tang, French angelfish, garden eels, hawksbill turtles), none of which was confirmed for this specific site. Statia's diving receives relatively little traffic compared to most Caribbean islands, and marine life is reported as notably less accustomed to divers than at busier destinations.

## Dive Profile

Descent via mooring to the reef top, then navigate the slope following the guide toward the French anchor at approximately 18 meters. A typical dive covers 150–200 meters of reef before returning to the mooring. Maximum depth of 18 meters allows 50–60 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive operated by Golden Rock Dive Center or Scubaqua, Statia's main operators. Permanent mooring maintained by STENAPA. Entry by giant stride or backward roll. DSMB required for ascent.

## Tips and Recommendations

Carry a torch to examine the underside of the anchor's fluke, where cleaning shrimp and crustaceans concentrate in the shade. Statia's low diver traffic means the reef here feels unhurried — take time to explore the full depth range of the site rather than beelining to the anchor.

## Safety Considerations

All artifacts are protected — touching, moving, or collecting is illegal. Fire coral grows on some anchor metal — maintain appropriate distance. Current on the outer reef increases on incoming tides. Always carry a DSMB.

## Photography

Iron anchors against coral backdrop provide strong compositional elements. Wide-angle shots with an anchor as foreground and a diver in the midground are a signature image of Statia diving. Macro subjects around anchor encrustation are rich. The volcanic rock texture adds visual interest distinctly different from typical Caribbean reef photography.
