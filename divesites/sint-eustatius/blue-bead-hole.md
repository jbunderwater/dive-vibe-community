# Blue Bead Hole

A shallow shore dive famous for the cobalt-blue Dutch glass trade beads that centuries of current and wave action have deposited in sandy pockets along the seafloor — a living treasure hunt in Statia's ancient roadstead.

## Overview

Blue Bead Hole is one of Sint Eustatius's most singular diving experiences — a site renowned not for dramatic topography but for the extraordinary historical artifacts scattered across the sandy bottom. During Statia's commercial peak in the 17th and 18th centuries, when the island was a hub of Atlantic trade, cobalt-blue glass beads manufactured in Amsterdam circulated as a form of currency, including within the slave trade. How they came to be concentrated at this particular spot is genuinely unresolved: research into the site's own history (Life on the Golden Rock) presents two competing, unproven theories side by side — that a bead-laden ship sank here, or that freed people threw their beads into the sea after emancipation and they drifted to this location — without asserting either as fact. Divertrek separately raises a similar shipwreck-cargo theory tied to nearby Crook's Reef. This page treats the origin as an open question rather than settled history. STENAPA strictly protects the site — beads must not be collected, but divers can observe and photograph them in situ, and about a hundred divers a year reportedly find one under Divertrek's account.

## Marine Life

Scubaqua reports pike blennies, sailfin blennies, scorpionfish, mantis shrimp, and flying gurnards at this site over the sand-and-grass bottom; DAN's "A Salute to St. Eustatius" independently confirms flying gurnards here as well. (An earlier version of this page listed blue tang, parrotfish, sergeant majors, garden eels, flounder, and green sea turtles as if site-specific — none of these traced to a source confirming them at Blue Bead Hole itself, and the list has been corrected.)

## Dive Profile

Divers follow the sandy depressions in the 3–17 meter range to look for the blue bead concentrations. Local guides know the current bead-finding areas, which shift over time as sand moves. Snorkellers can access the shallowest bead pockets without scuba equipment.

## Entry and Exit

Access details for this site are not consistently confirmed across current sources — Scubaqua includes it as one of its regular boat-accessible sites, while older descriptions describe a shore entry near Oranjestad. Divers should confirm entry type with their operator. Booties or water shoes are worth carrying in case of a rocky shore entry.

## Tips and Recommendations

Blue Bead Hole is best experienced with a local guide who knows the current bead concentration areas — these shift seasonally and a guide dramatically improves the probability of finding beads. The beads are small, pentagonal, and dark cobalt-blue — typically found after storms that stir up the sand and expose them. Photograph rather than collect: removing any artifact is illegal under STENAPA regulations. An excellent pairing with Crooks Castle or Double Wreck for a full day of historical Statia diving.

## Safety Considerations

All artifacts including blue beads are protected by STENAPA and Dutch law — collecting is illegal. Shore entry over rock requires careful footing. Visibility can reduce after rain events. Current is light but can develop in the afternoon. Always dive with a buddy and inform someone ashore.

## Photography

The dark-blue pentagonal beads on white sand make striking macro subjects. A close-up lens at 1:2 or 1:1 ratio captures the rich blue colour and any encrustation on older beads. Ambient light in the 3–17 meter range is generally good throughout the day for natural-light photography of the sand and grass areas.
