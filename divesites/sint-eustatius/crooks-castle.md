# Crooks Castle

Shallow reef below the ruins of a historic Dutch colonial fort and wharf, matched almost exactly on PADI's and Zentacle's independent site listings for depth, difficulty, and access.

## Overview

Crooks Castle sits along the southwestern coast of Sint Eustatius, directly below the ruins of the historic Dutch colonial fort and wharf of the same name. PADI's dive-site database and Zentacle both list Crooks Castle at matching coordinates to our record and agree on its core stats: a reef dive, boat access, beginner level, maximum depth of about 32.8ft (roughly 10m) — an exact match to the depth already recorded here. STENAPA's own park page names Crooks Castle specifically as a spot where "there is always a sea turtle or stingray hanging around." A local history blog documents onshore ruins and weathered stonework in the same bay, and blue beads have historically been found there too, though it is a separate location from the dedicated Blue Bead Hole site. STENAPA (Sint Eustatius National Parks Foundation) manages the marine park, and all artifacts are strictly protected.

**Note (2026-09-11 re-audit):** earlier text specified "anchors, ballast stones, cannons, and ceramic fragments" at this site. Current sources confirm cannons and general historic stonework/ruins in the area but do not corroborate ballast stones or ceramic fragments specifically at Crooks Castle; the description below has been softened to avoid overstating unconfirmed detail.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Southwest coast, Sint Eustatius, Caribbean Netherlands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 10 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-30 meters
- **Current**: Usually light
- **Best Time**: March to June (calm seas, strong visibility)
- **Water Temperature**: 26-29°C (79-84°F)

## Marine Life

STENAPA's own site description names sea turtles and stingrays as characteristic sightings at Crooks Castle specifically. *Removed during the 2026-09-11 re-audit: French/queen angelfish, spotted drums, yellowhead jawfish, reef squid, and frogfish — not confirmed for this site by any current source. (PADI's page for this site carries a generic "sharks, whales, dolphins, turtles" tag that is applied identically to unrelated site pages across the island and was not treated as site-specific evidence.)*

## Dive Profile

This is a relaxed, multi-level dive in 3-10 meters of water. Divers typically navigate among the scattered colonial ruins and coral heads before ascending to explore the shallowest sections. Extended bottom times are possible given the depth. A safety stop at 5 meters over the sand is easy to maintain.

## Entry and Exit

Access is exclusively by boat; there is no shore entry from the fort ruins. Dive operators in Oranjestad run regular morning two-tank trips that combine Crooks Castle with a second site. Mooring buoys are maintained by STENAPA — anchor drops are not permitted in the marine park. Entry via giant stride; the mooring line serves as a descent reference.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a dive light even during the day — crevices around the old stonework shelter small macro life
- Photograph the historic cannons and stonework but never touch or remove artifacts
- The site is ideal for a relaxed second dive after a deeper morning site
- STENAPA dive fees are collected by local operators; ensure your operator is licensed

## Safety Considerations

Boat traffic along the southwest coast requires deploying an SMB on ascent. Fire coral encrusts some of the colonial stonework — watch fin placement. Dive within sport diving limits; the maximum depth is well within recreational range.

## Photography

Wide-angle shots capturing encrusted cannons and stonework against healthy coral create compelling historical-underwater compositions. Morning light angles from the east provide good ambient illumination at these shallow depths.
