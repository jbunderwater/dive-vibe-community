## Snoopy Island Reef

Snoopy Island Reef is an artificial reef/wreck dive site off Al Aqah, Fujairah, UAE.

## Overview

**Geographic note:** Despite being listed under the Oman destination in OSM, this site's coordinates (25.49°N, 56.36°E) place it at Al Aqah in Fujairah, United Arab Emirates — not in Oman. The site is classified as a protected area in OSM data under Oman in error.

This site corresponds to the dive site commonly called **Snoopy Deep Reef** (also known as "New Reef"), located approximately 500 metres east of the well-known shallow Snoopy Island. Depth reports vary slightly by source: Coral Creek Diving and Reef Oasis Diving Center both cite approximately 24 metres, while PADI's dedicated page cites 26 metres (85ft); this description uses 24m per the two concurring dive-shop sources, but treat the maximum as approximate. Snoopy Deep Reef is an artificial reef built around a purposely-sunk dive boat and trailer, now encrusted in hard and soft corals, with additional structure from tyres and ropes laid on the sea floor. The wreck provides structure for reef fish including angelfish, boxfish, snapper schools, wrasse, moray eels, and well-camouflaged seahorses on the railings and ropes — consistent across Coral Creek and Reef Oasis. PADI's broader, community-sourced listing additionally mentions sharks, dolphins, and turtles as occasional sightings; this is not corroborated by either dive-shop source and is noted here only as an occasional/unverified addition, not a typical encounter (an artificial reef at this depth would not typically see whales as a routine sighting).

This site should not be confused with the shallow Snoopy Island itself (5 to 8 metres depth, beginner-friendly), which is a separate, shallower dive/snorkel site accessible from shore or by short boat ride. The 24-26 metre depth of this site requires at least Advanced Open Water certification.

**Wreck-history note:** Snoopy Deep Reef is a modern purpose-sunk artificial reef (a scuttled dive boat and trailer), not a historic wreck with a documented ship name, build date, tonnage, or sinking cause — no such history exists to fabricate or cross-check, consistent with anti-hallucination policy.
