# Land's End

Likely the same site Manta Ray Bay Resort and Visit Yap list as "End of the Land" — a near-vertical wall from 18 to 40 meters with minimal current, soft corals, crinoids, and resting (not hunting) sharks

## Overview
"End of the Land" — likely the source of this database's "Land's End" entry — is a near-vertical wall on Yap's outer reef, dropping from about 18 meters (60 feet) to the ocean floor at roughly 40 meters (130 feet), covered with soft corals and crinoids. Pink and purple soft corals are notable just below the wall's upper edge, around 18 meters. A gentler reef terrace with bommies and fissures sits above the wall edge. Both sources describing this site emphasize minimal current and excellent visibility rather than the high-current, big-animal drama the site's name might suggest.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Yap, Federated States of Micronesia (matches "End of the Land" per mantaray.com/visityap.com)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 21-30+ meters (70-100+ feet)
- **Current**: Minimal, 0-3 km/h (0-2 mph)
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life
Divers commonly encounter resting sharks and black-spotted stingrays on the sandy floor below the wall's edge. Sea turtles frequent the site, cruising the wall and sheltering in canyons that lead to the reef structure. No source confirms grey reef shark aggregations, tuna, or hammerhead sharks at this site — those claims from the prior description have been removed as unconfirmed. If a distinct "Land's End" site with different conditions exists separately from "End of the Land," it has not been documented in any source found.

## Dive Profile
Descend along the near-vertical wall from the reef terrace at approximately 18 meters. With minimal current, divers can move along the wall face at a controlled pace to take in the pink and purple soft corals near the top and the crinoid-covered deeper sections. No source gives a specific turnaround depth or dive-time plan; standard advanced no-decompression planning applies given the 40m maximum.

## Entry and Exit
Boat access from Colonia, consistent with Yap's other outer-reef wall sites. No source gives site-specific entry/exit detail beyond "boat dive" with a wall descent.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Look for resting sharks and black-spotted stingrays on the sand below the wall edge rather than expecting them in open water
- The minimal current here (relative to other Yap walls) allows more deliberate photography and exploration of the soft coral bands
- Watch depth carefully on the featureless vertical wall face

## Safety Considerations
Both sources describe minimal current, but the wall's depth (down to 40m) still demands standard advanced wall-diving discipline: constant depth awareness, a planned no-decompression profile, and a safety stop. No source documents downcurrents, strong current events, or pelagic hazards at this specific site — divers should still follow their guide's site-specific briefing, as conditions can vary from what's documented here.

## Photography
No site-specific photography guidance was found in the sources checked. The near-vertical wall, pink/purple soft corals at 18m, and minimal current suggest wide-angle wall photography would suit the site, but this is inference rather than a sourced claim.
