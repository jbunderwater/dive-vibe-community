# The Indians

The Indians is one of the BVI's most iconic and recognisable dive sites — four dramatic rock pillars rising from a sandy bottom around 15–18 meters off the western tip of Norman Island, creating an underwater landscape of arches, channels, and richly encrusted rock faces. It is regularly cited as the second most-dived site in the BVI after the RMS Rhone.

## Overview

The four spire-like rock formations of The Indians are a landmark visible from miles away as you sail the Sir Francis Drake Channel. Underwater, the rocks' sides are steeply vertical and covered with coral and sponge growth, with sea fans, black coral, and encrusting sponges in oranges, purples, and yellows. Channels and swim-throughs between the pillars, including a shallow saddle connecting two of the pinnacles, create a three-dimensional exploration space. Operator listings put the working depth at roughly 3–18 meters, with dive shops rating the site Intermediate; it is also popular as a snorkel site given how close the shallow zones and the saddle come to the surface. Visibility regularly exceeds 20–25 meters. Sources disagree on difficulty — one operator markets it as Beginner-friendly while two others rate it Intermediate — reflecting that the shallow saddle is easy while the outer rock faces and any current exposure call for more experience.

## Marine Life

Confirmed site-specific sightings include hawksbill turtles, blue chromis, creole wrasse, angelfish, rock beauties, and black triggerfish around the rock faces, with nudibranchs on the shallower ledges. One of the caves at the site holds a resident school of glassy sweepers. Claims of nurse sharks being "almost guaranteed" and regular Caribbean reef shark visits could not be confirmed by any source consulted and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The pillars are best approached from the boat to the shallow saddle at around 2 meters between two of the pinnacles. Explore the channels between the rocks first, then work down the outer faces toward 15–18 meters for the densest encrustation. Ascend along the rock faces to maximise time in the most productive zones.

## Entry and Exit

The Indians sees heavy boat traffic — most BVI dive operators include it on every itinerary. Giant stride entry; SMB recommended on ascent given the boat concentration around the site. Mooring buoys are typically available to prevent anchor damage.

## Tips and Recommendations

- One of the BVI's most-dived sites — plan for multiple dives to cover the rock faces properly
- The site is best dived in the early morning before day charter boats and snorkel tours arrive
- Spend time in the mid-water channels between pillars — the encrustation quality here is exceptional
- Pair with The Caves at Norman Island for a full Norman Island half-day

## Safety Considerations

The site's popularity means the area around The Indians can have multiple boats simultaneously. Surface with an inflated SMB and be alert for approaching vessels. Currents in the open channel position can increase unexpectedly.

## Photography

The Indians is a strong wide-angle photography site. The vertical rock pillars and the channels between them create sweeping compositional opportunities, and the dense encrustation of sea fans and sponges rewards close-focus wide-angle work. Natural light is good throughout the shallow zone.

## Additional Resources

- Most BVI dive operators include The Indians on Norman Island itineraries
- The Bight at Norman Island: standard yacht anchorage — the site is a short dinghy ride from the anchorage
