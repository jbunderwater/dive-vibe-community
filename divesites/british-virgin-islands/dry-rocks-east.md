# Dry Rocks East

Dry Rocks East is an open-water ridge dive on the eastern side of Cooper Island, between Cooper and Ginger Islands — a barely-breaking ridge that acts as a natural gathering point for pelagic fish and a favorite of local dive leaders, rated Advanced by every operator consulted.

## Overview

The "dry rocks" are exposed rocky outcrops at the surface that extend underwater into a ridge descending to roughly 26 meters. The ridge supports black coral, sea fans, and other encrusting organisms on the rocks and sandy bottom. Sitting in open water in the channel between Cooper and Ginger Islands gives the site good visibility but a tradeoff of rougher conditions and occasionally strong currents. Water temperature holds around 27°C.

## Marine Life

Confirmed site-specific sightings include nurse sharks, cobia, barracuda, huge clams, southern and slipper lobsters, hawksbill turtles, moray eels, horse-eye jacks, African pompano, Atlantic spadefish, French angelfish, grunts, black durgons, sergeant majors, chromis, creole wrasse, hinds, coneys, filefish, goatfish, highhats (notably clustered beneath a car-sized boulder near the mooring line), pufferfish, and trunkfish. Claims of Caribbean reef sharks and southern stingrays specific to this site could not be confirmed and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

Navigate the ridge from the shallows to the deeper base. The open-water position and occasional strong currents mean this is more demanding than a simple slope dive — follow the ridge and explore between formations. The most interesting terrain is in the 15–26 meter range where ledges create sheltered zones for larger species and pelagic fish gather at the ridge crossroads.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Peter Island or Road Town operators. Giant stride entry; SMB required on ascent. Pair with a Norman Island or Peter Island site for a productive two-tank southern BVI day.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Less visited than the headline BVI sites — a good choice for experienced divers seeking quieter conditions
- Explore the deeper ledge zones for nurse shark and moray eel encounters
- Check under the large boulder near the mooring line for a highhat aggregation
- The open-water exposure means conditions should be confirmed with the operator before committing

## Safety Considerations

Currents can be moderate to strong on this open-water ridge — this is why the site is rated Advanced. Position yourself appropriately and monitor air supply. SMB deployment before ascending is important given the open Sir Francis Drake Channel exposure.

## Photography

The layered rocky terrain creates three-dimensional photographic compositions. Wide-angle shots through rocky overhangs with fish in the background work well. Deeper ledge sections provide dramatic shadow-and-light contrasts with a strobe setup.

## Additional Resources

- Peter Island Resort dive operation and Road Town operators: include Dry Rocks East on southern island itineraries
- Good pairing with Norman Island's The Caves or The Indians for a southern BVI two-tank day
