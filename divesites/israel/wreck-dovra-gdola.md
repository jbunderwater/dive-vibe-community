## Wreck Dovra Gdola (Big Barge / Trinacria Primo)

Officially the Italian-built pontoon barge Trinacria Primo, this is reported to be the largest sunken vessel in Israeli waters — a 105–108 metre construction barge that was damaged in a storm during work on the Hadera coal pier and later scuttled off Haifa's Dado Beach.

## Overview

The barge was brought from Italy in the early 1980s to help build the coal unloading pier at the Orot Rabin power station in Hadera, using stabilizer legs to drive piles into the seabed. On the night of 10 December 1980, a severe storm tore the barge loose from its mooring cables and drove it against the breakwater, badly damaging the hull; the crew was rescued without casualties. Deemed uneconomical to repair, the barge was deliberately sunk as an artificial reef, according to sources, in 1981.

The wreck lies roughly 2.5 kilometres off Dado Beach in southern Haifa. Sources give slightly different maximum depths — most commonly around 36 metres, though one source describes the deck at approximately 32 metres — so treat 32–36 metres as the working range. The hull has a large longitudinal crack and multiple openings leading into former crew quarters, work areas, and engine spaces; local dive clubs describe cautious penetration as possible for certified, experienced wreck divers only, with narrow and potentially disorienting passages.

## Site Information

- **Location**: ~2.5 km off Dado Beach, southern Haifa, Israeli Mediterranean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Maximum Depth**: 36 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 5–15 metres (seasonal)
- **Current**: Can be present in open water off Haifa
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Local dive clubs report large groupers and dense schools of sea bream around the hull, along with sponges, sea cucumbers, and other encrusting life on the structure. No further site-specific species records were found — beyond this, assume general Israeli-Mediterranean wreck fauna rather than a confirmed list.

## Dive Profile

A deep boat dive to a very large, mostly intact pontoon hull. The flat deck (covered in algae and scattered work-equipment remnants) is a useful orientation point before deciding whether to explore the exterior only or, for suitably trained/certified divers, the interior openings. Given the depth, plan conservative bottom times and a safety stop.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Accessed via Haifa-area dive clubs (e.g. Indigo, Putsker) — boat-only, advanced certification expected
- Penetration passages are narrow; only attempt with wreck-specific training
- Bring a torch for the interior openings and engine spaces

## Safety Considerations

At 32–36 metres, no-decompression time is limited — dive computer and gas planning required. Interior passages are described as narrow and potentially hazardous for untrained divers; do not attempt penetration without wreck-diving certification. Carry an SMB for ascent in open water.
