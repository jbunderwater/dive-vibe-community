## White Rock

One of Koh Tao's most popular and versatile dive sites — raised granite shelves with reliable grouper sightings, Christmas tree worms, and a well-regarded night dive.

## Overview

White Rock is located on the west side of Koh Tao and is one of the island's most frequently visited sites due to its accessibility, variety of depth zones, and reliable marine life. The site consists of a series of raised granite shelves from around 5 m all the way to 22 m, with hard coral growth, anemone fields, and rocky overhangs providing habitat diversity. Christmas tree worms in multiple colour forms are found across the rock surfaces. The site's shallow sections make it accessible to Open Water divers while the deeper shelves provide interest for intermediate divers. Black Turtle Dive and Dive Happy both describe White Rock as a popular, well-regarded night dive site, though it is not independently confirmed as "the best" across all Koh Tao operators.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West side of Koh Tao, Gulf of Thailand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 22 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 m
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: May to August

## Marine Life

Confirmed species include giant grouper, Christmas tree worms in multi-colour forms, titan triggerfish, pufferfish, sea turtles (green and hawksbill, occasional), blue-spotted ribbon-tail rays, butterflyfish, banded sea kraits, cuttlefish, whale sharks (occasional), and angelfish. Barracuda patrol the outer reef sections, and hermit crabs are seen hunting on night dives. Note: some sources associate honeycomb (hexagon) grouper with White Rock, but this was not confirmed across the three operator sources cited for this site, so it is not listed as a confirmed sighting here.

## Dive Profile

Descend to 20–22 m to explore the deeper rock formations and then ascend progressively up through the shelf zones. The middle depth at 8–15 m is the most productive for fish life. The 2–5 m shallows host the densest Christmas tree worm concentrations. Allow ample time in the shallows before completing the safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Koh Tao west coast (5 minutes). Entry via back roll or giant stride. Currents are mild. SMB deployment for ascent is recommended.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Night dives at White Rock are well regarded — book in advance
- Explore the rock shelves methodically; crevices hide groupers and moray eels
- Christmas tree worms retract instantly when approached — move slowly for best views
- Combine with a morning dive at HTMS Sattakut for a varied two-site day
- The shallow sections are well suited to open water training dives

## Safety Considerations

Currents are generally mild but can increase on the outer reef. Long-spined black sea urchins on the rock shelves cause injuries if contacted — maintain spatial awareness. Night dives require dive lights and a close buddy system. Lionfish inhabit overhangs throughout the site.

## Photography

Christmas tree worms in multi-colour clusters make outstanding close-focus wide-angle or macro subjects. Grouper portraits are achievable with a patient approach. Night dive photography reveals hunting barracuda and hermit crabs.
