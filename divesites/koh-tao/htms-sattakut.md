## HTMS Sattakut

A former US Navy Pacific War landing craft deliberately sunk in 2011 — Koh Tao's most accessible wreck dive and a growing artificial reef.

## Overview

The HTMS Sattakut was built as USS LCI(L)-739 (Landing Craft Infantry, Large) at Commercial Iron Works in Portland, Oregon — laid down 30 January 1944, launched 27 February 1944, and commissioned 6 March 1944. She was reclassified LCI(G)-739 (Gunboat) on 31 December 1944 and again to LCI(M)-739 (Mortar variant) on 30 April 1945. During World War II the vessel earned three Battle Stars serving in three major Pacific operations: the capture of the Western Caroline/Palau Islands (September–October 1944), the assault on Iwo Jima (February–March 1945), and the assault on Okinawa Gunto (March–June 1945). Naval records (Navsource) date her transfer out of US Navy hands to 10 May 1947, though several dive-industry sources cite 1946 for her Royal Thai Navy commissioning — sources disagree on the exact transfer year. She served as HTMS Sattakut, hull number 742, named for a Thai military victory in the Franco-Thai War of 1941, until decommissioning in 2007.

On 18 June 2011, Thailand's Department of Marine and Coastal Resources deliberately sank the cleaned and stripped vessel as an artificial reef. The initial scuttling placed the wreck on its starboard side; a salvage team righted and repositioned it to its current upright, north-facing orientation in early August 2011, roughly 40 m south of the Hin Pee Wee pinnacle. Naval archive Navsource and dive-industry sources agree the vessel measures approximately 48 m (158'6") in length with a 7 m beam, and displaced 246 tonnes light / 419 tonnes loaded. Depth reports vary slightly by source: the bridge/top of the structure sits between 18–20 m and the keel between 27–32 m on a sandy bottom; two deck guns (bow and stern) remain on the hull. Since sinking, the structure has been colonised by one-spot snappers, brown sweetlips, giant groupers, banded sea kraits, scorpionfish, and schools of barracuda.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West of Koh Tao, approximately 800 m offshore, ~40 m south of Hin Pee Wee pinnacle
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 32 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 2–15 m (variable; can be poor)
- **Current**: Mild to moderate
- **Best Time**: May to August

## Marine Life

Confirmed species at the wreck include one-spot snappers in large schools, brown sweetlips, giant groupers, banded sea kraits, scorpionfish near doors and portholes, and Durban dancing shrimp on the interior staircases. Barracuda, batfish, and lionfish are documented around the superstructure. Moray eels inhabit interior spaces. Whale sharks are occasional visitors to the area, not residents of the wreck itself.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the bow or stern (guide selects based on current orientation) and perform a systematic circuit of the hull. Start at the superstructure at 18–20 m and work down to the keel at 27–32 m. Multiple penetration entry and exit points are accessible for qualified wreck divers. Ascend via the mast structure and transition to the adjacent Hin Pee Wee pinnacle for extended air use and a safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Koh Tao (approximately 15–20 minutes). Entry via back roll. Mooring lines are attached to the wreck. SMB deployment recommended for open water ascent away from the mooring.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Plan your dive to start at depth and ascend progressively to make the most of no-decompression time
- The wreck pairs naturally with Hin Pee Wee pinnacle in a single dive
- Bring a torch for examining the interior spaces — Durban dancing shrimp on the staircases are a highlight
- Nitrox extends bottom time on the keel at 27–32 m
- Visibility can be poor (2–10 m is typical); adjust expectations and plan accordingly

## Safety Considerations

Swim-throughs and internal penetration should only be attempted by divers with wreck diving training or certification. Do not penetrate beyond the light zone without a line and appropriate training. Scorpionfish on the hull are well-camouflaged — do not touch any surface. Always dive with a buddy and carry redundant lights for any interior exploration.

## Photography

Wide-angle captures the full hull profile and accompanying fish schools. One-spot snapper schools around the superstructure create compelling school shots. Macro work on the encrusting soft corals and the cannon guns. The bow profile with a diver in frame provides the classic wreck portrait.
