## Alung banua

Alung banua is a gently sloping vertical wall on Bunaken Island's south coast, cut with the cracks, crevices and small caves typical of this stretch of reef.

## Overview

Alung banua sits on Bunaken Island's south coast among the well-known south-wall sites. Divezone.net describes it as a vertical wall "with a lot of cracks, crevices and small caves," with diving depth reported between 18 and 25 metres, visibility 15-25 metres, and a light 1-2 knot current — conditions consistent with its Beginner rating, though at least one general guide lists the site as better suited to more confident divers, so ask your operator about current on the day.

## What to Expect

The wall is covered in hard coral and supports schooling snappers and fusiliers, along with parrotfish, butterflyfish, sea snakes, sea turtles and numerous anthias. Blacktip and whitetip reef sharks are occasionally reported along the wall base. The site also has a reputation as a good night dive and a productive spot for macro photography, thanks to the cracks and crevices that riddle the wall face.

## Conditions

- **Depth range:** 18-25 m
- **Visibility:** 15-25 m
- **Currents:** Light, around 1-2 knots
- **Water temperature:** 25-30°C
- **Best season:** May-October

## Tips

The cracks and crevices are the main draw here — bring a torch and go slowly rather than covering distance. Conditions are calm enough that this also works as a night dive.
