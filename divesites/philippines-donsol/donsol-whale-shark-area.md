## Donsol Whale Shark Area

Donsol Whale Shark Area is a snorkel-only interaction zone in Donsol Bay, Sorsogon Province, Luzon. This is not a scuba dive site — scuba equipment and motorized underwater propulsion are prohibited by the WWF-led code of conduct.

> **SNORKEL ONLY — scuba diving is not permitted at this site.**

## Overview

Donsol Bay is home to the largest documented whale shark aggregation in Southeast Asia (excluding provisioning sites). Between 2006 and 2020, researchers conducted 1,985 surveys (over 895 days) encountering 6,786 whale sharks, with 614 individuals photo-identified — about 49% of the Philippines' known population. Peak-season aggregation size is estimated at 47–61 individual whale sharks present at any one time. The wider Ticao-Burias Pass ISRA (Important Shark and Ray Area) profile records three documented neonate whale sharks (46cm, 64cm, and 60cm total length) in 2009 and 2020, among the relatively few confirmed neonate records anywhere in the region.

The WWF Philippines helped establish Donsol's eco-tourism program, training locals as Butanding Interaction Officers (BIOs) who enforce the code of conduct from each boat. Whale sharks aggregate here to feed on the plankton and krill blooms fed by the Donsol River and Burias/Ticao Pass nutrient upwelling.

## Marine Life

- Whale sharks (*Rhincodon typus*) — wild, unbaited, free-swimming individuals
- Occasional manta rays and reef fish

## Interaction Program

Each boat carries a captain, spotter, and BIO. Registration is approximately 300 PHP for foreign visitors (100 PHP for Filipino nationals); boats are rented separately, with reported rates ranging from roughly 3,500 to 5,000 PHP depending on when and where you book — confirm current pricing locally, as rates have been rising. The BIO signals when to enter the water and monitors compliance. Most encounters last a few minutes; swimmers must keep 3 meters from the tail and must not touch, ride, or restrict the shark. Maximum 6 snorkelers per whale shark; one boat per shark.

**Season:** November to June, peak February to May.

## Entry/Exit

Entry and exit from a bangka (outrigger boat) in open bay waters. Snorkelers must be comfortable with open-water entry, as the boat remains in motion tracking the shark. Life jackets are provided.

## Tips

- Strong swimming ability is recommended despite the beginner rating — encounters require quick water entry and sustained pacing with a moving animal.
- Avoid peak day-tripper crowds by booking early-morning departures.
- No flash photography is permitted underwater.
- Visibility can drop to 2 meters during heavy plankton blooms — exactly the conditions that concentrate whale sharks.

## Site Information

- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Snorkel
