## Donsol Whale Shark Area

Donsol Whale Shark Area is a snorkel-only interaction zone in Donsol Bay, Sorsogon Province, Luzon. This is not a scuba dive site — scuba equipment and motorized underwater propulsion are prohibited by the WWF-led code of conduct.

> **SNORKEL ONLY — scuba diving is not permitted at this site.**

## Overview

Donsol Bay is home to the largest documented whale shark aggregation in Southeast Asia. Between 2006 and 2020, researchers conducted 1,985 surveys (over 895 days) encountering 6,786 whale sharks. Peak sightings reach an estimated 47–61 individual whale sharks in the bay at any one time. The Ticao-Burias Pass ISRA (Important Shark and Ray Area) designation confirms this as globally significant habitat — the only location in Southeast Asia with documented whale shark neonates.

Donsol's whale shark tourism dates to December 26, 1997, when a diving instructor and colleagues found the sharks to be non-aggressive while diving in the bay; the town's whale shark code of conduct was adopted from a model developed by Western Australia's Department of Conservation and Land Management. WWF Philippines helped formalize the program from 1998, training locals as Butanding Interaction Officers (BIOs) who enforce the code of conduct from each boat. Whale sharks aggregate here to feed on the plankton and krill blooms fed by the Donsol River and Burias/Ticao Pass nutrient upwelling. Scuba diving is not permitted anywhere in Donsol Bay itself — divers wanting to dive (rather than snorkel) with the area's marine life head instead to Manta Bowl and San Miguel Island off Ticao Island.

## Marine Life

- Whale sharks (*Rhincodon typus*) — wild, unbaited, free-swimming individuals
- Occasional manta rays and reef fish

## Interaction Program

Each boat carries a captain, spotter, and BIO. Registration costs 300 PHP for foreign visitors (100 PHP for Filipino nationals); boats are rented separately. The BIO signals when to enter the water and monitors compliance. The official code of conduct requires swimmers to stay at least 3 meters from the shark's head and 4 meters from its tail, and prohibits touching, riding, or restricting the shark's movement. Encounters are often brief — some accounts report average interactions lasting under a minute. Maximum 6 snorkelers per whale shark; one boat per shark (though this is not always strictly enforced).

**Season:** November to June, peak February to May.

## Entry/Exit

Entry and exit from a bangka (outrigger boat) in open bay waters. Snorkelers must be comfortable with open-water entry, as the boat remains in motion tracking the shark. Life jackets are provided.

## Tips

- Strong swimming ability is recommended despite the beginner rating — encounters require quick water entry and sustained pacing with a moving animal.
- Avoid peak day-tripper crowds by booking early-morning departures.
- No flash photography is permitted underwater.
- Visibility can drop to 2 meters during heavy plankton blooms — exactly the conditions that concentrate whale sharks.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Donsol Bay, Sorsogon Province, Luzon, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Snorkel
