## Fowey Rocks

Fowey Rocks is the shallow reef around the 1878 Fowey Rocks Lighthouse in Biscayne National Park, mainly a boat-access snorkel and shallow dive site.

## Overview

Fowey Rocks Light stands about seven miles southeast of Cape Florida, was completed in 1878 and automated in 1975; it is the northernmost of the six Florida Coral Reef Lights, known as the "Eye of Miami" (Wikipedia; National Park Service; Snorkel Around the World). Earlier text on this page gave 1876 as the date; sources consulted say 1878, with Snorkel Around the World saying only that construction was in the 1870s. The Arratoon Apcar ran aground a few hundred yards from where the lighthouse was being built and was lost in 1878 (National Park Service). The lighthouse is the newest addition to Biscayne National Park's Maritime Heritage Trail; access is by boat only, snorkeling is good around the base, and the structure itself is closed to the public (National Park Service). Average depth is about 15 feet (4.5 m) (Snorkel Around the World).

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 6 meters

## Marine Life

Snorkel Around the World reports coral gardens (mainly soft corals), schooling French grunts and sergeant majors, blue angelfish, porkfish, trumpetfish, stingrays and nurse sharks, and remains of a small steamer northeast of the tower. Earlier text listing groupers and yellowtail could not be traced to a source and has been removed.

## Entry and Exit

Boat only (National Park Service). Navigation near the reef requires care; the lighthouse itself is not open for access.
