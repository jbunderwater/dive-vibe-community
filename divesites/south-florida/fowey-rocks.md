## Fowey Rocks

A shallow reef surrounding an 1878 lighthouse at the northern gateway to Biscayne National Park, with the scattered remains of a 19th-century steamer nearby.

## Overview

Fowey Rocks (also called Fowey Reef or Fowey Light) is a shallow reef area surrounding the Fowey Rocks Lighthouse — first lit in 1878, not 1876 as sometimes reported — at the north edge of Biscayne National Park in Miami-Dade County, just north of the Florida Keys proper. Nearby lie the scattered remains of the SS Arratoon Apcar, an iron-hulled steamer that ran aground here in 1878, part of the park's Shipwreck Trail. The site sits in 10-20 feet of water and suits both diving and snorkeling.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 6 meters

## Wreck History

The SS Arratoon Apcar was an iron-hulled sail-and-steam merchant ship built in Scotland in 1861. Steaming toward Havana on the night of February 20, 1878, her captain miscalculated the ship's position and ran her aground on the reef at Fowey Rocks — near the lighthouse, which was still under construction at the time; signals from construction workers went unnoticed before the coal-laden steamer struck the reef just a few hundred yards away. Her coral-encrusted lower hull, iron beams, and remnants of her rudder and mast remain visible on the bottom today.

## Marine Life

The reef supports groupers, angelfish, and yellowtail snapper along with general coral growth typical of the shallow Biscayne reef tract; site-specific species beyond these general reports are limited in available sources.

## Dive Profile

The Fowey Rocks Lighthouse (110 feet tall, first lit 1878) marks the site from the surface. The Arratoon Apcar wreckage lies in 10-20 feet of water a short distance from the lighthouse, with visible hull plating, iron ribs, and rudder/mast fragments encrusted in coral. The shallow depth makes this accessible to both scuba divers and snorkelers.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, typically run by Biscayne National Park-area charters and the park's own boat tours.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The site works well as a combined dive/snorkel outing given its shallow depth.
- The lighthouse structure itself is a protected historic landmark within Biscayne National Park — observe from the water without touching or climbing on it.

## Safety Considerations

- Shallow depth means long bottom times, but also more exposure to sun and surface chop — plan sun protection.
- Boat traffic near the lighthouse and reef can be significant; maintain a dive flag and stay alert on ascent.
- The wreckage has sharp, coral-encrusted iron edges; avoid contact.
