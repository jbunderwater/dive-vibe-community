## Tracey

A 131-foot drug-smuggling seizure turned artificial reef, renamed in memory of local dive instructor Ken Vitale.

## Overview

Originally named Tracey, this 131-foot vessel was seized by U.S. Customs for drug trafficking and deployed as an artificial reef off Fort Lauderdale on March 2, 1999, as part of the "Fort Lauderdale Wreck Trek" alongside the nearby Jay Scutti and Merci Jesus. The site is now more commonly known by its memorial name, honoring local dive instructor Ken Vitale, with a plaque marking the wreck in his memory.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 22 meters

## Vessel History

Seized by U.S. Customs for drug trafficking, the 131-foot vessel was deployed as an artificial reef off Fort Lauderdale on March 2, 1999, sitting in approximately 72 feet of water. She was later renamed in memory of Ken Vitale, a well-known local dive instructor, and carries a memorial plaque.

## Marine Life

The wreck functions as a typical southeast Florida artificial reef, attracting reef fish and grouper species common to the area's wreck sites.

## Dive Profile

The wreck sits upright, bow pointing north, in about 72 feet of water as part of a cluster of three wrecks (Tracey/Ken Vitale, Jay Scutti, and Merci Jesus) that make up the Fort Lauderdale Wreck Trek. Divers commonly visit more than one of the three wrecks on a single trip, current permitting.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, run by Fort Lauderdale-area charters, often as part of a multi-wreck Wreck Trek itinerary.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your operator whether the trip covers more than one of the three Wreck Trek sites.
- Look for the memorial plaque honoring Ken Vitale.

## Safety Considerations

- Depth (~22m/72ft) is within standard recreational limits.
- Any interior exploration requires wreck-penetration training and equipment; treat all overhead sections with caution.
