## Rodeo 25

A 215-foot Dutch freighter sunk before an estimated 100,000 spectators to mark the 25th Pompano Beach Fishing Rodeo.

## Overview

Rodeo 25 is a 215-foot Dutch freighter intentionally sunk off Pompano Beach on May 12, 1990 to mark the 25th anniversary of the Pompano Beach Fishing Rodeo, an event reportedly witnessed by around 100,000 spectators. The wreck's twin masts rise 68 feet from the wheelhouse and are a favorite photography subject; the forward mast has since fallen to starboard.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 38 meters

## Vessel History

The 215-foot freighter was deliberately sunk off Pompano Beach on May 12, 1990 as part of the 25th Pompano Beach Fishing Rodeo, drawing an estimated 100,000 onlookers. She now rests in approximately 100-125 feet of water.

## Marine Life

As with many South Florida wrecks of this depth, expect resident grouper and snapper along with schooling baitfish; site-specific species reports beyond this are limited in available sources.

## Dive Profile

The wreck sits at a maximum depth of approximately 38 meters (125 feet), making this an advanced dive. The signature feature is the twin masts rising 68 feet above the wheelhouse — the forward mast has since fallen to starboard, while the aft mast remains standing and is a favored subject for silhouette photography against the surface light.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, run by Pompano Beach and Fort Lauderdale-area charters.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Plan for a deep dive with limited bottom time; this is not a beginner wreck.
- The standing mast makes for strong wide-angle silhouette shots against the surface.

## Safety Considerations

- Depth (up to 38m/125ft) requires solid buoyancy control, gas planning, and awareness of narcosis risk.
- The fallen forward mast and surrounding debris are entanglement hazards — maintain distance unless trained for penetration.
- Wreck penetration, where attempted, should only be done with proper training and equipment; it is never risk-free.
