## Boca Artificial Reef Ledge

A natural limestone ledge off Boca Raton with a supplemental cluster of 1970s-era concrete "Erojacks," best known for its resident sea turtles.

## Overview

Boca Artificial Reef Ledge is an outer natural ledge running parallel to the Boca Raton shoreline, with the highest coral and sponge cover on its west-facing main ledge. A supplemental artificial component of 20 concrete "Erojacks," placed in the late 1970s in partnership with Florida Atlantic University, sits nearby in a sandy depression; several were displaced by Hurricane David (1979) and later storms. The site is typically dived as a drift dive.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Marine Life

Divers regularly report angelfish, Bermuda chub, barracuda, and spiny lobster along the ledge, along with resident sea turtles. Goliath grouper and hammerhead sharks are reported on occasion, though not on every dive.

## Dive Profile

Depths on the site run roughly 40-70 feet. The western-facing main ledge holds the highest concentration of marine life, rising 10+ feet with a jagged, heavily overgrown top edge of soft corals, sponges, and algae. The 20 Erojacks — originally stacked about 6 feet high — sit in a sandy depression nearby, with several toppled from the pile by past hurricanes. Divers typically start in the north or south hook (depending on current) and drift along the main ledge.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, run by Boca Raton-area charters as a drift dive along the ledge. Visibility is generally good, commonly in the 50-55 foot range.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Light to moderate currents are typical; slack tide gives the calmest conditions.
- Ask your operator which hook (north or south) they'll be starting from based on the day's current direction.
- The Erojacks are a secondary feature — most of the marine life concentrates on the main western ledge.

## Safety Considerations

- This is a drift dive; carry a surface marker buoy and stay with your group.
- Watch for fire coral and hydroids on the undercut ledge faces.
- Give sea turtles and any resident groupers or sharks space rather than approaching directly.
