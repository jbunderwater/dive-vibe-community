## Lauderdale-by-the-Sea Beach

The Datura Avenue beach portal in Lauderdale-by-the-Sea is the main shore entry to nearshore reefs and the Shipwreck Snorkel Trail.

## Overview

The Town calls itself Florida's Beach Diving Capital and says its coral reefs lie within 100 yards of the beach (Town of Lauderdale-by-the-Sea). Datura Avenue and El Mar Drive is one of the most popular spots for divers and snorkelers in Broward County, with a tank rack and a Shipwreck Snorkel Trail in 10 feet of water (Town; Discover LBTS). Gold Coast Scuba puts the first reef 100-400 feet from the beach, and Force-E says you can swim out about 100 yards to a reef.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 5 meters

## Marine Life

Gold Coast Scuba reports star, brain and finger corals and sea fans; grunts, snappers and parrotfish; sergeant majors, blennies, juvenile drum and trumpetfish; sea turtles, nurse sharks and tarpon; and occasional stingrays, moray eels and octopus. At the Shipwreck Snorkel Trail it lists arrow crabs and scorpionfish. Force-E mentions "potholes" with many juvenile fish. Earlier text listing spiny lobster as a regular sighting could not be traced to a sighting report and has been removed.

## Dive Profile

Gold Coast Scuba gives the Shipwreck Snorkel Trail at 8-10 feet, the first reef line at 12-20 feet with coral heads rising 3-4 feet, and a second reef line at 30-50 feet roughly 30 minutes' swim out. Force-E gives an 8-20 foot range and puts the second reef at about 12-15 feet; these differ. Bottom times of 60-90 minutes are common in the shallows (Gold Coast Scuba), and visibility of 50-70 feet is reported on calm days.

## The Shipwreck Snorkel Trail

Dedicated in 2002 by Jean-Michel Cousteau and built by the Marine Archaeological Council, the trail has an anchor, five concrete cannons and a ballast pile (Town of Lauderdale-by-the-Sea); Force-E describes two replica cannons on the north side and three on the south within a 100 x 20 foot area. Wreck maps are available at the town's dive shops, Town Hall and the Chamber of Commerce.

## Entry and Exit

The Datura Avenue portal has a tank rack; Hibiscus Avenue and El Prado Park are alternatives (Gold Coast Scuba; Force-E). Parking is metered with PayByPhone. A dive flag is required in Florida. Spearfishing and lobster harvesting are allowed with a current FWC license (Force-E).

## Safety Considerations

- Fly and tow a dive flag; boats and swimmers share the area.
- Fishing line from the nearby pier is a snag hazard.
