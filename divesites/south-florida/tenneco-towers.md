## Tenneco Towers

Tenneco Towers is an artificial reef of decommissioned Gulf of Mexico oil platforms placed off Fort Lauderdale and Hallandale Beach in October 1985.

## Overview

The Tenneco Oil Company made the platforms available in 1985 to Broward County's artificial reef program, and they were placed at their present location in October 1985 (Duikersgids; WFLX). Duikersgids describes three sites (West Tenneco, East Tenneco and the deep Tenneco's) with five platforms in total, while a 2006 WFLX article says Tenneco donated three rigs, so the count differs by source. The rigs run west to east in about 115 feet of water east of Hallandale Beach near the Dade/Broward line (WFLX). Three platforms are within recreational limits with top decks at about 60, 80 and 100 feet (18, 24 and 30 m); the other two lie at roughly 200 feet (about 60 m) and are technical-diving territory (Squalo Divers; Florida Scuba Diving; Duikersgids). Squalo Divers and Florida Scuba Diving give the maximum recreational depth as 130 feet (40 m).

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

- **Operator and guide reports:** Squalo Divers lists many sponges, hard and soft corals, angelfish, hogfish, turtles, barracuda, tuna and sharks. Florida Scuba Diving lists barracuda, bull sharks, amberjack, lionfish, turtles, angelfish, hogfish, tuna and mackerel. Duikersgids lists barracuda, bull shark, amberjack, lionfish, mackerel, turtle and tuna.
- **Diver account (WFLX, 2006):** barracuda and amberjack on the descent, soft corals, schools of baitfish, gray angelfish, a level filled with grunts and snappers, and hermit crabs, damselfish and eels on a lower deck.
- **Scientific survey:** A 1986 diver census of the "Tenneco II" platforms, 10 months after relocation, recorded 47 fish species. Jacks (bar jack and greater amberjack) were 53% of identified observations, snappers (gray and blackfin) 24%, surgeonfishes 13% and great barracuda 7%; the top deck at about 18 m was dominated by bluehead wrasse and bicolor damselfish (Bulletin of Marine Science, via UCSB Platform Science). This reflects conditions less than a year after sinking, not today.
- Earlier text on this page named parrotfish, blue tangs and graysby, and said lionfish are culled by some operators. None of that could be traced to a source and it has been removed (lionfish presence is reported by Florida Scuba Diving).

## Site History

- Placed October 1985 (Duikersgids; WFLX; Florida Scuba Diving says 1985). Earlier text on this page gave the exact date as October 3, 1985; no source consulted supports a specific day, so only the month is stated.
- Duikersgids says some platforms now lean at about 45 degrees as a result of Hurricane Andrew in 1992. Earlier text said the two deep platforms were the ones tilted; the source does not specify which. WFLX says parts of the rigs have collapsed and fallen into one another while the middle rig still stands tall with three decks of grating.

## Dive Profile

Descending, divers first reach the top platform at about 18 m (Duikersgids). WFLX describes the middle rig as having three decks, the top about 65-70 feet deep. Visibility of 10-30 m is reported (Duikersgids; Florida Scuba Diving). Strong currents can occur depending on the tide (Duikersgids; Florida Scuba Diving).

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only. Advanced Open Water and Deep Diver training are the stated minimums for the recreational platforms (Squalo Divers); the deepest two platforms need technical training.

## Safety Considerations

- Currents can be strong depending on tide; plan with the operator.
- The steel framework is a complex overhead environment in places; do not enter structure without appropriate training and equipment.
- The deep platforms are beyond recreational limits.
