## Mercedes I

The freighter that famously ran aground in socialite Mollie Wilmot's Palm Beach backyard in 1984, later sunk as a Fort Lauderdale artificial reef.

## Overview

Mercedes I is a 194-foot freighter built in Hamburg, Germany in 1952. Under a succession of earlier names, she broke free in a storm on November 23, 1984 and grounded on the seawall of socialite Mollie Wilmot's Palm Beach estate — a widely reported incident at the time. Purchased and salvaged, she was deliberately scuttled with explosives off Fort Lauderdale on March 30, 1985 as an artificial reef, where she rests upright in about 97 feet of water, now twisted into two pieces.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Vessel History

Built in Hamburg in 1952 as the *Jacob Rusch*, the 194-foot, 496-gross-ton freighter was later renamed *Rosita Maria*, then *Rita Voge*, and finally *Mercedes I* in 1976. On November 23, 1984, she broke her mooring in a storm and was driven ashore by 15-foot swells, crashing into the seawall of Mollie Wilmot's Palm Beach beachfront property — her 12-man crew stepped safely onto dry land. After a costly salvage effort, Broward County purchased the hulk and scuttled her with explosives off Fort Lauderdale on March 30, 1985 to create an artificial reef. She now rests upright in approximately 97 feet (30m) of water and has since broken into two pieces.

## Marine Life

Black grouper, hogfish, and glassy sweepers are commonly reported around the structure, along with large baitfish schools that draw in predators.

## Dive Profile

The wreck sits upright but twisted into two separate sections, with large openings into the interior. Depth reaches approximately 30 meters (97-98 feet). The broken structure creates swim-through opportunities, but the openings and interior spaces carry entanglement risk.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, run by Fort Lauderdale-area charters. Depth and structure suit advanced divers.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The wreck's twisted, broken structure makes for interesting photography, particularly around the openings where light penetrates the hull.
- Ask your operator about current conditions of the day — this is a deep dive with limited bottom time.

## Safety Considerations

- Penetration of the broken hull sections carries real entanglement risk from loose cable, wiring, and debris; only attempt interior swim-throughs with appropriate wreck training and equipment, and never treat any wreck penetration as risk-free.
- Depth (~30m/97ft) requires careful gas and no-decompression-limit management.
- Confirm current conditions with your operator before descending.
