## Mercedes I

The Mercedes I is a German-built freighter that grounded on a Palm Beach seawall in 1984 and was sunk as an artificial reef off Fort Lauderdale in March 1985.

## Overview

The wreck rests in about 97 feet of water (Wikipedia; South Florida Diving Headquarters) off Fort Lauderdale. Storms have damaged her: South Florida Diving Headquarters says the wreckage is twisted into two pieces with large holes and the midship has largely collapsed, PADI describes large collapsed sections and hull pieces scattered in the sand, and Sea Experience says Hurricane Andrew and later storms left the bow and stern nearly detached. Depth conflict: PADI gives a maximum of 9 meters, which appears to be an error against 97 feet (about 30 m) from Wikipedia and South Florida Diving Headquarters; Sea Experience gives 75-95 feet.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

South Florida Diving Headquarters calls the wreck "a great fish haven" but names no species. PADI's page lists generic regional animals (sharks, whales, dolphins, turtles) that are not credible site-specific reports, so no species are attributed to this wreck here. Earlier text naming black grouper, hogfish and glassy sweepers could not be traced to a source and has been removed.

## Vessel History

- **Name and build:** Built in Hamburg, Germany in 1952 as the *Jacob Rusch*; later names include *Rosita Maria* and *Rita Voge* before *Mercedes I* (Wikipedia; South Florida Diving Headquarters; the 1976 renaming is Wikipedia only).
- **Length:** 194 feet (Wikipedia; South Florida Diving Headquarters, which adds a 30.6-foot beam). PADI and Sea Experience give 197 feet, and a Miami New Times article also calls her a 197-foot freighter, so length varies between 194 and 197 feet.
- **Grounding:** On November 23, 1984 she broke from her moorings in a storm and grounded off Palm Beach (Wikipedia; South Florida Diving Headquarters; PADI). Wikipedia says she struck the seawall of the home of socialite Mollie Wilmot.
- **Sinking:** Sold for $29,000 to the Broward County Environmental (Quality) Control Board and deliberately sunk on March 30, 1985 as an artificial reef (Wikipedia; South Florida Diving Headquarters; Sea Experience gives the same date). Gross tonnage (496 GRT) and the 350-pound explosive charge appear only in Wikipedia and are not stated as confirmed.

## Dive Profile

Advanced Open Water or equivalent is recommended (South Florida Diving Headquarters; PADI). South Florida Diving Headquarters warns of entanglement hazards in the twisted wreckage.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive. The wreck is broken, with torn plating and openings; penetration should not be attempted without wreck training, lights, a reel and a plan.

## Safety Considerations

- Entanglement and silting are real hazards in the collapsed sections.
- Depth near 100 feet limits no-decompression time.
