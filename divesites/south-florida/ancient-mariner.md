## Ancient Mariner

A WWII Coast Guard cutter turned Fort Lauderdale restaurant, later scuttled off Deerfield Beach as an artificial reef.

## Overview

The Ancient Mariner began life as the U.S. Coast Guard cutter *Nemesis* (WPC-111), built in 1934 by the Marietta Manufacturing Company. She served on Gulf Sea Frontier duty during World War II, including rescue operations and depth-charge attacks against U-boats in 1942. Decades later she was converted into a Fort Lauderdale restaurant, closing after a 1986 hepatitis outbreak, before being purchased by a local dive club and sunk as an artificial reef off Deerfield Beach on June 9, 1991.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Vessel History

Launched July 7, 1934 and commissioned that October as USCGC *Nemesis* (WPC-111), the cutter defended the Gulf Sea Frontier during World War II, rescuing survivors of torpedoed vessels and conducting depth-charge attacks against enemy submarines in 1942. In 1979 she was converted into a Fort Lauderdale restaurant named Livingston's Landing (later renamed Ancient Mariner); the restaurant closed following a 1986 hepatitis outbreak among patrons. She was subsequently sunk as an artificial reef off Deerfield Beach on June 9, 1991.

## Marine Life

A resident large Goliath grouper is commonly found in the wheelhouse, and the wreck sits close to the Berry Patch Tug, with both sites often dived together in the right current.

## Dive Profile

The wreck sits upright with a slight list to port at approximately 21 meters (70 feet) depth. Divers commonly explore the deck structure and wheelhouse, where the resident Goliath grouper is the signature sighting.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, run by Deerfield Beach and Pompano Beach-area charters, often paired with the nearby Berry Patch Tug on the same trip.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Check with your operator about pairing this dive with the Berry Patch Tug, which sits close by.
- The wheelhouse is the best spot to find the resident Goliath grouper.

## Safety Considerations

- Any interior exploration of the wheelhouse or deck structures should be limited to areas with clear ambient light and an easy exit; full penetration requires wreck-diving training and is never without risk.
- Depth (~21m/70ft) is within standard recreational limits but monitor bottom time and gas as usual.
