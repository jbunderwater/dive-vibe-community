## Jupiter Wreck Trek (Zion Train / Miss Jenny / Esso Bonaire)

A three-wreck drift dive off Jupiter Inlet built around one of the largest year-round Goliath grouper populations on the Florida coast.

## Overview

The Jupiter Wreck Trek strings together three separate wrecks in roughly 90 feet of water about a mile north of Jupiter Inlet: the Zion Train, the Miss Jenny barge, and the Esso Bonaire III. The site is a major Goliath grouper aggregation area (August-September) and hosts seasonal lemon shark activity (January-February). Drift-dive conditions are typical; divers are usually dropped up-current and picked up down-current after covering all three wrecks. ## Vessel History

**Esso Bonaire III** is the largest and oldest of the three wrecks. A former oil tanker built in 1939, she was intercepted by the U.S. Coast Guard on March 19, 1989 roughly 50 miles east of the Abaco Islands carrying a marijuana cargo that sources report at between 50,000 and 55,000 pounds. Palm Beach County purchased the forfeited vessel and deployed her as an artificial reef on July 23, 1989; during the sinking she initially refused to go down and had to be rammed by a tug to send her to the bottom. She now sits upright in about 90 feet of water with her deck at roughly 75 feet, her holds wide open for easy viewing.

**Zion Train** is a 164-foot cargo ship scuttled in June 2003. Hurricane-driven storm surge (Frances and Jeanne in 2004) subsequently broke the hull apart; she now lies on her port side with her bow separated from the rest of the wreck.

**Miss Jenny** is a small hopper/dredge barge donated to the artificial reef program by the Maritime Tug & Barge Company. She was sunk north of the Zion Train and rests upside-down, providing habitat for Goliath, gag, and black grouper.

Some sources add further detail to the Zion Train's pre-scuttling history (an earlier build in the Netherlands, a violent incident involving her crew, and a prior grounding off Miami Beach), but these specifics could not be confirmed against a second independent source and are omitted here pending verification.

## Marine Life

Goliath grouper are the signature sighting, with an August-September spawning aggregation that can bring 40-60+ fish onto the wrecks; smaller year-round numbers (often described as several individuals on the Zion Train) are typical outside the season. Lemon sharks pass through the site in numbers, particularly January-February. Divers also report gag and black grouper sheltering under the Miss Jenny.

## Dive Profile

The standard route runs the length of all three wrecks as a single drift: starting on the Esso Bonaire's open holds and cabin structure, continuing to the inverted Miss Jenny, and finishing on the broken, listing Zion Train. The Esso Bonaire's cabin area can be entered by divers with appropriate wreck-diving training, but her lower compartments should not be entered without full penetration training and equipment — wreck penetration always carries risk regardless of experience level.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, typically run as a live-boat drift out of Jupiter-area operators. Divers descend on the up-current wreck and are picked up by the boat at the end of the drift with a surface marker buoy deployed.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Carry a surface marker buoy and reel; this is a drift dive with boat pickup, not an anchor dive.
- Plan the dive around the current direction — operators typically start divers on whichever wreck is up-current that day.
- Goliath grouper spawning season (Aug-Sep) and lemon shark season (Jan-Feb) are the two highest-demand booking windows.

## Safety Considerations

- Do not enter the Esso Bonaire's lower compartments without wreck-penetration training and proper equipment; the cabin area is more forgiving but still carries entanglement and overhead-environment risk.
- Currents can be brisk; stay with the group and signal the boat promptly at the end of the drift.
- Depth (up to 90 feet) puts this dive within recreational no-decompression limits but leaves little margin — monitor bottom time and ascent rate.
- Lemon sharks and Goliath grouper aggregations draw crowds of divers in season; maintain distance and avoid crowding the animals.
