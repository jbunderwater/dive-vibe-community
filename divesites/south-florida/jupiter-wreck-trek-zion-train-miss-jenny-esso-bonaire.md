## Jupiter Wreck Trek (Zion Train / Miss Jenny / Esso Bonaire)

The Jupiter Wreck Trek is a three-wreck drift dive off Jupiter Inlet strung together by the Zion Train, the upside-down barge Miss Jenny and the Esso Bonaire, all in roughly 80-90 feet of water.

## Overview

Divers drift south to north past the bow and stern remains of the Zion Train, the small barge Miss Jenny, and the Esso Bonaire (Reef Smart Guides; Palm Beach County Diving Association). The wrecks lie in roughly 80-90 feet of water (Undercurrent trip report, which gives 80-90 feet; ScubaVerse and Florida Scuba Diving give about 90 feet) about a mile north of Jupiter Inlet (DiveSpots). A short gap of about 100 feet separates Miss Jenny from the Esso Bonaire (Palm Beach County Diving Association). Earlier notes on this page claimed the site lies outside the destination's bounding box; the destination bounds in destinations.json run to 27.0 N, so that flag was incorrect and has been removed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters

## Marine Life

- **Goliath grouper:** Resident goliath groupers are reported on the Zion Train, Miss Jenny and Esso Bonaire (PADI; ScubaVerse). Counts during the late-summer aggregation differ widely by source: PADI says as many as six or seven on the Zion Train, ScubaVerse says up to 20 on the Zion Train, Florida Scuba Diving says 40 to 60 during mid-August to September spawning, and the Palm Beach County Diving Association says 60 to 100 plus in August and September.
- **Lemon sharks:** PADI and ScubaVerse report up to about two dozen lemon sharks circling a pile of concrete beams just east of the Zion Train in January and February (PADI places the viewing spot at 80 feet).
- **Other:** PADI lists gag and black groupers at Miss Jenny. An Undercurrent trip report lists hogfish, grunts and snappers on the wreck trek.

## Wreck History

- **Zion Train:** A freighter built in the Netherlands in 1962 and 164 feet long (Palm Beach County Diving Association; DiveSpots), scuttled June 2, 2003 (DiveSpots; Palm Beach County Diving Association says June 2003; Florida Scuba Diving says June 2003). Hurricanes Frances and Jean in 2004 broke up the hull and scattered pieces, leaving mainly the stern section (Palm Beach County Diving Association; Florida Scuba Diving), and PADI describes the bow as broken off by a hurricane. DiveSpots reports the ship's earlier history as a Miami River incident and later seizure; that account is from a single source and is not repeated here.
- **Miss Jenny:** An upside-down barge (PADI; ScubaVerse; Palm Beach County Diving Association). ScubaVerse says it was sunk in 1990; one search summary described it as a 55-foot dredge barge donated by Maritime Tug & Barge Company. These details come from single sources.
- **Esso Bonaire:** A freighter 146 feet long and the oldest of the three, sunk in the 1980s (Palm Beach County Diving Association; Florida Scuba Diving says 1980s). It remains upright because it was not disturbed by the 2004 storms (Palm Beach County Diving Association). ScubaVerse calls it the largest of the three, with its deck at about 75 feet. Exact build and sinking dates were not confirmed by two sources, so none are stated.

## Dive Profile

Boat drift dive, typically with a north-flowing current: Zion Train first, then Miss Jenny, then the Esso Bonaire (Reef Smart Guides; Palm Beach County Diving Association). ScubaVerse says rebar placed every ~10 m in the sand marks the path to the Esso Bonaire. The Palm Beach County Diving Association says the wrecks are valued for fish aggregations rather than for serious penetration.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Jupiter-area operators. Any entry into the Esso Bonaire or other structure requires wreck-diving training and equipment; earlier text on this page about a specific enterable "cabin area" could not be verified and has been removed.

## Safety Considerations

- Gulf Stream current runs strongly along this stretch; use an SMB.
- Depth of about 90 feet limits no-decompression time.
