## Berry Patch Tug

A compact 1940s Bahamas sand-barge tug, sunk in 1987 within easy reach of the nearby Ancient Mariner.

## Overview

The Berry Patch Tug is a 62-foot, 16-foot-beam tug built in 1940 as the *A.L. Spencer* and originally used to tow sand barges from the Bahamas to Florida. She was sunk as an artificial reef on August 15, 1987 and now rests upright, settled into the sand with her bow facing south, close to the Ancient Mariner wreck.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Vessel History

Built in 1940 as the *A.L. Spencer*, the 62-foot tug spent her working life towing sand barges from the Bahamas to Florida. She was deployed as an artificial reef on August 15, 1987 and sits upright in about 70 feet of water, bow facing south.

## Marine Life

The tug's compact structure is covered with growth and hosts typical southeast Florida reef fish; it is commonly dived alongside the nearby Ancient Mariner when current allows.

## Dive Profile

A small, easily circuited wreck at approximately 21 meters (70 feet) depth. Given its size and proximity to the Ancient Mariner, many operators run both wrecks on a single dive when conditions and air consumption allow.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, typically combined with the adjacent Ancient Mariner on the same trip out of Deerfield Beach or Pompano Beach.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Given its small size, this wreck is often paired with the Ancient Mariner on the same dive — ask your operator about the swim distance and current before planning to do both.

## Safety Considerations

- Depth (~21m/70ft) is within standard recreational limits.
- Interior spaces on small tugs like this can have sharp, corroded edges and limited room to maneuver — avoid entering without appropriate training.
