## Berry Patch Tug

The Berry Patch Tug is a small harbor tug sunk off Deerfield Beach in August 1987, a short swim from the Ancient Mariner.

## Overview

A small steel tug, originally the *A.L. Spencer* built in 1940, that towed sand barges from the Bahamas to Florida before being sunk as an artificial reef on August 15, 1987 (South Florida Diving Headquarters; Florida Go Fishing's Broward reef list also gives a 1940 tug named A.L. Spencer). She sits upright, settled into the sand with the bow facing south, in about 65-70 feet of water (South Florida Diving Headquarters; a Western Oregon University diver's site list gives 65 feet).

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Marine Life

South Florida Diving Headquarters says only that the wreck is covered with growth and tropical fish. No species are named by the sources consulted.

## Vessel History

- **Name and build:** *A.L. Spencer*, built 1940 (South Florida Diving Headquarters; Florida Go Fishing).
- **Length:** 62 feet by 16 feet (South Florida Diving Headquarters); the county reef list gives 65 x 16 feet and also lists a 50-foot steel houseboat under a second "Berry Patch" entry, so the length differs by source.
- **Service and sinking:** Used to tow sand barges from the Bahamas to Florida; sunk August 15, 1987 as an artificial reef (South Florida Diving Headquarters). Only that source gave the sinking date, so it is single-sourced.

## Dive Profile

South Florida Diving Headquarters describes an anchor dive suitable for Open Water divers. Aqua Life Divers describes reaching the tug from the Ancient Mariner by swimming about 150 feet on a heading of 150 degrees from the bow.
