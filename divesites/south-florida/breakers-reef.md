## Breakers Reef

Breakers Reef is a north-south ledge reef offshore from the Breakers Hotel at Palm Beach, dived as a Gulf Stream drift in 50-65 feet of water.

## Overview

A reef line running north to south in 50-65 feet of water, named for the Breakers Hotel; boats often drop divers at a spot called Fourth Window, in line with the fourth window on the hotel's north section (Force-E). The ledge on the west side is 5-10 feet high, is the shallowest part of the reef, and has the most marine life; the deeper east side is used for spearfishing and lobstering (Force-E; Stuart Scuba). Stuart Scuba lists it as a drift dive suitable for Open Water divers with a maximum depth of 65 feet.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters

## Marine Life

Force-E and Stuart Scuba report loggerhead, green and hawksbill turtles, especially in spring and summer. Stuart Scuba also lists angelfish, parrotfish, grunts, porkfish, sergeant majors and juvenile French angelfish, plus octopus, southern stingrays, spotted eels, stonefish and lemon sharks. Florida Scuba Diving adds goliath grouper, barracuda, green moray eels, jacks and spadefish and reports 15-30 turtles on single dives in season; it also lists many shark species, including great whites, which no other source supports and which are not repeated here. Earlier text on this page claiming lemon sharks are more common in cooler months, and that spiny lobster and Caribbean reef octopus shelter on the western ledge, could not be traced to a source and has been removed.

## Dive Profile

The reef features continuous ledges, undercuts and crevices (Stuart Scuba; Florida Scuba Diving). Florida Scuba Diving gives a depth of 42-60 feet, an inside wall rising from 30 feet in the sand to 20 feet on top with a 4-8 foot ledge covered in barrel sponges and soft corals, a statue of King Neptune at the north end, and remnants of a pier destroyed in the 1928 hurricane. Those details come from that one source. Visibility is reported as 60-70 feet on good days, with water temperatures of 73-80 F (Florida Scuba Diving).

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only; Force-E and Stuart Scuba run it on their schedules. Divers are typically dropped on the up-current end and picked up down-reef as a drift.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Carry an SMB and a reel; the dive is a Gulf Stream drift.
- Stuart Scuba suggests a camera, dive computer, dive flag, SMB and wetsuit, with a reef hook optional.

## Safety Considerations

- Current can be strong; do not try to swim against it.
- Boats are common off Palm Beach; ascend with an SMB.
