## Sea Emperor (Aqua Zoo)

The Sea Emperor is an overturned hopper barge and its spilled concrete culverts off Boca Raton, nicknamed the Aqua Zoo.

## Overview

A 171-foot hopper barge loaded with concrete drainage culverts, sunk off Boca Inlet in about 50-70 feet of water. When it was sunk the barge flipped, scattering the culverts and coming to rest upside down with the bow pointing south (South Florida Diving Headquarters; Florida Scuba Diving). It is one of a three-wreck triangle with the United Caribbean and Noulla Express, linked by a line of limestone boulders (South Florida Diving Headquarters; Florida Scuba Diving; Reef Smart Guides).

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Marine Life

South Florida Diving Headquarters and Florida Scuba Diving both report Goliath groupers, southern stingrays, moray eels (green moray per Florida Scuba Diving) and nurse sharks, along with large numbers of smaller fish. Reef Smart Guides notes the nickname "Aqua Zoo" for the abundance of life.

## Vessel History

- **Type and size:** Hopper barge, 171 feet long (South Florida Diving Headquarters; Florida Scuba Diving; Florida Go Fishing's reef list also gives 171 ft). Beam is given as 45 feet by South Florida Diving Headquarters and 43 feet by Florida Scuba Diving.
- **Why it was sunk:** Donated as part of a fine levied on a dredging company that damaged reef habitat off Palm Beach County (South Florida Diving Headquarters; Florida Scuba Diving, which names Great Lakes Dock and Dredge Company and says damage occurred in 1988).
- **Cargo:** Concrete drainage culverts, about 1,600 tons per South Florida Diving Headquarters and about 1,500 tons per Florida Scuba Diving's history section (which elsewhere repeats 1,600).
- **Date:** Florida Scuba Diving gives October 3, 1991; no second source consulted confirms the date, so it is not stated as fact.

## Dive Profile

The barge is upside down next to a pile of culverts; light reaches into some chambers according to South Florida Diving Headquarters. Depth is 50-70 feet (South Florida Diving Headquarters), 55-70 feet (Florida Scuba Diving) (a Western Oregon University diver's list also gives a maximum of 70 feet).

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive. Both operator-oriented sources describe the interior as being open to light and describe penetration as safe; that characterization is not repeated here. Any overhead entry into an inverted barge and tumbled culverts carries entrapment and entanglement risk and requires training and proper equipment.

## Safety Considerations

- Stay out of voids you cannot see the exit from; the culverts can shift.
- Use an SMB and watch for boat traffic.
