## Sea Emperor (Aqua Zoo)

An overturned hopper barge off Boca Raton whose scattered cargo of concrete drainage culverts created the swim-through playground nicknamed the "Aqua Zoo."

## Overview

The Sea Emperor is a 171-foot hopper barge sunk off Boca Raton as part of a legal settlement after a dredging company damaged local reef habitat. She went down carrying approximately 1,600 tons of concrete drainage culverts in roughly 70-72 feet of water. During the sinking the barge overturned, scattering the culverts into a stacked-pipe swim-through structure that divers nicknamed the "Aqua Zoo."

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Florida, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Vessel History

The Sea Emperor was deployed as a fine levied against a dredging company after its work destroyed reef habitat off Palm Beach County. The 171-foot hopper barge was loaded with about 1,600 tons of concrete drainage culverts and deliberately sunk; the controlled sinking did not go as planned and the barge flipped upside down, spilling the culverts across the seafloor next to the hull.

## Marine Life

The Aqua Zoo's stacked culvert pipes and the overturned hull are reported to shelter large southern stingrays, nurse sharks, and moray eels, with Goliath grouper appearing seasonally.

## Dive Profile

The dive covers both the overturned barge hull and the adjacent field of scattered and stacked concrete culverts, which create swim-through passages for divers comfortable with light overhead sections. Depth across the site runs roughly 70-72 feet.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only, run by Boca Raton-area charters.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The stacked culverts offer swim-throughs, but treat them as an overhead environment — maintain good buoyancy and avoid silting.
- Southern stingrays and nurse sharks are commonly found resting in and around the culvert piles; approach slowly.

## Safety Considerations

- Swim-throughs among the culverts carry entanglement and overhead-environment risk; only enter with appropriate buoyancy control and a clear exit in sight.
- The overturned hull has sharp, corroded edges in places — maintain distance when not actively viewing a feature.
- Depth and any current should be confirmed with your operator before the dive.
