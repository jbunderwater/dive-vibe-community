## Julia Tuttle Causeway Artificial Reef

The Julia Tuttle Artificial Reef Site in Biscayne Bay is a fisheries-enhancement reef built up in stages since 2018 from limestone boulders, a barge and recycled concrete.

## Overview

Miami-Dade County's deployment log shows a multi-stage site rather than one structure: concrete deployed in February 2018 (about 30 tons, 24 ft depth, 6 ft relief); about 727 tons of limerock boulders placed on April 18, 2019 on top of the previously deployed Larson Barge (32 ft depth, 17 ft relief); about 45 tons of demolished concrete intermixed with the boulders on December 5, 2019; 73 bridge pilings from the FDOT SW 1st Bridge on February 19, 2020 (27 ft, 8 ft relief); about 2,316 tons of demolished Dinner Key Pier concrete on February 28, 2020 (23 ft, 9 ft relief); about 345 tons of Bentley Bay Pier concrete on December 19, 2022 (27 ft, 8 ft relief); and about 225 tons of dock/gazebo concrete plus about 700 tons of limerock from Fisher Island on July 7, 2023 (27 ft, 13 ft relief) (Miami-Dade County). Industrial Divers Corp, the contractor, states it built a 727-ton limestone boulder reef near the Julia Tuttle Causeway in April 2019 to the specifications of Miami-Dade DERM biologists.

## Marine Life

No marine-life reports were found in the sources consulted; neither the county log nor the contractor describes species at the site. The regional fauna applies, but none is attributed here. Earlier text that the reef "attracts diverse marine species" could not be traced to a source and has been removed.

## Dive Profile

Deployment depths in the county log are 23-32 feet. The sources are construction records, not dive reports, so no dive profile or navigation is described.

## Entry and Exit

Entry type "boat" is carried over from the dataset; none of the sources describes access. The county publishes an artificial reef locator map (Miami-Dade Reef and Buoy Finder) and coordinates for each deployment; the April 2019 boulder deployment is listed at 25 deg 48.899 N, 80 deg 10.269 W.

## Safety Considerations

- Stacked boulders, pilings and concrete create pinch points and entanglement hazards.
- The site is in a busy nearshore Miami corridor; a dive flag and SMB are advisable.
