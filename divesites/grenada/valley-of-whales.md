# Valley of Whales

A wall and drift dive on Grenada's southwest coast near St. George's — beginning in a shallow forest of brown corals and continuing down a shelving slope of gorgonians to about 28 metres, past an encrusted Admiralty anchor, with a drift toward Dragon Bay when the tide is running.

## Overview

Valley of Whales (locally known as Happy Valley) is a wall and drift dive on Grenada's southwest coast, near St. George's harbour. The site begins in a shallow forest of brown coral at around 6 metres and follows a shelving slope covered in gorgonians and soft coral, pillar coral formations, and elkhorn coral down to approximately 28 metres (90 feet). An ancient, heavily encrusted Admiralty anchor rests amid the flora at mid-depth — confirmed by three independent sources (turq.com, PADI, ECO Dive). When tide is running, the site becomes a pleasant drift dive flowing gently southward toward Dragon Bay. Visibility averages 20–30 metres. Water temperature is 26–28°C.

**Site identity note**: Grenada dive guides are not fully consistent about "Valley of Whales" naming. turq.com describes a whale-and-calf sighting as the naming origin for a site it calls "Valleys of the Whale" — but places that story at a shallower ~18m (60ft) within the separate "Valleys" section of Boss Reef further north, not at this 28m wall site. No second independent source could be found to corroborate the whale-sighting story despite a targeted search, so it is not stated as established fact here — this site's depth, wall profile, Admiralty anchor, and drift-to-Dragon-Bay character match what PADI and ECO Dive both catalogue as "Happy Valley" far more closely. A previously-reported "vehicle graveyard (cars and buses)" at 90ft was removed on 2026-08-05 re-audit: a direct, current fetch of the PADI page for this site contains no such mention, and the other source it had been attributed to (Sportif Dive) is no longer reachable, so the claim could not be re-verified.

## Marine Life

The soft coral-rich slope at Valley of Whales hosts the reef fish community typical of Grenada's southwest wall sites. Spotted drums, yellowtail snapper, bara jacks, and banded jawfish are confirmed residents. Lobsters, spotted morays, and nurse sharks (sleeping) inhabit the reef. Long-snout seahorses and pipefish are occasionally spotted in the reef. The ancient Admiralty anchor at mid-depth is a distinctive artificial structure that attracts encrusting life. Note: "Caribbean reef sharks, eagle rays, and tiger groupers as regular sightings" from previous descriptions were not confirmed by primary sources for this specific site, and (as of the 2026-08-05 re-audit) neither is the whale sighting the site's name suggests — see the site identity note above.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the shallow coral forest at 6–8 metres and follow the shelving slope as it descends to about 28 metres. The Admiralty anchor is a useful landmark at mid-depth. This is typically dived as a gentle drift when current is running. Ascend to the shallow brown coral zone for the safety stop. Time at 28 metres should be brief to allow an adequate safety stop at the shallower sections. There is no confirmed vehicle graveyard or similar deep-water landmark on this dive — do not plan the profile around one.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from True Blue Bay or Grand Anse operators, with the southern tip location typically requiring a 20–30 minute run. Permanent moorings are attached to the reef crest. Entry by backward roll or giant stride. DSMB required.

## Tips and Recommendations

Look for the Admiralty anchor at mid-depth — it is one of Grenada's least-publicised historical artefacts on a dive site. The site has a drift character when current runs toward Dragon Bay; plan accordingly. The shallow brown coral forest at the start of the dive rewards slow exploration before descending the slope — look for seahorses and pipefish in the reef here.

## Safety Considerations

Check current conditions before diving — the site can run as a drift when the Guiana Current is active. DSMB is essential. Depth management is important given the range from 6–28 metres; ascend progressively and complete the full safety stop in the shallower reef zone.

## Photography

The wall and slope topography creates strong compositional opportunities — wide-angle shots of the shelving coral slope with the open blue behind, pillar coral formations, and the Admiralty anchor encrusted with marine life are the defining subjects. Macro shooters will find seahorses and pipefish. Note: "eagle ray passes through channels" referenced in previous descriptions was not confirmed by primary sources for this site.
