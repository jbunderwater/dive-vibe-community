## Perpendicular Wall

Perpendicular Wall is Christmas Island's signature wall dive, located at the northwest tip of the island, where the reef drops nearly vertically from the surface to beyond 36 metres.

## Overview

Located at the northwest tip of Christmas Island, Perpendicular Wall is considered the premier wall dive on the island. The cliff face runs almost exactly perpendicular to the ocean's surface, dropping from a shallow reef at around 5 metres to depths beyond 36 metres, with the wall dropping far beyond recreational limits. Corals grow out horizontally from the face to catch the passing current, and large gorgonian fans extend perpendicular to the wall. Fusiliers school vertically along the wall, while other reef fish — including blue-lined angelfish and feather stars — travel horizontally along it. DivePlanit and X-Ray Mag both note the presence of hydrocorals and abundant sea fans. Nigel Marsh's dive log documents an overhang thick with gorgonians at the start of the dive, soft corals, whip corals and sponges along the wall face, masked morays, and a harlequin hind (a rockcod normally found deeper) — plus batfish, trevally, and barracuda cruising past in open water. The exposed location means strong currents and open-ocean swell are common — this is an Advanced site. One scalloped hammerhead sighting is documented in Nigel Marsh's dive log, in which the shark departed before other divers could see it; schooling hammerheads are not confirmed for this site. **Correction (2026-07-23 re-audit):** earlier text claimed dolphins were "frequently encountered before entry" and that manta rays and whale sharks were "occasionally seen along the wall" — neither claim is supported by any of the three cited sources for this specific site, and both have been removed. Whale sharks and mantas are documented Christmas Island regional species (seen at other sites and around the island generally) but are not confirmed at Perpendicular Wall specifically in available sources.
