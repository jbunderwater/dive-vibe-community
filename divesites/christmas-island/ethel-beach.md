## Ethel Beach

Ethel Beach is a sheltered shore dive on Christmas Island's northern coast with a fringing reef noted for its unspoiled coral formations and accessible conditions.

## Overview

Ethel Beach sits on the eastern coast of Christmas Island, reached by a short (~50 m) path from the road. A boat ramp cut through the rock at the beach is used by dive operators as an alternative launch point during the northwest monsoon season (roughly November–March), when swell makes the main Flying Fish Cove ramp unusable and boats work the west coast from here instead. The fringing reef just offshore is described by local sources as among the most pristine on the island, with colourful corals and abundant fish life visible in shallow water, and the site is suitable for beginners and snorkellers. Sources disagree on turtle nesting here: Christmas Island National Park names Dolly Beach and Greta Beach as the island's primary green turtle nesting sites and does not mention Ethel Beach in that context, while several tourism sources describe occasional turtle nesting and sightings at Ethel Beach too. No site-specific species counts have been verified beyond general reef fish observations; the broader reef fauna that applies to Christmas Island's north coast generally includes surgeonfish, wrasse, and butterflyfishes. Strong currents can develop beyond the reef, so divers should stay within the protected shallows.
