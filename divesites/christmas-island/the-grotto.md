## The Grotto

The Grotto is a small limestone cave and swimming hole on Christmas Island's northeast coast, where a shallow freshwater-and-seawater pool connects to the ocean through an underwater tunnel that creates a distinctive surge and swirl.

## Overview

**Correction (2026-07-23 re-audit):** this listing previously described The Grotto as a 12-metre scuba dive. On re-checking sources, the two previously-cited references (Australian Geographic's "Christmas Island Caving and Diving" article and the Christmas Island Tourism scuba-diving page) do not actually mention The Grotto at all — the Christmas Island National Park geology page's only reference was a photo caption. Independent visitor accounts (multiple TripAdvisor reviews) and caving references (showcaves.com) instead consistently describe the pool itself as shallow, around a metre deep in most places, with a rope used to climb down into the water. Depth and difficulty have been corrected accordingly, and the description below has been rewritten to reflect what is actually documented.

The Grotto sits near the northeast tip of Christmas Island, about ten minutes' drive from Settlement and a short (30-100 m) walk from Golf Course Road. It is a small limestone cave, roughly 20 metres long, with a pool fed by both freshwater seepage and seawater. The pool connects to the open ocean through an underwater tunnel, and incoming swell drives a surge and swirl effect inside the chamber — some visitors describe a "roaring" sound as waves push through. It is used locally mainly as a swimming hole and snorkelling spot; low tide is recommended, since high tide makes the water choppier. Whether The Grotto is used as a genuine scuba dive site — as opposed to a swim/snorkel feature that some divers pass through via the underwater tunnel to reach the open reef — is not confirmed by any dive-specific source found; local operators should be consulted for current diving access and conditions. No site-specific marine life data has been found for The Grotto; the surrounding reef fauna typical of Christmas Island's northeast coast applies generally, but no species have been confirmed as sighted at this specific location.
