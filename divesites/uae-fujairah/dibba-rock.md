## Dibba Rock

Dibba Rock is a small rocky island off Dibba on the UAE's east coast, dived by boat in a circuit around the rock.

## Overview

Dibba Rock is a small island surrounded by coral reef. Sources describe sloping sides covered in soft and boulder corals, with green and purple whip corals on the seaward side. One travel source describes a roughly 200 m pinnacle whose first part is a steep wall and whose second part is a gentler slope ending in boulder corals; operator and listing sources describe the site mainly as a sloping reef, so it is classed here as a reef. The site is easy to navigate by swimming around the rock. One operator describes it as the oldest marine protected area in the UAE and a no-fishing zone for more than 15 years (an operator's own claim that this research did not independently confirm). A 2007 Gulf News report on Cyclone Gonu said the site lost more than half its coral, so older descriptions of its coral cover may not apply.

Marine life reported at this site: turtles (green turtles named by Nemo Diving Center, seen sleeping between rocks at night), blacktip reef sharks (reported by Freestyle Divers and The National, not guaranteed), clownfish and jawfish (Nemo Diving Center), and moray eels, parrotfish, trumpetfish, cuttlefish and rays (Freestyle Divers). Whale sharks are described by Freestyle Divers as a rare sighting.

## Site Information

- **Location**: UAE - Fujairah, Middle East
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 16 meters

## Dive Notes

Sources disagree on maximum depth: about 13 m (a Divers Down review via Undercurrent), 14 m (The National) and 16 m (Freestyle Divers). One operator describes the inshore side as shallow and the deeper sections as being on the east side. Currents can be strong at tidal changes and thermoclines occur; one operator suggests diving the sea-facing north side at low tide. Visibility is reported as about 10 m by Nemo Diving Center. Turtles often sleep here and should not be disturbed. Some listings use the name "Dibba Island" for this site.
