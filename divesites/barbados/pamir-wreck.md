# Pamir Wreck

A former freighter — sources disagree on whether she was 160, 165, or 180 feet (49–55 metres) — sunk in the mid-1980s off Barbados's north coast near Speightstown. She sits upright and largely intact, with an accessible air pocket inside and dense schools of Bermuda chub and blue chromis swarming the hull.

## Overview

The Pamir is a former inter-island freighter and the sister ship of the Friars Craig (see separate entry). She was deliberately sunk as an artificial reef off the northwest coast of Barbados near Almond Beach Village in St Peter parish — some distance north of Carlisle Bay. Sources disagree on the exact sinking year (1983 or 1985 are both cited) and on her length: 160 feet per Caribbean Beat, 165 feet per Dive Hightide and Loop Barbados, and 180 feet per NUMA — consistently in the 160–180 foot (49–55 metre) range but without an agreed figure. Depth is also reported inconsistently, from as shallow as 25 feet at the bow up to a commonly cited maximum of around 60 feet (18 metres); this entry uses the majority-cited ~18 metre maximum. She rests upright on the sandy bottom, largely intact close to the reef edge, with hull openings cut for diver access. The wreck has developed productive colonisation: gorgonians and whip corals have established on the hull, and large fish schools congregate around the structure. The upright position makes navigation straightforward. Visibility averages 15–25 metres. Water temperature is 26–28°C.

## Marine Life

The Pamir is particularly noted for its fish aggregations. Hundreds of Bermuda chub and blue chromis school around the wreck, creating dramatic fish-in-motion photographs. Gorgonians and whip corals cover sections of the hull. Garden eels inhabit the sandy bottom around the wreck. The surrounding reef edge attracts the reef fish community typical of Barbados's north coast. Hawksbill turtles patrol the area.

## Dive Profile

Descent to the wreck deck at 5–8 metres and systematic exploration from bow to stern, dropping to the sandy base at approximately 15–18 metres (sources vary). The upright position makes navigation intuitive. Bottom time is very comfortable at this depth. Safety stop at the upper deck.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from north coast Barbados operators. The Pamir is typically paired with its sister ship Friars Craig (further south) for two-tank north/south wreck days, or combined with nearby reef sites.

## Tips and Recommendations

The Bermuda chub schools are the visual highlight — approach slowly to avoid scattering them. Dive Hightide, operating from the north coast, includes the Pamir regularly on dive programs and knows current wreck conditions. Combine with a nearby reef site for a two-tank day.

## Safety Considerations

Standard wreck diving precautions apply. Do not enter enclosed spaces without wreck training. DSMB required. Light currents are possible on the north coast.

## Photography

The dense chub and chromis schools swarming the Pamir hull are the defining photographic image — wide-angle shots of the school against the hull in the blue water are possible at this depth. Gorgonian close-ups and garden eel portraits on the sandy bottom are secondary subjects.
