## Batu Kapal

Batu Kapal ("Boat Rock" or "Ship Rock") is a pinnacle dive site north of Pulau Pisang in the Banda Islands. The main rock breaks the surface in a shape resembling a boat hull. Submerged pinnacles extend below — a plateau at around 5–8 m gives way to further pinnacles, with a second plateau reported around 45 m by one source, before the site drops beyond 50 m — making this one of the deeper and more current-exposed sites in the Banda archipelago.

## Overview

Multiple sources describe Batu Kapal as a multi-pinnacle site rather than a single wall: large sponges and orange gorgonian sea fans cover the rock faces, with butterflyfish and red-tooth triggerfish sheltering among them. Strong, shifting currents sweep the pinnacles and concentrate pelagic and reef fish: potato cod, grey reef sharks, rainbow runners, longnose emperors, and yellowtail barracuda are reported here, alongside giant and honeycomb moray eels in the crevices, octopus, and sea kraits. (Note: an earlier version of this page described a 15-metre swim-through at Batu Kapal — that feature actually belongs to Pohon Miring, a separate Banda Besar site, and has been removed from this description.)

Visibility at Batu Kapal is exceptional — sources report 40–50 m on good days, particularly between February and May. Strong and unpredictable currents mean diving is impossible at certain tidal states, and the site demands advanced diving skills.

## Dive Profile

Reef flat and plateau from around 5–8 m, with multiple pinnacles dropping beyond 50 m. The 10–35 m range has the densest life. Currents change direction unpredictably — timing dives around slack water is recommended. Best visibility: February–May (40–50 m reported). Typical visibility: 20–30 m. Water temperature: 27–29°C.

## Marine Life

- Grey reef sharks and potato cod
- Rainbow runners, longnose emperors, yellowtail barracuda
- Butterflyfish and red-tooth triggerfish on the pinnacle faces
- Giant moray eels and honeycomb moray eels in crevices
- Octopus and sea kraits
- Large orange gorgonian sea fans and barrel sponges

## Entry / Exit

Boat dive from liveaboard or Banda Neira day-trip vessel. Strong currents mean entry and exit timing must be planned with the guide. Negative entries may be required on some dives.

## Safety

Strong and unpredictable currents — this site is rated Advanced. A surface marker buoy (SMB) is essential. Dive with an experienced local guide who knows the current patterns. Diving may be impossible at some tidal states.

## Photography

The pinnacles' gorgonian fans and barrel sponges are a classic wide-angle subject. High-visibility periods (Feb–May) are ideal for wide-angle open-water work; look for grey reef sharks and rainbow runners passing in the current.
