## Jackpot

Jackpot is an advanced pinnacle dive site most sources place at Serua Island, a remote volcanic outpost southeast of the main Banda archipelago; one source (X-Ray Mag) instead places a site of this name at nearby Suanggi Island. The two islands are visited on the same "forgotten islands" liveaboard routing, and it is possible some operators use the name loosely for either stop — divers should confirm with their operator which island a scheduled "Jackpot" dive refers to.

## Overview

The site features deep-water pinnacles covered in large coral formations and substantial pink barrel sponges. Strong currents sweep the pinnacle tops and flanks, concentrating fish in impressive aggregations. Divers commonly perform negative entries and use reef hooks to shelter from the current before positioning to watch the show. Schooling jacks, large tuna schools, grey reef sharks, and groupers are regular presences.

Sources disagree on how reliable hammerhead encounters are at Jackpot specifically. A first-hand liveaboard trip report (Original Diving) describes Jackpot itself as a reef-structure and schooling-fish dive where "large grey reef sharks might come and say hello," and states that hammerhead and mobula ray encounters are more consistently associated with Manuk Ridge and Palm Point — different sites, at Manuk Island, elsewhere on the same itinerary. Other secondary sources describe hammerhead sightings as possible at Jackpot/Serua during the South East wind season (roughly June–September) and the October–November peak season across the Banda Sea. Given this conflict, hammerheads should be considered a possible but unconfirmed bonus at this specific site rather than a routine sighting.

The site earns its "Jackpot" name from the unpredictability typical of remote Banda Sea pinnacles — on a productive dive, divers can encounter schooling fish, coral walls, and huge barrel sponge formations in a single dive. Conditions vary considerably.

## Dive Profile

Pinnacle dive from the surface to 40+ m. Most action at 15–35 m on the pinnacle shoulders and in the water column above. Strong to very strong currents — negative entry is standard. Visibility variable but can reach 25–30 m in good conditions. Water temperature: 27–29°C.

## Marine Life

- Grey reef sharks in the water column (the primary shark encounter reported by first-hand trip accounts at this site)
- Scalloped hammerhead sharks (possible during SE wind upwelling, June–September, and October–November peak season per secondary sources; a first-hand trip report attributes more reliable hammerhead encounters to Manuk Ridge/Palm Point rather than Jackpot itself — treat as unconfirmed for this specific site)
- Schooling jacks and large tuna schools
- Large groupers
- Large pink barrel sponges
- Hard and soft coral formations on pinnacle faces

## Entry / Exit

Boat dive from liveaboard. Serua Island is only accessible by liveaboard. Negative entry is standard. Drift exit with SMB pickup by tender.

## Safety

Advanced dive due to strong currents and remote location. Surface marker buoy mandatory. Liveaboard guide essential — local knowledge of current windows is critical for safe entry and productive positioning.

## Photography

Wide-angle pinnacle photography — the barrel sponge formations and schooling fish make strong subjects. A hammerhead encounter in clear water would be exceptional, but is not a reliable expectation at this specific site.
