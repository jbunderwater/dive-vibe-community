## Lava Flow

Lava Flow is a coral reef site on the northern flank of Gunung Api, the active volcano that rises from the Banda Sea. The 1988 eruption sent lava cascading into the ocean, and the coral recovery on the volcanic substrate is one of the most celebrated reef regeneration stories in the region. Marine scientist Dr. Charles Veron reported some table corals here growing to over 4 metres in diameter in under 10 years — a rate operators attribute in part to the nutrient content of volcanic ash. A peer-reviewed study of the site (Tomascik & Sander, *Coral Reefs*) independently documented rapid post-eruption colonisation, with a diverse coral community established within five years and tabulate acroporids as a dominant component.

## Overview

The black volcanic substrate has been colonised by exceptionally fast-growing coral. The site now showcases staghorn, plate, table, and cabbage-leaf corals at high density. Table corals grow to impressive proportions across the shallower sections, while deeper areas (20–30 m) feature fusilier aggregations. The dark lava rock creates a striking visual contrast against colourful coral growth.

Napoleon wrasse, batfish, and mobula rays are reported at this site. Note: mandarinfish mating displays are a Banda Neira harbour phenomenon associated with Mandarin City near Hotel Maulana, not with Lava Flow itself — sources describing Banda's mandarinfish dives consistently place them at the harbour pier, not at Gunung Api.

## Dive Profile

A gently sloping reef from the shallows to around 30 m. The shallower sections (3–15 m) hold the densest coral formations. Deeper areas transition to more open substrate with scattered coral blocks and fusilier schools. The site is accessible as both a day dive from Banda Neira and as a liveaboard stop.

## Marine Life

- Napoleon wrasse, batfish, mobula rays
- Red-tooth (clown/black) triggerfish, green-snout parrotfish
- Dense fusilier schools
- Cuttlefish

Mandarinfish are not reported at this site — for mandarinfish, see Mandarin City in Banda Neira harbour.

## Entry / Exit

Boat dive from liveaboard or from Banda Neira-based operators. The site is within reach of day-trip boats from Banda Neira.

## Tips

- Shallow sections (3–10 m) are ideal for snorkellers and beginner divers — the lava flow topography rewards slow exploration
- For a mandarinfish dive, plan a dusk visit to Mandarin City in Banda Neira harbour instead — it is a separate site from Lava Flow

## Photography

One of the Banda Sea's premier wide-angle reef photography sites — the black lava contrasts vividly with colourful coral formations.
