## Lava Flow

Lava Flow is a coral reef site on the northern flank of Gunung Api, the active volcano that rises from the Banda Sea. The 1988 eruption sent lava cascading into the ocean, and the coral recovery on the volcanic substrate is one of the most studied reef regeneration events in the region. A peer-reviewed survey of the site found coral colonies — including tabulate Acropora ("table" corals) — reaching over 90 cm in diameter and covering more than 60% of the substrate within just five years of the eruption, an exceptionally fast colonization rate for reef-building coral.

## Overview

The black volcanic substrate has been colonised by fast-growing coral. The site showcases staghorn, plate, table, and cabbage-leaf corals at densities that drew the original scientific survey of the lava flow; a dive operator guide describes the coral here as having grown roughly three times faster than typical post-disturbance recovery. Table corals reach notable size across the shallower sections, while deeper areas (20–30 m) feature fusilier aggregations. The dark lava rock creates a striking visual contrast against the coral growth.

Mandarinfish (*Synchiropus splendidus*) are reported in the coral rubble at Lava Flow by one liveaboard operator, which schedules dusk dives here to catch the mating display — though Mandarin City in Banda Neira harbour remains the far better-documented and more reliable site for this behaviour, with multiple independent sources confirming mandarinfish there. Napoleon wrasse, batfish, and mobula rays are reported at Lava Flow by multiple sources.

## Dive Profile

A gently sloping reef from the shallows to around 30 m. The shallower sections (3–15 m) hold the densest coral formations. Deeper areas transition to more open substrate with scattered coral blocks and fusilier schools. The site is accessible as both a day dive from Banda Neira and as a liveaboard stop, but — like all Banda Sea sites — is rated Advanced in line with the destination's remote, liveaboard-only character.

## Marine Life

- Fusiliers (dense schools, multiple sources)
- Napoleon wrasse, batfish (round and pinnate species reported)
- Mobula rays, dogtooth tuna, blue trevally
- Clown and black triggerfish, green-snout parrotfish
- Cuttlefish
- Mandarinfish (reported by one source at Lava Flow; Mandarin City in Banda Neira harbour is the primary, best-documented mandarinfish site)
- No site-specific reports of additional species beyond those listed above were found; regional Banda Sea fauna (see destination overview) may also be present but is not confirmed for this specific site

## Entry / Exit

Boat dive from liveaboard or from Banda Neira-based operators. The site is within reach of day-trip boats from Banda Neira.

## Tips

- Schedule a dusk or late-afternoon dive if hoping to see mandarinfish, though Mandarin City is the more reliable choice for that behaviour
- The shallow sections (3–10 m) have the densest coral and reward slow, careful exploration to avoid contact with the fast-growing formations

## Photography

One of the Banda Sea's premier wide-angle reef photography sites — the black lava contrasts vividly with colourful coral formations. Dusk dives offer macro opportunities if mandarinfish are present.
