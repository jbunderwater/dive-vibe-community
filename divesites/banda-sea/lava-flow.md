## Lava Flow

Lava Flow is a coral reef site on the northern flank of Gunung Api, the active volcano that rises from the Banda Sea. The 1988 eruption sent lava cascading into the ocean, and the coral recovery on the volcanic substrate is one of the most celebrated reef regeneration stories in the region. Scientists have documented some table corals here growing to over 4 metres in diameter in under 10 years — a rate attributed in part to the nutrient content of volcanic ash.

## Overview

The black volcanic substrate has been colonised by exceptionally fast-growing coral. The site now showcases staghorn, plate, table, and cabbage-leaf corals at densities that have drawn scientific study. Table corals grow to impressive proportions across the shallower sections, while deeper areas (20–35 m) feature fusilier aggregations. The dark lava rock creates a striking visual contrast against colourful coral growth.

Napoleon wrasse, batfish, and mobula rays are reported at this site. Note: mandarinfish are the signature attraction at nearby Mandarin City in Banda Neira harbour, a dedicated shallow muck site; sources are split on whether they are also present at Lava Flow — one liveaboard operator lists mandarinfish here, but the majority of sources describing this site (including one that explicitly places Banda's mandarinfish dive at a separate shallow "house" site) do not mention them at Lava Flow, so no mandarinfish claim is made for this site.

## Dive Profile

A gently sloping reef from the shallows to around 35 m. The shallower sections (3–15 m) hold the densest coral formations. Deeper areas transition to more open substrate with scattered coral blocks and fusilier schools. The site is accessible as both a day dive from Banda Neira and as a liveaboard stop.

## Marine Life

- Napoleon wrasse, batfish, mobula rays
- Clown triggerfish, black triggerfish, green-snout parrotfish
- Dense fusilier schools
- Cuttlefish

## Entry / Exit

Boat dive from liveaboard or from Banda Neira-based operators. The site is within reach of day-trip boats from Banda Neira.

## Tips

- Shallow sections (3–10 m) are ideal for snorkellers and beginner divers — the lava flow topography rewards slow exploration
- For a mandarinfish dusk dive, head to Mandarin City in Banda Neira harbour rather than Lava Flow

## Photography

One of the Banda Sea's premier wide-angle reef photography sites — the black lava contrasts vividly with colourful coral formations.
