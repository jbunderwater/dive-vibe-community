## Mandarin City

Mandarin City is a shallow muck dive site in Banda Neira, located in front of Hotel Maulana near the old town harbour. It is specifically noted for a dense concentration of mandarinfish (*Synchiropus splendidus*) in 2–3 m of water, making it snorkeller-accessible as well as suitable for divers. Multiple sources describe it as one of the most reliable mandarinfish mating display sites in the Banda Islands.

## Overview

The site consists of shallow rubble, broken coral heads, and jetty rubble in front of the Banda Neira waterfront. Mandarinfish inhabit the small coral heads and rubble piles, emerging at dusk to perform their brief mating display. Divers settle over the rubble in just a few metres of water as the fish emerge in pairs.

By day, the site also rewards slow exploration: scorpionfish and crocodilefish rest on the rubble, pipefish hide among the structure, and juvenile batfish inhabit the old stone surrounds near the adjacent pier. The shallow depth and shore-entry access make this the physically easiest dive in the Banda Islands. It is nonetheless rated Advanced here in line with the destination's remote, liveaboard-only access — most operators recommend Advanced Open Water certification as a baseline for the itinerary as a whole, even for individually calm sites like this one.

## Dive Profile

Shore entry to a maximum of 12 m, with most productive mandarinfish habitat in 2–8 m. No significant current. Ideal for dusk dives (30–60 minutes before sunset) and night dives.

## Marine Life

- Mandarinfish (dense colony; best at dusk during mating display)
- Scorpionfish and crocodilefish on rubble
- Pipefish and juvenile batfish
- Moray eels in stone rubble

## Entry / Exit

Shore entry from the Banda Neira waterfront in front of Hotel Maulana. One of the few accessible shore dives in the Banda Islands.

## Tips

- Arrive 30–45 minutes before sunset and settle into position over the rubble before the mandarinfish emerge
- Use a small red-filtered torch for the dusk approach — bright white lights cause the fish to retreat
- Avoid touching or stirring up the rubble substrate, which disturbs resting fish

## Photography

A dedicated macro site. Use a macro lens or wet diopter for mandarinfish portraits. The dusk light creates a challenging low-light environment — set your camera for low-light macro before the dive.
