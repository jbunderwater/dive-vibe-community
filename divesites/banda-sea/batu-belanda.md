## Batu Belanda

Batu Belanda ("Dutch Rock") is a wall dive on the western side of Banda Besar, named after the Dutch landing in the Banda Islands in 1599. The wall is distinguished by a large cave and an unusual mix of low-light-adapted marine life, and is one of the few Banda Islands sites where the rare Lembeh sea dragon has been recorded.

## Overview

The western Banda Besar wall receives limited direct sunlight. Large barrel sponges, cup corals, and sea fans cover the wall face. A cave, reported at around 13 m depth by two independent sources, features a ceiling shaft that channels light onto soldierfish and moontail bullseyes sheltering inside. Orangutan crabs and black-spotted moray eels are also reported in and around the cave.

The Lembeh sea dragon — a pipehorse with the elongated body of a pipefish and head of a seahorse — has been recorded here by two independent sources, making this one of the few confirmed Banda sites for the species. Scorpionfish (species not specified by sources), red-fire gobies, and nudibranchs are also reported on this wall. Napoleon wrasse and bullethead parrotfish inhabit the wall and cave surrounds.

## Dive Profile

Wall diving to around 30 m, with a cave feature at approximately 13 m. Currents are typically mild on this sheltered western face. Visibility averages 20–30 m. Rated Advanced in line with the destination's remote, liveaboard-only character.

## Marine Life

- Soldierfish and moontail bullseyes (inside the cave)
- Lembeh sea dragon / pipehorse (rare; recorded at this site by two independent sources)
- Scorpionfish (species not specified by sources)
- Orangutan crabs, black-spotted moray eels
- Red-fire gobies and nudibranchs
- Napoleon wrasse, bullethead parrotfish
- Large barrel sponges, cup corals, and sea fans on the wall face

## Entry / Exit

Boat dive from liveaboard or Banda Neira day-trip vessel. The site is on the western coast of Banda Besar, a short transit from Banda Neira harbour.

## Tips

- The cave at approximately 13 m is the highlight — bring a torch for the interior to illuminate the soldierfish and any nudibranchs
- Low-light conditions suit photographers interested in sponge and cup coral macro work
- The Lembeh sea dragon is cryptic; alert your guide to watch for it on black crinoids

## Photography

Well-suited to natural-light and strobe-lit sponge photography. The cave shaft creates atmospheric light-ray images when conditions are right. A macro setup is rewarding for pipehorse and scorpionfish.
