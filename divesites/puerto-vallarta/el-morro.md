# El Morro

Series of rock pinnacles 6 miles west of Las Marietas outside Banderas Bay — a 15-meter submarine tunnel entered at 27-30m, gorgonians, giant oceanic mantas, nurse and whitetip sharks, and whale sharks in early summer; minimum 25 dives required

## Overview

El Morro is a series of rock pinnacles located approximately 6 miles west of Las Marietas Islands, outside the mouth of Banderas Bay in exposed Pacific waters. The 90-minute boat crossing from Puerto Vallarta delivers divers to a site with a completely different character from the sheltered bay: cleaner water, stronger current, and the pelagic marine life that comes with genuine open-ocean exposure.

The defining feature of El Morro is a submarine tunnel that passes completely through the main pinnacle formation, entered at approximately 27-30 meters (90-100 feet) and exiting at approximately 15-18 meters (50-60 feet), roughly 40 meters long. Adjacent to the tunnel entrance, a large cavern extends at 21-27 meters — a substantial overhead environment requiring a torch and controlled buoyancy. Outside the cavern and tunnel, the rock walls carry significant growth of gorgonians and sea fans, and the open water around the pinnacles sees regular visits from giant oceanic mantas and seasonal whale sharks. Humpback whales pass through Banderas Bay generally during winter, but this is a regional seasonal pattern rather than a claim confirmed specifically at El Morro — divers should not expect guaranteed in-water encounters. A minimum of 25 logged dives is required by most operators.

## Site Information

- **Location**: 6 miles west of Las Marietas, outside Banderas Bay; ~90 min from Puerto Vallarta
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 46 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 12-36 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 19-28°C (66-82°F)
- **Current**: Moderate to strong
- **Best Time**: May to November

## Marine Life

Giant oceanic manta rays are El Morro's signature encounter, drawn by the current-rich water sweeping the exposed pinnacles — one source describes individuals exceeding 12-foot wingspans. Whale sharks appear in early summer (May-June), attracted by warm-water plankton concentrations in the open Pacific. Nurse sharks and whitetip sharks are reported at the site, along with sea lions and turtles (loggerheads specifically, per one source). Gorgonians and large sea fans cover the exposed wall faces. The tunnel and cavern walls host nudibranchs, octopus, and cleaning shrimp. Dolphins and sailfish are reported at El Morro by one source (Just Gotta Dive); three other sources checked for this site (Banderas Scuba Republic, Divinglore, Sayulita Entourage) do not mention them, so treat these two as possible but less-certain than the mantas, whale sharks, and nurse/whitetip sharks that multiple sources agree on. Marlin and eagle rays, previously listed here, are not attributed to El Morro by any of the four sources re-checked and have been removed as unconfirmed. Humpback whales migrate through Banderas Bay in winter, but this is a regional seasonal pattern rather than a site-specific claim — divers should not expect a guaranteed in-water encounter at El Morro itself.

## Dive Profile

Dives at El Morro begin at the cavern at 21-27 meters before navigating the 15-meter submarine tunnel — entering at 27-30 meters and exiting at 18 meters on the other side. A torch is required throughout the tunnel. After the tunnel, divers work the outer walls and watch the blue water for pelagics before ascending. Current around the exposed pinnacle faces requires active management and good trim. The depth range and overhead environment demand Advanced certification; the 25-dive minimum reflects genuine skill requirements rather than a formality.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access, approximately 90 minutes from Puerto Vallarta marina. Exposed open-ocean conditions — surface chop and swell may be significantly rougher than inside the bay. Giant stride entry; ascend with SMB deployed from depth. Two-tank trips are standard given the transit time. Minimum 25 logged dives required by most operators.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Carry a primary and backup dive torch — the tunnel is completely dark and both lights are essential for safe passage
- Whale shark encounters are most likely in early morning in May and June, before boat traffic increases at the site
- Humpback whales are present in Banderas Bay in winter, but treat an in-water encounter at El Morro itself as a bonus, not an expectation — no site-specific source confirms regular dive-time sightings here
- Manta rays appear most reliably when current is running; flat, windless days with no current often mean fewer pelagic sightings
- The 25-dive minimum is a genuine experience requirement — the tunnel, cavern, open-ocean current, and depth demand solid skills from every diver in the water
