## Peltastis

The Peltastis is a Greek cargo vessel originally built at the Kremer Sohn shipyard in northern Germany, launched in late 1952 and delivered in early 1953 under the name *Alsterpark* (874 tonnes), owned by P/R Alsterpark of Hamburg. A Piraeus-based Greek company bought her in 1967 and renamed her *Peltastis*. These build details are independently corroborated by two sources (EL-PI Tours and Total Croatia News), satisfying this project's two-source bar for wreck history.

Sources largely agree the vessel sank during the night of 7–8 January 1968 (03:50am per Total Croatia News) in a severe Bora storm — reported wind speeds vary between roughly 150 km/h (Krk Croatia) and 200 km/h (Total Croatia News, EL-PI Tours) — striking the cliffs between Šilo and Klimno bay on Krk island after her anchor and engine failed to hold position. Note: Dive Center Krk and Dive Loft Krk both list the sinking date as January 4, 1968, which conflicts with the four other sources consulted (EL-PI Tours, Scuba Diving Croatia, Krk Croatia, Total Croatia News) that converge on 7–8 January; the weight of evidence favours 7–8 January. Eight people died in the sinking — most sources describe this as seven crew members plus Captain Theodoros Belesis — and four crew survived with serious injuries. She was carrying a cargo of timber (roughly 500m³ per Total Croatia News) bound for Rijeka. She is locally known as "the Greek" (*Grk*).

Sources disagree on the vessel's length: EL-PI Tours and Total Croatia News both independently give "just over 60 metres" (consistent with the 60.2m figure in wreck/OSM records), while Dive Center Krk and Dive Loft Krk both list her as 70 metres. Given two independent sources on each side of this discrepancy, both figures are presented here rather than picking one. The wreck lies roughly halfway between Šilo and Klimno, about 0.5 nautical miles offshore.

## Overview

The Peltastis rests upright, though reported depths for individual features vary by source: the top of the foremast is placed at roughly 6–8 metres, the deck between about 15–28 metres, and the seabed/hold at roughly 30–33 metres depending on source; EL-PI Tours alone cites the debris extending to 40 metres at its deepest point. The hull is substantially intact and the cargo hold, gangway, and captain's cabin are all accessible to explore. The wreck is extensively colonised by sponges and soft corals. Kvarner visibility is typically 10–20 metres in summer. Advanced Open Water certification or equivalent is required.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Between Šilo and Klimno bay, off Krk Island, Kvarner, Croatia (approx. 0.5nm from Šilo)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 32 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 meters (5–10m in winter)
- **Current**: Mild to moderate with occasional unpredictable surges
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Marine Life

Sources from local dive operators and dive guides confirm the presence of: large schools of fish, scorpionfish throughout the hull, a resident conger eel reported at approximately 2 metres length, grouper, moray eels, octopus, damselfish, and sponges/soft corals on the structure. Dive Center Krk and Dive Loft Krk additionally report rockfish and crayfish. Pelagic species (including seasonal barracuda and amberjack, per EL-PI Tours) visit during summer months.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the foremast (reported at roughly 6–8m depending on source), then work down to the deck at 15–28m. The cargo hold, gangway, and captain's cabin can be explored. Descend to the seabed at 30–33m for the deepest sections. Ascend via shot line and complete a 3-minute safety stop at 5 metres. The relatively manageable depth allows solid bottom time, but gas management remains important.

## Entry and Exit

Dive boats from Šilo, Krk, or Punat operators moor directly to the wreck buoy. Entry by giant stride or back roll. The Kvarner can be rough in Bura conditions — confirm sea state before departure.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A torch is essential for illuminating the hold, cabin, and encrusted surfaces
- The captain's cabin is an atmospheric penetration for trained wreck divers
- Nitrox extends comfortable bottom time at 25–30m
- The shallow foremast (roughly 6–8m) is excellent for a decompression stop and a final look at the upper structure
- This is regarded as one of the best intermediate-to-advanced wreck dives in Croatia

## Safety Considerations

The 30+ metre maximum depth and the loss of eight lives in the original sinking make this a site to treat with respect. Advanced Open Water certification or equivalent required. Never penetrate enclosed spaces without wreck training, a reel, and redundant lighting. Carry an SMB and dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The intact hull rising from the seabed to the shallow foremast provides outstanding wide-angle compositions. The encrusted superstructure, resident scorpionfish, and large conger eel are compelling macro and portrait subjects. The shallow foremast allows photography in excellent natural light.
