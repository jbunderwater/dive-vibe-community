## Banjol

Banjol (also called "Banjole" or "Banjol Island Caves") is an islet with three caves off the coast near Rovinj/Vrsar in Istria — **not** the town of Banjol on Rab Island in the Kvarner, despite the name overlap. This site's coordinates (45.07406, 13.61062) match the official PADI dive-site listing for "Banjol" (45.07499, 13.61094) to within about 100 metres, and are consistent with Istria's Rovinj Riviera, not Rab. Four independent sources (PADI, the Istria Tourist Board, Starfish Diving Center, and Rovinj Diving) confirm this location and its cave system.

## Overview

Banjol is one of the best-known cave/cavern sites on Istria's western coast, reached by a roughly 30-minute boat trip from Rovinj or Vrsar. The islet contains three distinct caves: an upper cave entrance open to the sky, a second cave/grotto at around 12 metres, and a third cave with a collapsed ceiling at around 5 metres that can be swum through. According to the Istria Tourist Board, the reef itself reaches 35 metres, with the shallowest cave entrance at 5 metres. The cave and grotto walls are described as exceptionally colourful, covered in sponges.

## Marine Life

Multiple independent sources (PADI, Starfish Diving, Rovinj Diving) consistently report scorpionfish, and occasional sightings of conger eels and lobsters, in the caves and along the walls. Rovinj Diving additionally reports large spider crabs and octopus. PADI and Starfish Diving both specifically report John Dory at 26–30 metres along a small wall. PADI also notes a submerged statue of St. Euphemia (patron saint of Rovinj) at 16 metres near the descent line — a notable, source-confirmed feature, though this is a placed artefact rather than natural marine life.

## Dive Profile

Descend via the anchor/descent line, passing the statue of St. Euphemia at around 16 metres. Explore the first cave (entrance around 12 metres, with light effects from an opening to the sky), then the second cave/grotto, and finish at the shallower third cave (around 5 metres) with its collapsed ceiling — swimming through this section is reported as easy given its wide diameter and pebble floor. A short wall section at 26–30 metres is where John Dory have been reported, for divers with the training to go that deep. Only cave-trained divers should enter beyond the cavern-zone daylight limits. Ascend slowly and complete a safety stop at 5 metres.

## Entry and Exit

Dive boats from Rovinj or Vrsar-area operators (e.g., Starfish Diving Center, Rovinj Diving) access this site, roughly a 30-minute boat ride. Entry by giant stride or back roll.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A torch is essential for the cave interiors and to bring out the colour of the sponge-covered walls
- The three-cave structure suits a range of experience levels — the shallow, wide third cave is comfortable even for less experienced divers, while full cave penetration requires cave-diving certification
- Look for the statue of St. Euphemia near the descent line at 16 metres
- Divers comfortable at depth can look for John Dory on the small wall at 26–30 metres
- Recommended gear per PADI: boots, gloves, hood, dive computer, SMB, and torch

## Safety Considerations

This is a mixed cave/cavern/reef site with real overhead sections — only cave-trained divers should go beyond areas with visible natural light, and all divers should carry a torch and remain oriented to an exit. The deeper wall section (26–30 m) requires appropriate depth experience. Dive with a buddy and carry an SMB.

## Photography

The cave entrance with its sky opening and light beams is the site's signature composition, confirmed across multiple sources as one of the most scenic parts of the dive. The colourful sponge-covered cave and grotto walls reward macro work, and the statue of St. Euphemia at 16 metres offers an unusual subject.
