## Jesse Beazley Reef

Jesse Beazley Reef is the smallest and northernmost reef of Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site reachable only by liveaboard.

## Overview

Jesse Beazley Reef lies roughly 20 kilometers north of Tubbataha's main North and South Atolls, making it a compact, mushroom-shaped structure typically dived en route to or from Puerto Princesa as liveaboards transit the park during the March-to-June season. The reef starts as shallow as 7 meters and drops beyond recreational limits, and currents here are reported as unpredictable, able to change speed and direction within a single dive — surface conditions can also turn rough given the site's exposure to open sea. This combination of depth, current, and exposure makes it one of the more demanding sites in the park, generally reserved for the tail end of a Tubbataha itinerary.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Palawan, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 29 meters

## Marine Life

Sources consistently report grey reef and whitetip reef sharks patrolling the site, along with barracuda, Spanish mackerel, and Napoleon wrasse in the upper sections, and schools of unicornfish and surgeonfish sweeping across the reef top. Hammerhead sharks are reported as an occasional, less predictable sighting in the area rather than a guaranteed encounter.

## Dive Profile

Dives are typically split between the North End and South End of the reef. Given the unpredictable currents, dive plans are made on the day based on conditions, with divers briefed to expect changes in current strength or direction mid-dive.

## Entry and Exit

Liveaboard boat dive only — Tubbataha is not accessible from shore and has no permanent dive infrastructure. Entry and exit are via tender/skiff from the liveaboard vessel.

## Safety Considerations

Currents can be strong and unpredictable, and surface conditions can be rough given the site's open-water exposure. This site is not suitable for divers without genuine advanced experience and strong buoyancy and navigation skills; always dive with a guide familiar with current conditions of the day.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Best visited March-June when the Sulu Sea is calmest; April is often cited for the best visibility and conditions
- Carry a surface marker buoy and reef hook if provided, given the current conditions
- This is often the final stop on a liveaboard itinerary as vessels transit back toward Puerto Princesa
