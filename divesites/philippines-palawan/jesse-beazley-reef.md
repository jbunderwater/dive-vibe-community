## Jesse Beazley Reef

Jesse Beazley Reef is the northernmost and smallest of Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park's three reef structures — a remote, mushroom-shaped wall reachable only by liveaboard.

## Overview

Jesse Beazley Reef lies about 20 kilometers north of Tubbataha's North Atoll, within the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park (en.wikipedia.org). It is accessible only by liveaboard from Puerto Princesa, and only during the park's open season, roughly March to June. The reef is mushroom-shaped: it starts as shallow as 7 meters and slopes to a recreational shelf around 12-30 meters, with sources reporting the undercut wall continuing beyond recreational limits, past 40-50 meters (bluewaterdivetravel.com, dive-the-world.com). Currents here are known for changing speed and direction unpredictably, so this is a site for experienced, current-comfortable divers.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Palawan, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

Jesse Beazley Reef is best known for hammerhead shark sightings, particularly on its northwest side (bluewaterdivetravel.com, dive-the-world.com). Large barracuda, Spanish mackerel, tuna, and grouper are reported patrolling the reef, along with Napoleon wrasse, unicornfish, and surgeonfish over pristine stands of Porites and Acropora coral (asiadivesite.com, dive-the-world.com).

**Correction note (2026-08-21 re-audit):** grey reef sharks were previously listed for this site but no source found in this audit confirms them specifically at Jesse Beazley Reef (that claim is more consistent with Tubbataha's main atoll walls generally); it has been removed here rather than carried over as an assumption.

## Dive Profile

Dives typically work the reef's shallower shelf, checking the undercut plateau and drop-off edge while watching the blue water for passing hammerheads and other pelagics. Jesse Beazley is often dived at the end of a Tubbataha liveaboard itinerary, as the boat begins heading back toward Puerto Princesa.

## Entry and Exit

Liveaboard-only access; entry and exit are managed by the dive boat crew, typically via tender/skiff drop-off at the site.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Currents can shift quickly and unpredictably here — stay close to your guide and carry a surface marker buoy
- Watch the blue water off the northwest side for hammerhead activity
- This is one of the more remote stops on a Tubbataha itinerary — confirm with your operator whether it's included on a given trip, as it depends on conditions and time

## Safety Considerations

This is a remote, liveaboard-only site with fast, unpredictable currents and depths that extend well beyond recreational limits off the reef edge. Dive within your certification and experience level, monitor your position relative to the boat closely, and always carry a surface marker buoy and whistle/signaling device in case of separation from the group.
