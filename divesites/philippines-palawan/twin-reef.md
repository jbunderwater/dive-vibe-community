## Twin Reef

Twin Reef is Port Barton's signature dive and snorkel site — two coral formations a short boat ride from the village — though a 2025 survey found it has lost roughly half its coral cover to bleaching and anchor damage.

## Overview

Twin Reef consists of two distinct coral formations close to Port Barton, historically prized for the diversity and density of its hard coral growth. However, current sources paint a more sobering picture than older descriptions suggest: a 2025 reef-condition survey found the 2024 mass coral bleaching event (part of a global bleaching event confirmed by NOAA and documented across Palawan, including Port Barton, by Philippine press coverage) has significantly reduced live coral cover at Twin Reef, and a local reef monitor quoted in 2024 press reporting estimated the site has lost about half its original coral cover, attributing ongoing loss partly to boat-anchor damage from tourism. The best-surviving coral at the site is reported to be hispidose and branching Acropora and foliose Montipora, though healthy patches are now limited to a smaller area than in past years. The site is still snorkeled and dived regularly, but visitors should expect a mix of recovering and degraded coral rather than uniformly pristine reef.

## Entry and Exit

Reached by banca (outrigger boat) from Port Barton as part of an island-hopping or dedicated dive trip. Entry by giant stride or back roll; visibility around the site is commonly reported around 10 meters, reflecting its offshore location and current water-quality conditions.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Set expectations around coral condition — this is a recovering reef, not the pristine site older descriptions may suggest
- Avoid contact with the reef and be mindful of fin kicks near remaining healthy coral patches
- Ask your operator about current anchoring practices, since anchor damage is a documented contributor to coral loss here

## Safety Considerations

This remains a gentle, shallow site suitable for beginners; the main consideration is diver behavior around fragile and already-stressed coral rather than technical difficulty.
