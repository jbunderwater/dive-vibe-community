## Twin Reef

Twin Reef is Port Barton's most popular snorkel and shallow-dive stop — a small, offshore coral patch reef a short boat ride from the village.

## Overview

Twin Reef is a compact reef, roughly 100 meters across, made up of two adjoining coral patches (rafsreefbrief.com). The reef flat sits in just 2-3 meters of water and can be exposed at low tide, with drop-offs beginning around 5 meters — this is a shallow site, not a deep dive. A 2024-2025 coral condition survey found healthy patches of hispidose and branching Acropora and foliose Montipora growing right up to the waterline, but also significant dead coral between the healthy patches, consistent with a 2024 regional bleaching event; anchor damage from mooring boats was also flagged as an ongoing concern (rafsreefbrief.com).

**Correction note (2026-08-21 re-audit):** maximum depth has been corrected from 20m to 8m. The site is a shallow reef-flat/snorkel site, not a 20-meter dive, per the 2024-2025 survey and multiple tour-operator descriptions of it as a snorkeling destination.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Port Barton, northern Palawan, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 8 meters

## Marine Life

Twin Reef supports hispidose and branching Acropora, foliose Montipora, Heliopora, and coral recruits in its healthier patches (rafsreefbrief.com). Turtles are commonly sighted here (tikigo.com, rafsreefbrief.com). No source reviewed for this update confirmed additional species (e.g. sharks or rays) at this specific site, so a wider species list is not claimed here.

## Dive Profile

A shallow, easy dive/snorkel across the reef flat and short drop-off. Given the shallow depth, this suits a relaxed single dive or a snorkel stop combined with other Port Barton sites in the same day.

## Entry and Exit

Port Barton banca (outrigger boat) day trips; entry by giant stride or roll-in from the boat, with a short swim to the reef.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Watch your buoyancy and fin position over the shallow reef flat — it can be exposed at low tide and is sensitive to contact
- Do not anchor or moor directly on the reef; anchor damage has been noted as an ongoing issue here
- Good option to pair with a deeper site earlier in the day, given the shallow depth here

## Safety Considerations

Depths are shallow throughout, but boat traffic around this popular mooring spot means divers and snorkelers should stay visible and close to their group.
