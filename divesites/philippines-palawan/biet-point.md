## Biet Point

Biet Point, also known as South Miniloc, is El Nido's best-known dive site — a current-swept channel dive between Miniloc and Simizu islands.

## Overview

Biet Point sits in the channel between Miniloc and Simizu islands, roughly 45 minutes by boat from El Nido town. The site centers on a triangular, current-fed cabbage/lettuce coral garden at around 12 meters, with sources reporting an overall depth range across the site of roughly 5-30 meters. Moderate to strong current is typical, which is what feeds the site's dense coral growth and fish activity — this is best treated as a drift dive rather than a static reef dive. Conditions are generally best from November to May.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Bacuit Bay, El Nido, Palawan, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Marine Life

Multiple sources independently confirm the cabbage coral garden supports large schools of snapper (reported variously as big-eye or yellow snapper), plus tuna, mackerel, jacks, and barracuda holding in the current. Reef residents reported at this specific site include groupers, moray eels, fusiliers, ribbon eels, frogfish, ghost pipefish, cuttlefish, octopus, nudibranchs (including Spanish dancers), angelfish, unicornfish, parrotfish, and pufferfish. Sea turtles are commonly seen. One source lists sharks among the site's regular sightings without specifying species; blue-spotted stingrays are also reported, with eagle, manta, and devil rays noted as possible but less frequent encounters.

## Dive Profile

Divers typically drop onto the coral garden around 10-12 meters and let the channel current carry them along the triangular reef structure, working the current-facing edges where fish activity concentrates. The site's depth range extends both shallower and deeper than the central garden depending on where you enter relative to the channel.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive; entry by giant stride or back roll into the channel. Current strength varies with tide, so time and direction of entry are typically planned by the dive guide around the tidal window.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Current can be moderate to strong — this site rewards divers comfortable with drift conditions
- Best visibility and calmest conditions are typically November-May
- Watch the current-facing edge of the coral garden for the densest fish activity

## Safety Considerations

Current can build quickly in the channel between Miniloc and Simizu. Stay close to your guide, carry a surface marker buoy, and be prepared for a drift-style dive profile rather than a fixed route.
