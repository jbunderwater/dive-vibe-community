## Biet Point

Biet Point — better known as South Miniloc — is one of El Nido's most consistently recommended dive sites, sitting in the current-fed channel between Miniloc and Simizu islands.

## Overview

South Miniloc lies at the southeast tip of Miniloc Island, adjacent to El Nido's Small and Big Lagoons. The site centers on an expanse of cabbage (lettuce/paperscroll) coral around 12m, with the wider reef spanning roughly 5-35m depending on which operator's profile you follow. Moderate to strong currents run through the channel between the islands, feeding the reef and drawing in open-water species. A widely repeated claim that the site was "discovered by Jacques Cousteau in the 1970s" could not be confirmed by any dive-operator or reference source checked for this update, so it has been dropped rather than repeated.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Philippines Palawan, Asia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 21 meters

## Marine Life

Large schools of yellow (big-eye) snappers and tuna are consistently reported hovering over the cabbage-coral garden, along with jacks, barracuda, squid, and cuttlefish in the current-swept water beyond the reef. Turtles (both green and hawksbill) and blue-spotted stingrays are regularly seen, and multiple operators mention moray eels, frogfish, and nudibranchs among the coral. One source also notes puffer fish, garden ribbon eels, and octopuses at the site. Reports of eagle rays and mantas exist but are described as occasional rather than routine.
