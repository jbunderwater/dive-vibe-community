## Akitsushima

Akitsushima is one of the largest and most-dived WWII wrecks in Coron Bay — a former Imperial Japanese Navy seaplane tender sunk in a single US carrier air raid on 24 September 1944.

## Overview

Akitsushima was a seaplane tender built by Kawasaki at Kobe and commissioned in April 1942. Wikipedia records her length as 114.8m overall; several dive-industry sources round this to 118m, and both figures are given here since they could not be reconciled. Displacement is consistently reported at 4,650 long tons standard.

She was sunk at Coron Bay on 24 September 1944 during the same US Task Force 38 strike that destroyed the rest of the Japanese supply fleet sheltering there. Accounts of exactly how she went down conflict: the wartime US Navy action record (combinedfleet.com) attributes the loss primarily to two direct bomb hits amidships followed by a further bomb hit near the stern catapult track, with a note that a possible additional hit further aft — bomb or torpedo, undetermined — may have occurred. Several dive-operator sources instead describe a torpedo strike igniting the aviation-fuel tanks as the cause of the hull rupture. Both bomb and torpedo damage are plausible given the scale of the attack; this description does not pick one version over the other.

The wreck today rests on its port side, with the upper hull around 20-22m and the deepest sections between roughly 34-38m (most commonly cited as 36m). The hull is split open behind the midsection, separating the crane and funnel area. Divers can see the seaplane-handling crane resting on the sand, anti-aircraft gun mounts, and — for those with wreck training — penetration routes into the engine room, where four diesel engines are still in place. Unexploded ordnance has been reported on the wreck, which is a further reason penetration should only be attempted with proper training and a qualified guide; it is never a "safe" or casual activity.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Coron Bay, Philippines Palawan, Asia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 36 meters

## Marine Life

Multiple dive-operator accounts describe barracuda, tuna, batfish, grouper, and snapper patrolling the wreck, though several note that overall fish density is modest compared to other Coron wrecks. No other species are specifically confirmed for this site in the sources consulted.
