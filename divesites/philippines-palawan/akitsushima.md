## Akitsushima

Akitsushima is the most historically significant wreck in Coron Bay, a Japanese Imperial Navy seaplane tender lost in a WWII air raid.

## Overview

Akitsushima was a Kawasaki-built seaplane tender of the Imperial Japanese Navy, laid down 29 October 1940, launched 25 July 1941, and completed 29 April 1942 — dates confirmed by both Wikipedia and naval-encyclopedia.com. She was sunk on 24 September 1944 by U.S. Navy Task Force 38 carrier aircraft while at anchor in Coron Bay; a dive-operator account (divers-guide.com) describes the fatal blow as a torpedo strike to her fuel tanks. Sources disagree slightly on her dimensions: Wikipedia and naval-encyclopedia.com give a length of 114.8 meters, while dive-operator sources (scubatechphilippines.com) cite 118 meters. Standard displacement is consistently reported around 4,650 long tons.

The wreck now lies on its port side. Sources also disagree on the deepest point — scubatechphilippines.com and the site's original survey data put it at 36 meters, while divers-guide.com reports the wreck bottoming out at 38 meters — with upper surfaces consistently reported around 22 meters. The ship's crane, originally used to hoist seaplanes aboard, is the wreck's most recognizable feature and a magnet for reef fish. Divers report entering through the hull toward the engine room and other interior spaces still fitted with machinery; as with any wreck penetration, this requires proper wreck-diving training and equipment and carries inherent risk regardless of experience level.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Coron Bay, Philippines Palawan, Asia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 36 meters

## Marine Life

Dive-operator reports describe fish life including barracuda, jacks, groupers, lionfish, mackerel, and scorpionfish around the structure. No other species have been independently confirmed for this specific wreck.
