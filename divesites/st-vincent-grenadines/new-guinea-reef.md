## New Guinea Reef

New Guinea Reef, at Questelles on St. Vincent's leeward coast, is a shore-entry multi-level dive known for exceptional black coral diversity and a terraced slope that plunges from a shallow inner bay to advanced-only depths at the point.

## Overview

New Guinea Reef (also known as Questelles Reef) is reached via a shore entry off the Leeward Highway near the Questelles police station — a short walk from parking to the water. The site is a terraced slope: the inner bay starts around 9 meters (30 feet) and is calm roughly 95% of the time, making it accessible for a multi-level dive, but the slope continues down to about 46 meters (150 feet) near the point, where currents are typically strong and the dive is rated for advanced divers only. Columnar cliffside overhangs are encrusted in sponges and draped in black coral. Correction: this page previously listed a flat 14-meter depth and Beginner difficulty and described this as a boat dive — fresh research from a dedicated shore-diving guide and independent site database both confirm shore entry and the much greater depth range, and this page has been corrected accordingly.

## Site Information

- **Location**: St. Vincent and the Grenadines, Caribbean (Questelles, leeward coast)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 46 meters

## Marine Life

New Guinea Reef is noted for an unusually rich variety of black coral species and colors draping the cliffside overhangs, along with sponge growth on the columnar underhangs. Sources describe "healthy coral gardens" and frequent turtle and large-school fish sightings, though no source itemizes specific fish species for this site beyond that general description — so no specific species list is stated here beyond black coral and sponges, which are directly confirmed.
