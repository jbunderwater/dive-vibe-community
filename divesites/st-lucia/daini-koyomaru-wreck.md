## Daini Koyomaru Wreck

The Daini Koyomaru is an Advanced wreck dive off St. Lucia's south/southwest coast near Anse Cochon — a 244-foot Japanese dredger scuttled in September 1996 as an artificial reef after completing dredging work at the port of Vieux Fort.

## Overview

The Daini Koyomaru was purchased by the St. Lucia government in the early 1990s to maintain the dredged access channel at Vieux Fort's banana port. When that work was finished, the Department of Fisheries scuttled the vessel in September 1996 to create an artificial reef, sinking it at the south end of Anse Cochon. **Length is disputed between sources**: divefairhelen.com and scubaweather.com both give 244 feet (approximately 74-75 metres), while a fresh re-fetch of divingaway.com today gives 350+ feet instead. 244 feet is treated as the better-corroborated figure since it recurs across more sources, but the conflict is flagged rather than silently resolved. **Tonnage is not included here**: only one source consulted (divefairhelen.com, citing "16,000 tons") gives a specific figure, and no second independent source could confirm it, so per this database's two-source rule for wreck dimensions the tonnage claim has been omitted rather than stated as fact.

**Sources also disagree on the wreck's final orientation and depth.** divefairhelen.com states the vessel "landed on its side" during the 1996 sinking, at a maximum depth of approximately 33 metres (108 feet) — matching scubaweather.com's figure. A fresh re-fetch of divingaway.com today instead describes a "trapped air pocket" that caused the vessel to flip and turn upside down as it sank, coming to rest at a maximum depth of around 100 feet (about 30 metres). Rather than pick one account, both are presented here: "on its side" (divefairhelen.com, scubaweather.com) versus "upside down" (divingaway.com). An earlier version of this page attributed the sideways orientation to Hurricane Lenny toppling an originally upright wreck in 1999; that claim could not be found in any source consulted on this or the previous pass and has been dropped in favour of the two better-documented, source-backed accounts above.

Much of the interior was deliberately left intact, and the site functions as both a wreck dive and, given its unusual final orientation, effectively a wall dive — a combination that makes it a genuinely Advanced dive.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South end of Anse Cochon, St. Lucia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters

## Marine Life

divefairhelen.com specifically reports eels and barracuda around the wreck, noting that some of the barracuda are believed to have accompanied the vessel during its tow from Vieux Fort to its resting site. A fresh re-fetch of divingaway.com today additionally reports "large jack schools" and "several resident sea turtles" — a single-source claim not corroborated by any other source consulted, so it should be treated as tentative rather than fully confirmed. Pufferfish and French angelfish, listed in an earlier version of this page, still could not be reconfirmed by any source consulted this pass and remain removed.
