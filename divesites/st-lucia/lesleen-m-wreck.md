## Lesleen M Wreck

The Lesleen M is one of St. Lucia's most-dived wrecks — a 165-foot freighter deliberately scuttled by St. Lucia's Department of Fisheries in 1986 near Anse Cochon.

## Overview

Multiple independent sources agree on the core facts: the Lesleen M is a 165-foot freighter (scubastlucia.com, islanderkeys.com, PADI, and divingaway.com all agree on 165 feet), sunk by St. Lucia's Department of Fisheries as a deliberate artificial reef. Scuba St. Lucia's own live dive-site listing, re-fetched today, states directly that the wreck was "sunk in October 1986." **A conflict was found today**: four sources (scubastlucia.com, islanderkeys.com, caribbean-diving.com, PADI) agree on 1986, while a fifth, divingaway.com, states 1985. 1986 is treated as the well-corroborated figure; the single 1985 outlier is noted here rather than silently dropped. The wreck rests upright and intact on its keel (all sources agree).

**Depth is reported with some variation across sources**: caribbean-diving.com gives approximately 22m (60ft) with an alternate range of 20-30m; islanderkeys.com gives 60ft; Scuba St. Lucia gives 65ft maximum with the deck at 30ft (about 9m); PADI gives "40 to 65 feet (12 to 20 meters)"; divingaway.com gives a maximum of 60ft with the pilothouse accessible at 40ft. Taken together, sources put the wreck's depth range at roughly 18-22 metres (60-72 feet), with the accessible deck/pilothouse around 9-12 metres. The rudder and propeller remain intact, and the cargo hold is open and accessible, making this one of the more beginner-friendly wreck dives in the area.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Anse Cochon, St. Lucia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive

## Marine Life

Moray eels are confirmed across three independent sources (scubastlucia.com, islanderkeys.com, caribbean-diving.com). Lobsters are confirmed by three sources (scubastlucia.com, islanderkeys.com, divingaway.com). islanderkeys.com reports turtles, spider crabs, shrimp, angelfish, red snapper, barracuda, and tuna/pelagics passing in the blue; divingaway.com independently adds turtles again plus octopus, nudibranchs, and coral-banded shrimp; caribbean-diving.com adds scorpionfish; Scuba St. Lucia reports frogfish and general fish schools around deep sea fans, sponges, and hydroids that colonise the hull; PADI separately confirms French angelfish. stolentime.com independently describes the wreck as "ideal habitat for many juvenile fishes such as Nassau grouper, Queen and French Angel fishes" — a third source corroborating a juvenile-fish nursery role. A previously reported naming-origin story for the vessel (a "Pappy Mac" and McQuilkin family history) appeared in only one source found in an earlier pass and could not be corroborated by any source consulted since, so it remains omitted per this database's anti-hallucination policy on single-sourced historical narratives.
