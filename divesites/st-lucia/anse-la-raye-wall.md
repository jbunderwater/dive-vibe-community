## Anse La Raye Wall

Anse La Raye Wall — named for the "Bay of Rays" — is a mix of steep sloping reef and vertical wall on St. Lucia's northwest coast, with a main wall that PADI reports starting at 18 metres and descending to 34 metres.

## Overview

The dive begins over a shallow wall covered in brightly coloured fire coral before dropping onto huge boulders and then a densely coral-covered slope and vertical wall face. PADI's own site guide puts the main wall between 18 metres (60 feet) and 34 metres (110 feet), lined with gorgonian fans, sea whips, and huge barrel sponges — a combination reconfirmed independently by a fresh search of divein.com's St. Lucia dive guide, which cites the same depth range and features. PADI recommends Advanced Open Water certification and drift-diving experience for this site, noting it as excellent for wide-angle underwater photography.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Anse La Raye, St. Lucia, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

Stingrays and eagle rays "abound" at this site per PADI's dive guide — the most consistently corroborated sighting here. A newly consulted source, stolentime.com, names "Anse La Raye" directly and adds jacks, Bermuda chub, and spotted drums, and describes it as "probably the best place for spotting turtles" in the area (the hedge is the source's own wording, preserved here rather than stated as fact). These species were removed in a prior pass as unconfirmed, but this fresh, directly-named source restores them.

**A naming ambiguity was found on this pass**: Scuba St. Lucia's live dive-site page also separately lists a "Piton Wall" site at the base of Petit Piton, described with "sea whips, gorgonia, delicate soft corals and lots of large feather duster worms" — wording almost identical to what a previous version of this page attributed to Anse La Raye Wall. stolentime.com repeats that same feather-duster-worm description under its own separate "Piton Wall" entry, not under Anse La Raye. This suggests some cross-listing confusion between these two adjacent wall dives across sources. The feather-duster-worm detail has therefore been removed from this page rather than asserted with uncertain attribution.
