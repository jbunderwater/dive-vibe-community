## Mary Island

Mary Island (also known as Mborokua) is an uninhabited, jungle-covered volcanic island in Western Province, roughly halfway between the Russell Islands and Marovo Lagoon.

**Correction (2026-09-18): This site was previously placed inside Marovo Lagoon at coordinates roughly 150 km from its actual position. Wikipedia gives Mborokua's location as 9°01′S 158°44′E — corrected here.**

## Overview

Mborokua/Mary Island is a small (about 4 km²) uninhabited volcanic island sitting between the Russell Islands and the Marovo Lagoon barrier reef system, rather than inside the lagoon itself. Bilikiki Cruises, which runs trips to the site, describes a wall with a large amount of hard coral and abundant fish life. Large sea fans are a feature of the wall face.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Mborokua Island (Mary Island), Western Province, Solomon Islands — between the Russell Islands and Marovo Lagoon
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20–40 meters
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

**Updated 2026-09-18 (re-audit):** Bilikiki Cruises, the operator source for this site, describes an uninhabited island with a large amount of hard coral and fish life, including large schools of fish, sharks, sea turtles, and bumphead parrotfish. The wall itself is noted for large sea fans. More granular species claims from the previous version of this page (staghorn/Porites corals, surgeonfish, grouper, wire corals, crinoids, hawkfish, whitetip/grey reef shark identification, eagle rays, trevally, Napoleon wrasse, moray eels, nudibranchs) were not confirmed by this or any other source checked and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

Begin on the reef top in 5–8 meters to orientate and observe the coral garden before descending along the wall. Work your way along the wall face, staying in the 15–25 meter range for the best combination of sea fan and coral coverage while conserving bottom time. Move shallower in the second half of the dive and spend the last portion of bottom time on the reef top before a safety stop in the shallows. Current along the wall is usually manageable but can strengthen with tidal changes.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by boat from liveaboards operating in the area (Mborokua is a common stop between the Russell Islands and Marovo Lagoon on multi-day itineraries). Entry via giant stride or back roll; follow the reef line to locate the wall edge. Deploy an SMB for ascent. Coordinate with the boat for pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Often combined with Russell Islands or Marovo Lagoon dives on the same liveaboard itinerary
- Look carefully in crevices and under overhangs for sleeping reef sharks and resting turtles
- A wide-angle lens with two strobes brings out the best in the sea fan and wall scenes
- Buoyancy control is critical on wall dives — avoid involuntary depth gain on the drop-off
- Morning dives typically offer the best light on the reef top

## Safety Considerations

Currents here are generally moderate, though tidal state can affect conditions along exposed wall sections. Depth management is important — wall dives can lead to unintentional deep descents if divers focus too much on the wall and too little on their depth gauge. Stay shallower than 30 meters unless specifically planning a deep dive. Always carry an SMB. Dive with a guide familiar with local tidal patterns.

## Photography

Mary Island's wall is a wide-angle photographer's subject. The combination of large sea fans, colourful soft corals, and the deep blue beyond the wall face creates compelling compositions with significant depth and colour. Shooting upward from the base of the sea fans toward the surface light produces dramatic silhouettes. On the reef top, natural light and macro work on coral details reward a second lens setup.
