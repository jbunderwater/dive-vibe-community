## Sandfly Passage

Sandfly Passage is a current-swept channel in the Florida Islands area of the Solomon Islands that delivers large pelagic encounters, including manta rays, in a high-energy environment.

**Note: Coordinates in this record were corrected from the original OSM import value (-8.0833, 156.85), which placed the site approximately 250 km away near Gizo in the Western Province. Sandfly Passage is located in the Florida Islands, north of Guadalcanal, near Tulagi. The corrected coordinates (-9.03, 160.08) reflect this location.**

## Overview

Sandfly Passage connects water between islands in the Florida Islands group north of Guadalcanal, generating strong tidal currents that bring nutrient-rich water and attract pelagic predators. The site is typically visited by Florida Islands-based operators and liveaboards working the area around Tulagi. The reef structure along the passage walls provides a backdrop for the pelagic action, with hard coral gardens on the shallower sections giving way to sea fans and sponge-encrusted walls as depth increases. The advanced rating reflects both current strength and the open-water nature of the pelagic encounters.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Florida Islands, Solomon Islands (north of Guadalcanal, near Tulagi)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20–40 meters
- **Current**: Strong (tidal-dependent)
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

**Updated 2026-09-18**: Independent travel-press coverage of this Nggela Islands current system (which some sources call "Sandfly Passage" and others "Mbike Passage") reports manta rays as a headline species alongside sweetlips, jacks, and bumphead parrotfish, with huge schools of fusiliers and anthias. Grey reef sharks are also commonly reported patrolling the current-swept zone. Because different operators and travel articles use "Devil's Highway," "Sandfly Passage," and "Mbike Passage" somewhat interchangeably for manta encounters in this general area, treat the exact site-to-species mapping with some caution and confirm with your local operator which channel they are running. Beyond mantas, sharks, and the schooling fish above, other species previously listed here (whitetip reef sharks, barracuda, bigeye trevally, dog-tooth tuna, eagle rays, parrotfish, Napoleon wrasse, grouper, moray eels) are plausible for this reef system generally but are not confirmed for this specific passage in the sources consulted, and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The dive strategy depends on tidal state and current direction. Strong incoming or outgoing flow pushes divers along the passage wall, and a good guide positions the entry to drift divers past the aggregation points where mantas and sharks are most concentrated. In strong current, divers may deploy a reef hook to hold position in a prime viewing spot without having to fin against the flow. Depths of 15–25 meters cover the most productive zones. The maximum depth of 30 meters is available for divers with the gas management to use it, but the majority of life is encountered at shallower depths. Dives run 45–60 minutes depending on current speed.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Gizo-based operators or liveaboards. Entry is timed to the tidal window recommended by the local guide. The boat follows the group and picks up divers at the end of the drift or at a pre-agreed exit point. Deploy an SMB before ascending to ensure boat crew visibility.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A reef hook is a valuable piece of equipment here — it allows stationary observation of manta and shark activity without burning energy fighting the current
- Follow the guide's tidal timing closely; arriving outside the productive window can result in slack, featureless diving
- Streamline all equipment before entry
- Blue-water photography setups — wide-angle with strobe — are ideal for manta and shark encounters
- Combine with other Florida Islands sites such as HMNZS Moa, USS Kanawha, or Hundred Thousand Dollar Point for a full dive day in the Tulagi area

## Safety Considerations

Strong current requires solid drift diving skills. Divers should be comfortable with fast-moving water, aware of the risks of separation, and capable of deploying an SMB accurately. The open-water nature of the site means divers can be carried away from the reef if they lose their reference. Establish buddy protocols before entry and agree on procedures for separation. A dive guide with local knowledge is not optional here — incorrect tidal timing can result in genuinely dangerous current speeds. Carry a whistle and SMB.

## Photography

Sandfly Passage's open-water encounters favour wide-angle and fisheye lenses. Shooting upward at a manta feeding against the light-filtered surface is the defining photographic subject, with circling grey reef sharks as a secondary target. Video divers will find the passage produces dramatic footage with minimal effort if the timing is right. The reef structure alongside the passage provides complementary coral and fish life for macro photographers during slack periods.
