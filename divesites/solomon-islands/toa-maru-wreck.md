## Toa Maru Wreck

The Toa Maru is widely considered one of the finest wreck dives in the Solomon Islands and a highlight of any liveaboard or land-based dive itinerary around Gizo in the Western Province. This large Japanese transport ship lies on her starboard side in exceptional condition, with her holds still containing military equipment and personal effects from the war.

## Overview

Sunk on 25 November 1943 by the American submarine USS Searaven, the Toa Maru lies on her starboard side with the bow accessible from approximately 7–12 metres and the stern between 37 and 40 metres. Sources vary slightly on both the ship's length (Wikipedia gives 153.1m/502.3ft and 10,052 tons; other dive sources cite approximately 140m) and the stern depth (Wikipedia states 40m, DiveGizo states 37m) — figures are presented as ranges here rather than picking one. The wreck is extraordinarily intact — divers who peer into the holds encounter a light tank (sources differ on the model: one dive-specific source identifies a Type 95 Ha-Go, another a Type 21), a motorbike, bombs, sake bottles, ammunition, medical supplies, and office equipment that has remained on the seabed for over 80 years, along with accessible interior spaces including a workshop, crew quarters, and dining area. The vessel has an extensive coral garden covering its topside. It sits approximately 7 kilometres north of Gizo, accessible by boat in about 20 minutes, and was recognised by Diver magazine in 2010 as one of the world's top 20 wreck dive sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Gizo, Western Province, Solomon Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 37 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20–40 meters
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

**Updated 2026-09-18 (re-audit):** the previous list of species was not confirmed by any source checked for this wreck and has been replaced. DiveGizo and DivePlanIt report frequent visits from turtles and rays, regal angelfish, and spinecheek anemonefish sheltering in bulb and orange-tentacled anemones, plus notably large nudibranchs. The wreck's topside is covered in an extensive coral garden supporting general reef fish life.

## Dive Profile

The dive begins with a descent to the accessible parts of the wreck at around 7–12 metres, giving an initial orientation to the scale of the vessel before dropping deeper. The wreck lies on her starboard side, so the port side faces upward — divers work along the exposed port deck and hull before dropping to explore the cargo holds. Most divers explore the upper sections and accessible cargo holds — the light tank and motorbike are the most sought-after features. The stern sits at around 37–40 metres. Bottom time at depth should be managed carefully; many divers plan a staged exploration across two dives rather than attempting everything in one descent. A safety stop on the shallower hull sections is recommended.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by boat from Gizo. Local dive operators and liveaboards run regular trips to the site. The site is marked by a buoy. Entry by giant stride or back roll; follow the mooring line down to the wreck. Deploy an SMB for ascent when leaving the structure.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Plan a multi-dive visit — the wreck has enough content for two or three separate dives
- A powerful torch is essential for the cargo holds; a backup torch is strongly recommended
- Nitrox extends bottom time at these depths and is strongly recommended for intermediate divers
- Do not disturb or remove artefacts — this is a war grave and a protected site
- The morning is generally the best time for visibility and light penetrating the upper decks

## Safety Considerations

The 37-metre maximum depth at the stern is at the recreational limit. Divers should be comfortable with depth and aware of their no-decompression limits before descending to the deepest sections. NDL management is critical — the wreck rewards slow exploration, which is difficult at maximum depth. Penetration of the cargo holds should be treated as overhead environment diving; a torch and line are advisable if venturing deep into the holds. The site is generally current-free but conditions can vary with tidal state. Always dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The Toa Maru is exceptional for wide-angle wreck photography. The cargo hold's tank and motorbike, visible in natural silhouette when a diver's torch creates backlighting, produce some of the most memorable wreck images in the Pacific. The topside coral garden offers colourful ambient-light wide-angle subjects. Macro photographers will find nudibranchs and anemonefish throughout the encrusted hull.
