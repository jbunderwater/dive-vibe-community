## Toa Maru Wreck

The Toa Maru is widely considered one of the finest wreck dives in the Solomon Islands and a highlight of any liveaboard or land-based dive itinerary around Gizo in the Western Province. This large Japanese transport ship lies on her starboard side in exceptional condition, with her holds still containing military equipment and personal effects from the war.

## Overview

Sunk on 25 November 1943 by the American submarine USS Searaven, the Toa Maru lies on her starboard side with the bow accessible from approximately 7 metres and the stern at around 37 metres. Sources disagree on the ship's length: Dive Gizo, the local dive operator, describes a 140-metre vessel, while Wikipedia and other maritime-history sources give 153.1 metres (502.3 ft) and a tonnage of 10,052 tons — this page uses the operator's diver-facing depth figures but flags the length as unresolved between roughly 140 and 153 metres rather than picking one. Depth is reported slightly differently between sources too: Dive Gizo gives 7-37 metres (the figures used here, since it is the operator actually running the dive), while other wreck-history sources put the stern nearer 40 metres and the shallowest accessible point at 12 metres — treat 37-40 metres as the realistic range for the deepest point.

The wreck is extraordinarily intact — divers who peer into the holds encounter at least one Japanese light tank (most wreck-history sources identify it as a Type 95 Ha-Go), motorcycle sidecar combinations, sake bottles, ammunition, and medical supplies that have remained on the seabed for over 80 years. The vessel has accumulated dense coral growth across its exterior — Dive Gizo describes an "extensive coral garden" covering the top of the wreck. It sits approximately 7 kilometres north of Gizo, accessible by boat in about 20 minutes.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Gizo, Western Province, Solomon Islands
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 37 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20–40 meters
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: April to November

## Marine Life

Dive Gizo, the local operator running this site, reports "frequent visits from turtles and rays." Beyond that, the operator's own description focuses on the wreck's coral garden rather than a fish list. Earlier drafts of this page named nine additional species (glassy sweepers, cardinalfish, grouper, Napoleon wrasse, lionfish, scorpionfish, clownfish, whitetip reef sharks, barracuda) with no site-specific source confirming any of them at this wreck — that list has been removed. Assume the general reef-fish life typical of an intact, coral-encrusted Western Province wreck in addition to the confirmed turtles and rays, but treat anything more specific as unconfirmed.

## Dive Profile

The dive begins with a descent to the accessible parts of the wreck at around 7–12 metres, giving an initial orientation to the scale of the vessel before dropping deeper. The wreck lies on her starboard side, so the port side faces upward — divers work along the exposed port deck and hull before dropping to explore the cargo holds. Most divers explore the upper sections and accessible cargo holds — the light tank(s) in the holds and the motorcycle sidecar combinations are the most sought-after features. The stern sits at around 37 metres. Bottom time at depth should be managed carefully; many divers plan a staged exploration across two dives rather than attempting everything in one descent. A safety stop on the shallower hull sections is recommended.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by boat from Gizo. Local dive operators and liveaboards run regular trips to the site. The site is marked by a buoy. Entry by giant stride or back roll; follow the mooring line down to the wreck. Deploy an SMB for ascent when leaving the structure.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Plan a multi-dive visit — the wreck has enough content for two or three separate dives
- A powerful torch is essential for the cargo holds; a backup torch is strongly recommended
- Nitrox extends bottom time at these depths and is strongly recommended for intermediate divers
- Do not disturb or remove artefacts — this is a war grave and a protected site
- The morning is generally the best time for visibility and light penetrating the upper decks

## Safety Considerations

The 37-metre maximum depth at the stern is at the recreational limit. Divers should be comfortable with depth and aware of their no-decompression limits before descending to the deepest sections. NDL management is critical — the wreck rewards slow exploration, which is difficult at maximum depth. Penetration of the cargo holds should be treated as overhead environment diving; a torch and line are advisable if venturing deep into the holds. The site is generally current-free but conditions can vary with tidal state. Always dive with a buddy.

## Photography

The Toa Maru is exceptional for wide-angle wreck photography. The cargo holds, with trucks and motorcycles visible in natural silhouette when a diver's torch creates backlighting, produce some of the most memorable wreck images in the Pacific. The superstructure at mid-depth offers colourful sponge and fan coral coverage ideal for ambient-light wide-angle. Macro photographers will find nudibranchs, flatworms, and small crustaceans throughout the encrusted hull.
