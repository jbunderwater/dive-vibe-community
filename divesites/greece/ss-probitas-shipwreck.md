## SS Probitas Shipwreck

## Location Warning

The coordinates of this site (39.87°N, 20.01°E) place it in Saranda Bay, Albania — not in Greece. PADI, Mapcarta, the Albanian Diving Federation, and multiple independent diving sources consistently list this as an Albania dive site. The OSM node appears to have been incorrectly included in the Greece dataset. This entry is retained with its original coordinates and OSM ID pending review.

## Overview

The Probitas was originally launched as the Ansaldo San Giorgio Primo, built at the Ansaldo San Giorgio shipyard in Muggiano and completed in March 1919 (construction no. 162): 5,084 tons, 115.2 metres long and 15.7 metres wide (Albanian Diving Federation). Renamed Probitas, the Italian cargo vessel was part of a convoy leaving Saranda (then "Porto Edda") on 25 September 1943. A mechanical breakdown prevented it from departing with the rest of the convoy; the motor ship Salvore and a transport from Dubac continued and, despite taking heavy damage from German Junkers Ju 87 (Stuka) attacks, made it back to Italy. The Probitas, still moored at the dock, was sunk that afternoon by the Stukas. Traces of the bomb damage remain visible on the wreck's sides.

**Date discrepancy:** The Albanian Diving Federation and OSM both record 25 September 1943 as the sinking date; a lower-authority blog source gives 26 September 1943 with a different framing (troop evacuation, six soldiers killed) that could not be corroborated elsewhere. 25 September 1943 is used here as the better-sourced date.

The vessel rests on its port side in Saranda Bay. Distance from shore is reported inconsistently — from roughly 50 metres (PADI) up to 300 metres (other sources) — so treat this as approximate. **Depth is also reported inconsistently across sources: PADI states 3–22 metres, the Albanian Diving Federation states 3–25 metres, and Silver Sky Diving describes typical dive depths of 20–30 metres.** The wreck is largely intact. Marine life reported by PADI and dive operators includes lionfish, grouper, bream, moray eels, nudibranchs, octopuses, and sponges. The shallow sections are accessible to snorkellers; scuba divers can explore decks, cargo holds, and remnants of wartime equipment. Open Water certification is the minimum recommended level; Advanced is recommended to reach the deeper-reported sections.

- **Location**: Saranda Bay, Albania (NOT Greece — OSM node misassigned)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive

- **Depth Range**: 3–25 metres (sources vary; see Overview)

- **Sunk**: 25 September 1943 by German Ju 87 Stuka aircraft, following convoy breakdown
