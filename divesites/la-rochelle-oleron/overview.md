## La Rochelle & Île d'Oléron

Atlantic wreck diving in the Pertuis Charentais off France's Nouvelle-Aquitaine coast, headlined by the 141-metre WWII Sperrbrecher Sauerland and a cluster of war and merchant wrecks between the islands of Ré, Aix and Oléron.

## Description

The waters between La Rochelle, Île de Ré and Île d'Oléron — the Pertuis d'Antioche and its approaches — hold one of the densest concentrations of divable wrecks on France's Atlantic coast. La Rochelle's La Pallice harbour was a major German U-boat base from 1941, and the mining and counter-mining campaigns around it left a legacy of wrecks: the giant blockade-breaker Sauerland, the minesweeping tug Cornélis, the auxiliary minesweeper M4204, the submarine U-667, the post-war mine victim Saint-Yves, and a B-17 bomber, complemented by the modern coaster Nautilus off Ars-en-Ré and rocky shore diving at Chaucre on Oléron. Depths range from about 13 to 40 m.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Wreck Diving**: Seven documented wrecks from 13 m (B-17) to around 36 m (Sauerland, M4204), most from the 1940–1947 period. The Sauerland, with its vertical knife-edge bow rising to 20 m and stern gun, is the region's signature dive
- **Boat Diving**: All offshore wrecks are boat dives, typically 30 minutes to over an hour offshore depending on the site; the Sauerland lies roughly 24 nautical miles west of La Rochelle
- **Shore Diving**: Rocky plateau and ledge diving at Chaucre on the northwest coast of Île d'Oléron, used by the local club for training and exploration dives

### Conditions & Seasonality

- **Water Temperature**: Roughly 12–16 °C in summer; drysuits or thick semi-drys are standard year-round
- **Tides & Currents**: The Pertuis Charentais has strong tidal streams — all sites are dived at slack water, and schedules follow the tide tables
- **Visibility**: Modest and highly weather- and tide-dependent; the offshore sites fare better than the inner Pertuis
- **Season**: Local clubs and operators dive mainly from late spring to early autumn, when the Bay of Biscay is calmest

### Accessibility

- **Getting There**: La Rochelle has a TGV rail link from Paris and a regional airport (La Rochelle-Île de Ré, LRH); Bordeaux airport is about two hours south. Île de Ré is reached by bridge from La Rochelle, Île d'Oléron by bridge from the mainland south of Rochefort
- **Dive Operators**: Dive operators and clubs are based around La Rochelle and the port of Soubise, on Île de Ré (Ars-en-Ré) and on Île d'Oléron (Saint-Georges-d'Oléron / La Côtinière). Offshore wreck trips run to tide and weather windows
- **Requirements**: The deeper offshore wrecks (Sauerland, Cornélis, M4204, Saint-Yves) are dived from French Level II / equivalent Advanced certification upward; the Saint-Yves additionally sits in the commercial shipping channel to La Pallice and is rarely visited

### Safety Considerations

- Strong tidal currents at all offshore sites; dive at slack water with a local operator
- Cold water and variable, often low visibility require appropriate training and exposure protection
- U-667 is a war grave and should not be penetrated; wreck penetration anywhere requires proper training and equipment
