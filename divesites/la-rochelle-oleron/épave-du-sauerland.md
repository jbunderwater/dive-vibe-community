## Épave du Sauerland

The flagship wreck dive of the Charente-Maritime coast: a 141-metre German Sperrbrecher (mine-clearing blockade ship) sunk in August 1944, lying in open Atlantic water northwest of Île d'Oléron.

## Overview

Built in 1929 as the grain carrier *Sauerland*, the ship was converted into *Sperrbrecher 7* in 1939 and operated out of La Rochelle from 1941, clearing mines along the Biscay convoy routes. On 12 August 1944 she was attacked about 24 nautical miles west of La Rochelle by RAF Coastal Command Beaufighters, which disabled her engines; the drifting ship was then finished off by an Allied naval force including the cruiser HMS Diadem, the destroyer HMS Onslow and the Polish destroyer ORP Piorun, which struck her amidships with a torpedo. Naval chronicles record her at about 7,080 GRT; most dive sources give her length as 141 m, though some report 150 m.

The wreck rests on sand at around 35 m (sources report maximum depths between 35 and 40 m), with the superstructure and the strikingly intact, knife-edge vertical bow rising to about 20 m. A 105 mm gun is still visible at the stern. Sheltered parts of the hull hold pollack, conger eels and lobsters, with seabass patrolling above.

This is an exposed offshore site with strong tidal currents and swell; visibility varies with weather and tide, and local operators dive it around slack water with divers of French Level II and above. Summer water temperatures are typically 12–16 °C, so a drysuit or thick semi-dry is standard.
