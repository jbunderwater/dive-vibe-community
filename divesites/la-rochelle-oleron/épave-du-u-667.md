## Épave du U-667

A Type VIIC U-boat lost with all hands in the closing weeks of the German occupation, resting at recreational depth at the mouth of the Pertuis d'Antioche.

## Overview

*U-667* was returning to La Rochelle in late August 1944 when contact was lost just short of the port; sources date the loss to 25–26 August 1944. Historical research found no record of an Allied attack, and the accepted explanation is that she struck an air-dropped British mine. There were no survivors. In 1973 French divers located a submarine wreck with a heavily damaged bow at the exit of the Pertuis d'Antioche, at 46°06.437'N, 001°35.205'W, identified as U-667.

The submarine lies listing to port on a bottom of about 25 m, with both periscopes still recognisable. Conger eels, seabass, crabs and lobsters shelter in and around the hull. As a war grave and an intact submarine, the wreck should not be penetrated — and as everywhere in the Pertuis, tides dictate the diving window and visibility.
