## Chameau

Chameau ("Camel") is a two-hump shoal approximately 14 kilometres offshore from Nosy Be, named for its distinctive underwater profile. MadaBlu, the operator that runs this site, and an independent 2013 diver trip report both describe a small vessel deliberately sunk on the northwest side as an artificial reef (MadaBlu dates the sinking to April 2007; this date has not been independently verified) — but a separate operator's site listing for the same location describes it as a natural formation with no wreck present. That direct conflict is unresolved; see below.

## Overview

The site takes its name from the two-hump shape of the shoal, which lies along the SE-to-NW tidal current axis. The standard dive profile runs with a falling tide: divers descend to the southeast end of the first hump at around 22 metres, then ascend to 13 metres as the current carries them to the second hump. Reef passages between the humps are lined with gorgonians in the shallower sections. This falling-tide profile is well within Intermediate range.

At high tide with a northwest current, a deeper route starts at approximately 33 metres on a northern shoal, tracking southeast past the reef passages and the wreck exterior before finishing on the second hump. This deeper, current-driven profile is more demanding than the standard route and divers should be comfortable at depth in moving water before attempting it.

**Wreck — sources disagree on whether it exists.** MadaBlu describes a small vessel sunk on the northwest side, reported as April 2007, sitting at roughly 30–33 metres; the source's own wording is that the wreck "contains" or is associated with a large Napoleon wrasse, which most likely means the fish is now resident at the wreck rather than something present at the time of sinking — the phrasing is ambiguous and shouldn't be read as a historical claim. An independent 2013 trip report with photographs (Smart Travels blog) separately corroborates a wreck at a site it calls Chameau, describing it as a small fishing trawler and diving to about 30 metres. No vessel name, builder, dimensions, or tonnage are documented in any source beyond these two operator/eyewitness accounts, and none should be assumed or invented.

Against those two accounts, Aqua Diving's own site listing for the same location ("Camel," 14–28 m) states explicitly that this is a natural rocky-upwelling formation and "not a wreck dive," and lists a different marine life set (white-tip sharks around the rocky upwellings, blue-spotted stingrays, leaf fish, schooling snappers) than the vessel-focused description above. This is a direct, unresolved conflict between sources on the most basic fact about the site — whether an artificial wreck is present at all. Given that MadaBlu is the operator who actually runs the dive and describes the same tide-dependent two-hump profile in detail, and the 2013 trip report independently photographed a wreck while diving what it identified as this site, this write-up follows their account as primary, but readers should treat the wreck's existence as corroborated by two sources rather than certain, and note that a third source disputes it outright.

Confirmed marine life from the MadaBlu/Smart Travels account: lobsters, sea turtles, and lionfish at depth, plus barracuda and peacock mantis shrimp in the reef sections, and gorgonians lining the shallower passages. The wreck interior, if present, should never be assumed safely penetrable without appropriate training and equipment.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Nosy Be, northwest Madagascar (approximately 14 km offshore)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters
