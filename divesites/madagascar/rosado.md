## Rosado

Rosado is recorded in OpenStreetMap under this name, but every local dive operator at Nosy Be lists this site as **Rosario** — the name used here reflects the OSM source data. The site is a shallow reef plateau at 14–19 metres, known for large gorgonian sea fans and good macro diving.

## Overview

The site that operators call Rosario is a broad, gently sloping reef plateau lying at 14–19 metres, with some sections rising shallower toward the end of the dive. Scuba Nosy Be and MadagasCAT both independently describe it this way, and both note it as an easy, gentle drift dive depending on tidal current strength. The reef is covered in large sea fan formations (gorgonians) and smaller coral pinnacles, making it a popular choice for a second morning dive before conditions pick up.

The site is noted specifically for cryptic reef species: fan corals, nudibranchs, stonefish, and a "scorpion leaf fish" (scorpionfish/leaf-scorpionfish) are named by both Scuba Nosy Be and MadagasCAT for this site, along with crocodilefish and large schools of fish. Aqua Diving separately highlights gorgonians and the rare Rhinopias frondosa (weedy scorpionfish) specifically at "Rosario Bank" — a notable, if single-sourced, highlight for macro divers.

Whale sharks are specifically associated with this site during October–November: both Sakalav Diving and Aqua Diving name Rosario/Rosario Bank directly (not just the broader Nosy Be area) as a whale shark location during that window, which is stronger evidence than a generic regional claim. MadagasCAT's page also describes a group of similar Nosy Be plateau sites — including Rosario — as having tops around 13–16m with the reef base dropping to 27–40m and hosting king mackerel, trevally, tuna, occasional sharks, and seasonal humpback whales and mobula/manta rays; because that description covers several similar sites collectively rather than Rosario individually, it's noted here as a general characteristic of the site class rather than a confirmed Rosario-specific sighting. No site-specific reports of reef sharks, stingrays, or barracudas at this location were found in operator listings that describe Rosario by name; those species are more commonly associated with deeper or more exposed Nosy Be sites such as Shark Point. The site is generally described as easy and suitable for beginners or divers seeking a relaxed second dive.
