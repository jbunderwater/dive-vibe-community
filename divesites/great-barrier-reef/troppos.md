## Troppo's

Troppo's is a reef dive site at the northern point of Norman Reef on the outer Great Barrier Reef, Queensland, Australia.

## Overview

**Location corrected 2026-08-03**: earlier versions of this page placed Troppo's "near Lizard Island." Its coordinates and independent sources (divessi.com, reefexperience.com.au) confirm it sits at the northern point of Norman Reef, not Lizard Island. Divessi describes it as beginner-friendly, with a shallow sandy lagoon full of nooks and crannies to explore, alongside a deeper, more scenic reef wall with a sloping sandy bottom for divers wanting more depth. "Troppo" is Australian slang related to tropical heat-induced eccentricity; no source confirms a specific naming story for this site, so the origin is left unstated rather than guessed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern point of Norman Reef, outer Great Barrier Reef, Queensland, Australia (corrected from "Lizard Island area")
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–30 metres
- **Current**: Variable
- **Best Time**: August to November

## Marine Life

No source consulted names specific species for Troppo's. General Norman Reef fauna can be expected, but the barracuda, Spanish mackerel, coral grouper, Napoleon wrasse, sweetlips, batfish, and nudibranch/crinoid claims in an earlier version of this page were not corroborated and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The shallow sandy lagoon with its nooks and crannies suits beginners and those wanting an easy, exploratory dive. Divers seeking more depth can move to the reef wall with its sloping sandy bottom, within our data's 18-metre maximum.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from Cairns or Port Douglas operators running trips to Norman Reef, or from liveaboard operators. Typically a moored dive with entry by giant stride and exit via boat ladder or platform.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Look for cleaning stations along the reef — large fish queue patiently at these points and are uncharacteristically approachable
- The reef crest in the first few metres is excellent for snorkellers and less confident divers
- Check with the boat crew for current direction before descent — the dive plan should account for drift along the reef

## Safety Considerations

Standard outer reef dive site. Sun protection is critical in tropical Queensland. Tidal currents on the outer reef can increase rapidly — monitor the conditions on descent and signal the boat if current is unexpectedly strong.
