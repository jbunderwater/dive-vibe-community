## My Lady Lata II Wreck

My Lady Lata II is a half-submerged wreck just off Pangaimotu Island near Nuku'alofa harbour — an easy, extremely shallow snorkel-and-dive site and one of the most visited wreck attractions in Tongatapu.

## Overview

**A note on this entry**: this site's official chart data (LINZ hydrographic ID 3857299) lists it under the name "Marmer," with an alternate name of "Lady Lata." Research into that alternate name turned up a real, well-documented wreck matching this exact location and depth: My Lady Lata II, a popular half-submerged wreck just off Pangaimotu Island, a short boat trip from Nuku'alofa. This entry has been renamed accordingly, with "Marmer" kept as the alternate/chart name.

My Lady Lata II sits in very shallow water — around 2 to 2.4 meters — close to Pangaimotu's main beach, rusted but largely intact, with sunlight visible through her windows and doorways and stairways and ladders still identifiable inside the hull. Sources disagree on exactly when and how she came to rest here: one account (written in 2014) describes her as having been wrecked by a cyclone roughly twelve years earlier, which would place the event around the early 2000s; a separate account associates wreck material at Pangaimotu with Cyclone Gita, which struck Tonga in February 2018. It is not clear from available sources whether these describe the same vessel or two different wrecks at the same general location, so both accounts are noted here rather than treated as settled fact.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Pangaimotu Island, near Nuku'alofa, Tongatapu, Tonga
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 2 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 meters
- **Current**: Light; sheltered, very shallow water
- **Water Temperature**: 23–27°C (73–81°F)
- **Best Time to Dive**: Year-round; calm conditions preferred given the shallow depth

## Marine Life

No site-specific marine-life reports were found beyond general mentions of "new marine life" colonizing the hull. Tongatapu's regional reef fauna is present in the surrounding waters, but no source names specific species observed on or around this wreck, so none are listed here.

## Dive Profile

At 2-2.4 meters, this is effectively a snorkel-depth site that can also be dived on scuba. The wreck is explored by swimming around and, where structurally sound, through the hull's windows and doorways, taking in stairways and ladders still visible inside. Bottom time is essentially unlimited at this depth, and the appeal is close examination of the wreck itself rather than depth or navigation.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by the regular boat service from Faua Wharf in Nuku'alofa to Pangaimotu Island (roughly a 10-minute crossing), or via other Pangaimotu day-trip operators. The wreck lies close to the island's main beach, and both shore-adjacent and short-swim access have been described by visitors.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This is a good beginner or non-diver-friendly site — a mask and snorkel are enough to see the whole wreck
- Watch buoyancy and hand placement given the very shallow depth and the wreck's rusted, aged condition
- Combine with a day trip to Pangaimotu's beach and Big Mama's Yacht Club
- Because sources disagree on the wreck's exact history, treat any specific date or storm story you hear locally with some skepticism

## Safety Considerations

The main hazard here is sea state rather than depth or air supply — at 2 meters, surge and swell concentrate quickly and can push a diver or snorkeler into the rusted hull. Only visit in calm conditions, and be mindful of sharp or unstable metal edges on the aged wreck structure.

## Photography

Direct sunlight at this shallow depth makes for vivid natural-light photography with no strobes required. Wide-angle shots capturing the hull's windows and doorways with visible interior structure, and the play of light through the rusted metal, are the strongest compositions here.
