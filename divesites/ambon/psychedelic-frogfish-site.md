## Psychedelic Frogfish Site

Psychedelic Frogfish Site marks the "Laha I" location where the psychedelic frogfish (Histiophryne psychedelica) was first photographed in January 2008 and its holotype collected — the species' type locality, though sightings here in recent years are not well documented.

## Overview

On re-verification, "Psychedelic Frogfish Site" does not appear as an independently named dive site on any currently-live dive-shop or database page checked (starfish.ch, neptuneliveaboards.com, dive-the-world.com, bottles-up-diving.com, bluewaterdivetravel.com). Wikipedia ties the species' 2008 discovery and holotype collection specifically to "Laha I" — a named sub-section of the broader Laha muck complex — rather than to a distinctly named site. This entry is retained in the database as a way to mark that specific historically significant location within Laha, but readers should understand it is a descriptive label rather than a name used by local operators.

In January 2008, guides Buck Randolph, Fitrie Randolph, and Toby Fadirsyair photographed the psychedelic frogfish at Laha I for the first time; a holotype specimen was collected on 2 April 2008, and ichthyologists Theodore Pietsch, Rachel Arnold, and David Hall formally described the new species in the journal Copeia in 2009. These facts are all confirmed on Wikipedia's current article for the species.

**Recency caution**: this species is reported as very rare. The most recent specific sighting report located during re-verification is a ScubaBoard discussion from February 2019 describing a single small individual, with posters noting no further confirmed updates and actively discouraging publicizing exact locations to protect the species from disturbance or collection. Divers should not expect a reliable or current sighting and should treat any encounter as an exceptional event rather than a routine one.

According to Wikipedia and the original species description, the fish was found at depths of 5–7 meters, approximately 20 meters from the shoreline, within 100 meters of the commercial jetty. The habitat is described as coral rubble in shallow muck conditions — the standard Laha substrate. The species has so far been positively identified only at Ambon Island, though it may eventually be found elsewhere in Indonesia.

The psychedelic frogfish is small (maximum 15 cm), pale yellow-brown to peach in colour with unique swirling white-stripe patterns — each individual's markings are distinct, enabling researchers to track specimens over time. It walks on its pectoral fins and uses jet propulsion to move. Sightings are not guaranteed on any given dive; local guides who know the site are the most reliable means of locating individuals.

Note: This site overlaps geographically with the Laha and Twilight Zone dive areas — these are not sharply delineated separate dive sites but sections of the same north-bay muck slope that different operators and guides name slightly differently.

## Marine Life

The psychedelic frogfish (Histiophryne psychedelica) is the site-defining species, confirmed by Wikipedia at 5–7 meters depth on coral rubble at Laha I — though, per the recency caution above, confirmed recent sightings are scarce. Beyond the frogfish itself, no source specifically confirms other species at this precise sub-location; the broader Laha complex (of which this is a part) supports a wide range of muck-diving fauna, but attributing that regional list to this specific spot would overstate what is actually documented here. Divers should expect the general Laha muck-diving character (frogfish, cephalopods, cryptic macro life) to apply, without assuming any specific species beyond the psychedelic frogfish itself is guaranteed at this precise location.

## Dive Profile

Very shallow dive — 5 to 7 meters of depth for the primary target zone. Shore entry from the Laha I area near the Spice Island Divers / former Maluku Divers jetty. Move slowly over coral rubble, inspecting crevices and overhangs. A local guide is essential; this species is cryptic and easily missed without trained eyes. Allow long bottom times — the shallow depth permits extended dives on a single tank.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the Laha I access point. Confirm the exact entry location with your dive operator, as access points shift with the local working-jetty activity. Exit from the same location.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Book with a guide who knows the Laha I area specifically — this species is extremely cryptic and, per recent reports, rarely encountered
- Morning dives before sediment disturbance from boats tend to have better visibility
- Photograph carefully without disturbing the fish if found; it is a rare, easily-stressed species, and some in the diving community discourage publicizing exact locations to protect it
- Treat a sighting as an exceptional event, not an expectation — the most recent confirmed report found dates to 2019

## Safety Considerations

The site is very shallow (5–7 meters) and generally calm. The main hazard is boat traffic in the active harbour area — always surface with awareness of overhead vessels. Always dive with a guide familiar with the area.

## Photography

The psychedelic frogfish is one of the most photographed rare species in the Indo-Pacific. At 5–7 meters, natural light is adequate but strobes produce better colour on the pale, strikingly patterned skin. Use a diopter or macro lens; maximum body size is 15 cm. Patience and stillness are more important than any equipment choice.
