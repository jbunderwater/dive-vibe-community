## Rhino City

Rhino City is a sandy slope with stones, coral patches and anemones on the north side of Ambon Bay, named for the rhinopias scorpionfish reported there.

## Overview

Dive the World describes Rhino City as an extension of the Laha slope to its west, and says it is "named after the Rhinopias - a rare type of cryptic weedy scorpionfish that you can find here." Starfish describes slopes with stones, coral patches, sponges and soft corals, and treats it as part of the same Ambon Bay muck stretch that runs through the Twilight Zone to Laha 3. Bottles Up Diving lists "Rhino City / Twilight Zone" as a single entry. Because operators group and name these sections differently, the boundaries between Laha, Twilight Zone and Rhino City are approximate.

Starfish reports rhinopias here in yellow, blue, red, pink-to-peach and brown, and describes them as "not very shy". It adds that Indonesia has weedy (Rhinopias frondosa) and Eschmeyer's (R. eschmeyeri) scorpionfish, and that the lacy scorpionfish (R. aphanes) does not occur in Indonesia. Indo Pacific Images says that at certain times of year there can be up to six resident rhinopias.

None of the sources reviewed gives a depth figure for Rhino City specifically; the 20 m listed below is an approximate database value, not a sourced maximum. Dive the World suggests the shallows are a good place to spend much of the dive.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West of Laha, north Ambon Bay
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Not given in the sources reviewed
- **Current**: Not described in the sources reviewed

## Marine Life

- **Dive the World**: rhinopias scorpionfish, black-and-white anemonefish, anemone shrimps, porcelain crabs, ribbon eels, emperor and commensal shrimps, arrow crabs, black razor shrimp, hairy crabs, orang-utan crabs, jawfish, robust ghost pipefish, short-snout pipefish, nudibranchs and dragonets.
- **Starfish**: rhinopias in several colour forms.
- **Indo Pacific Images**: rhinopias, including a pink paddleflap rhinopias in a photo caption.
- **Bottles Up Diving** (combined "Rhino City / Twilight Zone" entry): rhinopias and other scorpionfish, seahorses, octopus, cuttlefish, crabs, and mandarin fish at dusk.
- **DivePlanit**: solar-powered nudibranchs.

## Dive Profile

Sources describe a slope with sand, stones, anemones and coral patches; no source gives a prescribed route or turnaround depths.

## Entry and Exit

The sources reviewed do not describe the entry point. The database lists the site as shore entry; confirm access with your operator.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A guide who dives the site regularly is the practical way to locate rhinopias, which are camouflaged.
- Dive the World highlights the shallows as productive for anemone life.

## Safety Considerations

No site-specific hazards are described in the sources reviewed. Dive within your certification, use a surface marker, and be aware that boats operate in Ambon Bay.
