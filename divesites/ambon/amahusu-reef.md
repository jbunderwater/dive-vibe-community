## Amahusu Reef

Amahusu Reef is a slope on the east side of Ambon Bay where sandy areas lead to patchy reef and a coral block, with small critters reported by several sources.

## Overview

The database name "Amahusu Reef" corresponds to the Amahusu/Amahusa area on the east side of the bay; Starfish lists a related site as "Devion Point (Amahusa)" and Bottles Up Diving lists "Amahusa", and the match between these names is an assumption. Bottles Up Diving describes fairly steep slopes with a sandy slope leading to a nice patchy reef. Starfish describes a large coral block with sandy areas nearby. Indo Pacific Images describes Amahusu as a steep bay slope on the east side, and with the nearby Dark Blue Jetty among the most regularly dived sites there.

No source reviewed gives a depth figure for this site. The 25 m in the database is an approximate value, not a sourced measurement.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Eastern Ambon Bay, Amahusu/Amahusa area
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Not given in the sources reviewed
- **Current**: Not described in the sources reviewed

## Marine Life

- **Bottles Up Diving (Amahusa)**: leaf scorpionfish, hinge-beak shrimp, nudibranchs, anemonefish, clown frogfish, decorator spider crabs, jawfish and featherstars.
- **Starfish (Devion Point)**: breeding cardinalfish, a small clown frogfish and leaffish on the coral block; jawfish digging burrows in sand.
- **Indo Pacific Images (Amahusu)**: a resident colony of Saron shrimps.

## Dive Profile

Sources describe a sandy slope leading to patchy reef and a coral block; none gives a route or depths.

## Entry and Exit

Not described in the sources reviewed. The database lists shore entry; confirm with your operator.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Look closely around the coral block and patchy reef; sources report camouflaged subjects such as leaf scorpionfish and clown frogfish.
- Check the sand for jawfish burrows (Starfish).

## Safety Considerations

No site-specific hazards are described in the sources reviewed. The slopes are described as fairly steep, so monitor depth.
