## Coral Gardens Grace Bay

Coral Gardens (also called Bight Reef) is a shallow, shore-access reef in Princess Alexandra National Park, used mainly by snorkellers.

## Overview

Visit TCI describes Bight Reef as the most popular snorkeling site in the Turks and Caicos, a single main coral ridge extending about 350ft (107m) from the beach with water 15-20ft (4.5-6m) deep at the outer reaches. Access is from shore via beach paths off Lower Bight Road; Islands.com likewise describes a footpath from Grace Bay to the buoyed snorkel area. Visit TCI does not mention scuba, so treat this as a very shallow snorkel or beginner-level site. It is inside Princess Alexandra National Park, with a roped conservation zone that is off limits, boat traffic just outside the swim zone and no lifeguards. Visit TCI notes the reef has declined somewhat over the past 20 years.

## Marine Life

Visit TCI names green turtles, southern stingrays, spotted eagle rays and occasionally nurse sharks, plus stoplight and blue parrotfish, Nassau grouper, queen angelfish, barracuda, queen triggerfish, spotted moray eels, Caribbean spiny lobster and banded coral shrimp; it also warns of lionfish. Islands.com independently names green and hawksbill turtles, stingrays, eagle rays, nurse sharks, stoplight parrotfish, queen angelfish, spotted moray eels and queen triggerfish. The resort site Coral Gardens on Grace Bay mentions sea turtles.
