## Norwegian Bay

Norwegian Bay is a remote, shallow-lagoon dive and snorkel area approximately 80 kilometres north of Coral Bay, accessible only by liveaboard sailing vessel, and home to the ruins of a historic shore-whaling station and several separately-documented shipwrecks nearby.

## Overview

Norwegian Bay (also referred to in historical records as Point Cloates) was the site of a shore-based whaling station built in 1915 by Norwegian whaling interests. Whaling was interrupted by WWI, and the station did not resume operation until 1922, when it was purchased by the North West (Aust.) Whaling Company. After a period of financial difficulty the company was forced into liquidation in 1925 and leased the station to a Norwegian operator; whaling continued intermittently through the 1920s, was suspended after 1929, and the station was later revived and modernised after WWII before finally closing in 1957, when operations moved to Carnarvon. The foundations, flensing decks, slipways, and rusted machinery remain accessible on shore.

Several shipwrecks lie in the wider area, but they are not all connected to the whaling station's history — a distinction the WA Museum's maritime archaeology survey of the site makes clear:

- **The Fin** — a whale chaser purchased new by the North West (Aust.) Whaling Company in 1922 — is the vessel actually linked to the station's operations. On 15–16 January 1923 a cyclone struck the station, breaking the moorings of the Fin and her sister chaser Frey and driving both ashore on a small sand island near the Point Cloates lighthouse (recorded on old charts as "Fraser Island," since washed away). The Frey was refloated, but the Fin, reported at the time as standing "high and dry" on the sand at low water, became a total wreck. Dive/snorkel operator sources describe the wreck as lying partly submerged in shallow, clear water and reachable as a snorkel site; the museum survey did not include a modern site description, so this is sourced to divingwawrecks.com rather than the archaeological report. Precise tonnage and hull material for the Fin are not consistently documented in available sources and are not stated here to avoid guessing.
- **The Zvir**, a 1900-built steel/iron screw steamer (J. Priestman & Co., Sunderland; roughly 100 x 14 x 8 metres, around 3,350 gross tons), ran aground nearby on 27 November 1902 while carrying a sugar cargo from Java to Port Adelaide — thirteen years before the whaling station existed. The wreck lies in 8–10 metres of water about 400 metres from the outer reef and has been surveyed by WA Museum divers since 1978.
- **The SS Perth** (originally the Penola, built 1863 in Glasgow, later lengthened and enlarged to roughly 449 gross tons) stranded at Point Cloates in September 1887 — 28 years before the whaling station was built. Sources give the wreck date as either 13 or 15 September 1887. A subsequent inquiry attributed the stranding to a strong southeast current and hazy weather, with no blame found against the master or crew.
- **An unidentified barge** lies a few hundred metres north of the old jetty, first noted from aerial photography and later surveyed by side-scan sonar in 2009. The museum's technical report does not give a length, cause of loss, or name for this wreck, so those details are left unstated here rather than estimated.

In short: only the Fin is properly "of" the whaling station; the Zvir and Perth are older, unrelated wrecks that happen to lie in the same stretch of coast, and the barge remains undocumented beyond its rough position.

The diving and snorkelling here is shallow — typically 3–4 metres over sandy lagoons and fringing reef — and the area's remoteness means minimal visitor pressure. Sail Ningaloo operates liveaboard tours that include Norwegian Bay as a destination.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Norwegian Bay / Point Cloates, Ningaloo Marine Park, Western Australia (~80km north of Coral Bay; liveaboard access only)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 4 meters
- **Wrecks nearby**: Fin (whale chaser, wrecked January 1923, linked to the whaling station), Zvir (1902, unrelated cargo steamer), SS Perth (1887, unrelated cargo steamer), an unidentified barge north of the jetty
- **Access**: Liveaboard only; no day-boat access
