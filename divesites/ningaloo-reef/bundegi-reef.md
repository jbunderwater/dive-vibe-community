## Bundegi Reef

Bundegi Reef is a shallow fringing reef in the protected Exmouth Gulf, reached by a 200–300 metre swim from Bundegi Beach, one of Exmouth's most accessible snorkel sites.

## Overview

Bundegi lies inside Exmouth Gulf rather than on the exposed outer Ningaloo Reef, giving it calm, protected conditions year-round. Snorkelling directly from the beach means a 200–500 metre swim across a boating channel (roughly 8 metres deep) to reach the reef edge, which sits around 300m offshore at the boat ramp and extends a further 200m. For better snorkelling, two Parks WA kayak moorings within the Bundegi Sanctuary Zone ("Nook and Crannies" and "The Cove") are reached via a 3.5km paddle south-southwest along the inside of the reef. Depths at both the near-shore reef and the sanctuary zone moorings run roughly 1.5–4 metres.

Confirmed marine life at the site includes big plate coral bommies and healthy staghorn coral supporting sea turtles (present year-round, with nesting from November to March), reef sharks, groper, large schools of drummer, angelfish, small stingrays, anemone fish, lionfish, damselfish, estuary cod, and coral trout. Manta rays and, occasionally, humpback whales visit the wider Exmouth Gulf area during August–September.

Bundegi Reef suffered severe coral bleaching during the 2011–12 marine heatwave, when water temperatures rose up to 5°C above normal and as little as 1–6% of corals survived after eight months of sustained heat stress. By 2013, recovery remained patchy — some sections showed limited regrowth while others were still coral rubble. The remaining structure continues to support a substantial range of marine life. Snorkelers are advised to enter at high tide (minimum 1.2m) to ensure adequate water depth over the reef.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Exmouth Gulf, Ningaloo Marine Park, Western Australia (less than 15 minutes north of Exmouth)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 3 meters
- **Tides**: Best at high tide (minimum 1.2m); kayak moorings available
