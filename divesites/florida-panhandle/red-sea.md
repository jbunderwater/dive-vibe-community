## Red Sea

Red Sea is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A 125-ft tugboat originally built in 1943 for the Panama Canal Commission under the name San Pablo. She spent nearly 50 years maneuvering the crane barges used to dredge and maintain the canal and helping steer freighters and cargo ships into the canal's narrow locks, before being sold and renamed San Pablo I in 1990. She changed ownership and name twice more (including Aquilon) before moving from Panama to the US, where she was renamed Red Sea and sunk as an artificial reef on June 24, 2009, as part of a combined effort by state, local, and private interests in Bay County. The wreck is relatively intact and sits upright on sand; the top of her wheelhouse now rests upside down on the seabed just off her port side, and the large funnel that once dominated her midship has collapsed into the room below, making that particular interior space impossible to penetrate, though there remain penetration opportunities elsewhere for divers with appropriate experience. Large numbers of snapper and jacks gather around the wreck, drawn to the schooling bait fish here, along with several damselfish species (cocoa damselfish, beaugregories, sergeant majors) and whitespotted soapfish sheltering closer to the hull, blue and Queen angelfish, and schools of tomtates and other grunts in the shadowy interior; cobia are often seen patrolling the surrounding waters.
