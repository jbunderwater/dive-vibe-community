## Red Sea

Red Sea is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A 125-ft tugboat sunk as an artificial reef on June 24, 2009, as part of a combined effort by state, local, and private interests in Bay County. **Sourcing note**: the guidebook traces her back to a 1943 build for the Panama Canal Commission under the name San Pablo, with several subsequent renamings (San Pablo I, Aquilon) before arriving in the US as Red Sea. An independent source (a ScubaBoard thread from local divers) instead states she was built in Philadelphia in 1929, with no mention of any Panama Canal or San Pablo history — a direct conflict rather than a minor discrepancy, possibly indicating the guidebook has confused this wreck with a different vessel (there is also an unrelated, separately documented "San Pablo" wreck near Pensacola). Given the conflict, her build year, build location, and any prior names should be treated as unresolved rather than stated as fact. The wreck is relatively intact and sits upright on sand; the top of her wheelhouse now rests upside down on the seabed just off her port side, and the large funnel that once dominated her midship has collapsed into the room below, making that particular interior space impossible to penetrate, though there remain penetration opportunities elsewhere for divers with appropriate experience. Large numbers of snapper and jacks gather around the wreck, drawn to the schooling bait fish here, along with several damselfish species (cocoa damselfish, beaugregories, sergeant majors) and whitespotted soapfish sheltering closer to the hull, blue and Queen angelfish, and schools of tomtates and other grunts in the shadowy interior; cobia are often seen patrolling the surrounding waters.
