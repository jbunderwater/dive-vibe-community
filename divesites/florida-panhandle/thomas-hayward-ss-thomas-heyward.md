## Thomas Hayward (SS Thomas Heyward)

Thomas Hayward (SS Thomas Heyward) is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

Built July 1942 in Mobile, AL (Alabama Drydock & Shipbuilding Co.), one of 2,711 Liberty Ships built to ferry WWII supplies to Europe; named for Thomas Heyward Jr., a South Carolina signer of the Declaration of Independence. Survived striking a floating mine off Europe in 1946, was repaired, then laid up in the reserve fleet before the Florida Department of Natural Resources purchased her and deployed her as an artificial reef on April 17, 1977 — the largest artificial reef in Okaloosa County. Length 440 ft (134m), beam 57 ft (17.5m), tonnage 7,200 grt; all superstructure and upper decking were removed before deployment, leaving only the hull and a few interior bracing walls. Sits upright at roughly 86 ft (26m); has split into two, with a small stern section separated and 15 ft (4.5m) out of alignment. A portside hull section near the bow collapsed during the 2020 storm season. Added to the Florida Panhandle Shipwreck Trail in 2020. Note: the ship's historic name is spelled "Thomas Heyward," but local dive-site naming commonly uses "Thomas Hayward" — a spelling discrepancy worth flagging. Almaco jacks, tomtates, reef and spotfin butterflyfish, cocoa damselfish, spotted eagle rays, and sandbar and nurse sharks reported regularly; whale sharks and manta rays have made rare summer/fall appearances; Spanish mackerel swim through the water column above.
