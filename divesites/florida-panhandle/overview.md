## Florida Panhandle

Gulf of Mexico wreck diving from Pensacola to Panama City Beach, anchored by the USS Oriskany — the world's largest artificial reef — plus dozens of intentionally sunk freighters, tugs, Liberty ships, and Navy vessels.

## Description

The Florida Panhandle / Emerald Coast (Pensacola, Destin, Panama City Beach) is a year-round Gulf of Mexico dive destination built around artificial-reef wreck diving rather than natural coral reefs. Pensacola is home to the USS Oriskany, and Panama City Beach is nicknamed the "Wreck Capital of the South" for its density of sunken vessels, ranging from WWII-era Liberty ships and minesweepers to modern drug-interdiction seizures sunk under artificial reef programs.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Wreck Diving**: The dominant style of diving in the region — historic Navy vessels (USS Massachusetts, USS Strength), WWII Liberty ships (Joseph L. Meek, Thomas Hayward), and purpose-sunk artificial reefs (USS Oriskany, Antares, Pete Tide II) span a wide depth range from shallow tugs to advanced-only wrecks past 40 m.
- **Rigs-to-Reefs**: Former oil platform jackets (Chevron Oil Rig) donated and deployed as artificial reefs attract dense marine growth.
- **Boat Diving**: Nearly all sites are boat-access, typically 3-25 nautical miles offshore from Pensacola, Destin, or Panama City Beach.

### Accessibility

- **Getting There**: Pensacola International Airport (PNS), Northwest Florida Beaches International Airport (ECP, serving Panama City Beach), or Destin-area airports.
- **Dive Operators**: Charter operators run daily trips from Pensacola, Destin, and Panama City Beach out to the wreck sites.

### Conditions

- **Water Type**: Warm Gulf of Mexico saltwater; temperatures range roughly 60°F in winter to 85°F in summer.
- **Season**: Best conditions generally run late spring through early fall (April-October), when visibility is highest and currents calmest.
- **Visibility**: Varies by distance from shore — inshore/nearshore sites typically run 20-50 ft, offshore sites can reach 60-100 ft.

### Conservation

Several sites are designated Underwater Archaeological Preserves; treat historic wrecks (Vamar, San Pablo/Russian Freighter) as protected sites. Do not treat wreck-interior penetration as safe without proper wreck-diving training and equipment.

## Additional Information

- **Currency**: US Dollar
- **Language**: English
- **Safety**: Standard offshore wreck-diving precautions apply — boat traffic, current, and depth/decompression planning for the deeper wrecks. Several sources explicitly warn against unguided interior penetration on major wrecks like the USS Oriskany and Antares.
