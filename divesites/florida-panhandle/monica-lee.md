## Monica Lee

Monica Lee is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A small, 49-ft tugboat, deployed as an artificial reef by Okaloosa County on May 10, 2011 in an area known as Conch Reef, with Turn Key Marine Service (a boating company near Destin) involved in the deployment. **Sourcing note**: the guidebook traces her back to a 1955 build and an earlier career with McCulley Marine Services of Stuart, FL, before her 2006 sale to Turn Key. Independent maritime registries turned up two different tugs named Monica Lee, built in 1970 and 1975 respectively — neither matches the 1955 build date or the McCulley Marine origin story, and neither is otherwise connected to this reef. The Turn Key Marine connection and 2011 deployment are corroborated; the vessel's earlier history should be treated as unverified. Anchors the center of an X-shaped artificial reef area known as Conch Reef, with pyramid-shaped modules extending outward in lines hundreds of feet away (not usually visible from the wreck itself). Sits upright on a sandy seafloor with a slight list to port; her depth is what makes her accessible to experienced divers only - top of the wreck reaches 100 ft (30.5m), seabed at 117 ft (35.5m). The forward wall of the wheelhouse has been torn away, with jagged metal from the window casings making that area difficult to access for all but the most experienced wreck divers; access points to the tug's small interior remain at the first level of the superstructure. Schooling tomtates and large red snapper are common, along with barracuda, yellow jacks, and greater amberjacks; whitespotted soapfish, blue angelfish, and several damselfish species also frequent the site.
