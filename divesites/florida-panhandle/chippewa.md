## Chippewa

Chippewa is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

Originally built as a Navajo-class fleet ocean tug in 1943 in Charleston, South Carolina, classified AT-69 and later reclassified ATF-69 in 1944 (the line was eventually renamed the Cherokee class). During her naval career she ventured as far east as Casablanca, where she laid buoys during WWII, and operated south to Trinidad and north to Newfoundland, Canada. Decommissioned in 1947 and laid up in the Texas reserve fleet, she was given to the Navy Experimental Dive Unit in Panama City in January 1989, used in an explosives training project, and eventually sunk as an artificial reef in February 1990. At over 200 ft in length, she is one of the larger naval tugs deployed as an artificial reef in Northwest Florida. Decades underwater have not been kind to her: there is extensive wall damage, most of the wheelhouse structure is gone, and a large crack runs through the deck and hull near the stern; her large center tower now lies on the seafloor off her port side. She is accessible to most divers, but at a maximum depth of just over 100 ft (30.5m), the wreck is better suited to experienced divers, though the most interesting shallower parts remain accessible to open water divers. Divers are almost guaranteed to see Atlantic spadefish schooling around the wreck, grunts (including tomtates) sheltering in the shadowy interior, whitespotted soapfish and cocoa damselfish near the structure, and barracuda, jacks, and snapper patrolling above; a large resident goliath grouper is a regular sight.
