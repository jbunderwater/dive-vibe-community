## Charles H. McClenahan Memorial Reef (Mac's Reef)

Charles H. McClenahan Memorial Reef (Mac's Reef) is a reef dive site in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

NOTE: shares the nickname 'Mac's Reef' with a different, unrelated site already in this database (Bay County, named for diver Richard McCullen) - use the full name to avoid confusion. Named for Charles 'Mac' McClenahan, a former Department of Defense employee with 50 years of service: 20 years (from 1964) on the Eglin Air Force Base Explosive Ordnance Disposal team, followed by 28 years in Civil Service specializing in guns and munitions, focused on concrete structures and how best to defeat them. The memorial reef is fittingly made up of old concrete targets from Eglin AFB. A total of 981.5 tons of concrete material sits at the center of Okaloosa County's Fish Haven 13 permitted site: a pile of targets dropped November 7, 2015 (rising 12 ft/3.5m above the seabed), plus concrete culverts to the south of the pile - some nearly 5 ft (1.5m) in diameter, large enough for divers to swim through. A 9/11 memorial featuring steel and concrete pieces from the World Trade Center and the Pentagon was added to the site's northeast corner in September 2020. The site stretches roughly 184 ft (56m) north to south. Tomtates are particularly abundant, with mackerel and barracuda in the water column above, plus moray eels, angelfish, and flounder camouflaged in the sand; southern stingrays generally stick to the periphery.
