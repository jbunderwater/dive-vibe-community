## YDT-14

YDT-14 is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

US Navy Yard Diving Tender (YDT), originally designated YF-294, part of a series of "covered lighter, self-propelled" vessels used by the Navy in a supporting role during WWII, possibly serving as an inter-island supply vessel in the Caribbean as part of the 10th Naval District. After the war, transferred to Norfolk/5th Naval District; renamed Phoebus in 1965, then redesignated YDT-14 and assigned to the Washington D.C.-based Navy Diving and Salvage Training Center in 1974, relocating with the Center to Panama City in 1980 until decommissioned. Acquired by the Escambia County artificial reef program and deployed as an artificial reef in April 2000 alongside her sister ship YDT-15 (which sits over 500ft/150m to the southeast, rarely explored on the same dive). One of the original members of the Florida Panhandle Shipwreck Trail; her bow is tilted upward so she sits slightly higher than her stern. Deck level is around 85ft(26m) — perhaps best suited to divers with deep diving experience. Two main swim-throughs pass through the superstructure; the bridge remains intact for divers with good buoyancy control. Barracuda, snapper, butterflyfish, amberjacks, and Atlantic spadefish commonly seen schooling above the wreck; gag and scamp grouper often found inside.
