## SS Tarpon

SS Tarpon is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A twin-screw steamship built in Delaware in 1887 (originally christened Naugatuck), used to carry cargo and passengers between Connecticut and New York and as an icebreaker on the Housatonic River. Sold in 1902 to the Pensacola, St. Andrews & Gulf Steamship Company, lengthened 30 ft and renamed SS Tarpon in 1891, she ran a weekly Mobile, AL-Carrabelle, FL route for 35 years, calling at Pensacola, Panama City, and Apalachicola, under the command of Captain Willis Green Barrow for the better part of three decades. On her 1,735th trip, overloaded with roughly 200 tons of cargo against inspectors' warnings, she sank in a gale roughly 9 miles southwest of Panama City on September 1, 1937. Sources conflict on the death toll: the guidebook cites 18 lives lost among 31 passengers and crew, while an independent source (Wikipedia) puts the crew at 25 with 24 drowned and a single survivor — deckhand Addley Baker, who reportedly swam 25 miles to shore. Captain Barrow was among those lost; his age at the time and the ship's exact tonnage were not independently confirmed. She became Florida's sixth Underwater Archaeological Preserve in 1997. The wreck is a complex debris field on a sand and rock seabed, facing roughly due west at a depth of approximately 95 ft (29m). Notable features include the main boiler (the largest structure on site, rising close to 10 ft off the bottom), twin donkey boilers, the engine, propeller drive shafts, anchor chain, and hull plates. It's an open site with no penetration opportunities, supporting a high density and diversity of fish including scrawled filefish, burrfish, spotfin butterflyfish, gag, scamp, and black sea bass, plus lobster and frogfish; large amberjacks, barracuda, and sharks are frequently seen in the water column above the wreck.
