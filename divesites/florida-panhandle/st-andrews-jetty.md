## St. Andrews Jetty

St. Andrews Jetty is a reef dive site in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

The most popular shore dive in the Panama City area, at St. Andrews State Park, known for high biodiversity and simple beach access. The jetty runs roughly northeast-southwest, extending about 750 ft into the Gulf. Two access points serve the site: the Kiddie Pool (a shallow lagoon behind the jetty) and the open Gulf-side beach. The channel side bottoms out at a maximum depth of about 75 ft (23m) adjacent to the wide beach separating the Kiddie Pool from the Gulf, shallowing to around 45 ft (13.5m) toward the jetty's end; the Gulf side is much shallower than the channel side. Marine life includes damselfish, sergeant majors, butterflyfish, grouper, snapper, and angelfish; divers regularly report octopuses, seahorses, and batfish among the rocks, with stingrays and flounder at the interface between rock and sand, and spotted eagle rays cruising through the channel. The best time to dive is at high slack tide, when current is low and visibility is typically best. A surface marker buoy is required unless staying in the Kiddie Pool. Divers should avoid venturing into the middle of the boat channel, which poses a significant traffic hazard.
