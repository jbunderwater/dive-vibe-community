## SS Empire Mica

SS Empire Mica is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A British tanker built in 1941 in Middlesbrough, England, that transported fuel oil during WWII. Sources report her length as 465-479 ft and her cargo capacity as roughly 11,200-12,000 tons; she was loaded with kerosene aviation fuel in Baytown, TX, bound for Britain to supply the Royal Air Force. Her captain sought a sheltered overnight anchorage to avoid German U-boat attack, but the shallow bays along the northwest Florida coast couldn't accommodate the fully loaded ship, forcing her to push on through the night. Just after midnight on June 29, 1942, the German submarine U-67 struck her with two torpedoes, igniting her kerosene cargo into a fireball visible nearly 30 miles away; only one lifeboat with 14 crew escaped, and 33 of her crew were lost. The wreck is a complex debris field stretching approximately 500 ft, consisting largely of twisted steel beams and hull plates, with significant entanglement risk from overhanging debris. Her three giant boilers are the highest points on the wreck, reaching up to 87 ft (26.5m); the seabed sits at approximately 105 ft (32m). Her original propeller was salvaged years ago and is now displayed at Capt. Anderson's Restaurant in Panama City Beach, but a 16-ton solid cast bronze spare propeller, about 18 ft across, remains in a recessed section at the center of the wreck. Divers may see lobsters, nurse sharks, grouper, and moray eels among the debris, while large barracuda, amberjacks, and sharks - including sandbar, lemon, and bull sharks - are commonly seen in the water column above.
