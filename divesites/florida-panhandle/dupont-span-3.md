## DuPont Span #3

DuPont Span #3 is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

Originally a section of the DuPont Bridge (built 1927, a Parker Through Truss design typical of the period) that carried US Route 98 across the water connecting Panama City to Tyndall Air Force Base; also known as the Tyndall Bridge or Old East Bay Bridge. The bridge was replaced in 2008, and three of its spans were deployed as artificial reefs later that year. Span #3 was the last sunk, with an official deployment date of December 19, 2008, and remains the most upright and intact of the three (the other two, dropped nearby in late October and earlier December, have deteriorated more noticeably). The seafloor beneath the bridge bottoms out at 102 ft (31m) while the roadway sits slightly above at 100 ft (30.5m), making the full site more suitable to Advanced-certified divers - but the bridge beams forming the support trusses top out at 69 ft (21m), within reach of less experienced divers. The truss structure is heavily encrusted with soft corals, sponges, and macroalgae. Schools of grunts, particularly tomtates, are common throughout, with jacks and snapper cruising between the trusses, and grey triggerfish, Atlantic spadefish, and barracuda as regular visitors; Queen and French angelfish patrol the bridgeway in pairs while whitespotted soapfish watch from the railings.
