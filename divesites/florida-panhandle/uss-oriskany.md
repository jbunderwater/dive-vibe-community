## USS Oriskany

USS Oriskany is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A 911-foot Essex-class aircraft carrier, built at the Brooklyn Navy Yard, NY, launched 1944 but only 85% complete when WWII ended; refitted for the jet-fighter era and commissioned 1950. Served in the Korean War, then completed six tours in Vietnam (10 battle stars total), at one point carrying Navy pilot John McCain, the future US Senator and presidential candidate. Decommissioned 1976 due to budget cuts, sold for scrap in 1995 but reclaimed by the Navy in 1997; made available to Florida in 2004 for deployment as an artificial reef. Deliberately sunk on May 17, 2006 approximately 22.5 nautical miles southeast of Pensacola Pass with 26 explosive charges and $19 million in funding, becoming the marquee name on the Florida Panhandle Shipwreck Trail (launched 2012) and widely cited as the world's largest artificial reef — nicknamed "The Great Carrier Reef." Flight deck sits at roughly 44 m (145 ft) — outside recreational scuba limits — down to 65 m (212 ft) on the sandy seafloor; the conning tower ("the island"), shallowest at 24 m (80 ft), is the most accessible and most-explored part of the wreck. The hangar bay is accessible via the escalator at the base of the conning tower for experienced technical divers only. Large grouper, jacks, snapper, and amberjacks frequent the conning tower; sharks often patrol below the flight deck edge; whale sharks and manta rays occasionally cruise by. Florida Fish and Wildlife explicitly advises against unguided interior entry given the wreck's scale and depth — this is not a wreck where penetration should be treated as casually safe.
