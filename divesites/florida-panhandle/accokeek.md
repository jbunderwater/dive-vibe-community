## Accokeek

Accokeek is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

Originally an auxiliary ocean tugboat built for the US Navy in Orange, TX, commissioned in October 1944 as USS ATA-181. Her first major tour was with the Pacific Fleet, playing an active role in the assault and occupation of Okinawa in 1945, towing damaged warships to safety, and earning one battle star. She crossed back through the Panama Canal in 1946 to join the Atlantic Fleet, was renamed Accokeek in July 1948, and provided submarine and cruiser support for decades along the US Eastern Seaboard, with operations as far north as Labrador, Canada and into the Great Lakes. Decommissioned in 1972, she was later used by the Naval Diving and Salvage Training Center at Naval Coastal Systems Center, Panama City, for a Navy salvage and ordnance training program: first scuttled in February 1987, she was re-sunk and refloated multiple times over the next 13 years before finally going to the bottom for good on July 9, 2000, as an artificial reef. The wreck is in remarkably good condition; the highest point reaches 57 ft (17.5m) while the seabed sits at 100 ft (30.5m). Divers can explore three levels - main deck, upper deck, and wheelhouse - though the collapsed funnel limits access and penetration in that area. Fish life includes grunts sheltering in the interior, blue angelfish and cocoa damselfish foraging along the deck, Atlantic spadefish, amberjacks, snapper, large barracuda, and rainbow and blue runners; goliath grouper have also been spotted at this site.
