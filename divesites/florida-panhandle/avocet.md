## Avocet

Avocet is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

Avocet is a 247ft(76m) long clamshell dredger that sits on a sandy seafloor at a depth of 114ft(35m). Not much is known about the massive dredge except that she was originally built in 1943 and was sunk as an artificial reef in May 1991. At one point, her multi-level structure topped out at a depth of 60ft(18.5m), but the many decades spent underwater have collapsed her upper levels. The debris field that sits to the south of the wreck is all that remains of that upper structure. Despite her degraded state, Avocet is still considered one of the best dives in the area due to her size and depth. The bulk of her 55ft(16.5m) wide main deck remains largely intact, although a crack runs across her beam near the stern, up to 7ft(2m) wide near the port side and narrowing to just a few inches on the starboard side. The main deck sits at a depth of 107ft(32.5m) and sports a mix of debris, holes and twisted poles still anchored to it. The dredge's massive fore and aft holds are open and accessible to divers; they are deep and some contain debris that could constitute an entanglement risk. Divers are almost guaranteed to see plenty of jacks and snapper schooling around this deep, offshore wreck. Grey, cubera and lane snapper are common, while almaco jacks and greater amberjacks of impressive size are also regularly encountered. African pompanos and rainbow runners are also frequently seen in the waters above the artificial reef, while bull sharks occasionally patrol the area just off the wreck. This is also a popular spot for lionfish, with divers regularly collecting dozens of these invasive creatures on each dive.
