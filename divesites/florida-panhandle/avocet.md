## Avocet

Avocet is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

This 247-ft (76m) clamshell dredge, built in 1943, has been resting on the seafloor since May 1991. Details of her working life are scarce, but her decades underwater tell a story of their own: what was once a multi-level structure cresting at 60 ft (18.5m) has largely collapsed, leaving a debris field south of the main wreck. Even in that degraded state, her size and depth keep her ranked among the area's better dives. Most of her 55-ft-wide (16.5m) main deck is still intact, split by a crack near the stern that widens to 7 ft (2m) on the port side and pinches to a few inches on the starboard. The main deck sits at 107 ft (32.5m), littered with debris, holes, and twisted poles; her fore and aft holds are open and explorable but deep enough, and cluttered enough, to pose entanglement risk. Expect grey, cubera, and lane snapper in numbers, plus sizeable almaco jacks and amberjacks, African pompano, rainbow runners overhead, and the occasional bull shark patrolling nearby. The site is also popular with spearfishers targeting invasive lionfish.
