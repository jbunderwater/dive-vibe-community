## Jeff-A Hovercraft

Jeff-A Hovercraft is a historic wreck dive in Florida Panhandle, North America.

## Overview

A prototype Amphibious Assault Landing Craft (AALC) designed for the US Navy in 1979, built by Aerojet General Corporation of Sacramento, CA, one of two competing 1970s hovercraft prototypes (the other, Jeff-B, became the basis for today's Navy Landing Craft Air Cushion). Made of aluminum, 96 ft long. Sunk under the Florida Artificial Reef program in August 1995; despite weighing over 160 tons, she took two days to settle to the bottom. She now rests on sand and rubble at about 75 ft (23m), with the top of the structure at roughly 65 ft (20m). Her hatches were removed prior to sinking, so divers can explore the interior, though these spaces are confined and better suited to experienced divers; her skirt was left in place and has become an interesting spot for marine organisms to hide. The hull has little marine growth but still attracts plenty of fish and crustaceans, including toadfish commonly found tucked into recesses with just their heads showing.
