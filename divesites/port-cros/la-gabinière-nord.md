## La Gabinière Nord

La Gabinière Nord is a wall dive site at Port-Cros National Park, French Mediterranean coast.

## Overview

La Gabinière Nord marks a mooring/access point on the northern side of the La Gabinière islet, within Port-Cros National Park — Europe's oldest marine protected area, established in 1963. Note on sourcing: no dive operator or published source independently names or describes a "northern face" of La Gabinière as a distinct characterised dive — all available sources (Lavandou Plongée, Mio Palmo Plongée, World Adventure Divers, Tribloo) describe La Gabinière as a whole, structured around the eastern drop-off (Tombant Est), western slope (Tombant Ouest), and dark canyon (Calanque Sombre). This entry is therefore treated as an alternate approach to the same well-documented islet rather than a separately verified site, and the general Gabinière fauna and flora below are the confirmed, sourced facts for the islet as a whole rather than claims specific to a north-facing wall.

Divers can expect a rock face descending toward 40 metres, draped with the same Mediterranean life found around the rest of the islet: gorgonian sea fans (red and yellow species), sponge coverage, and yellow cluster anemones. Moray eels occupy crevices throughout. Shoals of seabream, wrasse, and bogue move along the wall. La Gabinière as a whole holds the densest brown grouper concentration recorded in the park — sources describe seeing around ten groupers in a single dive rather than a large fixed population figure — and a school of roughly 200 barracuda has patrolled the islet at 10–20m since summer 2001; these figures should not be read as specific to a "north wall" subset.

Access is by boat from Hyères, La Londe-les-Maures, or Le Lavandou dive centres. The northern wall is typically combined with other La Gabinière dive sites on multi-dive days, often serving as a second dive on the same mooring trip. Mooring buoys are available around the islet; anchoring is prohibited within the national park.

## Dive Profile

- **Entry**: Boat only
- **Depth range**: Surface to 40 m along the wall
- **Conditions**: Moderate to strong current typical of La Gabinière generally; no source-specific data distinguishing this approach from the rest of the islet
- **Visibility**: Mediterranean average; typically 15–25 m in summer
- **Best season**: May–October

## Marine Life

Brown groupers are present, consistent with the islet's overall status as the densest grouper concentration documented in the park (roughly ten seen per dive, per independent sources, rather than a specific "north wall" count). Moray and conger eels inhabit deeper crevices. Shoals of seabream, bogue, wrasse, and mullet cruise the mid-water column. Gorgonian fans (red and yellow), sponges, and anemones cover rock faces. Nudibranch diversity is notable for macro photographers. These are general La Gabinière observations applied to this access point — no source distinguishes fauna specific to the northern approach.
