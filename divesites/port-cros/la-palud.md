## La Palud

La Palud is a reef dive site in Port-Cros, Europe.

## Overview

La Palud is the most celebrated site in Port-Cros National Park — Europe's oldest marine park, established in 1963. The official park page confirms a free, unguided marked underwater trail (mask, fins and snorkel — no scuba required) departing from La Palud beach, open 15 June to 15 September, taking roughly 40 minutes and marked by six submerged buoys with explanatory panels; average depth along the marked trail itself does not exceed about 4 metres. Scuba divers exploring the wider bay beyond the marked trail reach depths up to 12 metres over rocky drop-offs and sandy banks.

Species are confirmed per habitat zone along the trail: the posidonia seagrass beds (from about 2m) hide sea urchins and pen shells (nacre) and host castagnoles, green wrasse, sars and saupes; dark crevices shelter encrusting anemones and sponges; shallow, sunlit rocky patches have wrasse, scorpionfish and starfish; and the deeper boulder scree — reached by divers going beyond the snorkelling trail — is where groupers, moray eels and octopus are found. Decades of strict protection have produced a noticeable "reserve effect," with fish approaching divers and snorkellers at close range. Port-Cros National Park as a whole is documented to host more than 180 fish species; that figure describes the park's overall biodiversity rather than a species count specific to La Palud.
