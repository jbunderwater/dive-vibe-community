## Trompeuse Rocks

Trompeuse Rocks is a submerged granite formation approximately 24 km northeast of Mahé, where strong and unpredictable currents sweep divers past thick coral growth and diverse reef fish.

## Overview

The Trompeuse Rocks protrude from the Indian Ocean approximately 24 km off Mahé's northeast coast. The site takes its name from the French word for "deceptive," reflecting the unpredictable nature of the currents here. Seychellen.com puts the rocks' underwater foundations at roughly 65 feet (20 m), extending to around 25 metres in places, and the rocks rise significantly above the waterline. Seyvillas and Seychellen.com both note that currents at this site can be strong, and diver awareness and experience with drift conditions is important.

**Correction from prior version**: this page previously credited "fusiliers and surgeonfish" as the site's regular marine life. Neither species could be re-confirmed for this specific site on re-audit — Equinoxe Diving School's current site listing does not mention Trompeuse Rocks at all, and Seyvillas' current Mahé guide notes only that "diverse underwater life" is present without naming species. Seychellen.com's dedicated page for the site instead reports large fish schools, tuna, and small sharks, and that description is used here in place of the unconfirmed one.

Because this site sits well offshore in open ocean, it is best visited when sea conditions are settled; Seyvillas notes it is not recommended outside the September–May window when swells can make the 24 km crossing challenging.
