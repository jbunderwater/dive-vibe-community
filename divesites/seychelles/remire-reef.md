## Remire Reef

Remire Reef surrounds a small, uninhabited atoll in the Amirantes group, approximately 240 km southwest of Victoria — one of the Seychelles' most remote and least-dived reef systems, accessible only by liveaboard.

## Overview

Remire Reef lies in the Outer Islands of the Seychelles, roughly 235–240 km southwest of Victoria (sources vary slightly) near the eastern edge of the Amirantes Ridge. Wikipedia describes it as a reef that dries in patches at low water and extends approximately 4.9 km south-southwest with a width of 2.2 km and a total area of about 9.5 km². The reef has no islets of its own; nearby Remire Island sits roughly 2.5 km to the southwest across a deep channel. Aldabra Expeditions notes that Remire Island was once worked as a coconut plantation and a source of guano, and was known historically as "Eagle Island" after a ship that visited in 1771 — though this is history of the adjacent island, not the reef itself. Aldabra Expeditions lists the Amirantes group's outer reefs generally as offering excellent diving, with "a hundred or more species of fish in a single dive," including angelfish, butterflyfish, lionfish, Napoleon wrasse, and parrotfish, plus larger pelagics such as sailfish, manta rays, and whale sharks in deeper water — but this is a group-wide description, not a report specific to Remire Reef.

The isolation of Remire from any permanent habitation and the very limited number of liveaboards that visit the Amirantes group means diver pressure here is extremely low. Re-audit confirms that no source located names species observed at Remire Reef itself; the destination-wide Amirantes species list above should not be read as a site-specific report. Conditions are consistent with the broader Amirantes reef ecosystem, but divers should expect variation from any single site's actual sightings.
