## Shark Bank

Shark Bank is one of the Seychelles' premier offshore pinnacles, a granite seamount roughly 7–8 km northwest of Mahé that reliably draws large pelagics including reef sharks, hammerheads, and seasonal whale sharks.

## Overview

Shark Bank rises from the deep ocean floor to top out at around 18–20 metres below the surface, with its flanks dropping to 30–35 metres (the surrounding sea floor itself is far deeper). The site sits in open water roughly 7–8 km northwest of Victoria Harbour, accessible only by boat. Equinoxe Diving School describes it as a "huge rock" lying between Mahé and Silhouette islands, and Blue Rides describes a "lunar-like landscape of smooth granite boulders interspersed with sand."

Shark species reported here vary by source rather than being a single settled picture: Blue Rides reports grey reef sharks patrolling the plateau edges and nurse sharks nestled in boulder crevices, while koek.sc separately describes nurse sharks as the near-certain sighting; PADI additionally lists hammerhead sharks as recorded at the site, though less consistently than the reef and nurse sharks. Large schools of yellow-striped snappers, jacks, batfish, and fusiliers fill the water column above the pinnacle, along with giant trevally and yellowfin tuna. Marble rays, bull rays (wingspans reported over 2 metres), eagle rays, stingrays, and turtles are also regularly seen. Whale sharks pass through seasonally — Blue Rides cites two windows, March–May and September–November, while other operators describe a broader August–November season; treat both as approximate rather than guaranteed. Experienced divers report the best conditions early in the morning before surface chop and current pick up. PADI recommends Advanced Open Water certification as the minimum level for this site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: ~7–8 km northwest of Victoria, Mahé, Seychelles
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
