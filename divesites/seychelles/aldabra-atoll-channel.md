## Aldabra Atoll Channel

Aldabra Atoll Channel is a tidal drift dive through one of the passes linking the Indian Ocean to the Aldabra lagoon — one of the world's most remote and ecologically intact marine environments, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1982.

## Overview

Aldabra is the world's second largest raised coral atoll, measuring roughly 34 km east-west by 13 km wide, with a total area (including its lagoon) of about 380 km² sitting within a 35,000-hectare protected special reserve; it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site on 19 November 1982. It is accessible only by liveaboard expedition. Aldabra has three main tidal passages connecting the lagoon to the open ocean, with Wikipedia recording tidal currents "of over 3 m/s at Passe Gionnet, and over 1 m/s at most other channels" — among the strongest tidal flows of any atoll pass, which is what drives the drift-diving experience here. Aldabra Expeditions describes dives as carefully timed to tidal cycles, with divers entering a main channel and drifting as the lagoon floods or empties, and reports visibility at nearby Assumption reaching 60 metres, reflecting the minimal human impact on this entire ecosystem.

The channel drift brings divers past dense schools of fusiliers and yellow snappers, with curious, territorial potato groupers also reported by Aldabra Expeditions. Hammerhead sharks, grey reef sharks, and silvertip sharks have been recorded passing through the channel. Wikipedia additionally documents Aldabra's waters as home to at least 13 cetacean species (including humpback whales, dolphins, and orcas) and confirms the atoll as an important breeding ground for hawksbill and green sea turtles — turtles are described by Aldabra Expeditions as a routine channel sighting ("countless sea turtles will lazily try to avoid us"). Manta rays and barracuda are also documented in Aldabra's waters generally per Wikipedia, though not confirmed specifically within the channel itself. Dives require careful current planning and Advanced certification at minimum; most operators require significant diving experience before taking guests to this site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Aldabra Atoll, Outer Islands, Seychelles (liveaboard only)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
