## Pain de Sucre

An easy, beginner-friendly reef near Boucan Canot beach, named for a rock formation on the shoreline and cut through with fissures, arches and small caves.

## Overview

Pain de Sucre sits just off Boucan Canot beach on Reunion's west coast, taking its name from a squared-off block of rock that broke from the cliff and now stands upright on the beach above the site. Underwater, the terrain is a reef platform worked by fissures, canyons, arches and small caves, giving way to white sand and scattered rocks. Sources generally agree on a 6–20 metre depth range for the main dive, though one dive-blog account describes a shallower 3–12 metre profile — likely reflecting a different entry point on the same platform. It's a straightforward, low-current site frequently used for beginner certification and discovery dives, and is also dived at night.

## Marine Life

Clownfish and anemones are consistently reported, along with resident sea turtles and large jacks (carangues) that pass through in numbers. Barracuda and Napoleon wrasse are occasionally seen but are described as uncommon here.

## Dive Profile

A shallow reef dive across the fissured platform, typically 6–20 metres, well suited to beginners and open-water certification dives.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access just offshore from Boucan Canot beach.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Boucan Canot beach, Saint-Paul area, Reunion, Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
