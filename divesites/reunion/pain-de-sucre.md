## Pain de Sucre

Pain de Sucre is a fractured reef plateau near Boucan Canot beach, named for the rectangular boulder that fell from the cliff and now sits on the sand above the site — a favourite for introductory dives thanks to its fissures, canyons, and small caves.

## Overview

Pain de Sucre lies just offshore of Boucan Canot beach on Réunion's west coast. The dive covers a broken-up reef plateau riddled with fissures, canyons, small caves, and arches, opening onto patches of white sand scattered with rocks of different sizes. Depth ranges from about 6 to 20 metres, and the easy profile makes it one of the island's most popular baptism (introductory dive) sites — operators note it also works well as a night dive.

## Marine Life

Jacks (carangues) are a signature sighting here, often moving through in groups, alongside sea turtles, clownfish and their host anemones, and barracuda. Napoleon wrasse are seen occasionally.

## Dive Profile

The dive is a shallow reef tour through the site's collapsed-plateau terrain — fissures, small caves, and arches — bottoming out around 20 metres, with much of the swim in the 6-15 metre range. The site is rated for beginners and is commonly used for first-time dives.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Boucan Canot beach, west Réunion
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
