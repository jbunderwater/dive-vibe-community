## Hai Siang

A Taiwanese fishing vessel deliberately sunk in the 1980s off Cap Homard, sitting flat on white sand at 54 metres — one of Reunion's two deep wrecks off Saint-Gilles, reserved for technical divers.

## Overview

The Hai Siang was a Taiwanese fishing/lobster boat (some sources translate the name loosely as "wild boar") deliberately sunk in the 1980s roughly 3km off Les Aigrettes, near Cap Homard on Reunion's west coast. Sources consistently report a length of approximately 49 metres; her build date and tonnage are not documented in any source found. The wreck rests flat on white sand at 54 metres, well preserved, with two masts still standing and three rear entrances that lead into the holds. Diving it requires Level 3 (technical) certification, a steep ~10-minute descent without a guide line, and awareness that currents at depth can be strong.

## Marine Life

Sources report schools of snapper, moray eels, barracuda, jacks, grouper, tuna and sea bream, along with flying scorpionfish and a large resident stingray. Divers are cautioned that stonefish and scorpionfish are present and should not be handled.

## Dive Profile

A deep technical dive: a fast vertical descent to 54 metres along the wreck's structure, with limited bottom time given the depth. Not a dive for anyone without deep/technical training.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry roughly 3km offshore from Les Aigrettes/Cap Homard.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Les Aigrettes, near Cap Homard, Saint-Gilles area, Reunion, Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 54 meters

## Safety

This is a deep, technical-level wreck dive with a fast descent and strong currents reported at depth. Level 3/technical certification is required by every source consulted. As with any wreck, interior spaces (the holds) should only be entered with proper wreck-penetration training and equipment — it is never routine or without risk.
