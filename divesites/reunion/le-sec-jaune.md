## Le Sec Jaune

An isolated rock pinnacle off Saint-Leu, its shallow crown blanketed in flamboyant yellow soft coral — tribloo.com's own English listing names it "the Yellow Pinnacle."

## Overview

Le Sec Jaune ("the yellow shoal") is a rock formation rising to the surface off Saint-Leu, facing the protected Pointe au Sel area. Its submerged section is covered in vivid yellow soft coral, giving the site its name — and its structure as an isolated rising rock is why this entry is reclassified here from reef to pinnacle, matching how tribloo.com's own English-language listing titles it, "The Yellow Pinnacle." A coral plateau begins around 5 metres and levels out near 10 metres before overlooking a drop-off; sources disagree on how deep that drop-off runs, with figures from 20 to 40 metres reported (a personal dive-log account specifically recorded a 26-metre wall base). Less experienced (Level 1) divers typically stay on the shallower plateau close to shore, while more advanced divers head further out to two caves at the base of the wall.

## Marine Life

Turtles, eagle rays and stingrays are commonly reported, along with clownfish, scorpionfish and blue surgeonfish on the plateau. One detailed dive-log account also documents giant moray eels (up to 2.4m), saddleback grouper over a metre long, cleaner shrimp, and specific nudibranch species on the wall face. Yellow gorgonians, soft corals and encrusting algae cover the rock itself.

## Dive Profile

From the shallow (5–10m) coral-covered crown, Level 1 divers work the near plateau while more experienced divers descend the wall toward two caves at its base. Described by one source as an easy dive of about 60 minutes.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry roughly 10 minutes out from Saint-Leu.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Saint-Leu, facing Pointe au Sel, Reunion, Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
