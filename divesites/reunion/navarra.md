## Navarra

A fishing vessel scuttled by Reunion's diving federation in 1996 to seed an artificial reef off Saint-Gilles, now broken into two sections after storm damage and colonized by pelagic fish.

## Overview

The Navarra was a fishing vessel, abandoned by its owner and seized by the French Navy, that the local branch of the FFESSM (French diving federation) had scuttled off the port of Saint-Gilles in early 1996 specifically to create an artificial reef and dive site. Sources agree on a length of 47 metres; her build date and tonnage are not documented in any source found. A later cyclonic swell broke the hull into two sections, separated by roughly 10 metres. Reported depth for the wreck varies by source — figures of 50 and 55 metres both appear — so treat 50–55 metres as the working range rather than a single confirmed number. Diving here requires Level 3 certification or deep-specialty training.

## Marine Life

Sources report small groupers, filefish, snappers, leopard moray eels, octopus and electric rays, along with large schools of pelagic fish that have colonized the broken structure.

## Dive Profile

A deep wreck dive across two separated hull sections at 50+ metres, requiring deep/technical training and careful gas planning given the depth.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry off the port of Saint-Gilles, near the Hai Siang site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Pointe des Aigrettes, near Saint-Gilles port, Reunion, Africa
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 53 meters

## Safety

A deep wreck requiring Level 3/deep-specialty certification per every source consulted. The hull is broken into two separated sections; divers should not attempt interior penetration without specific wreck-penetration training and equipment.
