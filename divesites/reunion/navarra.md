## Navarra

Navarra is a 47-metre fishing vessel scuttled off Saint-Gilles in early 1996 by Réunion's diving federation to create an artificial reef, now lying in two pieces at 40-53 metres.

## Overview

The Navarra was a fishing vessel abandoned by its owner and seized by the French Navy. The regional delegation of the FFESSM (Fédération Française d'Études et de Sports Sous-Marins) proposed giving it a "second life" as a dive site, and it was deliberately sunk off Pointe des Aigrettes near Saint-Gilles port in early 1996, at a depth of just over 50 metres. This account is corroborated independently by a local dive shop's site page and a 2012 dive report from a second operator, both giving the vessel's length as 47 metres and describing the same sinking circumstances — a level of agreement across independent sources that the Hai Siang and Antonio Lorenzo histories on this site could not fully match.

A cyclonic swell later broke the wreck into two sections roughly ten metres apart; the bow is reported to be oriented west. No Wikipedia article exists for this specific vessel (a Wikipedia search for "Navarra" surfaces only the unrelated Spanish cruiser Navarra of 1923). No tonnage figure was found in any source and none is stated here.

## Dive Profile

One of two deep wrecks accessible off Saint-Gilles alongside Hai Siang, requiring Level 3 certification. The wreck sits at 40-53 metres in two separated sections.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Pointe des Aigrettes, near Saint-Gilles port, Réunion
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 53 meters

## Safety

A deep wreck requiring Level 3 / advanced certification. As with any wreck, entering the structure requires specific training and equipment and carries inherent risk regardless of experience level.
