## Daharat Abida

Daharat Abida (also Dahrat Abid) is a remote island on Sudan's southern reef chain, on a pillar that drops steeply into deep water.

## Overview

Dive The World describes Dahrat Abid as the southernmost reef, a 400 m island about 220 km south of Port Sudan, with three wrecks on its northwest side, steep drop-offs, large gorgonians on the wall and black coral dominating below 30 m. Divernet describes it as a sun-blasted patch of sand on top of a pillar that drops sheer into hundreds of metres of water, with a small submerged plateau and lush soft corals. Blue Force Fleet calls it the southernmost island of Sudanese waters and mentions the remains of a single wreck on top of the reef. Sources therefore disagree on the number of wrecks (three versus one). Earlier claims about specific plateau depths (22-40 m), the distance to the Eritrean border and "silky sharks" could not be matched to the sources re-checked on 2026-10-09 and have been removed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Southernmost Sudanese Red Sea, 220km south of Port Sudan, ~30km from the Eritrean border
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Expert
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

Dive The World lists whitetip, silvertip, grey reef and hammerhead sharks (sometimes schools of over 100), jacks, great barracuda, tuna and amberjacks. Blue Force Fleet names pelagic fish and sharks as the main attraction in deeper water.
