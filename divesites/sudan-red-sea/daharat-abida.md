## Daharat Abida

Daharat Abida (Dahrat Abid) is a remote island pillar in the far south of Sudan's Red Sea, known for sharks and strong current.

## Overview

Dive the World describes a 400 m island, home only to birds, about 220 km south of Port Sudan, with three unnamed wrecks on its northwest side. Divernet describes the top of a pillar that drops sheer into many hundreds of metres, with a small submerged plateau swept by current and lush soft corals. Dive the World reports large black coral bushes below 30 m on the southwest plateau and a northeast plateau whose outer wall drops into deep water. A Scuba Diving feature describes a wall dropping from about 35 m (115 ft). Access is by liveaboard.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Far south Sudan Red Sea, about 220 km south of Port Sudan
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Expert
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

Dive the World lists whitetip, silvertip, grey reef and hammerhead sharks (sometimes schools of over 100), jacks, great barracuda, tuna and amberjack. A Scuba Diving feature also reports around 100 hammerheads passing in one encounter.

## Safety Considerations

Dive within your certification limits, use a surface marker buoy, and confirm current conditions and official travel advisories with your liveaboard operator before booking.
