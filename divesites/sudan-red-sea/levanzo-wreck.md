## Levanzo Wreck

The Levanzo Wreck (SS Isola di Levanzo) is an Italian cargo steamer lost on Elba Reef in 1923. She lies upside down on the south plateau and drops to technical depths.

## Overview

Two independent sources (Wannadive and dive3d.eu) report that the ship ran aground on Elba Reef on 14 March 1923 while sailing from Genoa to Durban with salt and general cargo.

**Details reported by a single source (Wannadive).** Built by Fratelli Orlando, Livorno, launched 1901, 339.6 ft (about 103.5 m), 3,713 gross tons, with a salvage attempt in which she broke a towing hawser, drifted onto rocks and sank on 28 March 1923. These items were not corroborated by a second source in this review. Other sources differ: Red Sea Diving describes the ship as about 113 m long, and one catalog search result lists 3,875 grt. A Wikipedia shipwreck list records a vessel named Levanzo running aground 'off Elba' on 16 March 1923 and sinking on 3 April but does not mention the Red Sea, so it is not treated as confirmation of the same event.

**Wreck.** Royal Evolution and dive3d.eu both describe the wreck as upside down. Depths are reported inconsistently: the stern and propeller at about 22 m (Royal Evolution) or about 27 m (dive3d.eu); a broken section around 40 m; and the bow sloping to about 65-70 m (Royal Evolution, dive3d.eu), with Wannadive listing a site maximum of 82 m. Penetration is described as difficult, though diving under the hull is possible in places. Below about 40 m this is technical diving.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Elba Reef south plateau, Egypt-Sudan border
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 65 meters
- **Wreck Top**: 22-27 meters

## Marine Life

Reported at the wreck: jackfish and barracuda (Sadko Safari Fleet), large groupers and schooling trevally (Royal Evolution), and moray eels (Red Sea Diving, Sadko Safari Fleet). Soft and black corals cover the hull.

## Wreck Notes

Wreck penetration requires appropriate training and equipment and is never risk-free. Depths beyond 40 m are technical diving.

## Safety Considerations

Dive within your certification limits, use a surface marker buoy, and confirm current conditions and official travel advisories with your liveaboard operator before booking.
