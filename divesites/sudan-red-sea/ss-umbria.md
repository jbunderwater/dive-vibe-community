## SS Umbria

SS Umbria is a WWII cargo ship scuttled by her own captain in June 1940 at Wingate Reef near Port Sudan, and is regarded as one of the finest wreck dives in the world.

## Overview

SS Umbria was built in Hamburg as the cargo liner Bahia Blanca (154.9 m, 10,076 GRT per Wikipedia; DAN gives about 506 ft and 10,000 gross tons). Sources disagree on the build year: Wikipedia gives a launch on 30 December 1911 and service from 1912, DAN says 1911, and PADI says 1912. Italy later acquired and renamed her. In June 1940, carrying war supplies bound for Italian-held East Africa, she was stopped by British warships near Wingate Reef off Port Sudan (Wikipedia dates this to 9 June 1940). Captain Lorenzo Muiesan and his crew scuttled her rather than let the cargo be seized; Wikipedia gives 9 June for the scuttling, while DAN gives no exact date. She lies on her port side. Reported cargo includes about 6,000 tons of bombs, 600 cases of detonators, 100 tons of weapons, over 2,000 tons of cement or construction materials, and three Fiat 1100 cars. Depth figures differ by source: Wikipedia gives a maximum of 38 m, DAN puts the stern at about 35 ft (11 m) and the bow at about 125 ft (38 m), and PADI lists 5-35 m. PADI describes the engine room, holds and bakery as accessible; penetration requires wreck training and appropriate equipment.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Wingate Reef, near Port Sudan, Sudan Red Sea
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 38 meters

## Marine Life

DAN reports the superstructure and gantries covered in stony corals, soft corals and sponges, with twoband anemonefish living in an anemone on the anchor chain. PADI reports snapper and featherstars under the large rudder and cleaner shrimp along a gangway near the collapsed funnel. No other site-specific species reports were verified, so none are listed.

## Wreck History Notes

Name, length and tonnage match between Wikipedia and DAN to within rounding. The build year (1911 vs 1912) and exact scuttling day are unresolved and stated as such above. Wreck penetration is never "safe" and requires proper training and equipment.
