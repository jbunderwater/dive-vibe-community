## SS Umbria

SS Umbria is an Italian cargo ship scuttled by her captain in June 1940 off Wingate Reef near Port Sudan. She lies on her port side and is widely described as one of the best wreck dives in the Red Sea.

## Overview

Wikipedia records the ship as built in Hamburg and launched on 30 December 1911 as Bahia Blanca (entering service in 1912), then bought by the Italian government in 1935 and renamed Umbria; a second source also dates the 1935 change of name. Dive sources variously give the build year as 1911 or 1912.

**Dimensions and tonnage (sources differ).** Wikipedia gives a length of 154.9 m, a beam of 18.1 m and 10,076 GRT. Other sources report a length of about 153 m, and dive operators round it to about 150 m. The 10,076 GRT figure appears only in Wikipedia among the sources reviewed; two other sources give a cargo capacity of about 9,000 tons.

**Loss.** Sources agree that on the outbreak of Italy's entry into the war in June 1940 the ship was at anchor near Port Sudan and that Captain Lorenzo Muiesan scuttled her to keep the cargo from British hands. Wikipedia states British warships (HMS Grimsby and HMS Leander) forced her to anchor at Wingate Reef. The date is given as 9 June 1940 by Wikipedia and 10 June 1940 by two other sources.

**Cargo (sources differ).** Wikipedia lists about 6,000 tons of bombs, 600 cases of detonators, 100 tons of assorted weapons, over 2,000 tons of cement and three Fiat 1100 cars. Other sources report roughly 350,000 to 360,000 bombs (one of them equates this to about 6,000 tonnes), 60 boxes of detonators, a total cargo of about 8,600 tons, and bottles of wine. The quantities should be treated as approximate.

**Wreck.** She lies on her port side. Reported maximum depth ranges from about 33 to 38 m depending on the source (Wikipedia and Dive the World give 38 m), with the shallowest parts reported at about 5 m. One operator describes easy penetration to the engine room, holds and bakery, a swim-through beneath the rudder and a collapsed funnel; penetration requires proper training and equipment and is never risk-free.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Wingate Reef, near Port Sudan
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 38 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-15 meters reported by one source

## Marine Life

Reported around the wreck: snappers, butterflyfish, barracuda and sweetlips (Blue Force Fleet, Dive the World), tiny cleaner shrimps on the gangways near the funnel, and featherstars near the rudder swim-through (PADI Travel).

## Safety Considerations

Dive within your certification limits, use a surface marker buoy, and confirm current conditions and official travel advisories with your liveaboard operator before booking.
