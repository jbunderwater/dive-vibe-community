## Sepok Wall

Sepok Wall is a wall dive site in Philippines Anilao, Asia.

## Overview

Sepok Wall runs along the northwest face of Maricaban Island, where a vertical drop from the reef crest at 5 meters to around 27 meters (one source describes a deeper section reaching approximately 40 meters — treat depths beyond 27m as unconfirmed/technical) is covered in barrel sponges and large gorgonian fans. Frogfish, leaf fish, twin-spot lionfish, and scorpionfish hide among the encrusted surfaces, while coral-covered bommies on the sandy floor offer complementary wide-angle compositions. Anilao is broadly celebrated for nudibranch diversity, but no currently accessible source specifically documents nudibranchs at Sepok Wall itself — that claim has been removed pending site-specific confirmation.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Philippines Anilao, Asia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters

## Marine Life

Sepok Wall's wall face is densely covered in soft corals (approximately 80% coverage per anilaodiving.com), gorgonian fans, and barrel sponges. Specific species confirmed at this site include frogfish (small), leaf fish, twin-spot lionfish, scorpionfish, and anemonefish. Trevally and barracuda passes are also reported at this exposed site. The coral-covered bommies at the sandy base provide wide-angle composition opportunities. Note: sources disagree on whether reef sharks occur here — one source describes "occasional sharks" on a deeper section of the wall, while the primary site description does not mention sharks at all; this is flagged as unconfirmed rather than stated as fact. Nudibranchs are not specifically confirmed for this site in currently accessible sources — Anilao broadly is known for exceptional nudibranch diversity, but that regional reputation should not be read as a site-specific confirmation here. Pygmy seahorses in sea fans are reported for Anilao wall sites generally but were not site-specifically documented here.
