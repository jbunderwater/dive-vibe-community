## Kirby's Rock

Kirby's Rock is two pinnacles off Tingloy Island with a current-swept canyon between them and a wall that plunges from 5 meters to over 80 meters — one of Anilao's few technical-leaning sites, prized for both macro critters and passing pelagics.

## Overview

Kirby's Rock sits off Tingloy Island in the Verde Island Passage. The site comprises two pinnacles separated by a canyon, with a wall face that starts around 5 meters and drops well past the recreational 30-meter limit to 80 meters or more — Blue Ribbon Divers, the closest local operator, describes it as "a mix of everything! Coral, Macro, Tech!" Currents are a defining feature of the dive and can shift direction mid-dive or vary between the two pinnacles, so this is not a beginner site. Multiple moray eels occupy crevices in the wall, and the exposure to open water brings in occasional pelagic visitors along with the site's better-known macro subjects.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Tingloy Island, Anilao, Batangas, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–20 meters
- **Current**: Strong and variable — can shift direction mid-dive
- **Best Time**: November to June (dry season); best at slack water

## Marine Life

Kirby's Rock's two pinnacles and connecting canyon are heavily adorned with hard and soft corals, gorgonians, crinoids, and sea fans. Golden pygmy seahorses on gorgonian branches have been documented, and Blue Ribbon Divers separately confirms pygmy seahorses at the site. Thousands of Colochirus robustus sea cucumbers are a distinctive feature, confirmed independently by both Blue Ribbon Divers and Scuba Diver Life (whose author initially mistook the dense sea-cucumber-covered rock for a bed of nudibranchs). Multiple moray eels occupy crevices — Scuba Diver Life notes several hiding in holes plus one free-swimming individual. Dragon shrimp are present on whip corals. Nudibranchs, scorpionfish, and lionfish inhabit the encrusted surfaces. Frogfish (including a large purple specimen per Scuba Diver Life) are confirmed at the site. Black corals are present at depth. The wall's exposure to the Verde Island Passage brings occasional pelagics through the canyon.
