## The Mandalay

The Mandalay is a historic wreck dive in Florida Keys, North America.

## Overview

The Mandalay is the best snorkeling shipwreck in Biscayne National Park, located in the eastern part of the park near Elliott Key. This shallow wreck lies in 12 feet of water, making it accessible for both beginning divers and snorkelers. The site is surrounded by soft corals and colorful reef fish, creating an excellent ecosystem for exploration.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Florida Keys, North America
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 3.6 meters

## Wreck History

The Mandalay was a 110-foot-6-inch steel-hulled auxiliary schooner built in 1928 by George Lawley & Son Corporation for $177,000, designed by John G. Alden Naval Architects for original owner Dr. Henry D. Lloyd of Brookline, Massachusetts, and originally named Hardi Biou. She was sold and renamed Valor in 1931, passing through five subsequent owners under that name before Michael Burke of Windjammer Cruises Inc. purchased, refitted, and renamed her Mandalay in 1965 for use as a "barefoot" luxury cruise ship outfitted in mahogany, brass, ivory, and teak. On the night of December 31, 1965 / early January 1, 1966, returning from a Bahamian cruise to Miami with 23 passengers and 12 crew, the 26-year-old Norwegian captain miscalculated the distance from Fowey Rocks; the schooner ran aground on Long Reef roughly 20 miles off her intended course. An SOS at 3:45 AM brought Coast Guard helicopters and patrol boats, and all aboard were rescued (one minor injury during the rescue). Before salvage tugs could arrive, the abandoned wreck was "picked to her skin and bones" by opportunistic local boaters — lead ballast blocks were stripped and resold as diving weights at $1/pound, and the masts were removed under contract for re-use elsewhere. The wreck remains embedded on Long Reef as one of six sites on Biscayne National Park's Maritime Heritage Trail.

## Marine Life

Florida Rambler's snorkeling account of this specific wreck reports trumpetfish, blue tang, four species of parrotfish, smooth trunkfish, and sergeant major schools around the hull frames, along with purple sea fans (per NPS) growing on the structure. This is a shallow, sunlit site well suited to spotting reef fish without deep-diving skills.

## Dive Profile

What remains of the Mandalay rests in very shallow water on Long Reef in the eastern portion of Biscayne National Park — approximately 12 feet, described by Florida Rambler as "aquarium-clear." NPS describes the wreck as a "skeleton" embedded on the reef and rates it "one of the best shallow dive spots in the park" as well as an outstanding snorkeling wreck. The shallow profile allows extended bottom time and surface-light photography across the hull frames.

## Entry and Exit

The Mandalay is reachable only by boat through Biscayne National Park, which maintains a Maritime Heritage Trail mooring at the site. Tie up to the mooring; no anchoring on the reef is permitted within the park.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The wreck is shallow enough for free-diving and snorkeling and is often combined into a single trip with the nearby Lugano (a few hundred meters north on the same Long Reef line), which is better suited to scuba.
- NPS publishes a waterproof site guide for the Maritime Heritage Trail interpreting visible features at each wreck.
- Biscayne National Park rules prohibit removal or disturbance of any artifact — a federal offense.

## Safety Considerations

- Sun exposure is intense at this exposed offshore reef; bring water and reef-safe sun protection for surface time.
- Exposed steel hull frames and remnants are sharp and degraded after 60 years of storms; avoid contact and watch fin placement.
- Long Reef sits in open water with boat traffic; deploy a dive flag and surface within sight of the dive vessel.
- Fire coral and stinging hydroids commonly encrust shallow Keys wrecks; maintain neutral buoyancy.
