## Wisteria

Wisteria is a wreck dive site in the Florida Keys.

## Overview

Wisteria Island (also known locally as Christmas Tree Island) is a small uninhabited federal island created by US Navy dredging of Key West harbor in the late 1890s and early 1900s, located about 645 yards northwest of Key West. The island takes its name from the US Public Health Service disinfecting steamer Wisteria — formerly a US Lighthouse Service light tender — which sank while moored at the then-unnamed spoil island in 1925 and burned to the waterline; the wreck was eventually salvaged. Wikipedia and Key West Weekly (Keys Weekly) both confirm this naming history independently, but neither source, nor any dive operator listing, describes what (if anything) remains of the Wisteria today, its depth, or its condition as a dive site. No dive operator currently lists it in their standard rotation; any visit should be coordinated directly with a Key West dive shop.

## What Is Documented — and What Isn't

Two independent sources (Wikipedia and Keys Weekly) confirm the vessel's identity and general fate: a US Public Health Service disinfecting steamer named Wisteria, previously a US Lighthouse Service tender, sank at moorings here in 1925, burned to the waterline, and was eventually salvaged. Neither the vessel's build date, length, tonnage, nor a third independent source has been located, so this record is **not** marked validated. No source reviewed describes the wreck's current condition, remaining structure, marine life, typical dive profile, currents, visibility, penetration potential, or photography conditions. Divers interested in this site should contact Key West dive operators directly to verify access and conditions before planning a dive.

## General Safety

Standard Florida Keys wreck-diving precautions apply: dive within your certification limits, do not attempt wreck penetration without proper training and equipment, monitor air and depth, and confirm conditions and access with a local operator before diving.
