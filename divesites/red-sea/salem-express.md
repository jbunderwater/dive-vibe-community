## Salem Express

The Salem Express is one of the most emotionally significant and historically important wreck dives in the Red Sea. The ro-ro passenger ferry struck Hyndman Reef near Safaga on the night of 14–15 December 1991, sinking within roughly 10–20 minutes while en route from Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, to Safaga, Egypt, after the captain took an unauthorized shortcut during stormy weather. The official death toll is 470, calculated by subtracting survivors (approximately 180) from the ship's licensed capacity of 1,256 rather than from an actual passenger count; the Egyptian Navy's salvage operation recovered roughly 850 bodies, and some accounts estimate the true toll may have exceeded 1,000. The majority of those aboard were Moroccan-French pilgrims returning from Mecca. Depth is reported inconsistently across sources — dive operator dive-the-world.com cites a maximum of 27 metres with a shallow point at 7 metres, while Wikipedia and other aggregated dive-site data put the bow as deep as 29–32 metres with the shallow port side around 10 metres; this description uses 30m as a representative maximum. The ship measured 115 metres in length and 4,771 gross tons.

## Overview

The Salem Express rests on her starboard side on a sandy slope adjacent to Hyndman Reef. She is relatively intact, which makes for a haunting and atmospheric dive. Coral growth has been colonizing the hull steadily since 1991, and the wreck now hosts a significant reef ecosystem. Many divers treat this site with particular solemnity given the loss of life associated with the sinking. Penetration of the interior is possible but should be undertaken only by wreck-qualified divers — the interior is silty, and the wreck still contains the personal effects of those who perished. Many operators and dive guides ask that visitors refrain from penetrating the passenger areas out of respect for the dead.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Hyndman Reef, Safaga, Red Sea, Egypt
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters (sources range 27–32m; see Overview)
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters (50-100 feet)
- **Current**: Moderate
- **Best Time**: October to May

## Marine Life

Reports on the Salem Express's coral and fish life vary — dive-the-world.com describes the wreck as having "limited biological attractions" with coral growth remaining sparse decades after the sinking, while other operator sources describe a more established artificial-reef community. Sources that discuss specific species agree on: large numbers of pipefish sheltering throughout the structure, schools of parrotfish and butterflyfish grazing off the hull, glassfish gathering in the deck openings, and lionfish patrolling shaded areas. Soft coral growth is present but reported as patchy rather than dense. Species such as moray eels, groupers, turtles, and dolphins that appeared in an earlier version of this page were not confirmed by any source consulted for this site specifically and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

Descend the mooring line to the upper hull at approximately 15 meters. From here, work along the exterior — the bow, bridge section, stern ramp, and lifeboat davits are all distinctive features. The deepest accessible point is around 27 meters at the sandy bottom beside the starboard side. Penetration divers with wreck certification can enter the vehicle deck through the stern ramp. Bottom time is limited at depth; plan a conservative dive with plenty of time for a safety stop and the ascent.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by liveaboard or day boat from Safaga harbor. A mooring buoy is typically attached to the wreck. Entry via giant stride. Descend directly to the wreck on the mooring line. Ascend on the line or do a blue-water ascent deploying an SMB at 5 meters.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Treat this as a memorial dive — avoid disturbing the interior passenger areas
- Wreck penetration requires proper training and a powerful dive torch
- Silt inside the wreck disturbs easily; maintain excellent buoyancy control
- Book with an operator who provides a proper briefing on the site's history and safety protocols
- An SMB and dive computer are essential for this site

## Safety Considerations

Interior penetration is forbidden on the Salem Express except in the cargo/vehicle area, per operator guidance (dive-the-world.com) — the passenger accommodation areas contain human remains and personal effects and the wreck is treated as a maritime tomb; do not enter these spaces regardless of training level. Where cargo-area penetration is permitted by an operator, it carries the usual overhead-environment risks — silt, disorientation, and entanglement — and should only be attempted by trained divers with proper equipment; penetration is never "safe," only manageable with the right training. Strong currents can develop near the reef edge. The stern ramp has sharp metal edges. Always ascend on the mooring line or deploy an SMB if ascending in open water. This is an emotionally demanding dive for many divers; be prepared.

## Photography

The scale of the wreck makes wide-angle and fisheye lenses ideal. The encrusted railings and davits photographed against blue water are iconic images. The schools of glassfish hovering over open deck areas are a compelling mid-water subject. Inside the vehicle deck, a wide-angle torch-lit shot can capture the otherworldly atmosphere. Macro subjects are found on every colonized surface of the hull.
