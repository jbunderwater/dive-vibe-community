## Blue Belt

Blue Belt is a wreck dive site near Port Sudan, Sudan — also known as the "Toyota Wreck" for its cargo of Toyota vehicles (reported as approximately 181 cars plus six trucks and trailers by some sources, approximately 190 vehicles by others). The Blue Belt was a Saudi Arabian general cargo vessel of 2,399 GRT and 103 metres in length, built by Howaldtswerke in Hamburg in 1950. On 2 December 1977 she struck a reef approximately 75 km north of Port Sudan while sailing from Jeddah to Port Sudan, sinking in poor winter weather. The Blue Belt was not a Sudanese vessel — she was Saudi-registered and her cargo of Toyota cars, trucks, and 4x4 vehicles can still be seen scattered around the wreck, now coral-encrusted after nearly five decades on the seabed.

## Overview

**Depth is a genuinely disputed point across sources and should be confirmed with your operator before diving.** The wreck originally lay upside down from approximately 20 to 36 metres. Multiple dive-industry sources report that in 2013 the hull slipped further down the reef slope, and that continuing instability has since carried it to depths beyond 60 metres, with at least one source stating the site may no longer be permitted for recreational diving in its current state. Other, more recent operator listings continue to describe an active dive from roughly 21 to 90 metres, with most recreational diving in the 21–40 metre range. Given this conflict, treat any depth figure for Blue Belt as provisional and verify current conditions and permitted status locally. The wreck (where diveable) is heavily encrusted with hard and soft corals, and scattered coral-encrusted Toyota vehicles lie outside the hull. A resident school of barracuda has been reported spiraling around the wreck in several source accounts. Visibility is reported as excellent at 25–40 metres.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Red Sea, Sudan (~75 km north of Port Sudan)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 90 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 25–40 meters (82–130 feet)
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: November to April

## Marine Life

The resident barracuda school is the headline attraction — often 300–500 individuals forming a continuously rotating tornado above the wreck. Lionfish inhabit the holds and sheltered spaces. Moray eels occupy the interior corridors. humphead wrasse and large grouper are regularly sighted. The exterior coral encrustation hosts anthias, butterflyfish, and chromis in large numbers. Eagle rays have been sighted in the blue water surrounding the wreck.

## Dive Profile

Descend to 21–30 metres where the upturned hull and scattered Toyota vehicles are the main features. The coral-encrusted cars and trucks are immediately recognisable — headlights, tyres, and steering wheels are still visible after nearly five decades underwater. Mid-dive, position yourself below the barracuda school and watch them spiral overhead. The shallower sections of the hull around 12–18 metres provide the most photogenic views of the barracuda against blue water. Safety stop at 5 metres before surfacing. The deeper sections of the wreck (beyond 40 m) are for experienced technical divers only.

## Entry and Exit

Remote site accessible by liveaboard from Port Sudan. Zodiac or tender boat transfers to the dive site. Blue water entry with descent to the wreck. Open water SMB deployment for pick-up is standard.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Approach the barracuda school slowly from below for the best circling effect
- Dawn and dusk dives see the most active fish feeding behaviour
- Wide-angle fisheye lenses are essential for the barracuda school shots
- Bring a torch for wreck interior inspection
- This is a liveaboard-only destination — plan Sudan itineraries carefully

## Safety Considerations

Sudan diving requires full liveaboard logistics and experienced guides. The wreck interior should only be entered with wreck penetration certification. Lionfish are present throughout the structure. Strong currents are possible in the open water around the site. Carry all appropriate signalling devices.

## Photography

Blue Belt offers some of the Red Sea's most dramatic wildlife photography. The barracuda tornado against the blue water background is an iconic subject. Wide-angle shots from beneath the school looking up toward the surface create stunning compositions. Wreck interior photography with torch-illuminated soft coral details provides atmospheric close-up work.
