## Alternatives

Alternatives is a sheltered pinnacle field just west of Ras Mohammed, Sinai Peninsula, Egypt, built around "Stingray Station" — a cleaning station where stingrays are reliably seen at close range — and shallow enough to suit divers and snorkellers of all levels.

## Overview

The site takes its name from its role as a calm fallback when the exposed headlands of Ras Mohammed National Park are unsuitable, but it is a distinctive dive in its own right rather than a lesser substitute. Alternatives consists of a series of coral-covered pinnacles ("ergs") rising from a reef plateau that extends for over a kilometre and sits only around 12 metres deep. The first and best-known pinnacle, Stingray Station, hosts a cleaning station where various stingray species are regularly seen at close range. Depth across the site runs roughly 10–15 metres, making it accessible to beginners and snorkellers as well as more experienced divers. Visibility is generally good but can drop sharply in rough weather or with southerly currents.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West of Ras Mohammed, Sinai Peninsula, Red Sea, Egypt
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Good in calm conditions; can drop sharply in rough weather or southerly current
- **Current**: Light to moderate; rough seas or southerly currents can significantly reduce visibility
- **Best Time**: September to May

## Marine Life

The signature attraction is the cleaning station at Stingray Station, where various stingray species can be observed at close range. Moray eels shelter in the reef crevices, and large groupers are also reported. Colourful shrimp and abundant reef fish are present throughout. Earlier claims of white-tip reef sharks patrolling the sandy channels and regular turtle sightings were not confirmed by the sources consulted for this site and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

The site is made up of a series of coral-covered pinnacles rising from a plateau around 12 metres deep, with the overall site running 10–15 metres. Stingray Station, the first and most notable pinnacle, is the highlight of the dive and where most bottom time should be spent. The shallow depth and gentle profile make this a comfortable, low-stress dive well suited to less experienced divers.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from Sharm el-Sheikh or Naama Bay operators. Standard giant stride or back roll entry. The site is typically calm enough for uncomplicated exits. Deploy an SMB on ascent for boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A good choice for beginners, snorkellers, or refresher dives when bigger sites feel overwhelming
- Spend the bulk of your time at Stingray Station for the best chance of a close stingray encounter
- Morning light provides the best visibility and reef colour
- Check with your operator for the latest conditions — visibility can drop quickly in rough or current-swept weather

## Safety Considerations

Conditions are generally benign, but currents can pick up along the reef edge, particularly at tidal change. Maintain buoyancy awareness over shallow corals to avoid contact damage. Always carry an SMB.

## Photography

The gentle slope and good ambient light make this an ideal wide-angle reef photography site. Macro subjects including nudibranchs, cleaner shrimp, and small gobies are common on the hard coral formations.
