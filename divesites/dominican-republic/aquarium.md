# Aquarium

*Bayahibe's most popular beginner site, named for the exceptional density and variety of Caribbean fish life in calm, clear shallow water.*

## Overview

Aquarium is a shallow fringing reef site operated from the Bayahibe/La Romana dive base, named for the sheer density and variety of Caribbean reef fish that populate it. At depths of 5–12 metres over a mixed reef-and-sand bottom, the site is accessible, calm, and consistently impressive for first-time divers and photographers. It pairs naturally with The Wall for a classic two-tank morning — The Wall done first at greater depth, followed by Aquarium as the shallower second dive. Visibility is typically 20–28 metres. Water stays at 27–29°C most of the year.

**Note on site identity:** Coral Point Diving and Dressel Divers, two of the largest operators in the area, both describe "The Wall" and "The Aquarium" as the paired dive sites on the east (wall) and west (shallow reef/aquarium) sides of Isla Catalina specifically — distinct from the separate "Catalina Wall" entry in this database, which cites a similar pairing. Reef Oasis Dive Club's site list, by contrast, lists an "Acuario" site closer to the Bayahibe mainland reefs, at a matching 12–14 m depth. Given this entry's coordinates and depth are consistent with either identification, and operators are not fully consistent with each other on naming, this description should be read as describing the "Aquarium"-type shallow reef experience common to Bayahibe-area operators rather than a single, unambiguously pinned location.

Seahorse sightings are reported at "The Aquarium" by both Coral Point Diving and Dressel Divers — guides are described as regularly able to locate them among the coral heads and sponges. This is a genuine, multi-source-confirmed draw of the site (under whichever exact location it refers to), not a generic regional claim.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Offshore Bayahibe, southeastern Dominican Republic
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20–28 metres (65–90 feet)
- **Current**: Minimal
- **Best Time**: Year-round; one of the most consistently accessible sites in the country

## Marine Life

Coral Point Diving and Dressel Divers both confirm seahorses at "The Aquarium," found by guides among coral heads and sponges. Coral Point Diving's broader description of the site (schools of colourful fish, coral cover) supports the general "fish density" reputation, and Dressel Divers describes clouds of schooling grunts and creole wrasse. Yellowtail snappers, French grunts, French angelfish, queen angelfish, parrotfish, hawksbill turtles, green moray eels, and cleaning stations are consistent with what Bayahibe/Catalina-area operators report for their shallow reef sites generally, but are not individually confirmed for this exact site by name in the sources consulted — treat these as regional-reef expectations layered on top of the two confirmed specifics (fish density, seahorses). The previously cited "over 30 species visible simultaneously" and "nurse sharks under ledges" claims could not be traced to any source and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

An exploratory shallow dive. Descend to 5–6 metres over the reef crest and move slowly over the reef, pausing at coral heads and overhangs. Drop to 10–12 metres where the reef transitions to sand. Average dive time is 50–65 minutes. Beginners can focus entirely on enjoying the marine life without concern about depth management.

## Entry and Exit

Boat from Bayahibe, typically paired as the second dive of a morning two-tank trip. Entry via giant stride or back roll. Exit via boat ladder in calm conditions.

## Tips

- Guides know where to find seahorses — this is the site's signature find, confirmed independently by two of the area's largest operators
- Move slowly and avoid sudden movements near fish — they are accustomed to divers but still more active when approached gently
- Cleaning stations on large coral heads reward patient observation — fish queue to be cleaned by shrimp
- Combine with The Wall (done first, deeper) for a classic Bayahibe two-tank morning

## Safety

One of the safest dive environments in the Dominican Republic. Fire coral — the white, branching variety — is present on the reef and causes a burning sting on contact. Maintain buoyancy and avoid touching the reef. Sea urchins occupy crevices. Follow dive guide instructions regarding approach to marine life.

## Photography

Aquarium is among the best sites in the Dominican Republic for reef fish photography. The fish density and calm behavior allow close approach. Wide-angle lenses capture sweeping reef scenes. The macro photographer will find seahorses (with guide help), spotted drums, and cleaning shrimp as subjects. Natural light photography produces excellent results in these shallow, clear conditions.
