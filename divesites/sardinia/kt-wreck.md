## KT Wreck

KT Wreck (KT12) is a World War II German military transport wreck lying in the Gulf of Orosei, Sardinia.

## Overview

The KT12 was a KriegsTransporter (armed military cargo vessel) built at the OTO shipyards in Livorno, Italy, and delivered on 19 May 1943 before commissioning into the German Kriegsmarine. On 10 June 1943, during her maiden voyage carrying fuel drums and vehicles bound for North Africa, she was intercepted roughly two miles off Marina di Orosei and struck by a torpedo fired by the British submarine HMS Safari. (One source dates the sinking to 10 May 1943, but this is an outlier contradicted by the majority of independently reviewed Italian maritime and dive-operator accounts, so 10 June 1943 is treated as correct here.) The torpedo struck near the bow, separating it from the hull; the bow sank immediately roughly 300 metres from the main hull, while the bowless, burning stern section sank about 40-41 minutes later.

Sources disagree on the vessel's dimensions. Most sources put her displacement at 834 tons, though one Italian dive operator estimates 1,200 tons — we report 834 tons as the better-corroborated figure but flag the discrepancy. Her exact length is not consistently documented across the sources reviewed: only one (Four Jandals) gives a specific figure, approximately 60 metres (225ft), which could not be corroborated elsewhere, so it should be treated as unconfirmed rather than fact.

The two sections rest on a sandy seabed at 24-34 metres depth (not the 27-34m/700-800m separation previously recorded here, which current sources do not support). The main hull section rests upright, with the stern section's gun still clearly visible on the aft deck, along with the propeller and rudder. Two cargo cranes lie on the seabed near the main hull. The separated bow section has tilted to its side. Scattered cargo fragments and debris create an extensive field around both sections. Marine life reported at the wreck by multiple independent sources includes large groupers, moray eels, scorpionfish, slipper lobsters, and nudibranchs.

Note: the wreck lies in the Gulf of Orosei on Sardinia's eastern coast, not the southern coast. Wreck penetration is possible with proper technical/wreck training but should never be attempted without it.
