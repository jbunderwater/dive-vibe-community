## Cala Gonone

Cala Gonone is the boat-accessible gateway to the Bue Marino cave system on Sardinia's Gulf of Orosei coast -- a vast karst network with a shallow recreational entrance and a far more extreme technical cave-diving branch.

## Overview

Cala Gonone is the departure point for boat trips into the Bue Marino cave system, part of a karst network that extends more than 70 kilometres inland. The north and south branches are dry, walkable tourist routes (about 800 metres of path) that most visitors explore on foot rather than by diving. The recreational entrance area that this listing describes is the halocline zone where fresh spring water meets the sea, at around 15 metres depth, with stalactites that formed when the caverns stood above sea level.

Separately -- and importantly not to be confused with the recreational entrance -- the system's central branch is a genuine technical sump-diving route requiring specialized cave certification, where sources describe a sandy floor around 52 metres giving way to a vertical wall descending to 75 metres or more. That branch is a distinct and far more demanding activity than the shallow boat-tour entrance and is not reflected in this listing's Intermediate difficulty rating; divers should not assume this rating covers the deep technical dive.

Outside the cave mouth, rocky reef and posidonia meadows provide habitat for typical Mediterranean reef species. No site-specific marine life report was found for the interior of the cave itself beyond the geological features noted above.
