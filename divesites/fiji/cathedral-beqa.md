# Cathedral Beqa

A swim-through cavern that doubles as Beqa Lagoon Resort's tiger and bull shark feeding dive — sunlight through the gorgonian-fringed roof and large sharks at the same site.

## Overview

Cathedral Beqa is named for a swim-through cavern at 15–20 meters where sunlight penetrates irregular roof openings lined with gorgonians and soft corals, producing filtered light beams inside — most pronounced on morning dives when the sun angle is favorable. A prior version of this description treated the Cathedral purely as a passive swim-through with grey reef sharks patrolling outside. Re-audit found that materially incomplete: the site is operated by Beqa Lagoon Resort as its Cathedral Shark Dive, a structured shark feeding experience built around the same cavern/stone-wall structure. Feeders draw sharks — tiger sharks are described as the headline species ("you would be unlucky not to see one"), alongside bull, grey reef, blacktip reef, whitetip reef, silvertip, lemon, and tawny nurse sharks — to a viewing position at the wall using tuna heads from a feeding bin. Beqa Lagoon Resort states a minimum certification of Advanced Open Water plus 30 logged dives for the dive, which is why difficulty has been raised from Intermediate to Advanced. A separate, more general Fiji travel guide still describes the Cathedral as suited to intermediate divers — that source predates or does not reflect the operator's own stricter requirement, so the operator's stated minimum is treated as authoritative here.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Beqa Lagoon, Pacific Harbour, Viti Levu, Fiji (operated by Beqa Lagoon Resort)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–25 meters (50–80 feet)
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: Year-round; operated Monday/Wednesday and either Thursday or Friday mornings depending on the source consulted; morning dives also best for the cavern's light-beam effect

## Marine Life

Tiger sharks are the dive's signature draw, described by the operator as reliably present. Bull sharks are also a headline species. Additional species documented at the feeding station: silvertip, tawny nurse, blacktip reef, whitetip reef, grey reef, and lemon sharks — up to nine species across sources. Inside the swim-through itself, batfish are documented circling slowly, and soft corals and sea fans on the cavern walls benefit from reduced UV exposure. Napoleon (Maori) wrasse and occasional sea turtles are noted at the safety-stop area. Lionfish and moray eels are reported in cavern crevices by earlier operator accounts, though this detail was not independently re-confirmed in this pass.

## Dive Profile

Divers descend along the reef contour or guide lines to the stone wall/feeding position at approximately 15–20 meters. Feeders present tuna heads (hand feed, then an aerial feed from a suspended bait bin) to draw sharks toward the viewing area. The dive runs approximately 40 minutes on Nitrox per Beqa Lagoon Resort. After the feeding portion, divers move through or past the cavern's swim-through section — sunlight through the roof openings illuminates soft coral on the interior walls — before finishing with a safety stop on the reef top, where small reef fish, Napoleon wrasse, and occasional turtles are seen.

## Entry and Exit

Accessed by boat from Beqa Lagoon Resort. The shark dive typically runs as two dives with a surface interval between them, on a fixed weekly schedule (reported as Monday/Wednesday/Friday by one source and Monday/Wednesday/Thursday by another — confirm current scheduling directly with the resort). Entry by giant stride; lagoon conditions are usually calm.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Confirm certification requirements before booking**: Beqa Lagoon Resort requires Advanced Open Water and 30+ logged dives for this specific dive — verify your logbook meets the threshold.
- **Dive it in the morning if the cavern light matters to you**: The swim-through's light-beam effect is angle-dependent; midday and afternoon dives produce flatter interior light.
- **Follow the feeding briefing exactly**: As with any shark feeding dive, loose gear, dangling gauges, and any movement toward the sharks are the primary risk factors.
- **Maintain horizontal trim in the swim-through**: A vertical position disturbs sediment from the cavern floor and reduces visibility for divers behind you.
- **Carry a small torch**: The cavern interior is dark enough to benefit from a dive light for soft coral color and crevice life.

## Safety Considerations

This is a shark feeding dive, not a casual swim-through — follow the operator's briefing without deviation, particularly around the feeding station where tiger and bull sharks are actively drawn in by bait. The swim-through itself is well-ventilated and does not require cave or cavern training, but divers with claustrophobia should confirm the route with the guide beforehand. Monitor depth through the cavern as the floor slopes. Never characterize proximity to actively-fed tiger or bull sharks as risk-free — maintain the position and distance the briefing specifies.

## Photography

The Cathedral offers two different photographic opportunities. At the feeding station, wide-angle shooting at 10–17mm captures tiger and bull shark portraits at close range — position with the light behind you and keep strobes clear of the housing. Inside the swim-through, the light-beam effect rewards wide-angle or fisheye timing to the morning sun angle — shoot from the dark interior toward the gorgonian-framed openings with a diver silhouetted against the light.
