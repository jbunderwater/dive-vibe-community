# Cathedral Beqa

A structured shark-viewing dive at a rock wall barrier in the Beqa Lagoon system, where bull sharks, occasional tiger sharks, and reef sharks gather in a controlled setting.

## Overview

Re-audit correction (2026-08-02): This description was substantially rewritten after re-pulling sources. The previous version described Cathedral as a natural swim-through cavern with light beams and batfish — that claim traced to a single source (fijitravel.org) and could not be corroborated by any other source checked today. Three independently re-pulled sources (Diveplanit, Dive Worldwide, and Fiji High) instead describe "The Cathedral" in the Beqa Lagoon area as a structured shark dive: divers descend via reef contour or fixed lines to a low rock or "stone wall," behind which they observe sharks drawn in by feeders working a feeding bin roughly 20 to 30 meters down. Diveplanit describes up to 30 bull sharks and one to three tiger sharks per dive, alongside grey reef, blacktip, lemon, and nurse sharks; Fiji High's account lists up to eight shark species total, consistent with the broader Beqa shark-diving character shared with Shark Reef Marine Reserve.

Operator attribution is itself unresolved: Diveplanit attributes this "Cathedral" to Beqa Lagoon Resort, while Fiji High attributes a same-named "Cathedral Shark dive" to Beqa Adventure Divers. It is possible these are two separately branded, similarly structured shark dives sharing a name, or that one source has the operator wrong. This description reflects the shark-dive character that multiple sources agree on, without asserting a specific operator. fijitravel.org's natural-cavern description is noted here as an unconfirmed, conflicting account — it may describe an entirely different, less-documented site that happens to share the name.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Beqa Lagoon, Pacific Harbour area, Viti Levu, Fiji
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–25 meters (50–80 feet)
- **Current**: Light to moderate
- **Best Time**: Year-round; the dive reportedly runs on a fixed weekly schedule (sources give differing days — confirm with the specific operator)

## Marine Life

Sources agree on the core shark roster: bull sharks are the primary volume species (up to roughly 30 individuals reported by Diveplanit), with occasional tiger sharks (one to three per dive), grey reef sharks, blacktip and whitetip reef sharks, lemon sharks, and tawny nurse sharks. Turtles and Napoleon wrasse are reported during the safety stop on the reef top. The previous version's claims of batfish, lionfish, and moray eels inside a cavern structure are removed — they were tied to the unconfirmed natural-cavern description and are not supported by the sources that describe this as a shark-viewing dive.

## Dive Profile

Divers descend by reef contour or a fixed line to the rock/stone wall barrier at depth, where a feeder works a feeding bin to draw sharks toward a designated viewing area over roughly 40 minutes (per Diveplanit, run on Nitrox). Ascent to the reef top follows for a safety stop, where turtles and Napoleon wrasse may be seen. Some sources describe this as run in two stages within a single outing (a deeper session and a shallower session); confirm the specific structure with your chosen operator, since it differs from source to source.

## Entry and Exit

Accessed by boat from the Pacific Harbour/Beqa Lagoon area. Entry by giant stride or back roll depending on the vessel. A pre-dive briefing on shark-dive protocol (no loose gear, hands in, no reaching toward sharks) is standard practice for shark-feeding dives in this region and should be expected here as well.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Confirm the operator and schedule directly**: Sources disagree on which operator runs this specific "Cathedral" dive and which days it operates. Book through a Pacific Harbour-based shark diving operator and ask them to confirm.
- **Expect a structured, feeder-led encounter**: This is not a casual reef swim — follow all pre-dive briefing instructions on positioning and behavior around the feeding area.
- **Bring a wide-angle setup**: The close-range shark activity around the feeding area rewards wide-angle or fisheye lenses.

## Safety Considerations

This is a shark-feeding dive and carries the inherent risks of any such dive. Strict adherence to briefing protocol — maintaining position behind the wall/barrier, no loose equipment, no hand-feeding by guests — is essential. Bull sharks and tiger sharks actively drawn by feeding require experienced, disciplined diving; this is not a beginner site.

## Photography

Wide-angle or fisheye lenses (10–17mm) suit the close-range shark activity around the feeding area. Position for clean water between you and the sharks to avoid backscatter, and shoot toward open water to silhouette sharks passing overhead.
