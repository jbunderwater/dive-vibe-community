## Fish Head

Fish Head — locally Mushimasmingili Thila — is one of the Maldives' most storied shark dives, a protected submerged pinnacle in North Ari Atoll that rises from around 40 metres to within 10 metres of the surface.

## Overview

Mushimasmingili Thila is a square-shaped submerged pinnacle roughly 80 metres across at its widest point, situated between the islands of Mandhoo and Maamigili in North Ari Atoll and protected as one of the Maldives' earliest Marine Protected Areas. The southeastern corner of the thila is locally called Fish Head and has historically been the focal point for shark encounters. Current operator and guide sources describe grey reef sharks as present on "virtually every dive," with schools of 10–20 individuals common along the deeper sections and outer edges; older accounts describe larger aggregations, and actual numbers on any given dive vary with season and current. The pinnacle receives strong tidal currents from multiple directions, which drive the ecology. The deeper sections below 30 metres feature sea fans and additional reef structure. The site is dived Advanced level due to current strength and depth.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Ari Atoll, Maldives (Mushimasmingili Thila Marine Park)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–40 metres
- **Current**: Strong; can be very strong on outer faces

## Marine Life

Grey reef sharks (schools of 10-20 reported on most dives) and whitetip reef sharks, big-eye trevally, Napoleon wrasse, anthias, grouper, eagle rays, spotted eagle rays, dog-toothed tuna, batfish, turtles, clownfish. Sea fans and black coral documented on deeper sections.

## Tips for Divers

Descend quickly to the top of the thila to orient in the current before being swept off. Position on the sheltered down-current face for best shark viewing. Maintain strict neutral buoyancy. Advanced certification and confirmed current-diving experience are essential. Current can be very strong on the outer faces.
