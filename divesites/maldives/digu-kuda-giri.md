## Digu Kuda Giri

Digu Kuda Giri corresponds to the dive site known in most operator listings as Kuda Giri Wreck, a small vessel deliberately sunk near the island of Gulhi in South Malé Atoll to form an artificial reef alongside an adjacent coral pinnacle (giri).

### Overview

The wreck lies upright on a slope with its bow around 18 m and the stern/cargo hold down to 30–32 m, sitting beside a separate reef pinnacle that gives the site its "giri" name. Some dive databases refer to the vessel as "MS Azieta," though this name is repeated mainly by aggregator listings and could not be confirmed against an independent primary or maritime record — it should be treated as unverified. No source consulted gives a build date, tonnage, or sinking cause; one low-reliability travel-aggregator page states a sinking year of 1994, but this is not corroborated elsewhere and is not included here.

### Diving

Currents are typically light to negligible, making this an accessible wreck for divers with at least intermediate experience. The hull is encrusted with orange, red, and yellow sponges and tubastrea (sun) coral. A cargo hold toward the stern and a couple of penetration points allow limited interior exploration for appropriately trained divers. The surrounding giri adds a reef-diving component to the same dive.

### Site Information

- **Location**: South Malé Atoll, Maldives (near Gulhi)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Depth**: Bow at 18 m; stern/hold to 30–32 m
- **Difficulty**: Intermediate
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive

### Marine Life

Confirmed by dive shop and database sources: Napoleon (humphead) wrasse sheltering under the deck stairs, leaf fish on the back deck, dense glassfish schools in the wheelhouse, batfish, gobies with shrimp partners, moray eels, octopus, nudibranchs, and red-toothed triggerfish. Turtles are also reported. No source confirms sharks or rays at this specific site.

### Tips for Divers

Currents are usually low, so this is a comfortable wreck for divers building penetration experience — though any entry into enclosed spaces still requires proper wreck training and a guide familiar with the layout. A torch helps pick out leaf fish and gobies against the encrusted hull. The adjacent giri is worth exploring on the same dive.
