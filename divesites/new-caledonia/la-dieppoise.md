# La Dieppoise

A former French Navy minesweeper (ex-HMCS Chaleur), scuttled on 19 January 1988 near Amadee Island. Sources disagree on her exact length (46-50 meters) and displacement (370-390 tons). Sitting upright with the ship's wheel still in place, her deteriorated wooden hull has opened up interior spaces at accessible depths -- rear deck at 22 meters, front deck at 18 meters, flybridge at 15 meters.

## Overview

La Dieppoise was originally HMCS Chaleur (MCB 144), a Canadian-built Bay-class minesweeper constructed by Port Arthur Shipbuilding in Ontario, laid down 8 June 1951 and launched 21 June 1952 -- this build timeline is confirmed by both Wikipedia and the French wreck-history site plongee-infos.com. The vessel served briefly in the Royal Canadian Navy before being transferred to the French Navy on 9 October 1954, where it was renamed La Dieppoise and commissioned on 13 November 1954. After serving in French Pacific waters and later as a patrol vessel based in Noumea from 1976, the vessel was decommissioned on 9 July 1987. Sources disagree on her exact dimensions: Wikipedia and Michael McFadyen's dive log both give a length of 46 meters and a displacement of 390 long tons, while plongee-infos.com gives 50 meters and 370 tonnes -- both figures are reported here rather than picking one. She is described by multiple sources as the last wooden (or wood-and-aluminium) patrol vessel in French Navy service. She was deliberately scuttled on 19 January 1988 near Amadee Island south of Noumea, via controlled explosive charges at the bow and stern, to create an artificial reef and dive attraction. The original scuttling date of 12 January was postponed due to Cyclone Anne -- this sequence is confirmed by Wikipedia, newcaledonia-diving.com, and plongee-infos.com.

The wreck sits upright on the sandy bottom in 26 meters of water. The ship's wheel remains in place on the bridge -- one of the most photographed features of the wreck. Two independent sources (newcaledonia-diving.com and plongee-infos.com) describe the wooden hull sections as significantly deteriorated after nearly four decades underwater, which has opened large gaps at the bow and stern and made much of the interior -- including the dining area, officers' quarters, and pilot house -- openly visible rather than requiring true penetration. The depth profile is diver-friendly: the rear deck sits at 22 meters, the front deck at 18 meters, and the flybridge at just 15 meters, making the upper sections accessible to less experienced divers.

The wreck has attracted large fish to the area, and the open, deteriorated structure makes exploration straightforward for divers with appropriate experience. This is widely considered one of the most accessible and enjoyable wreck dives near Noumea.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Amadee Island, south of Noumea, New Caledonia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 26 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 22-27 degrees C
- **Current**: Generally mild
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

The upright wreck structure has attracted large fish species including trevally schools that hunt through the superstructure, coral trout, and occasional turtles and eagle rays. Nudibranchs colonize the interior surfaces. The hull supports established coral and sponge growth after more than three decades of colonization. Banded sea snakes are also encountered in the vicinity.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the wreck -- the flybridge at 15 meters is the shallowest point. Begin with the upper decks and bridge area, where the ship's wheel is still in position. The front deck at 18 meters and rear deck at 22 meters are easily reached. The sand at the base of the hull is at approximately 26 meters. The deteriorated wooden hull has opened large gaps at the bow and stern, so much of the interior -- dining area, officers' quarters, pilot house -- is visible without full penetration. The multi-level depth profile allows you to start deep and work shallower as the dive progresses, ending on the flybridge for an efficient safety stop transition.

## Entry and Exit

Access by dive boat from Noumea -- approximately 40 minutes to the Amadee Island area. Entry by giant stride. The wreck has a permanent mooring.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The ship's wheel on the bridge is the signature photo -- arrive early in the dive while air supply is full
- The multi-level depth profile makes this wreck excellent for managing bottom time
- The easy interior access makes this a good introductory wreck dive for divers interested in wreck penetration
- Combine with a dive at nearby Boulari Pass or Amadee Island Reef

## Safety Considerations

While much of the interior is now open to natural light through deterioration, any deeper penetration beyond sight of that light remains an overhead environment and should not be attempted without wreck diving certification and appropriate equipment -- this is never a "safe" activity regardless of how open the structure looks. The maximum depth of 26 meters to the sand is moderate but requires proper gas management. Maintain good buoyancy to avoid disturbing silt inside the wreck.
