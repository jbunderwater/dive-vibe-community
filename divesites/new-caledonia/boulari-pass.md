# Boulari Pass

A drift dive through the outer barrier reef near Amadee Island, famous as one of the best sites in New Caledonia for manta ray encounters. Reef sharks, barracuda, and large fish in the pass. Average depth 18 meters, maximum 26 meters.

## Overview

Boulari Pass is a break in the outer barrier reef near Amadee Island, south of Noumea. The site is dived as a drift, riding the tidal current through the pass where nutrient-rich water from the open ocean funnels into the lagoon. This concentration of nutrients attracts large marine life, making Boulari Pass one of the premier big-animal dive sites in New Caledonia.

The pass is famous for manta ray encounters — resident reef mantas visit cleaning stations in the pass, hovering near coral heads while cleaner wrasse remove parasites. Reef sharks (grey reef and whitetip) are resident, and barracuda schools patrol the pass. Blue Caledonia Diving, a local operator running dedicated day trips to the pass, confirms it is "renowned worldwide for its underwater fauna, particularly manta rays and reef shark schools." Sources report slightly different depth profiles: newcaledonia-diving.com states an average of 18 meters and maximum of 26 meters, while Scuba Diver Life reports an average of 15 meters and maximum of 30 meters. The site is a designated marine reserve.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Outer barrier reef near Amadee Island, south of Noumea, New Caledonia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 26 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 22-27 degrees C (reported as 23 degrees C in October)
- **Current**: Moderate to strong (tidal)
- **Best Time**: Mantas are resident year-round; sources disagree on a peak season (accounts variously cite September-December, November-February, and March-September)

## Marine Life

Manta rays are the headline attraction and are present year-round at cleaning stations in the pass; sources disagree on which months see the highest concentrations, so no single peak season is asserted here. Grey reef sharks and whitetip reef sharks are resident. Large schools of barracuda patrol the pass, and Blue Caledonia Diving additionally lists groupers among the regulars. Trevally and sea turtles are also regularly sighted. Note: shark feeding at this site was banned by the New Caledonia government via a 2012 notice — re-confirmed directly from newcaledonia-diving.com during this QA pass ("feeding is no longer authorized in the passage") — and is no longer practised.

## Dive Profile

Enter the water upstream of the pass and drift through with the tidal current. The pass averages around 18 meters depth, with a maximum of approximately 26 meters. Maintain a comfortable depth of 12-18 meters to watch for mantas and sharks in the water column above and below. The drift ends as the pass opens into the lagoon, where the current dissipates and the boat picks up.

## Entry and Exit

Access by dive boat from Noumea -- approximately 40 minutes to the Amadee Island area. Entry by giant stride upstream of the pass. Exit by SMB pickup in the lagoon after drifting through the pass.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Manta rays are most frequently encountered during the September-December period when plankton blooms are heaviest
- A wide-angle lens is essential for the large marine life encounters
- The drift through the pass is exhilarating -- relax and let the current do the work
- The site is a marine reserve; regulations prohibit feeding or disturbing marine life

## Safety Considerations

The tidal current through the pass can be strong — this drift dive requires comfort with current diving. Stay with the group and follow the guide's signals. Deploy an SMB well before surfacing for boat pickup.
