# Gorgonia Valley

A sheer wall on Gadji Reef off the northwest corner of Isle of Pines, dropping from 10 meters to 35+ meters (one trip report puts the maximum at 50 meters). A virtual forest of gorgonian fans and soft corals lines a v-shaped canyon and heart-shaped swim-through, with a resident blacktip reef shark, leopard sharks, and pygmy seahorses.

## Overview

Gorgonia Valley is located on Gadji Reef off the northwest corner of the Isle of Pines (Ile des Pins), at the southern end of New Caledonia's lagoon. The site features a steep wall with a distinctive v-shaped canyon running through the reef structure and a heart-shaped swim-through as its centrepiece. Sources disagree on the exact maximum depth: Michael McFadyen's dive log describes the wall dropping from roughly 10 meters at the reef top to "over 35 meters," while a separate Just Gotta Dive trip report gives a maximum of 50 meters for the same site.

The canyon and wall are lined with a virtual forest of gorgonian sea fans — giving the site its name — plus colourful soft corals. A territorial blacktip reef shark, resident at the site for at least eight years per McFadyen's account, patrols regularly. New Caledonia Tourism and Just Gotta Dive both independently name leopard sharks as a highlight here, and both also confirm pygmy seahorses — notable since the first pygmy seahorses ever documented were found offshore of New Caledonia in the 1960s. The Isle of Pines' limited diving traffic means the reef is in excellent condition. Visibility is reported as excellent, commonly exceeding 30 meters in the clear lagoon waters. The Kunie Scuba Centre, the island's dive operator, runs guided dives at this site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Gadji Reef, NW corner of Isle of Pines, New Caledonia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-40 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 22-27 degrees C
- **Current**: Variable
- **Best Time**: September to December

## Marine Life

A territorial blacktip reef shark is regularly encountered at this site (resident per McFadyen's dive log for at least eight years). Leopard sharks and pygmy seahorses are confirmed site-specific sightings, named independently by both New Caledonia Tourism and Just Gotta Dive. Just Gotta Dive's Gorgonia Valley trip report also lists tangs, Moorish idols, butterflyfish, angelfish, orange anthias, cowries, shrimps, and nudibranchs at this site. The earlier "eagle rays" claim in this description has been removed -- no source found during this audit confirms eagle rays at Gorgonia Valley specifically.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the reef top at approximately 10 meters. Follow the wall down, exploring the swim-throughs as they present themselves. The wall drops to 35+ meters, so monitor depth carefully. The best coral growth and fish concentration is typically between 12-25 meters. Look for the resident blacktip reef shark in the blue water off the wall. Ascend along the wall face to the reef top for the safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Access by dive boat from Isle of Pines operators. The Gadji Reef location is on the northwest corner of the island. Entry by giant stride.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The swim-throughs are the highlight of this site -- carry a torch to illuminate the coral inside
- Watch for the resident blacktip reef shark in the blue water off the wall
- The Isle of Pines' remote location means excellent reef condition -- one of New Caledonia's best dive areas

## Safety Considerations

The wall drops to significant depths -- monitor depth carefully. Swim-throughs are overhead environments; maintain awareness of exit points. Variable currents are possible.
