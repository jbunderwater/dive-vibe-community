## Uçak Batığı

Uçak Batığı (Airplane Wreck) is an aircraft wreck dive near Bodrum, Turkey.

## Overview

Uçak Batığı is a retired Turkish Air Force Douglas C-47A Dakota (a military transport derived from the DC-3) deliberately sunk as an artificial reef at Karaada/Paçoz Bay near Bodrum by BOSAD, the Bodrum dive-schools association. Sualtı Gazetesi, Zentacle and taucher.net give the date as 1 July 2008, and X-Ray Magazine also says 2008; The Divers' Club gives 2007, so the year is reported with a minor conflict, 2008 being better attested.

**Dimensions**: Zentacle and taucher.net report about 19.57 m in length and 28.96 m wingspan with two Pratt & Whitney R-1830-93 engines; Sualtı Gazetesi gives about 19 m and 29 m; The Divers' Club says the fuselage is roughly 20 m. For comparison, Wikipedia lists the C-47B type at 19.43 m by 29.11 m, so the aircraft's dimensions are consistent with the type. No source consulted gives the airframe's serial number, build date or weight/tonnage, so none is stated.

**Correction**: this listing previously identified the wreck as a WWII-era Italian Savoia-Marchetti SM.79 at 58-71 m. No source supports that; all sources consulted identify a C-47A Dakota in far shallower water.

**Breakup and depth**: the wreck has moved since sinking, and sources conflict on how. Sualtı Gazetesi says a current split it in two in 2010, with the fuselage and tail shifting to about 28-34 m; taucher.net reports it slid down the slope in winter 2008/2009 and now lies at roughly 25-30 m; Zentacle also attributes the movement to winter storms and currents. Reported depth ranges therefore differ: 17-32 m (Aquapro, X-Ray Magazine), 25 m (Zentacle), 25-30 m (taucher.net), and a fuselage at about 34 m with cockpit around 23 m (The Divers' Club, which lists 36 m as the maximum for its Paçoz Wall route).

**Penetration**: Aquapro and X-Ray Magazine say the wreck is not penetrable except through a rear cabin door at about 29 m; Zentacle and taucher.net advise against entering because the structure is unstable with loose debris. Any wreck penetration requires proper training and equipment, and here the sources advise against it.

## Marine Life

Aquapro reports groupers, tuna, lionfish, scorpionfish and many sea bream at the wreck. X-Ray Magazine describes lionfish gathered in a recess at the base of the wreck, and taucher.net photos from September 2010 show a large grouper living on it.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Karaada/Paçoz Bay, Bodrum area, Muğla Province, Turkey (Aegean Sea)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 34 meters
