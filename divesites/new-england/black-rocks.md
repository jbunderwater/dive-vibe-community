# Black Rocks

Dark metamorphic outcrops off the Plum Island/Newburyport coast, one of the signature shallow-water reef dive clusters in northern Massachusetts.

## Overview

Black Rocks is a group of dark metamorphic rock outcrops in the shallow nearshore waters off the northern Massachusetts coast, in the area between Plum Island and the Merrimack River mouth near Newburyport. The site's name reflects the dark appearance of the local bedrock — typical of the Precambrian and Paleozoic formations that underlie much of coastal Essex County. At 10 metres maximum depth, this is an archetypal northern Massachusetts shallow reef dive: dense kelp over dark angular rock, typical of this stretch of coast. The site sits in close proximity to Badgers Rock, Lunts Rock, and Half Tide Rocks.

## Dive Profile

Access via boat from Newburyport, Plum Island, or Cape Ann. Descend to the rocky bottom at 8–10 metres and navigate the perimeter of the rock cluster. Work from the shallowest tops down to the base where the rocks meet the sand. The 10-metre maximum allows generous bottom time. Plan around slack tide for the Merrimack River tidal outflow. Complete a 3-minute safety stop at 5 metres.

## Entry and Exit

Boat charter required. The shoal-studded northern Massachusetts nearshore requires a captain familiar with local navigation hazards. Anchor in sand adjacent to the rocks. Deploy SMB before ascending.

## Safety Considerations

Cold water year-round — minimum 7mm wetsuit or drysuit. The Merrimack River tidal influence can create directional current near the river mouth. Rocky bottom creates boat navigation hazards. Deploy SMB before surfacing.

## Photography

The dark rock and dense kelp create excellent contrast for wide-angle reef photography. Natural light is strong at 10 metres on clear summer days.
