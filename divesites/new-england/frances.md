# Frances

The intertidal remains of a German iron-hulled bark driven ashore at Head of the Meadow Beach, North Truro, during a December 1872 gale — visible as a bow silhouette at low tide.

## Overview

The bark *Frances* was a German iron-hulled sailing vessel wrecked during a December gale in 1872 at Head of the Meadow Beach, North Truro, on the outer Cape Cod shore — not Wellfleet, as an earlier draft of this page incorrectly stated; the site's coordinates place it clearly within the North Truro / Cape Cod National Seashore stretch of beach. She was carrying a cargo of sugar and tin. U.S. Life-Saving Service crewmen responded by dragging a whaleboat overland from the bay side of the Cape across to the outer beach to reach the stranded vessel, and all hands were rescued alive. The German captain survived the wreck itself but died several days later from the effects of exposure; he is buried in North Truro. The wreck's bow silhouette is still periodically visible at low tide, more than 150 years later, when sand conditions expose it. Like other outer Cape wrecks, the site is buried and re-exposed in cycles by the shifting barrier beach sand. This is a historical and archaeological site rather than a conventional scuba destination.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Head of the Meadow Beach, North Truro, Massachusetts (Cape Cod National Seashore)
- **Entry Type**: beach
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 3 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 1–4 meters (surf zone; best on calm days at low tide)
- **Current**: Shore break and tidal surge; significant in surf
- **Water Temperature**: 10–22°C summer; colder off-season
- **Best Time**: Low tide, calm sea conditions

## Marine Life

No dive-shop, ScubaBoard, or dive-database source specifically documenting marine life at the Frances wreck was found. This is a surf-zone intertidal site rather than a diveable reef habitat. The regional cold-water outer Cape Cod fauna of the wider area likely applies to any exposed timbers, but no species have been confirmed observed at this specific site, so none are listed here.

## Dive Profile

Not a conventional scuba site. Access at low tide on a calm day allows wading to the edge of the exposed bow silhouette when sand conditions permit. Visibility of the wreck depends entirely on seasonal sand deposition — in some years it is clearly exposed; in others it is completely buried. This is primarily a historical reconnaissance and photography site rather than a dive.

## Entry and Exit

Access via the Cape Cod National Seashore's Head of the Meadow Beach parking area in North Truro. Walk the beach at low tide looking for the exposed bow silhouette. The National Seashore manages this coastline — removal of any archaeological material is prohibited. Exit before tide and sea state increase.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Visit at low tide — the wreck silhouette is a surface-level feature, not a submerged dive
- Sand cover is highly seasonal; the wreck may be fully buried depending on recent storm activity
- The National Seashore visitor center at Head of the Meadow or Province Lands can provide current information on beach conditions
- This is a protected archaeological site — do not disturb or remove any material

## Safety Considerations

The outer Cape Cod Atlantic beach is a high-energy surf environment. Waves can be deceptively strong even on apparently calm days. Shore break and undertow are genuine hazards. Never turn your back on the ocean. Hypothermia risk from cold Cape waters is significant even in summer without adequate protection.

## Photography

The exposed bow silhouette, when visible, provides an evocative subject in natural light at low tide. Wide shots including the sweep of the North Truro beach and dunes convey the isolation of this coastline.
