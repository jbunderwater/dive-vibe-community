# Davis Ledge

A rocky ledge on the South Shore of Massachusetts near Scituate, part of a productive cluster of named ledge dive sites south of Boston Harbor.

## Overview

Davis Ledge is one of a cluster of named submerged rocky ledges in the waters south of Boston Harbor, off the Cohasset and Scituate coastline of Massachusetts's South Shore. The ledges in this area — Davis, Enos, Harding, Martin, Mikes, Tobias, Three-and-One-Half-Fathom, and Utonia Ledges — appear on navigational charts as hazards south of the main shipping channels into Boston. For divers, these rocky outcrops at depths ranging from 12 to 18 metres provide productive cold-water reef diving within easy reach of South Shore charter operators. The South Shore ledge system is less traveled than Cape Ann but offers the same cold Gulf of Maine water and rocky-ledge structure, though no site-specific marine-life reports were found for Davis Ledge itself.

## Dive Profile

Access via boat charter from Scituate or other South Shore operators. Descend to the rocky ledge at 12–18 metres. Navigate the ledge perimeter, working from shallower to deeper sections. The sand-rock interface at the ledge base has the most structural relief. South Shore ledges can be combined with adjacent named sites on a single charter. Plan around slack tide. Ascend with adequate reserve and complete a 3-minute safety stop at 5 metres.

## Entry and Exit

Boat charter required from Scituate, Hull, or other South Shore operators. Local knowledge is valuable for identifying specific ledge locations. Anchor in sand adjacent to the ledge. Deploy SMB before ascending.
