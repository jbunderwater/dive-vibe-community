## Ann's Rock / Astrid's Wall

Ann's Rock sits alongside a steep wall in Maomao Bay, on the western side of Tawhiti Rahi Island. Rather than the coral-garden character often described for Poor Knights walls, seafriends.org.nz -- a New Zealand marine-science reference site -- describes the rock itself as covered in "a monotonous expanse of strapweed and stalked kelp," with the real drama coming from the adjoining wall and current. Small boats can anchor on the rock, but the site is exposed to swell.

## Overview

Ann's Rock lies beside a wall in Maomao Bay that seafriends.org.nz describes as reaching 70 metres deep, with large boulders rising to around 40 metres -- considerably deeper than the 25-metre figure on record for the rock itself, so treat the deeper numbers as describing the surrounding bay rather than the shallower rock top. The same source notes the site is "almost impossible to anchor outside the surging swell," and is really only accessible during calm seas, which somewhat qualifies the more casual "easy anchoring" framing sometimes used for this site. Divers can swim with or against the current depending on conditions, which is the site's main draw rather than reef structure.

The name "Astrid's Wall" for the adjoining feature could not be independently confirmed by any source found during this review -- it may be a local operator's name for the wall itself, distinct from Ann's Rock, but no source ties the two names together explicitly.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Maomao Bay, Tawhiti Rahi Island (West), Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters at the rock; the adjoining Maomao Bay wall continues to roughly 40-70m (see note above)
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 14-17°C (winter/May-Sept), 19-25°C (summer/Nov-April)
- **Current**: Variable; often present -- a defining feature of the site
- **Best Time**: Calm-sea days only; the site is difficult to anchor in swell

## Marine Life

The dominant marine growth reported here is strapweed and stalked kelp covering the rock itself, rather than the coral and sponge cover more typical of other Poor Knights walls. No source found during this review documents specific fish species seen at Ann's Rock; regional Poor Knights fauna (kingfish, snapper, blue and pink maomao) likely passes through given the current, but no source confirms sightings specific to this site.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the wall structure in Maomao Bay and follow the vertical profile. Current direction, when present, should guide your dive plan -- discuss it with your guide before entering the water. Because the rock is a kelp-dominated formation rather than a coral wall, expect a different visual character than sites like Oculina Point or Wild Beast Point.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride, conditions permitting -- this site is only accessible in calm seas due to swell exposure. Your guide will brief on current direction. Exit is via ascent and boat pickup at the surface.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Weather-dependent**: Confirm with your operator that conditions are calm enough for this site before planning around it
- **Current awareness**: The current is the site's defining feature -- plan your dive with or against it as briefed
- **Depth discipline**: The nearby wall drops well past the depth listed for the rock itself -- don't be drawn deeper than planned

## Safety Considerations

- Good buoyancy control essential to prevent unintentional deep descents given the nearby wall's depth
- Site access depends on calm sea conditions
- Always dive with a buddy
