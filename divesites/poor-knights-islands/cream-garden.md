## Cream Garden

Cream Gardens (the plural form used by most sources) sits on the northeast side of Tawhiti Rahi. The dive starts in a sheltered bay and heads out through a kelp forest before opening onto a wall studded with sponges — a mix of terrain rather than a single flat reef. In winter, when water clarity peaks, this is one of the Poor Knights sites where divers have reported large aggregations of bronze whaler sharks.

## Overview

Cream Gardens begins in a secluded bay with a sloping field of kelp — one source describes it as a swaying carpet of chartreuse kelp. Divers head through the kelp forest to reach a wall on the far side, described as holding hundreds of sponges and varied marine life. We could not confirm the origin of the site's name (a previous version of this page attributed it to "cream-coloured sponges," but no source supports that specific explanation, so it has been removed). The most notable reported feature is a winter aggregation of bronze whaler sharks — one account describes divers seeing 30 to 40 of them at once when visibility is at its best.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northeast side of Tawhiti Rahi, Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 22 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters (best in winter, when the shark aggregation is also reported)
- **Water Temperature**: 14-17°C (winter, May-Sept), 19-25°C (summer, Nov-April)
- **Current**: Mild to variable
- **Best Time**: Winter for visibility and the reported bronze whaler aggregation

## Marine Life

The one specific, sourced marine-life claim for this site is a winter aggregation of bronze whaler sharks — reportedly as many as 30-40 individuals in a single dive when conditions are clearest. Beyond that, the site's kelp forest and sponge-covered wall likely host the broader range of Poor Knights reef species (maomao, snapper, moray eels, nudibranchs), but we don't have a source confirming those specific sightings at Cream Gardens, so treat them as general regional fauna rather than confirmed observations.

## Dive Profile

Entry is into a sheltered bay, from which divers swim out through a kelp forest before reaching a wall on the far side. The wall carries dense sponge growth and is the more visually rich portion of the dive. Depth builds gradually to the site maximum of 22 meters. Winter dives here carry the added possibility of encountering the reported bronze whaler aggregation — approach any sharks calmly and let your guide lead the interaction.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride into the sheltered bay. Your guide will lead the route out through the kelp to the wall. Exit is via ascent and boat pickup at the surface.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Winter visit for sharks**: if a bronze whaler encounter is your goal, winter (May-Sept) is when the aggregation has been reported, coinciding with the best visibility
- **Expect kelp, not coral**: this is a kelp-forest-to-wall dive, not the coral-reef image the name might suggest
- **Sponge photography on the wall**: the sponge-covered wall on the far side of the kelp forest is the site's visual highlight

## Safety Considerations

- Current is generally mild to variable — confirm conditions with your guide
- Good buoyancy control helps avoid damage to the kelp forest and sponge wall
- Cold-water thermal protection required
- If sharks are present, follow your guide's briefing on distance and behaviour — do not attempt to touch or feed them
- Always dive with a buddy

## Photography

The sponge-covered wall beyond the kelp forest offers strong wide-angle and macro opportunities. If diving in winter with sharks present, a wide-angle lens is essential — bronze whalers are usually encountered in open water rather than close against structure.
