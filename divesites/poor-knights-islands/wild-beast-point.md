## Wild Beast Point

Wild Beast Point sits at the northernmost tip of Tawhiti Rahi, where nutrient-rich current sweeps around the point and shoals of fish either swim into it or take shelter in nearby Maomao Bay. The name reportedly comes from early divers being investigated by bronze whaler sharks every time they dived here — sharks remain the site's signature draw today, alongside the challenging current and depth that make this an advanced-only dive.

## Overview

This is one of the Poor Knights' more current-swept sites, and buoyancy control is genuinely challenging here because of the combination of depth and current. It's suited to advanced divers, though some operators will bring skilled, experienced divers here under close guidance. A dive or scooter run around the point may turn up a bronze whaler shark — or, more rarely, a hammerhead. Large snapper and other fish school in the current on the point itself.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northernmost tip of Tawhiti Rahi, Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 24 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 14-17°C (winter, May-Sept), 19-25°C (summer, Nov-April)
- **Current**: Often strong — this is the defining feature of the site, and buoyancy control is genuinely difficult here
- **Best Time**: May to September for best visibility; year-round diving possible with the right conditions

## Marine Life

Bronze whaler sharks are the confirmed signature species at Wild Beast Point — encounters are reported regularly enough that they're part of how the site got its name. Hammerhead sightings are also reported, though less commonly. Large snapper and other schooling fish gather in the current on the point. Beyond these specifically-reported species, we don't have a source confirming additional invertebrate or reef-fish life unique to this site.

## Dive Profile

This is a deep, current-exposed dive requiring careful management from the start. Current sweeps around the point, and fish either work with it or shelter in the relative calm of nearby Maomao Bay — plan your route with that pattern in mind. Watch for bronze whalers cruising in the blue; a slow, controlled approach gives the best chance of an unrushed encounter. Depth and current together make buoyancy control demanding, so this is not a dive to attempt without solid advanced skills already in place.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride with a full briefing on current direction and depth. Your guide will brief on where sharks are typically encountered and how to approach them. Exit via ascent with a safety stop and boat pickup — maintain buddy contact throughout given the current.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Advanced skills genuinely required**: this is not a site to attempt on a first Poor Knights trip
- **Shark etiquette**: approach bronze whalers calmly and let them dictate the distance — never chase or corner them
- **Current respect**: work with the current rather than fighting it; use Maomao Bay as a natural refuge if needed
- **Check conditions with your operator**: this site is only run when conditions suit advanced diving

## Safety Considerations

- Advanced open water certification with genuine current and depth experience required
- Buoyancy control here is harder than at most Poor Knights sites due to the combined depth/current
- Cold-water thermal protection required
- Maintain strong buddy contact — the current can separate divers quickly
- If sharks are encountered, do not attempt to touch or corner them; let your guide manage the interaction

## Photography

Wide-angle is the right choice here — bronze whalers and schooling snapper are typically encountered in open water rather than close against the wall. Position yourself with the current rather than fighting it to stay stable for a shot. This is a demanding site to shoot in given the current, so prioritize safety and buoyancy over the shot.
