## Wild Beast Point

Wild Beast Point is the northernmost tip of Tawhiti Rahi Island, where the island splits the current -- seafriends.org.nz describes it as "a cauldron of turbulence and wild water." A sloping kelp platform gives way to a steep abyss on the western side, dropping toward the 70-metre depths of neighbouring Maomao Bay. It's a serious current-driven advanced dive, not simply a dramatic-sounding name.

## Overview

Wild Beast Point sits where converging currents create genuinely turbulent water -- boats cannot anchor here and have to idle around the site rather than mooring, per seafriends.org.nz. Divers descend from a sloping kelp platform in the shallows to a steep drop-off on the western side, with relative shelter available there compared to the fully exposed eastern face. The wall hosts golfball sponges, yellow antler sponges, and beadlet coral, with pink maomao gathering in numbers in the deeper water. Shoals of fish are drawn into the nutrient-rich current the point generates.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern tip, Tawhiti Rahi Island, Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 24 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 14-17°C (winter/May-Sept), 19-25°C (summer/Nov-April)
- **Current**: Strong and often turbulent -- the site's defining characteristic, caused by the current splitting around the point
- **Best Time**: May to September for visibility; conditions here are current-dependent year-round

## Marine Life

Golfball sponges, yellow antler sponges, and beadlet coral cover the wall. Pink maomao gather in numbers in the deeper water off the point. Shoals of fish move into the nutrient-rich current at the point itself. These species are reported specifically for this site by seafriends.org.nz; broader Poor Knights fauna (kingfish, snapper, blue maomao) likely also passes through given the current, but is not separately confirmed here.

## Dive Profile

Descend onto the sloping kelp platform in the shallows, then work toward the steep drop-off on the western side, which offers relative shelter from the full force of the current compared to the exposed eastern face. Given the depth and current, plan a conservative profile with a clear turnaround point -- the "abyss" beyond the recreational profile continues well past sport-diving limits.

## Entry and Exit

Boats idle near the site rather than anchoring, due to the current. Entry by giant stride on your guide's signal, timed to the current. Deploy an SMB on ascent; exit is a current-aware boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Current respect**: This is a current-driven site by nature -- confirm with your operator that conditions are suitable before booking
- **Depth discipline**: The kelp platform gives way to a steep, much deeper drop -- set and hold a maximum depth before descending
- **Shelter on the west**: If current is strong, the western side offers relative shelter compared to the exposed eastern face

## Safety Considerations

**Requirements:**
- Advanced open water certification with deep dive specialty
- Strong current-handling skills -- boats cannot anchor here, which reflects how exposed the site is
- Excellent buoyancy control and depth discipline given the nearby drop into much deeper water
- Cold-water experience and appropriate thermal protection
- Never dive this site on your first Poor Knights visit
- Always dive with a buddy and maintain close contact given the current
