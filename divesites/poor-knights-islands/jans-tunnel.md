## Jan's Tunnel

Named after Jan Doak, wife of Poor Knights pioneer diver and ocean advocate Wade Doak, Jan's Tunnel is wide enough for a small boat to pass but for its low ceiling. Sources genuinely disagree on how deep and lively this site is -- see the note below before planning a dive here.

## Overview

The naming is well documented: Wade and Jan Doak began diving the Poor Knights in the 1960s, and several nearby features are named for the family (Jan's Tunnel and Brady's Cove, after her son). Beyond that, sources diverge sharply on the site's character. seafriends.org.nz -- effectively the Doak family's own detailed record of the area -- describes Jan's Tunnel as "only a few metres deep," with limited life beyond "spectacularly fat horse anemones and white anemones" on its southern side, plus longfinned boarfish in nearby deeper water and fish using the tunnel as a sheltered "sleepover" spot at night. It widens inside into an enclosed rockpool-like amphitheatre with a second exit under a natural bridge.

By contrast, PADI's user-submitted dive-site listing describes depths from roughly 10m to 30m, a "cenote"-like surfacing chamber with light beams, and a generic marine-life checklist (sharks, whales, dolphins, turtles) that reads more like a template than a site-specific report. Given seafriends.org.nz's authority as the site's namesake's own published record, this page leans toward that account, but the conflict is real and unresolved -- treat the deeper, PADI-style profile with caution until confirmed by another independent source.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 23 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters (regional average)
- **Current**: Sheltered; the tunnel interior is described as calm enough to serve as a fish "sleepover" spot
- **Best Time**: Year-round; the sheltered interior is not weather-dependent the way exposed sites are

## Marine Life

Confirmed for this specific site (seafriends.org.nz): large, healthy horse anemones and white anemones on the tunnel's southern side, described as unusually big because they catch plankton shrimp in the current; longfinned boarfish in nearby deeper water; fish using the tunnel as a sheltered overnight spot, with more activity from night predators after dark. Claims of blue maomao schooling in the darker sections and slipper lobster sightings, present in an earlier version of this page, were not found in any source and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

Given the depth conflict above, follow your dive guide's brief rather than any fixed profile. seafriends.org.nz describes a shallow, sheltered swim past the anemone-covered southern wall into a rockpool-like amphitheatre with a second exit under a natural bridge -- a short, gentle dive rather than a deep penetration.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride to the tunnel entrance. Follow your guide's depth and penetration guidance given the conflicting depth reports for this site.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Ask your operator first**: Given the real conflict in published depth figures, a current operator briefing is more reliable than any fixed number here
- **Look for the anemones**: The horse and white anemones on the southern wall are the best-documented feature of this site
- **Torch recommended**: Useful for the shaded interior sections regardless of which depth profile applies

## Safety Considerations

- Follow your guide's assessment of this site's actual depth and character on the day, given the source conflict
- The tunnel is an overhead environment in places -- maintain awareness of exit points
- Always dive with a buddy
