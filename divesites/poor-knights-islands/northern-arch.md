## Northern Arch

Located at the northern end of Puketuaho Island, Northern Arch is a narrow, deep cleft through a rocky promontory -- seafriends.org.nz describes it as "6-8m wide and 40m tall" -- swept by strong, unpredictable current that supports dense fish schools. Multiple sources describe it as one of the Poor Knights' more hazardous sites due to that current.

## Overview

Northern Arch cuts through the promontory as a strictly parallel cleft, with its base around 36-40 metres (two independent sources -- seafriends.org.nz and divepacific.co.nz -- agree on roughly this figure; an earlier version of this record listed 50m, which is not supported by either source and has been corrected). The anchoring bay nearby runs to around 70 metres. Boulders and rock on either side drop away steeply beyond the arch itself.

seafriends.org.nz describes Northern Arch as "one of the most dangerous" Poor Knights sites, citing multiple diver fatalities associated with its strong and unpredictable current over the years -- a serious characterization from a single detailed source that this review could not independently corroborate with a second source, so it should be treated as one expert account rather than an established count. What is corroborated by a second, independent source (divepacific.co.nz) is that the current here is genuinely strong and the arch genuinely deep -- this is not a casual dive.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern end of Puketuaho Island, Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Minimum Depth**: 15 meters (arch entrance)
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 14-17°C (winter/May-Sept), 19-25°C (summer/Nov-April)
- **Current**: Often strong and unpredictable -- described by seafriends.org.nz as one of the site's defining hazards
- **Best Time**: May to September when visibility is best

## Marine Life

seafriends.org.nz specifically documents stingrays, red-orange golden snapper, pink maomao, blue maomao, blue-and-green two-spot demoiselles, and grey porae at Northern Arch, along with beadlet corals and golfball sponges on the deeper rock. This is the source-confirmed list for this site; broader claims of kingfish or giant trevally here were not independently verified and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

This is a deep, current-swept arch dive requiring careful depth management. Descend to the arch (15-40m) and navigate the archway while monitoring depth closely -- the structure is narrow (6-8m wide) but tall (around 40m), and the bouldery bottom on either side drops away steeply beyond the site's working depth. Given the reported hazard history, a conservative profile, close buddy contact, and a guide experienced with current-reading at this specific site are strongly advised.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride. Your guide will brief on current direction, depth hazards, and safe descent procedures given the site's reputation for strong, unpredictable current. Exit via ascent with safety stops and boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Deep dive experience required**: Base depth of 36-40m -- verify your qualifications
- **Respect the current**: seafriends.org.nz's account of this site's hazard history is reason enough to treat current warnings seriously here
- **Cold water certification**: 5mm+ wetsuit or drysuit essential
- **Book with experienced guides**: Ask operators about current conditions before committing to this site on the day

## Safety Considerations

**Requirements:**
- Advanced open water certification with deep dive specialty
- Cold-water experience and appropriate thermal protection
- Excellent buoyancy control and depth awareness
- Strong current awareness and positioning skills -- this site has a reported history of serious incidents in strong current
- Never dive alone at this site; never dive it as your first Poor Knights arch
- Current can increase difficulty unexpectedly -- abort if conditions worsen
