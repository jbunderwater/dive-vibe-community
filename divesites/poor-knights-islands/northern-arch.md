## Northern Arch

Located at the northern end of Tawhiti Rahi on the tip of Te Araara Point, Northern Arch is regarded as one of the most spectacular arches at the Poor Knights Islands — too small to boat through, but a dramatic underwater passage. The arch is exposed to strong, current-fed conditions that support exceptional marine life. The arch itself bottoms out at around 40 meters, with the bouldery terrain on either side dropping beyond 50 meters and, by some accounts, as deep as 70 meters or more.

## Overview

Northern Arch is a short but very deep archway with an inverted-cone profile — overhangs and nooks on one side, a sheerer face on the other. The current that runs through it is exactly what makes the site spectacular: it delivers a steady supply of nutrients that feeds dense aggregations of fish and supports rich encrusting growth on the arch walls. Off the tip of the point, schooling fish patrol the open water, and divers occasionally get shark sightings looking out into the blue.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern end of Tawhiti Rahi, at Te Araara Point, Poor Knights Islands, Northland, New Zealand
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: The arch base is around 40 meters; the surrounding bouldery terrain drops beyond 50 meters and, per some accounts, to 70+ meters
- **Minimum Depth**: 15 meters (arch entrance)
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 meters
- **Water Temperature**: 14-17°C (winter/May-Sept), 19-25°C (summer/Nov-April)
- **Current**: Often strong and consistent—factor this into dive planning
- **Best Time**: May to September when visibility is best

## Marine Life

Northern Arch is documented as home to dense schools of pink and blue maomao, along with demoiselles, sweep, mado, and snapper holding in the current — species reported specifically for this site by dive charter descriptions. Golden snapper schools have also been reported here in the past, and divers occasionally spot a shark cruising the blue off the point. Beyond fish, the current-fed walls support colourful sponges and encrusting growth typical of current-swept Poor Knights sites, though we don't have a site-specific inventory of exactly which invertebrate species grow here.

## Dive Profile

This is a deep arch dive requiring careful depth management. Descend to the arch entrance (15-40m) and navigate the archway, keeping your depth monitored closely. The arch itself is short but very deep. The bouldery bottom on either side of the arch drops steeply beyond 50 meters, so stay focused on the structure rather than following it into deeper water. Allow time in shallower water (15-25m) to observe fish life without pushing depth limits. Current can be strong, so maintain excellent buoyancy and buddy contact. Plan conservative bottom times and adequate safety stops.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry via giant stride. Your guide will brief on current direction, depth hazards, and safe descent procedures. The arch passage is wide enough for comfortable navigation but depth requires focus. Exit via ascent with safety stops and boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations

- **Deep dive experience required**: This is a 40m+ site—verify your qualifications
- **Expect current**: The current that makes this site special also requires excellent buoyancy and positioning
- **Cold water certification**: 5mm+ wetsuit or drysuit essential
- **Conservative depth management**: Plan dives to 40m maximum with good safety stop time
- **Buddy contact essential**: Current and depth increase the importance of buddy awareness
- **Best fish viewing at 20-35 meters**: This is the depth range where schools concentrate
- **Book with experienced guides**: Ask operators about recent conditions and current strength

## Safety Considerations

**Requirements:**
- Advanced open water certification with deep dive specialty
- Cold-water experience and appropriate thermal protection
- Excellent buoyancy control and depth awareness
- Awareness of nitrogen narcosis potential below 30m
- Conservative air consumption planning
- Strong current awareness and positioning skills
- Never dive alone at this site
- Monitor your air pressure continuously
- Understand your personal depth limits and respect them
- Current can increase difficulty unexpectedly—abort if conditions worsen

## Photography

Wide-angle shots of the arch structure with fish schools are striking. The interplay of current and fish creates dynamic compositions. Macro photography of wall-encrusting organisms is possible. Position yourself carefully in the current to maintain stable shooting position. Best photography occurs at 25-35m where fish life is dense but light is still strong.
