## Rhone

**Data-placement note**: This wreck lies off Salt Island in the British Virgin Islands, not within US Virgin Islands waters. It is included in this destination's dataset because it is commonly paired with USVI charters, but it is geographically a BVI site and this record has been flagged for placement review rather than silently corrected, since removing it is outside the scope of this QA pass.

The RMS Rhone is widely regarded as the Caribbean's most famous wreck dive, a Royal Mail Steam Packet Company ship lost in the 1867 hurricane that split into two sections off Salt Island.

## Overview

The Rhone was built by Millwall Iron Works on the Isle of Dogs, London, and launched on 11 February 1865 — an iron-hulled steamer 310 feet (94m) long with a 40-foot (12m) beam and 2,738 gross registered tons. On 29 October 1867, while anchored near Great Harbour with the storm season believed to be over, she was caught by the second half of a hurricane; the wind shifted and drove her onto Black Rock Point, where her boilers, still under steam, ruptured on contact with cold seawater and broke the ship in two. Of the roughly 145 passengers and crew aboard, only 25 survived. The bow section now lies at about 24 meters (80 feet) and the stern in about 9 meters (30 feet), with sources giving a maximum depth in the 24-26 meter range. The wreck is well preserved — propeller, mast fittings, portholes, and other fittings remain recognizable — and its combination of shallow and deep sections lets divers of varying experience levels explore different parts of the site. The wreck was used during filming of the 1977 film "The Deep."
