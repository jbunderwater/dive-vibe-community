## WIT Shoal II Wreck

The WIT Shoal II is a large steel hulk resting upright on a sand plain off St. Thomas — not the "210-foot hopper dredge" described in an earlier version of this page.

## Overview

Wikipedia's article on USS LST-467 identifies the wreck as a Landing Ship, Tank built by Kaiser Shipyards in Vancouver, Washington and commissioned on 3 March 1943. She was decommissioned in 1946 and sold into civilian service, later converted to a log hauler in Quebec, renamed Frank J. Humphrey, and by 1984 operated as the inter-island freighter WIT Shoal II. Per Wikipedia, Tropical Storm Klaus damaged her in Krum Bay, St. Thomas, in November 1984; she was patched and raised for a tow to Puerto Rico for scrapping, but a patch failed in May 1985 and she sank about 2.2 miles west of Saba Island. Dive-tourism sources describe the 1984 Klaus event somewhat differently (e.g. sinking while carrying cement), so details of the sinking vary between sources. Length is reported as 328 feet (100 m) by Wikipedia, while local dive sites commonly cite about 400 feet. Depth is commonly given as about 27 meters (90 feet), with some sources giving as shallow as 21 meters; the top of the wreck is described as rising roughly 70 feet (21 m) from the bottom.

## Site Information

- **Location**: US Virgin Islands, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters

## Marine Life

The wreck is heavily colonized with orange cup coral and rises roughly 21 meters (70 feet) across five decks. Stormy Pirates reports a resident barracuda nicknamed "Baz", horse-eye jacks, turtles, rays, and sharks. Penetration of the interior spaces requires proper wreck training and equipment and always carries risk.
