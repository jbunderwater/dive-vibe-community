## WIT Shoal II Wreck

The WIT Shoal II is a large steel wreck, originally a WWII tank landing ship, resting upright in about 27 meters off St. Thomas.

## Overview

Wikipedia identifies the wreck as the former USS LST-467, laid down 17 October 1942 at Kaiser Shipyards in Vancouver, Washington, launched 21 November 1942, and commissioned 3 March 1943. She was decommissioned in 1946, sold, converted to a log hauler, and later renamed WIT Shoal II under the West Indies Trading Company. Wikipedia gives her length as 328 feet; Visit USVI says about 330 feet, whereas DreamWrecks says 400 feet, so sources report between roughly 100 and 122 meters. On the sinking, sources partly agree and partly conflict: Wikipedia reports she was wrecked by Tropical Storm Klaus in Krum Bay on 6 November 1984, then patched and raised for towing to Puerto Rico for scrapping, and that a patch broke loose under tow in May 1985, after which she sank about 2.2 miles west of Saba Island in about 90 feet (27 meters). DreamWrecks and Visit USVI also describe a hull patch failing under tow to Puerto Rico, while Dolphin Water Taxi gives only 1984 and Klaus and calls her a freighter carrying cement. This page therefore reports the date as 1984-1985 depending on source. All three dive sources place her at about 90 feet, upright on sand, and Dolphin Water Taxi gives her height as about 70 feet. Visit USVI rates the dive Intermediate. Interior penetration requires proper wreck training and equipment and carries real risk.

## Marine Life

DreamWrecks reports barracuda, turtles, southern stingrays, goliath grouper, reef sharks, a school of horse-eyed jacks at the stern, and orange cup coral covering much of the wreck. Visit USVI lists southern stingrays, gray reef sharks, and a large goliath grouper by the engine room; Dolphin Water Taxi lists barracuda, moray eels, and reef sharks.
