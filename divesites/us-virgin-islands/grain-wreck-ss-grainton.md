## Grain Wreck (SS Grainton)

The "Grain Wreck" off St. Thomas is the SS Grainton, a British cargo steamer that ran aground and sank in 1928 — a different vessel from the Suffolk Maid, which this listing was previously (and incorrectly) merged with.

## Overview

The Suffolk Maid, a 144-foot North Sea trawler scuttled in December 1985 in Butler Bay off St. Croix, is a separate wreck from this site. The name "Grain Wreck" and the coordinates recorded here correspond to the SS Grainton, a British steam cargo ship built in 1911 by Ropner at Stockton-on-Tees (6,042 gross tons; sources report a length of about 415-416 feet). She was bound from Vancouver to Hamburg when she struck rocks off Little Saba (Dry Rocks) in 1928 and then sank while under tow off St. Thomas. Sources differ on the date: one earlier account gives January 30, 1928, while MaritimeQuest's ship record lists May 31, 1928, so the exact date is treated here as unconfirmed. MaritimeQuest places the loss about 6 miles southwest of Charlotte Amalie. Dive sources report a depth of roughly 33-34 meters (108-110 feet); this is a deep, advanced-level dive, and the boilers and steam engine are described as largely intact. No second independent source for the vessel's builder or tonnage beyond MaritimeQuest was found, so those figures should be treated as single-source.

## Site Information

- **Location**: US Virgin Islands, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters

## Marine Life

Stormy Pirates reports barracuda, sharks, moray eels, and goliath groupers at the wreck, along with abundant coral and sponge growth. These species are attributed to that single source; other species named in earlier versions of this page (snapper, eagle rays, crustaceans) were not confirmed by a site-specific source and have been omitted.
