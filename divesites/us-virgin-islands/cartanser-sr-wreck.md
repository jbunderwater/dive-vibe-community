## Cartanser Sr Wreck

The Cartanser Sr (also documented as the Cartanza Senora or Cartanza) is a shallow, coral-encrusted wreck resting near Buck Island off St. Thomas — not, as an earlier version of this page claimed, in the BVI's Sir Francis Drake Channel.

## Overview

Multiple St. Thomas dive-tourism sources place the Cartanser Sr's origins as a WWII-era vessel, later put into service as a cargo/agricultural transport; one source gives her length as roughly 190 feet, though this figure appears in only one of the sources consulted here and should be treated as approximate rather than confirmed. According to accounts repeated by several local operators, the ship's crew allegedly used her to move contraband and abandoned her near St. Thomas to avoid a raid by local authorities; sources are not precise about when the original sinking occurred, but agree it was some time before 1979, when the hulk was moved by barge to a cove near Buck Island (a small island off St. Thomas, distinct from the Buck Island Reef National Monument off St. Croix). Hurricane Hugo broke the wreck apart in 1989, and it now rests in several pieces. Sources report the current depth variously as 12-18 meters (40-60 feet); this listing keeps the previously recorded 14 meters, which falls within that range. Local dive shops organized a round-the-clock underwater occupation of the site in the 1970s to stop the Army Corps of Engineers from demolishing it as a navigational hazard, and a plaque honoring dive-shop owner Bill Letts, who led that effort, was installed at the site in 2005.

## Marine Life

The wreck is a documented breeding ground for sergeant major fish, which lay eggs directly on its hull and defend the purple egg patches aggressively. It is also home to yellow-headed jawfish, which burrow tail-first into the surrounding sand. An earlier version of this page claimed hawksbill turtles are "frequently seen resting on the deck" — no source consulted for this update confirms turtles at this specific site, so that claim has been removed.
