## Cartanser Sr Wreck

The Cartanser Sr (also written Cartanza Senora) is a shallow, coral-encrusted wreck in Shipwreck Cove at Buck Island off St. Thomas.

## Overview

This is the Buck Island off St. Thomas, not Buck Island Reef National Monument off St. Croix. Visit USVI and a St. Croix author's account both describe a 190-foot World War II-era freighter later used as an agricultural transport; Dolphin Water Taxi and Stormy Pirates agree on the WWII origin and agricultural service. Those same sources repeat a story that the crew turned to smuggling and abandoned the ship ahead of a raid, which is presented here as reported lore. Sources disagree on how she sank: Virgin Islands Charter Yachts says she was originally sunk by divers, while others say she floated for some time and then sank in the harbor. All agree she was later moved from Charlotte Amalie harbor to the cove at Buck Island. One source (a personal blog) dates the move to 16 June 1979; that date is uncorroborated here. The same blog and a News of St. John article seen in search results say local dive shops led by Bill Letts protested a planned Army Corps of Engineers demolition. Stormy Pirates says Hurricane Hugo (September 1989) broke the wreck into several pieces. Reported piece counts vary (three, four, or 'several') and both engines remain visible (Stormy Pirates, Dolphin Water Taxi). Depth is reported at 20-60 feet (Virgin Islands Charter Yachts), about 45 feet (Visit USVI), and about 50 feet (blog), so this page keeps 14 meters.

## Site Information

- **Location**: US Virgin Islands, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters

## Marine Life

Virgin Islands Charter Yachts reports sergeant major fish laying eggs on the wreck and yellowhead jawfish living in the sand. Dolphin Water Taxi and Visit USVI both report sea turtles and reef sharks; additionally named are barracuda and an occasional eagle ray (Dolphin Water Taxi), and octopus and pufferfish (Visit USVI).
