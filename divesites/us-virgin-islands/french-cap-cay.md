## French Cap Cay

French Cap Cay is an exposed offshore pinnacle roughly 6 miles south of St. Thomas, reachable only in calm conditions and reserved for advanced divers.

## Overview

French Cap is an uninhabited island whose namesake dive site is a pinnacle rising from around 30 meters (100 feet) toward the surface — a formation local operators specifically call "French Cap Pinnacle," which is why this listing has been reclassified from "reef" to "pinnacle." Because the site is unprotected and exposed to open ocean, boats only run trips there in relatively calm conditions. Operators commonly run two dives here: a shallower dive to about 18 meters (60 feet) on the island's northwest side, and a deeper dive around the central pinnacle that includes a swim-through with a "cathedral" ceiling divers can surface into. An earlier version of this page described three depth zones at 35, 65, and 95 feet with specific figures not confirmed by the sources used for this update; those specific numbers have been replaced with the two-dive profile that sources do confirm.

## Site Information

- **Location**: US Virgin Islands, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

Sources report sharks, queen angelfish, barracuda, green moray eels, lionfish, and schools of fish around the pinnacle, along with abundant lobster and larger game fish such as hogfish and permit. An earlier version of this page named black triggerfish, octopus, and sea turtles at this site; no source consulted for this update confirms those species here, so they have been removed rather than carried forward unverified.
