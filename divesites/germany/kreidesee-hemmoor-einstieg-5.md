## Kreidesee Hemmoor - Einstieg 5

The northern entry along the steep north wall, for experienced divers, with a boat wreck, a cable drum and plenty of crayfish.

## Overview

The Kreidesee in Hemmoor (Lower Saxony, on the B73 between Cuxhaven and Hamburg) is a flooded chalk quarry. Chalk was extracted here from 1862 to 1976; the pit, which had reached a depth of almost 120 m, filled with water between 1976 and 1982, and divers discovered it from 1986 onwards. The lake measures roughly 1,300 x 500 m. Its maximum depth is given as about 60 m (water-level dependent) by the on-site dive base and 58 m by Taucher.Net. Numerous objects have been placed for divers - one source counts about 41 objects spread across the entry points. Diving is managed by the dive base on the western shore and requires a day dive permit.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Kreidesee, Hemmoor, Landkreis Cuxhaven, Lower Saxony
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 55 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Average 10-20 m at the entries; up to 40 m in optimal conditions, 5-10 m in poor conditions (best mid-week, Tuesday-Thursday)
- **Current**: None
- **Best Time**: Year-round with drysuit (the dive base operates all year); wetsuit diving April-October

## Underwater Features

Taucher.Net describes a steep wall on the north shore that drops almost vertically to 35 m and then slopes to 55 m. Pro-Taucher lists a drop-off, a boat wreck, a cable drum and abundant crayfish for Entry 5 and recommends heading left along the drop-off. OSM maps a debris field (10 m), small caves (10 m), 'Kabelrolle II' (35 m), a boat (40 m, position estimated), an ultralight aircraft (45 m, position estimated), a forest (25-55 m) and 'Steilhang II' (10-55 m) near this entry. Taucher.Net also lists an ultralight aircraft among the lake's objects; its position and depth are only estimated in OSM.

## Marine Life

The dive base lists trout, roach, perch, stickleback, eel and whitefish, plus freshwater mussels, snails, sponges, crayfish and (seasonally) amphibians. Top Dive reports char (See-Saibling) and salmon trout reaching 60-80 cm. Pro-Taucher notes trout throughout the lake and abundant crayfish, especially around Entry 5.

## Dive Profile

Follow the wall to the left at a depth that suits your certification; the wall offers a quick route to deep water.

## Entry and Exit

Access is a 3-5 minute walk from the base (Pro-Taucher). The dive base classes Entries 2 and 5 for experienced divers. Every diver needs a day dive permit (Tauchgenehmigung) from the on-site dive base; published day-fee figures differ between sources (EUR 11 per Top Dive and Pro-Taucher, EUR 20 per a 2020 blog), so check current prices. Top Dive lists cold-water-suitable equipment, a dive computer and two independently closable valves/first stages as mandatory. Pro-Taucher reports that checks of divers and dive computers are not uncommon and that the permitted maximum depth depends on certification level. Taucher.Net describes the entries as equipped with floating pontoons and stairs.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Head left along the drop-off (Pro-Taucher)
- Watch your computer on the wall - depth builds up quickly

## Safety Considerations

Water below the thermocline stays cold all year; a drysuit is needed outside the summer months (wetsuit diving is described as practical only April-October). Good visibility can make divers underestimate depth - the lake drops to about 60 m and several objects lie well below recreational limits. Stay within your certification and the depth limits set by the dive base.
