## Schweriner See - Lastensegler

A historic wooden cargo sailing vessel lying upright near the island of Ziegelwerder, dived by boat under a heritage-authority permit.

## Overview

The 'Lastensegler' is an old cargo sailing vessel (a so-called Kaffeekahn) resting upright on the bottom of the Schweriner See near the island of Ziegelwerder. Sources describe it as around 25-27 m long and very well preserved, with its anchor winch, a mast about 7 m tall, the cargo hold and the remains of a cabin still recognisable. Its age is not firmly established in the sources: taucher.net describes it as a Kaffeekahn from the early years of the 20th century and roughly a century old, while one taucher.net review of the Raben Steinfeld base calls it a 1927 cargo ship. The wreck is a protected monument.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near Ziegelwerder island, Schweriner Innensee, Schwerin, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 18 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Not documented for the wreck; taucher.net notes visibility is sometimes poor
- **Current**: None reported (lake)
- **Best Time**: Not documented

## Underwater Features

Upright hull with anchor winch, mast (about 7 m), cargo hold and cabin remains with furnishings, according to the dive-schwerin.de description. A mooring line leads directly down to the wreck (taucher.net).

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive only; the buoy line leads to the wreck. The wreck is a listed monument and dives require permission from the Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state heritage authority (Landesdenkmalpflege); taucher.net notes that the Raben Steinfeld dive base holds an annual authorisation and organises the dives.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Dive it with the local base that holds the heritage permit
- Keep off the wreck structure to avoid damage and silt-outs

## Safety Considerations

Taucher.net rates the site for advanced divers. Penetrating the hull is not recommended and would require proper training and equipment. Boat traffic on the lake requires a controlled ascent on the buoy line.
