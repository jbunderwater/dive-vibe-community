## Starnberger See - Allmannshausen Seeburg

A year-round wall entry at Allmannshausen where the Allmannshauser Steilwand drops away along the Starnberger See's east shore.

## Overview

The Seeburg / Kapelle entry at Allmannshausen gives access to the northern part of the Allmannshauser Steilwand, the steep underwater wall on the east shore of the Starnberger See. ColdWater Films describes an edge that descends to roughly 30-40 m over about 20 minutes of diving northwards, and lists the site as open year-round. Tauchclub Bamberg describes rocky formations visible from about 15 m to 40 m and deeper, an iron chain leading to a concrete block at about 15 m, and crevices along the wall.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Allmannshausen, below the Seeburg / small chapel, east shore of the Starnberger See
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 8-10 m on average, up to 30 m at best (Tauchclub Bamberg)
- **Current**: Not documented
- **Best Time**: Year-round according to ColdWater Films

## Underwater Features

- Iron chain leading to a concrete block at about 15 m (Tauchclub Bamberg)
- Steep rocky formations with crevices from about 15 m down (Tauchclub Bamberg)
- Underwater vegetation belt at 3-6 m in summer (Tauchclub Bamberg)
- Deep features beyond the 40 m air limit: a stone pile with a small statue at about 70 m and the wooden wrecks "Gertrud" (about 70 m) and "Josefine" (reported at over 80 m) (ColdWater Films; the Starnberger See travel guide also places the Josefine at 80 m). Sources disagree on the Josefine's size, so no dimensions are given here.

## Marine Life

Tauchclub Bamberg reports perch, pike and crayfish in summer, eels and burbot in the crevices, and char gathering in large numbers at 10-25 m during the autumn spawning season.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the chain and follow the wall edge northwards; ColdWater Films notes it reaches about 30-40 m over roughly 20 minutes. Turn around at or above 40 m, which is the legal limit for air.

## Entry and Exit

The road to the Allmannshausen entries requires an access permit (Zufahrtsgenehmigung); ColdWater Films lists this for all Allmannshausen spots. Tauchclub Bamberg describes entry via a small footpath down to the lake near the chapel or pebble beach; the road is residents-only, so equipment should be unloaded and the car driven back up to the forest edge. Diving in the Starnberger See is regulated by a general order (Allgemeinverfügung) of the Landratsamt Starnberg. Its key rules: solo dives are prohibited; diving with compressed air is limited to 40 m; diving is allowed until one hour after sunset; divers must carry cold-water-rated equipment with two separate, independently shut-off regulators (EN 250), thermal protection and a hood (a dive computer is recommended). A safety distance of at least 100 m must be kept from harbours, shipping lanes and landing stages, and private boat facilities, fish spawning areas, fishing gear and net enclosures are off limits.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The autumn char spawning season is highlighted by Tauchclub Bamberg as a special time to dive here
- Plan depth and gas carefully - the wall invites going deeper than intended

## Safety Considerations

The Allmannshausen wall is the accident hotspot of the lake: the Allgemeinverfügung cites 11 fatal diving accidents and 35 serious injuries since 1994, mainly around the Allmannshausen wall, and the Starnberger See travel guide attributes around 20 deaths over two decades to nitrogen narcosis. Depths beyond 40 m require mixed gas and appropriate technical training. Only experienced divers should dive here.
