## Schluchsee

A dark, peat-stained reservoir in the Black Forest at about 930 m altitude, dived from Seebrugg near the dam, with submerged remains of the pre-1932 lake shore.

## Overview

The Schluchsee is a Black Forest mountain lake about 7.5 km long and 1 km wide. It is of glacial origin and was dammed at Seebrugg in 1932 to its present size. According to a diver's report on quadruvium.club, the lake was covered with peat bales during the Second World War to protect the dam from Allied bombing; the bales became waterlogged, sank and still lie on the bottom, where they swallow light and are easily stirred up. The lake's deepest point is given as 62 m, but the dived areas reach about 26-30 m (taucher.net lists 26 m as the site maximum). Because the lake sits at around 930 m, every dive counts as an altitude dive. Registration is not required to dive, according to 200bar.de; a dive base has operated in the Seebrugg railway station building.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Seebrugg, Schluchsee, Baden-Württemberg (south end of the lake near the dam)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Poor: 1.5-4 m reported (200bar.de, Oct 2016), 2-3 m with a good light (taucher.net); dark below about 8-12 m
- **Current**: Not documented
- **Best Time**: Not documented (200bar.de reports about 18-20 °C at 10 m in summer, cooling rapidly with depth)

## Underwater Features

Sources describe peat formations with labyrinth-like passages and fallen trees, the old (pre-1932) dam wall with the former postal road (Poststraße) and a signal box, submerged building foundations, a sunken wooden boat and an underwater gallery installed in May 2000 that can be reached from the shore. The old wall and postal road lie at the original outflow of the natural lake; haubenliftler.de describes reaching it from a separate entry to the left of the Blasiwald yacht club, where the wall lies at about 18 m at a water level of 26.5 m. 200bar.de notes that the postal road is best reached by boat.

## Marine Life

200bar.de lists pike, perch, roach, whitefish and eels; quadruvium.club reports fish from about 10 m and photographed an eel. taucher.net describes little plant growth and few fish overall.

## Dive Profile

Beginners are best kept to the shore zone down to about 10-12 m. Below roughly 12 m every dive is effectively a night dive regardless of the time of day, so navigation is by compass, depth and time. Because the lake is a reservoir, the water level (readable at the dam) must be checked before each dive and planned depths adjusted.

## Entry and Exit

At Seebrugg, divers cross the road from the station building to a staircase that leads down into the water. The dive base has charged a small daily fee (sources quote EUR 3.50 and EUR 5.00 at different times) and keeps a sign-in/sign-out list; certification details are requested. Parking is available at Seebrugg. For the old-wall site near the dam there is usually no parking at the entry: gear is unloaded at the turning area across the dam and cars are left at a large car park about 400 m away (haubenliftler.de).

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a strong primary light and a backup; natural light disappears below about 8-12 m
- Practise compass navigation before diving here; visual orientation is practically impossible away from the shore
- Keep good buoyancy control: stirred-up peat can turn the water black
- A dry suit is recommended year-round (200bar.de); taucher.net considered a 4 mm wetsuit with hood sufficient in summer
- Consider a guided dive for a first visit

## Safety Considerations

This is an altitude lake (about 930 m), so altitude-adjusted decompression procedures apply. Visibility can drop to zero within seconds when sediment is disturbed, and a passenger boat line operates near the dam site, so check the timetable. The reservoir's water level changes, which alters actual depths at the underwater structures. Sources on this lake date from 2009-2016; check current dive-base status and conditions before travelling.
