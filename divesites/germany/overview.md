## Germany

Freshwater diving in flooded chalk pits and quarries, post-mining lakes, reservoirs, and alpine lakes with steep walls, sunken wrecks, and training platforms.

## Description

Germany's recreational diving takes place almost entirely in inland waters: former chalk and stone quarries (Kreidesee Hemmoor, Steinbruch Horka, Ammelshain), flooded lignite pits in central and eastern Germany (Geiseltalsee, Goitzsche, Helenesee, Kulkwitzer See), reservoirs (Edersee, Sorpesee, Möhnesee, Rursee, Diemelsee), glacial lakes in the north (Großer Plöner See, Schweriner See, Stechlinsee, Werbellinsee), and alpine and pre-alpine lakes in the south (Walchensee, Starnberger See, Tegernsee, Bodensee). Many sites have training platforms and sunken objects placed for divers; a few have genuine wrecks.

### Access Rules

Diving access is regulated lake by lake, and the rules differ between federal states. Many waters allow diving only at designated entry points or buoyed zones, some only in certain seasons, and some require registration, a day fee, a permit, or club membership. In Bavaria, diving with breathing apparatus is only allowed in waters released by the district authority. Each site page in this destination notes the conditions its sources document. Always check the current rules with the local dive base, club, or authority before diving.

### Waters Not Included

Several well-known lakes are deliberately not listed because diving is prohibited: Königssee, Alatsee, Eibsee, Laacher See, Pulvermaar, Weinfelder Maar, Gemündener Maar, the Schaalsee biosphere reserve lakes, and the Wolfssee (Wambachsee is the only diveable lake of Duisburg's Sechs-Seen-Platte). Other lakes where diving is not banned but no entry point or site is documented (for example Titisee, Schliersee, Scharmützelsee, Wittensee, Kellersee, Breiter Luzin, Markkleeberger See) are left out until reliable site information is available.

## Additional Information

- **Water Conditions**: Cold freshwater. Most sites have a pronounced summer thermocline, with cold water below it all year. Visibility changes a lot with season and algae blooms.
- **Equipment**: Sources for many sites recommend dry suits and two independent cold-water regulators.
- **Altitude**: Alpine lakes such as Walchensee (about 800 m) and Schluchsee (about 930 m) need altitude diving procedures.
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR)
- **Language**: German
