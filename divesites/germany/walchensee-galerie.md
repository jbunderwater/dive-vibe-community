## Walchensee - Galerie

Walchensee's classic wall dive, a vertical drop-off beginning right at the surface along the B11 between Urfeld and the village of Walchensee.

## Overview

The Galerie is described by all local sources as the lake's signature site: a steep wall that falls almost from 0 m along the whole length of the roadside parking area, with rock formations and overhangs. Tauchclub Bamberg stresses that it is not a site for beginners or unpractised divers, and besser-tauchen.de notes it attracts many technical divers because of the depth available directly from shore. Walchensee is an alpine lake at about 800 m altitude in the Bavarian Prealps (Landkreis Bad Tölz-Wolfratshausen), with a maximum depth reported between about 192 m (Wikipedia) and 196-198 m (local dive sources). Diving is permitted here under a legal ordinance of the Landratsamt Bad Tölz-Wolfratshausen, but only at the released sites and times; several sites are closed from 15 October to 1 February. The lake feeds the Walchensee hydroelectric plant, so the water level is drawn down in winter (dive sources cite 4-5 m; Wikipedia gives a range between 795 m and 802 m), which changes entries noticeably.

## Site Information

- **Location**: West shore of Walchensee on the B11 between Urfeld and Walchensee village, Kochel am See
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Not documented for this site; local dive bases describe 10-15 m as normal for the lake, varying with season and weather
- **Current**: None documented
- **Best Time**: 1 February to 14 October only - diving is closed from 15 October to 1 February (spawning protection for char, per besser-tauchen.de)

## Underwater Features

An old car wreck lies near the right-hand (second) water-level tower at about 36 m in summer and about 32 m in winter at low water (Tauchclub Bamberg; ColdWater Films gives 36-38 m). Sources disagree on the make - besser-tauchen.de reports it has been called both a Ford and a Lloyd. besser-tauchen.de also mentions an old Fiat at about 95 m, far beyond recreational limits. The scree slope at the front entry has boulders and sunken trees.

## Marine Life

Tauchclub Bamberg and besser-tauchen.de both report char (Saibling) on the scree slope, especially towards autumn. For the lake as a whole, local dive bases list lake trout, char (Saibling), pike, whitefish (Renke), perch, burbot (Aalrutte) and bullhead (Koppe).

## Dive Profile

Three entries are described along the parking area (viewed facing the lake): the front water-level tower on the left, a stone staircase in the middle, and the second (large) water-level tower on the right. From the middle staircase the wall starts at 0 m; from the front tower a boulder slope with sunken trees leads right towards the wall; from the rear tower a scree slope leads down to the car wreck. Sources emphasise that the wall tempts divers deeper and that 40 m should be the recreational limit.

## Entry and Exit

Shore entry from the roadside parking area. Parking is limited and shared with many windsurfers, so local sources recommend arriving very early. The staircase entry is easy in summer, but with the winter water level lowered by several metres the exit becomes strenuous, and a rope is recommended at the front tower entry in winter. If the Galerie parking is full, besser-tauchen.de describes "Galerie dazwischen", a little further along, as an alternative entry over a scree slope from which the wall can be reached, with remains of the old road embankment and a shallow area often holding perch and large pike.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Arrive very early - parking is limited and popular with surfers
- Enter at the middle staircase for the wall itself; use the right-hand tower for the car wreck
- Plan and hold a firm maximum depth before descending - the wall continues far beyond recreational limits
- Respect the closure from 15 October to 1 February

## Safety Considerations

Tauchclub Bamberg notes that there have already been several fatalities at this site. The bottomless wall demands excellent buoyancy control and strict depth discipline. Walchensee lies at roughly 800 m altitude, so altitude (Bergsee) decompression procedures apply. Local sources describe the water as very cold (about 4-8 °C) and recommend a dry suit and two independent cold-water regulators on a twin-outlet valve; lights are also listed as mandatory kit by one local source. Night diving is only allowed from one hour before sunrise until one hour after dark.
