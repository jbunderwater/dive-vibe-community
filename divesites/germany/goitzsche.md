## Goitzsche

A huge flooded lignite mine near Bitterfeld with plant-covered shallows, steep mining walls, sunken objects and depths to about 70 m, dived from a base at the Bernstein marina.

## Overview

The Großer Goitzschesee near Bitterfeld was formed from several lignite pits that were flooded within two days by the Mulde flood of 2002; the river water buffered the acidic mine water and brought in plants and fish, according to Das Tauchen Magazin. The lake covers more than 1,300 ha and reaches about 70 m (sources give 70-78 m). The dive base divides the lake into four zones: shallows to 6 m at the jetty, plant meadows and edges from 6 to 18 m, steep mining walls from 18 to 30 m and a deep zone from 30 to 70 m for deep and technical diving. Many of the best spots are reachable only by boat.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Bernstein Marina, Seepromenade, Bitterfeld-Wolfen, Saxony-Anhalt
- **Entry Type**: Shore or boat entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 70 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Highly seasonal: 5-20 m depending on area and season (200bar.de); spring algal bloom reduces visibility, late summer and winter are often clear; one 2015 visitor reported only 2-3 m
- **Current**: Dangerous currents near flooding inlets in some parts of the lake (200bar.de)
- **Best Time**: Late summer and winter for clearer water (dive base)

## Underwater Features

Sources describe submerged forests covered with zebra mussels, two railway tunnels several hundred metres long, a flooded footpath with information boards and land-art objects, a small boat wreck, a telegraph pole and an excavator boom at about 35 m next to a drowned gravel road with pipelines, and training platforms with a guide-line circuit in front of the base. An 8.5 m motorboat wreck was placed at 13 m in front of the former base (Tauchschule Bernsteinsee) in April 2012, according to Das Tauchen Magazin. Which objects are reachable from the current base is not documented; the base discusses sites and entries in its briefing.

## Marine Life

Pike, perch, crayfish, eel and wels are named by the dive base. 200bar.de also lists rudd, carp and zander, and hundreds of small freshwater jellyfish in late summer.

## Entry and Exit

The current base is at the Bernstein Marina (Seepromenade 7, Bitterfeld-Wolfen), with a short entry via the marina ramp; parking at the marina requires a ticket from the machine. A day ticket covers lake entry and diving. According to 200bar.de the whole lake is private property and all dives must be registered with the dive base, some parts of the lake also require registration with the nature conservation authority and are open only at set times to small groups, and diving is prohibited in some areas because of passenger ships and currents at flooding inlets. Boat dives are offered by the base.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Carry an inflatable surface marker buoy at all times; there is boat traffic across the whole lake
- Book a boat dive to reach the forests, tunnels and walls
- Bring a light and a drysuit if you plan to go below 18 m

## Safety Considerations

Steep walls lead quickly into the deep zone, which is cold (about 6-10 °C year-round at depth), dark and suitable only for divers with the right training and equipment. Some areas are closed to diving because of shipping and inlet currents. Diving in tunnels or other overhead environments requires proper cave/wreck training and equipment.
