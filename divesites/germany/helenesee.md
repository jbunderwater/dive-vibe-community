## Helenesee

A deep former lignite pit near Frankfurt (Oder) with clear water, a single regulated diver entry on the east shore and access currently restricted because of shoreline instability.

## Overview

The Helenesee near Frankfurt (Oder) is a flooded lignite mine of the former Finkenheerd district; mining in the "Helene" pit began in 1943 and stopped in 1958 after major water inrushes, according to the Seelöwen dive school. The pit was terraced in roughly 20 m steps; today's maximum depth is about 55 m (200bar.de and the Seelöwen author, citing echo soundings), although much larger figures circulate. Beginners can stay in the 10 m zone near the shore, while more experienced divers reach 30 m without a long swim. Since a shoreline landslide in March 2021 the lake has been subject to mining-authority closures; see Entry and Exit before planning a dive.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Oststrand, Helenesee, Frankfurt (Oder)-Lossow, Brandenburg
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Sources range from 1-8 m (BerlinTaucher) to 5-10 m year-round (dive base) and up to 12 m (lake operator); a 200bar.de report from September 2018 gave 6-7 m with a thermocline at 9 m and 7 °C below 20 m
- **Current**: None documented
- **Best Time**: Base season mid-May to mid-September (Wednesday-Sunday); outside the season by appointment on weekends

## Underwater Features

A training platform lies at 6 m close to the entry and a second at 8 m (unbuoyed). East of the entry are a pedal boat, a steel barge and a telephone booth; west of the entry a rigged sailing dinghy sits at 9.5 m on a plateau whose edge drops steeply to about 17 m. BerlinTaucher also lists a rowing boat and several training platforms within 10 m. In places steep break-off edges with exposed coal seams remain. A mini-submarine operates in a buoyed area in the north-west of the lake.

## Marine Life

BerlinTaucher describes sparse vegetation and limited fish. 200bar.de reports plenty of small fish and crayfish, and mentions reports of large pike on the dinghy plateau west of the entry.

## Dive Profile

The shoreline terraces allow a shallow dive around the platforms and objects in the 6-10 m range, with deeper water reachable quickly along the break-off edges. Buoys mark several objects and help with descent and ascent.

## Entry and Exit

Under the lake operator's Tauchordnung, diving is permitted only in marked areas and only after registration at the dive centre on the east beach (Taucheinstieg 1); members of the resident diving clubs use their own club entry (Taucheinstieg 2). An OWD/CMAS* or equivalent certification and a medical fitness certificate are required, a fee is charged per diver and day, the number of divers per day is limited, solo diving is not allowed, the maximum depth is 30 m (10 m at night) and diving under jetties and harbour installations is prohibited.

After a landslide on the north-east shore in March 2021 the mining authority (LBGR) closed the shoreline. On 3 July 2024 it lifted the closure for a roughly 400 m section of the east shore judged stable. A general order by the City of Frankfurt (Oder) dated 11 June 2026 prohibits bathing, scuba diving and small-boat use within 150 m of the slide-prone north, west and south shores and the northern half of the east shore. Check with the dive base that it is operating and which area is currently open before travelling.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Register in advance; the daily diver limit can be reached on busy weekends
- Visibility is generally worse from Friday to Monday because of heavy use and bathers near the entry
- Carry a light below the thermocline

## Safety Considerations

The authorities warn that slides of unstable shoreline slopes can create suction and surge waves that endanger swimmers and divers, which is why the 150 m exclusion zone exists. Stay within the released area. Water below 20 m can be around 7 °C even in late summer; cold-water regulators and adequate thermal protection are required by the Tauchordnung. Watch for surf and sailing boats when surfacing.
