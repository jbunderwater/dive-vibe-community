## Kreidesee Hemmoor - Einstieg 2

An entry for experienced divers with direct access to the sunken forest, shallow zones and steep drop-offs.

## Overview

The Kreidesee in Hemmoor (Lower Saxony, on the B73 between Cuxhaven and Hamburg) is a flooded chalk quarry. Chalk was extracted here from 1862 to 1976; the pit, which had reached a depth of almost 120 m, filled with water between 1976 and 1982, and divers discovered it from 1986 onwards. The lake measures roughly 1,300 x 500 m. Its maximum depth is given as about 60 m (water-level dependent) by the on-site dive base and 58 m by Taucher.Net. Numerous objects have been placed for divers - one source counts about 41 objects spread across the entry points. Diving is managed by the dive base on the western shore and requires a day dive permit.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Kreidesee, Hemmoor, Landkreis Cuxhaven, Lower Saxony
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 53 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Average 10-20 m at the entries; up to 40 m in optimal conditions, 5-10 m in poor conditions (best mid-week, Tuesday-Thursday)
- **Current**: None
- **Best Time**: Year-round with drysuit (the dive base operates all year); wetsuit diving April-October

## Underwater Features

Pro-Taucher describes Entry 2 as giving direct access to the sunken forest. The dive base groups Entries 2 and 5 as entries for experienced divers, with both shallow areas and steep drop-offs. OSM features mapped nearest this entry include the sailboat (12 m), the paved road (10-32 m), 'Betonbombe II' (23 m), shrubs and small trees (10-20 m) and a Piper aircraft wreck tagged at 53 m.

## Marine Life

The dive base lists trout, roach, perch, stickleback, eel and whitefish, plus freshwater mussels, snails, sponges, crayfish and (seasonally) amphibians. Top Dive reports char (See-Saibling) and salmon trout reaching 60-80 cm. Pro-Taucher notes trout throughout the lake and abundant crayfish, especially around Entry 5.

## Dive Profile

Not documented in detail; plan the dive around the forest and the drop-off and within your certified depth.

## Entry and Exit

 Every diver needs a day dive permit (Tauchgenehmigung) from the on-site dive base; published day-fee figures differ between sources (EUR 11 per Top Dive and Pro-Taucher, EUR 20 per a 2020 blog), so check current prices. Top Dive lists cold-water-suitable equipment, a dive computer and two independently closable valves/first stages as mandatory. Pro-Taucher reports that checks of divers and dive computers are not uncommon and that the permitted maximum depth depends on certification level. Taucher.Net describes the entries as equipped with floating pontoons and stairs.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Only for experienced divers according to the dive base
- Plan depth carefully - the drop-off leads quickly into deep water

## Safety Considerations

Water below the thermocline stays cold all year; a drysuit is needed outside the summer months (wetsuit diving is described as practical only April-October). Good visibility can make divers underestimate depth - the lake drops to about 60 m and several objects lie well below recreational limits. Stay within your certification and the depth limits set by the dive base.
