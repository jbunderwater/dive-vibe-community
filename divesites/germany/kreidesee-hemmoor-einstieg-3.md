## Kreidesee Hemmoor - Einstieg 3

The restricted deep entry reached by a wooden staircase, leading to the old quarry machinery - the Rüttler, the Meisterbude and the underwater stairs.

## Overview

The Kreidesee in Hemmoor (Lower Saxony, on the B73 between Cuxhaven and Hamburg) is a flooded chalk quarry. Chalk was extracted here from 1862 to 1976; the pit, which had reached a depth of almost 120 m, filled with water between 1976 and 1982, and divers discovered it from 1986 onwards. The lake measures roughly 1,300 x 500 m. Its maximum depth is given as about 60 m (water-level dependent) by the on-site dive base and 58 m by Taucher.Net. Numerous objects have been placed for divers - one source counts about 41 objects spread across the entry points. Diving is managed by the dive base on the western shore and requires a day dive permit.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Kreidesee, Hemmoor, Landkreis Cuxhaven, Lower Saxony
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 53 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Average 10-20 m at the entries; up to 40 m in optimal conditions, 5-10 m in poor conditions (best mid-week, Tuesday-Thursday)
- **Current**: None
- **Best Time**: Year-round with drysuit (the dive base operates all year); wetsuit diving April-October

## Underwater Features

Pro-Taucher lists the Rüttler (a funnel structure starting at 20 m), a Mercedes truck, the Meisterbude (foreman's office) at 32 m, wooden stairs at 43 m and 53 m and steel mats. Taucher.Net and Top Dive both mention the Rüttler as historic mining equipment. OSM maps 'Rüttler und Briefkasten' (20-32 m), 'Meisterbude und Gänge' (35 m), rockfall mats (10-20 m), a bridge construction (25 m) and 'Holztreppe' (42-55 m) near this entry. Depth figures for the Meisterbude differ (32 m per Pro-Taucher, 35 m per OSM).

## Marine Life

The dive base lists trout, roach, perch, stickleback, eel and whitefish, plus freshwater mussels, snails, sponges, crayfish and (seasonally) amphibians. Top Dive reports char (See-Saibling) and salmon trout reaching 60-80 cm. Pro-Taucher notes trout throughout the lake and abundant crayfish, especially around Entry 5.

## Dive Profile

A deep dive from the start: the first objects begin at about 20 m and the main attractions lie between 30 and 53 m.

## Entry and Exit

The dive base restricts Entry 3 to experienced divers with at least 40 logged dives including 5 cold-water dives, and no training is allowed here. Access is via a well-maintained wooden staircase, a 3-5 minute walk from the base (Pro-Taucher). Every diver needs a day dive permit (Tauchgenehmigung) from the on-site dive base; published day-fee figures differ between sources (EUR 11 per Top Dive and Pro-Taucher, EUR 20 per a 2020 blog), so check current prices. Top Dive lists cold-water-suitable equipment, a dive computer and two independently closable valves/first stages as mandatory. Pro-Taucher reports that checks of divers and dive computers are not uncommon and that the permitted maximum depth depends on certification level. Taucher.Net describes the entries as equipped with floating pontoons and stairs.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring proof of your dive count - the 40-dive / 5-cold-water-dive minimum applies
- Carry a light; the machinery lies in deep, darker water

## Safety Considerations

Water below the thermocline stays cold all year; a drysuit is needed outside the summer months (wetsuit diving is described as practical only April-October). Good visibility can make divers underestimate depth - the lake drops to about 60 m and several objects lie well below recreational limits. Stay within your certification and the depth limits set by the dive base.
