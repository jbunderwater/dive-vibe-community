## Arendsee - Ostufer

Germany's deepest sinkhole lake, dived from the official club entry on the east shore under a strict county regulation that permits only a few designated entry points.

## Overview

The Arendsee in the Altmark (Saxony-Anhalt) is a collapse lake over a salt dome, about 2.5 by 3.5 km, with a maximum depth of about 50 m (sources give 49.5-52 m) and an average depth of about 28-29 m. Large parts of the shore are a landscape protection area. The local diving club has its clubhouse on the east shore by the passenger-boat landing, next to the sailing club. Vegetation outside the reed belt is sparse, but fish are common.

## Site Information

- **Location**: East shore (Tauchclub entry next to the Segler-Club), Arendsee (Altmark), Saxony-Anhalt
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 50 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Highly variable, often around 2 m; below the summer thermocline (about 14 m) usually 1-3 m and completely dark; in winter 3-7 m down to 20 m
- **Current**: None documented
- **Best Time**: Winter generally brings better visibility (200bar.de)

## Underwater Features

A 2011 taucher.net report says the diving club has placed a number of objects near the entry; no inventory is documented. A sunken patrol boat lies elsewhere in the lake and is listed as a separate site (Arendsee - Wrack Patrouillenboot).

## Marine Life

Pike, perch, eel, roach, vendace (Kleine Maräne) and carp are listed by 200bar.de and a taucher.net report.

## Entry and Exit

Use of the lake is regulated by the Altmarkkreis Salzwedel county regulation (ArendseeVO, 2014). Scuba diving may start only from the designated entries: Schrampe (Regattaverein, between jetties 4 and 5), Arendsee at the Kaskade (between jetties 155 and 156), Arendsee Tauchclub (shore between jetties 53 and 54) and the Segler-Club jetties 50-53 with the owner's consent. Entering elsewhere requires a permit from the county and violations are fined; 200bar.de notes that checks are carried out. Entry through the reed belt or in the protected areas is not allowed. There is no commercial dive base; according to 200bar.de the club no longer fills tanks for visiting divers and does not rent equipment, while a 2011 report says visitors can use the clubhouse after phoning ahead.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring your own filled tanks and a primary light; it is dark below the thermocline
- Contact the diving club before visiting
- Stick to the designated entries to avoid fines

## Safety Considerations

Deep water, darkness and very poor visibility below the thermocline make navigation difficult; depth and air planning are essential in a lake that reaches about 50 m. Sailboats and other boats use the lake, so surface with a marker buoy.
