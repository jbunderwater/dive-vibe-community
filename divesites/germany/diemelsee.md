## Diemelsee

A designated diver's bay (Taucherbucht) in the Diemel reservoir on the Hesse/NRW border, reached over a rocky shore with a ledge dropping away underwater.

## Overview

Diving at the Diemelsee is confined to the Taucherbucht, a bay between the Diemel dam and Heringhausen, roughly 100 m from the dam wall below the Eisenberg. The bay is marked by two buoys. The tourism portal describes a rock ledge that starts on the shore and ends underwater with a nearly vertical edge; 200bar describes a steep wall on the left side of the zone and an otherwise rocky landscape of boulders. Sources give different maximum depths: the lake's official site quotes 30 m, 200bar reports 37 m at full reservoir level with typical dives to about 15 m, and a local club lists the bay at up to 16 m. As a reservoir, the water level and therefore the reachable depth vary. The site lies in a nature reserve.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Taucherbucht between the Diemel dam and Heringhausen, below the Eisenberg (L 912, Marsberg)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Highly variable - about 10 m quoted by the lake's website; a taucher.net review reports 5-6 m above and 10-15 m below an algae layer; 200bar logged 0.2-1.5 m in August 2017
- **Current**: Not documented
- **Best Time**: Not documented; the lake's website states there are no seasonal or time-of-day restrictions

## Underwater Features

- Rock ledge with a near-vertical edge at the shore and a steep wall along the left side of the zone
- Boulders and rock formations from an old quarry
- A small boat and a sunken car are reported by a local club; one taucher.net review also mentions gear/transmission parts
- A quarry area to the left lies outside the designated zone and under tour-boat traffic, and is not recommended

## Marine Life

The lake's website names pike, zander, perch, carp and whitefish. 200bar reports perch, eels and occasional large pike, and a local club reports large schools of perch.

## Entry and Exit

Access is via an informal path to the rocky shore; 200bar describes the entry as somewhat awkward because divers have to cross the road guardrail with their equipment, and a taucher.net review mentions a stone staircase and a small free parking area across the road between two bends. The lake's website states no special permit is required, whereas the regional tourism portal notes that, because the bay is in a nature reserve, demands on diver qualification are high and few divers use it. Local water regulations apply. There is no fill station at the site.

## Tips and Recommendations

- The bay is hard to find - the tourism portal says so explicitly; check the location before you arrive
- Stay inside the buoyed zone; the quarry area to the left is under boat traffic
- Expect depth and visibility to change with reservoir level and algae growth

## Safety Considerations

The ledge and wall give quick access to deeper water, and a dark algae layer can sharply reduce visibility. Bring your own air, as there is no filling on site. Cold-water thermal protection is needed.
