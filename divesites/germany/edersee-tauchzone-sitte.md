## Edersee - Tauchzone Sitte

Dive zone 2 of the Edersee, a terraced scree and rock slope dropping to about 36 m, with sunken boats and usually better visibility than Berich.

## Overview

The Edersee is a large reservoir in northern Hesse. The local dive base explains that, as a federal waterway, it is managed under a dam ordinance that designates only two dive zones. The zones are marked with yellow buoys and closed to boat traffic. Diving is allowed at any time of day or night without registration. Diving outside the zones is an administrative offence that the water police can enforce. The water level varies a lot over the year, so every depth given for the lake refers to the full reservoir level of 245 m above sea level: the current depth of an object is the current water level minus the object's elevation (Tauchbasis Edersee). Dive zone 2, "Sitte", is on the Ederseerandstraße at Waldeck-West towards the dam. It is a terraced slope of scree and rock with trees and shrubs in the shallows. According to the dive base, it has more fish and usually better visibility than zone 1, and it gives the best chance to see all the lake's residents.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Waldeck-West, Ederseerandstraße, Edersee, Waldeck-Frankenberg, Hesse
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 36 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Usually better than in zone 1; Edersee-Tauchen gives May to mid-July as the best period
- **Current**: None
- **Best Time**: May to mid-July (visibility)

## Underwater Features

- To the right of the stairs: a boulder field of block-shaped stones, then a terraced slope dropping south to about 36 m (Tauchbasis Edersee)
- To the left: two old boats at about 29-30 m (Tauchbasis Edersee); Edersee-Tauchen mentions several sunken rowing and paddle boats
- The old riverbed at about 37 m (at full level)
- Garden gnomes on the scree slope (TWC Delphin, Edersee-Tauchen)
- Relics of the old bomb/torpedo barrier, just outside the zone (TWC Delphin)
- From the "Rutsche" entry: an old gravel slide into the zone and a steep rocky slope to about 30 m with old oil drums and trees

## Marine Life

The dive base reports small schooling fish such as roach and eels among the trees and shrubs in the shallows, frequent large pike, and many pike and perch in the shallows. Edersee-Tauchen describes the zone as noticeably richer in fish than zone 1, with night dives recommended in June and July.

## Entry and Exit

"Sitte": park on the lake side of the road, cross it and go down a stairway. At full level, from about March to June or July, the stairs lead comfortably into the water. "Rutsche": about 300 m further towards the dam, from a lay-by via a stairway and a gravel slope. Dive only inside the buoyed Tauchzone; no registration or permit is required. The lake is in a landscape protection area, so running compressors requires an official permit. Divers have the right to ask anglers to stop fishing during dive operations (Tauchbasis Edersee). At low water in late summer the water can be turbid, and getting in and out may involve some scrambling (TWC Delphin).

## Tips and Recommendations

- Check the current water level before planning; the base gives all depths at full level
- Turning left from the Rutsche entry, you can easily end up outside the zone, so watch the buoys and your compass

## Safety Considerations

The slope reaches 30 m and more quickly, so plan depth, gas and decompression limits carefully. Diving outside the buoyed zone is prohibited and puts you in boat traffic.
