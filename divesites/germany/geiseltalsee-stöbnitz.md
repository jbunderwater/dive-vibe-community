## Geiseltalsee - Stöbnitz

Shore and boat diving from the campsite peninsula at Stöbnitz on Germany's largest artificial lake, with clear water, underwater forests and a training area with three platforms.

## Overview

The Geiseltalsee between Mücheln and Braunsbedra is a flooded lignite mine and, at almost 19 km², described as the largest artificial lake in Germany; it reaches about 80 m. Diving has been permitted since 2013/2014, but only after registration at one of the resident dive centres. The Stöbnitz centre is on the campsite peninsula above the lake; from the beach there are two shore entries, and further entry points are reached by boat. Sources stress that this is a natural lake landscape rather than a "Disneyland for divers".

## Site Information

- **Location**: Campsite peninsula, Strandallee, Mücheln (Geiseltal)-Stöbnitz, Saxony-Anhalt
- **Entry Type**: Shore or boat entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 78 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Around 15 m (geiseltalsee.com); 10-25 m, usually 10-15 m, according to the Frankleben base
- **Current**: None documented
- **Best Time**: Diving takes place year-round, including Christmas and New Year dives

## Underwater Features

At Einstieg 1 there is a training area with three platforms at 7.5 m, 12.5 m and 20 m, reached to the right of the entry via a cable drum or by following the buoys. Boat-accessed Einstieg 2 and 3 offer long seagrass meadows on one side and underwater forests on the other. The area around and under the boat jetty is also highlighted.

## Marine Life

Sources mention small and large fish without naming species, attached freshwater mussels and sponges, and the rare freshwater jellyfish.

## Entry and Exit

Diving in the Geiseltalsee is governed by a general order (Allgemeinverfügung) and allowed only via the resident dive centres. Divers need a certification, a dive medical, insurance and a valid dive registration. From the dive centre on the campsite, divers are shuttled to the beach entries on request. Carrying a surface marker buoy is mandatory on the lake; the bases rent them to divers who arrive without one.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Register in advance to avoid waiting, especially with groups
- Decide whether to change at the base or at the beach, as the shuttle runs only on demand
- Book boat entries to reach the forest and meadow sites

## Safety Considerations

Very deep water (to about 78 m) is within reach, so plan depth and gas carefully and stay within your certification. Boat traffic makes a surface marker essential. Water at depth is cold year-round.
