## Großer Plöner See - Plöner Stadtbucht

The main town entry at the former fishery slipway in Plön's town bay, with a guide line, placed objects, small boat wrecks and mussel-covered ground.

## Overview

The Großer Plöner See is the largest and deepest lake in Schleswig-Holstein. The tourism board gives a maximum depth of 58 m in the eastern Plön basin and 30 m in the shallower western Ascheberg basin, both formed by glaciers. It is a busy lake with scheduled passenger ships, sailing boats and canoes. The tourism board names the former fishery site in the Plön town bay (Eutiner Straße) as the entry for this part of the lake. The fishing lease changed hands in 2020, but the dive site remained (7oceans).

## Site Information

- **Location**: Plöner Stadtbucht, Plön, Kreis Plön, Schleswig-Holstein
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Beach dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: Highly variable - average about 6 m (tauchspots-kiel), often more than 10 m (7oceans), yet at times so poor you cannot see your hand (TSG Submarin); best in winter
- **Current**: None
- **Best Time**: Winter months for visibility

## Underwater Features

From the large slipway, a line leads over a drop-off at about 12 m to a group of placed objects. The usual course heads south until the bottom falls to nearly 20 m, then turns east in a wide arc around the bay; the bottom is covered with mussels and holds two small boat wrecks and assorted debris (7oceans). Tauchspots-kiel describes two routes: a shallow one along the shore and a deep one towards the islands of Olsborg and Hankenburg, reaching 35 m and more. TSG Submarin describes the fishery course as following the 5-10 m contours, with 6-8 m as the best depth.

## Marine Life

The tourism board lists sponges, crayfish, mussels, sticklebacks, pike, perch, vendace (Maräne) and zander, as well as roach-type whitefish, carp and tench. TSG Submarin reports small vendace, roach, perch, pike and eels as common and large vendace, bleak, zander and trout as rare, along with invasive zebra and quagga mussels and invasive amphipods. Tauchspots-kiel notes abundant mussel beds, crayfish and few fish.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the guide line over the drop-off at about 12 m, follow the slope south to about 20 m and return east around the bay. The deep route towards the islands is for divers qualified beyond 30 m.

## Entry and Exit

Entry is from the slipway into a small, very muddy harbour bay, a short walk from the car park (tauchspots-kiel). Parking on the fishery site is by arrangement with the fisher; public parking in Plön is very limited (7oceans, tauchspots-kiel). A buoy is mandatory in summer because of boat traffic (tauchspots-kiel). The regional tourism board requires divers to leave their dive certification clearly visible in their car and to inform the holder of the fishing rights before diving; questions go to the state coastal agency (LKN). Swimming and diving are prohibited within 100 m of the passenger-ship landings while the boats are running (Wednesday-Sunday 11:00-18:00, plus Friday 19:00-21:00). Divers must follow the rules of the Schleswig-Holstein diving federation (TLV-SH), must not take any WWII munitions they find, and should report them. The diving club TUK e.V. (page last updated 2022) names the Plöner See as a lake that requires a personal TLV-SH authorisation card, issued only to TLV-SH members. The tourism board does not mention this, so check the current access rules before diving.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Arrange parking and your dive with the fishery beforehand
- Plan around ship times and summer boat traffic
- Winter usually gives the best visibility

## Safety Considerations

Heavy boat traffic in summer makes a surface marker buoy essential. WWII munitions have been dumped in Schleswig-Holstein lakes - never touch suspicious objects. The thermocline brings a sharp temperature drop, so dress for cold water. Visibility can collapse without warning, so carry a light and stay close to your buddy.
