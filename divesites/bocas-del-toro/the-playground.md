# The Playground

A heavily coral-encrusted, "nearly unrecognizable" shipwreck in the sheltered inner archipelago of Bocas del Toro — so overgrown that most operators describe it only by its reef-like swim-throughs and small caverns, with excellent turtle encounters.

## Overview

The Playground is one of Bocas del Toro's most popular beginner-and-up dive sites, located approximately 5 minutes by boat from the dive centers on Isla Colón. therealdealtours.com states directly: "The Playground is home to a nearly unrecognizable shipwreck. The shipwreck is a playground for many colorful fish, colorful mollusks and nudibranchs hiding amongst the coral." No vessel name, build date, dimensions, or sinking cause are documented in any source consulted — the wreck's identity and history are undocumented, and none of these details are stated here to avoid fabricating them.

The heavy coral overgrowth explains why other operators (Bocas Dive Center, Tourism Panama, La Buga) describe the site purely in reef terms — "swim-throughs, small caverns, and coral formations" — without naming it a wreck. The two descriptions are not in conflict: the swim-throughs and caverns other sources mention are consistent with an overgrown wreck structure.

The site descends from 7 meters to a maximum of 22 meters, with the wreck/reef structure, swim-throughs, and cavern sections in the upper 15 meters and a sandy base below. The warm, calm conditions typical of Bocas del Toro's sheltered inner archipelago make this an accessible dive. Visibility is 15–20 meters.

## Marine Life

The site supports a typical Caribbean inner-reef community living on and around the encrusted wreck structure. Hawksbill turtles have been confirmed at the site (notably April–June per therealdealtours.com). Colorful fish, mollusks, and nudibranchs are noted sheltering among the coral growth on the wreck (therealdealtours.com). Sea stars are also mentioned. The swim-through and cavern sections of the wreck attract fish that shelter in reduced-light environments.

## Dive Profile

Descent to the wreck/reef structure, exploration of the swim-throughs and small caverns formed by the overgrown hull in the 7–15 meter range, then navigation to the sandy base at 22 meters if desired. The site's open structure allows divers to cover a large area without complex routing. Total dive: 50–65 minutes at an easy pace.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Bocas Town operators. Entry by giant stride into calm water. Suitable for open water certification dives and refresher dives.

## Tips and Recommendations

The swim-throughs and small caverns — formed by the coral-encrusted wreck structure — are what distinguish The Playground from other inner sites; a torch enhances the experience in the shaded sections. Sources disagree on the appropriate experience level: Bocas Dive Center recommends the site for "more experienced divers," while Tourism Panama lists it as ideal for beginners. Discuss with your operator if you are a newer diver. The site dives well as a morning dive paired with Hospital Point.

## Safety Considerations

Do not penetrate any enclosed sections of the wreck structure without wreck diving training and equipment — even where openings appear to be simple swim-throughs. The swim-throughs and small caverns require good buoyancy to avoid silt disturbance. Fire coral is present on some reef sections. DSMB recommended.

## Photography

The swim-throughs and cavern openings — remnants of the wreck's structure — make stronger compositional subjects than plain reef photography. Hawksbill turtles photograph well in ambient light. Wide-angle reef/wreck scenes with the swim-through entrances as focal points are achievable.
