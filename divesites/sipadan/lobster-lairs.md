# Lobster Lairs

A wall riddled with small caves and crevices sheltering spiny lobsters, moray eels, and octopuses — one of Sipadan's best macro sites, dived to depths of 25-40 metres

## Overview
Lobster Lairs sits on Sipadan's southwestern wall, named for the large numbers of lobsters that inhabit the countless small cracks, overhangs, and crevices along the wall face. The wall geometry here is less sheer than at some of Sipadan's other sites, featuring more irregular formations with numerous holes and ledges that create ideal shelter for crustaceans, cephalopods, and moray eels. This makes Lobster Lairs one of the island's best sites for macro diving. Sources disagree on how much current to expect here — some describe it as calm to light, others report moderate to strong current with a depth range extending to 40 metres — so treat it as a site requiring advanced-diver skills and confirm conditions with your operator before the dive.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Sipadan Island, Sabah, Malaysia (southwestern wall)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 metres
- **Current**: Sources disagree — reported as anywhere from calm/light to moderate-strong
- **Best Time**: Year-round; night dives are possible where operators offer them, though this review found no site-specific report describing Lobster Lairs' night diving specifically

## Marine Life
Spiny lobsters are the namesake inhabitants, found in nearly every crevice and overhang along the wall — their long antennae protruding from holes are often the first sign. Moray eels share the wall's crevice habitat. Scorpionfish and lionfish are confirmed residents of the rocky outcrops. Reef sharks, including whitetip reef sharks, cruise in the blue water off the wall. Seahorses, frogfish, and pipefish are reported at this site, making it one of Sipadan's stronger macro sites. Octopuses are common. Groupers, triggerfish, wrasse, and turtles round out the regular sightings. Manta rays pass through on occasion, though sightings are not routine. The wall itself features hard and soft coral in good condition.

## Dive Profile
A wall dive at moderate to deep depth, depending on the day's current. Descend along the wall to 25-30 metres (some sources report dives to 40 metres) and move along the wall, peering into the crevices, overhangs, and small caves that characterize this stretch of wall. The irregular wall profile means there are interesting features at all depths, so a multi-level profile works well when current allows — start deeper and work gradually shallower. Given the reported range of current conditions, follow the divemaster's plan for the day rather than assuming a leisurely drift. Safety stop on the reef crest.

## Entry and Exit
Boat dive from Mabul or Kapalai island resorts. Giant stride entry. The divemaster will assess current on the day before setting the entry point, since conditions here are reported inconsistently across sources. Deploy an SMB for boat pickup.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Shine your torch into crevices to spot lobsters, morays, and crustaceans sheltering in the shadows
- Move slowly along the wall — the macro life rewards patience and a careful eye
- Confirm current conditions with your divemaster before descending — reports on this site's current vary considerably
- Sipadan permits (176 per day) are required and must be arranged through your resort
- Pair with a morning dive at one of the pelagic sites for the ideal contrast between Sipadan's big-animal and small-animal diving

## Safety Considerations
Sources disagree markedly on current strength at this site, and depth reported ranges up to 40 metres — plan and dive it as a current-exposed, advanced-level site rather than assuming calm conditions. The irregular wall with its caves and overhangs can also create mild disorientation — maintain awareness of the wall face and open water at all times. An SMB and signalling device are essential. The nearest hyperbaric chamber is in Kota Kinabalu.

## Photography
Macro is the primary discipline here. A macro lens will find lobster portraits, moray eel close-ups, nudibranch details, and octopus behaviour shots. Use a snoot or focused strobe to isolate subjects against the dark wall background. Wide-angle can capture the wall structure with its many holes and overhangs.
