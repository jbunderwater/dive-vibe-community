# Staghorn Crest

Extensive staghorn coral formations blanket a shallow plateau above a current-swept wall — one of Sipadan's most colourful reef environments, but current here can run strong

## Overview
Staghorn Crest is named for the dense fields of staghorn coral (Acropora) that dominate the reef crest and a 15-metre plateau riddled with cracks and ledges, before the wall drops away beyond recreational limits. This site showcases Sipadan's reef health at its finest — the staghorn formations are extensive and pristine, creating a complex three-dimensional habitat that teems with reef fish. Most sources describing this site note that current can run strong, and it is generally reserved for advanced divers as a result, even though the shallow plateau itself is visually gentle. Turtles graze among the staghorn fields, and the reef supports a rich community of angelfish, gobies, groupers, and other reef fish.

## Site Information
- **Location**: Sipadan Island, Sabah, Malaysia (western side, north of South Point)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-30 metres
- **Current**: Reported as strong by most sources describing this site; a minority describe it as milder — treat it as a current-exposed site and plan accordingly
- **Best Time**: Year-round; afternoon light conditions are particularly good for photography

## Marine Life
The staghorn coral fields are the foundation of the ecosystem here. These branching formations provide shelter for thousands of small fish — damselfish, chromis, and anthias swarm above the coral, creating clouds of colour. Red seawhip and black coral formations add to the structural complexity. Barrel sponges are common. Angelfish, groupers, nudibranchs, triggerfish, shrimp, and gobies are all confirmed here. Green turtles are regularly sighted, resting on ledges and grazing on the crest. Whitetip reef sharks pass along the wall edge. One general dive guide (SSI) reports occasional manta ray, hammerhead, and thresher shark sightings at this specific site, but this has not been corroborated by the other sources reviewed here, including the site's own dedicated listing — treat such encounters as rare and unconfirmed rather than a reliable expectation.

## Dive Profile
Begin on the reef crest exploring the staghorn coral fields and their associated marine life. Work gradually toward the wall edge where the hard coral transitions to the wall face, featuring red seawhip and black coral formations. Descend along the wall — the site features a plateau at around 15 metres with many cracks and ledges full of life, followed by a wall that continues well beyond recreational limits. Given the current, plan this as a drift dive and be ready to adapt the profile to conditions on the day. A multi-level profile works well when current allows: spend time on the wall, then ascend to the shallow coral gardens for the safety stop phase. Afternoon dives here have particularly good natural light conditions on the western-facing wall sections.

## Entry and Exit
Boat dive from Mabul or Kapalai island resorts. Giant stride entry, timed by the divemaster around current direction and strength. Deploy an SMB during ascent — current can carry divers away from the entry point, and boat pickup along the drift is common.

## Tips and Recommendations
- Spend ample time on the reef crest — the staghorn coral gardens are the highlight and are best appreciated slowly when current allows
- Excellent buoyancy is essential in the coral gardens — the staghorn formations are brittle and easily broken by fin strikes
- Look for hawkfish perched on sea fans and gorgonians along the wall — they hold still for photographers
- Sipadan permits (176 per day) are required and must be arranged through your resort
- Confirm current conditions with your divemaster before the dive — sources disagree on how strong current typically runs here

## Safety Considerations
Most sources describing this site report strong current, and it is generally treated as a dive for advanced divers as a result — do not assume the shallow, visually gentle plateau means easy conditions. The brittle staghorn coral formations can cause cuts if a diver makes contact, and the damage to the coral is disproportionate — a single fin kick can destroy years of growth. Maintain a safe distance above the reef at all times. An SMB and signalling device are essential. The nearest hyperbaric chamber is in Kota Kinabalu.

## Photography
The staghorn coral fields photographed from above with natural light create striking reef-scape images. Afternoon dives here benefit from particularly good natural light conditions on the western-facing sections. Wide-angle with a slow shutter for ambient light works well in the shallow, bright conditions. Turtles resting on ledges provide strong foreground subjects. Macro opportunities include gobies on coral heads, nudibranch details, and the resident shrimp. Red seawhip corals on the wall make vivid close-focus wide-angle subjects when lit by strobe.
