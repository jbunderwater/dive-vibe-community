## Kealakekua Bay

A Marine Life Conservation District on Hawaii's South Kona Coast, Kealakekua Bay offers calm, exceptionally clear water, a gentle reef slope, and a well-documented population of tame reef fish — with spinner dolphins sighted on many morning dives.

## Overview

Established in 1969 under Hawaii Administrative Rules 13-29, Kealakekua Bay Marine Life Conservation District encompasses 315 acres of coastline approximately 30 minutes south of Kailua-Kona. Diving is concentrated in Ka'awaloa Cove, on the northern shore near the Captain James Cook monument — a white obelisk marking where Cook died in 1779. Access rules changed in 2013: State Parks restricted unpermitted kayak launching and landing at Ka'awaloa Flat (the previous source of most visitor traffic) and now requires a State Parks permit for any vessel transiting the bay, with motorized vessels required to operate at no-wake speed. This is not a full ban on motorized boats — licensed dive charter operators hold the necessary permits and remain the standard way divers access the site.

The MLCD protects against anchoring in Subzone A, extraction of marine life, and feeding of fish. This long-standing protection has produced unusually relaxed, large populations of reef fish with high fish density and notably calm behaviour toward divers.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Ka'awaloa Cove, Kealakekua Bay, South Kona Coast (approximately 30 minutes south of Kailua-Kona)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–36 metres (50–120 feet), frequently described as among Hawaii's clearest
- **Water Temperature**: 25–27°C
- **Best Time**: Morning dives offer best visibility and spinner dolphin encounter probability; May–September for calmest conditions

## Underwater Character

The reef in Ka'awaloa Cove slopes gently from the shoreline, with abundant coral growth on the lava substrate. The Hawaii Division of Aquatic Resources describes the fish as "quite tame" — a direct result of long-term protection since 1969. Over 100 fish species have been recorded in the bay. The diversity of corals is exceptional, with healthy hard coral cover across the slope.

## Marine Life

Hawaiian green sea turtles (Chelonia mydas) are regularly encountered resting on the reef. Spinner dolphins (Stenella longirostris) typically enter the bay shortly after dawn following overnight feeding trips and rest through the day (peak resting hours documented at 10am-2pm); they are not always present but are frequently sighted, especially on morning dives. A federal NOAA rule in effect since 2021 prohibits approaching within 50 yards of spinner dolphins by boat, kayak, or swimmer — divers should not attempt to approach resting pods. Parrotfish, tangs, surgeonfish, and triggerfish are documented resident species. Occasional manta ray sightings are reported but are not a reliable feature of this site.

Fish species counts for the bay are DISPUTED across sources: the Hawaii DLNR does not publish a specific count and simply describes fish diversity as "exceptional" and the fish as "quite tame"; other sources report figures ranging from roughly 100 to over 400 species with no clear methodology cited. Rather than pick one figure, this description notes only that diversity and density are well documented and exceptionally high due to over 50 years of protection.

## Practical Access

Only licensed dive operators with permits can take divers to Ka'awaloa Cove. Kona Honu Divers and other major Kona operators run charters; confirm which operators hold current access permits when booking. Arrive early — conditions and spinner dolphin encounters are typically best in the first hours after sunrise.

## Conservation

No anchoring in Subzone A (northern cove). Do not feed fish, touch corals, or interfere with spinner dolphins resting behaviour. Follow all divemaster and operator briefings. The bay's pristine condition is directly attributable to decades of strict protection.

## Safety Considerations

- Diving is only through licensed operators; no self-guided entry
- Stay within your depth limits and certification
- Maintain proper buoyancy to avoid damaging the reef
- Light currents are possible; discuss conditions with your operator
- Spinner dolphin encounters: federal law requires staying at least 50 yards away; allow dolphins to dictate any approach and never pursue them
