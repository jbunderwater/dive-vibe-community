## Aruba

Aruba's dive sites are dominated by wrecks, led by the SS Antilla, a German freighter scuttled in 1940 and lying on her port side in Malmok Bay.

## Description

Aruba is the westernmost of the ABC islands. Our database documents 14 entries (one of them a near-duplicate listing of the Antilla), ranging from about 8 to 37 metres in maximum depth, including wrecks, reefs and a drop-off site. Sources consulted describe most sites as accessible to Open Water divers, with deeper or current-exposed sites such as Skalahein, Kantil Reef and the California wreck rated Advanced.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Wreck Diving**: The SS Antilla is the best-known wreck. Published lengths range from about 121 to about 144 metres, and sources disagree on what finally sank her after her crew scuttled her on 10 May 1940 (see the SS Antilla Wreck page). Other wrecks are the Pedernales (the midsection of a tanker torpedoed by U-156 on 16 February 1942), the Jane Sea (a cement freighter sunk in 1988), the Convair and YS-11 aircraft off Renaissance Island, and the California (an 1891 steamship on the rough northeast coast, rarely dived).
- **Shore Diving**: Malmok Reef, Boca Catalina and Mangel Halto are described by sources as shore dives.
- **Drift Diving**: Visit Aruba and Guide to Caribbean Vacations describe De Palm Slope as a drift dive.
- **Reef Diving**: Arashi Reef, Mike's Reef, Skalahein Reef and Kantil Reef are described on their pages.

### Operators

JADS Aruba and Palm Beach Divers publish dive site lists that were consulted for this destination.

## Additional Information

- **Safety**: Sources differ on whether the Antilla and the YS-11 can be penetrated; any overhead-environment diving requires appropriate training and equipment. The Jane Sea can have medium to strong current (Duikersgids), and JADS Aruba states no penetration is permitted there. The outer reef at Mangel Halto can have strong current in windy conditions (Duikersgids).
- **Species and conditions**: Species reports are given on each site page and are attributed to the sources that reported them at that site; no destination-wide species list is given here.
