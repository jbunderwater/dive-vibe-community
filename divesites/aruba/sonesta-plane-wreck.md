## Sonesta Plane Wreck

Two aircraft lie in front of Renaissance Island: a storm-scattered twin-engine Convair and an Air Aruba NAMC YS-11 airliner that sits largely intact.

## Overview

Pure Diving Aruba describes two aircraft deliberately sunk as artificial reefs off Renaissance Island. The older is a Convair, scattered by storms; Scuba.com and Divernet report that Hurricane Lenny in 1999 shifted and broke it. Sources disagree on the model: Pure Diving Aruba says Convair 240, Palm Beach Divers and JADS Aruba say Convair 340, Scuba.com and Atlas & Boots say Convair 240 or possibly a DC-3, and Guide to Caribbean Vacations says Convair 400. Scuba.com and Divernet report it was seized from drug smugglers in the late 1980s before being sunk; operator pages do not mention this.

The YS-11 belonged to Air Aruba. Pure Diving Aruba says it sat abandoned beside the runway at Queen Beatrix International Airport for years; Scuba.com and Divernet report it was sunk in 2004. Its seats and doors were removed before sinking, which makes it easier to enter (Pure Diving Aruba, Palm Beach Divers), and Palm Beach Divers and Scuba.com describe it resting with its nose angled upward.

Depth reports: Pure Diving Aruba and Palm Beach Divers give the Convair from 14 metres (wing tip) to 22 metres (cockpit) and the YS-11 from 16 metres (cockpit) to 27 metres (tail). Scuba.com instead gives the YS-11 at about 14 to 23 metres and says the Convair moved to about 24 metres after Hurricane Lenny. JADS Aruba lists a maximum of 26 metres and permits penetration.

## Site Information

- **Location**: In front of Renaissance Island, western Aruba
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters

## Marine Life

Pure Diving Aruba lists barracuda (often under the YS-11 wings), octopus, moray eels, pufferfish, flounders, angelfish, silversides, stingrays and occasional spotted eagle rays, with banded coral shrimp in the YS-11 cockpit. Palm Beach Divers and Scuba.com describe the surroundings as soft coral and sponges.

## Penetration and Safety

Penetration of the YS-11 is described by operators as easier because seats and doors were removed, but it is still an overhead environment and requires appropriate training and equipment. The deepest parts are near 27 metres.
