## Pedernales Wreck

The Pedernales site is the surviving midsection of an oil tanker torpedoed by a German submarine on 16 February 1942, lying in about 8 metres of water off Palm Beach.

## Overview

The SS Pedernales was a lake tanker built in Italy in 1938 (Wikipedia: launched 14 July 1938 at Cantieri Riuniti dell'Adriatico, Monfalcone; Willemsubmerged agrees on Italy, 1938) and rated at 4,317 GRT (Wikipedia, Willemsubmerged). She carried crude oil from Lake Maracaibo to the refinery at Aruba. On 16 February 1942, as part of Operation Neuland, U-156 under Kapitanleutnant Werner Hartenstein attacked her; Wikipedia's Attack on Aruba article and Willemsubmerged both report a single torpedo and eight of her 26 crew killed. She caught fire but did not sink.

The damaged hulk was taken to the Lago dry dock and cut into three sections. The fore and aft sections were joined and the ship sailed to Baltimore, where she was rebuilt (Wikipedia, Willemsubmerged); Divernet and Willemsubmerged report she was scrapped in 1959. The midsection stayed in Aruba. Sources disagree on its later fate: Wikipedia says the Dutch Navy used it as a bombing target, which scattered the wreck into eight sections, while Willemsubmerged states the Dutch Navy shooting-practice account has no confirmation and that munitions found nearby suggest US Army Air Force bombing practice. Wikipedia reports the three largest sections are dived daily by local operators.

## Site Information

- **Location**: In front of Palm Beach, northwestern Aruba
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 8 meters

Depth is reported as 25 feet by Pure Diving Aruba and Palm Beach Divers, about 7.6 metres by Wikipedia, and 7 to 8 metres by Divernet. Pure Diving Aruba and Palm Beach Divers describe the wreck as suited to easy diving and beginners. Palm Beach Divers notes visible cabins, wash basins and pipelines.

## Marine Life

Pure Diving Aruba lists stingrays, turtles, pufferfish, scorpionfish, lobster, moray eels, nurse sharks and eagle rays, with schools of grunts and goatfish. Divernet describes macro life including shrimps, crabs and a tiny honeycomb moray. Guide to Caribbean Vacations reports lobsters, groupers and angelfish.
