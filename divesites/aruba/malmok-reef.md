## Malmok Reef

Malmok Reef is a shallow, calm shore dive on Aruba's northwest coast, well suited to training dives and underwater photography.

## Overview

Duikersgids describes Malmok Reef as a simple dive site with crystal-clear, shallow, calm water, suited to practice dives, underwater photography and children. Divers walk in over the rocky shoreline and can park by the water, and the sandy bottom has rays and turtles. The site can be crowded.

Other sources describe the name differently. Aruba.com places Malmok Reef near the Antilla and Debbie II wrecks with a maximum depth of 21 metres, and Guide to Caribbean Vacations describes Malmok Reef and the Debbie II fuel barge at about 70 feet roughly two miles off the northwest coast. It is unclear from these sources whether these refer to the same site as the shore dive.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Malmok, northwest Aruba
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters

Duikersgids lists limited current and visibility of 20 to 30 metres (average 25 metres), and recommends a surface buoy.

## Marine Life

Duikersgids names turtles, stingrays and starfish, and mentions sea urchins, feather worms, young fish, lobsters and crabs. Guide to Caribbean Vacations lists brain and leaf corals, lobsters, stingrays and giant barrel sponges. Aruba.com mentions large schools of fish and lobsters, and sponges hosting shrimp and crabs.
