## Jane Sea Wreck

The Jane Sea (locally Jane C) is a cement freighter sunk off Aruba's south coast on 4 September 1988 as an artificial reef, resting upright with her bow near 18 metres and propeller near 27 metres.

## Overview

Pure Diving Aruba reports the ship was launched in 1959 as the Blackthorn and later renamed Jane Sea; Willemsubmerged gives the same original and later names. Duikersgids and Willemsubmerged give the sinking date as 4 September 1988, and report she arrived from Costa Rica in 1984 with a cement cargo. Sources differ on why she was abandoned. A popular story holds that cocaine was found in the cement cargo (reported by Pure Diving Aruba and Willemsubmerged). Willemsubmerged, citing Aruba Ports Authority archives, and Duikersgids instead describe engine trouble, unpaid harbour dues and an embargo. Pure Diving Aruba also gives an alternative account of crew who were not paid abandoning her.

Reported length varies: Pure Diving Aruba and Willemsubmerged give 57 metres (190 feet), DreamWrecks says a 170-foot English freighter, and Divernet gives 76 metres. Depth figures also vary: Pure Diving Aruba gives the bow at 18 metres and the propeller at 27 metres, Duikersgids gives the deck at 18 metres and propeller at 27 metres, JADS Aruba lists bow 18 metres, top of the stern 15 metres and propeller 28 metres, and Divernet reports the propeller at 30 metres.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Aruba's south coast, near Barcadera
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters

JADS Aruba lists the Jane Sea as a boat dive with no penetration. Duikersgids rates the site easy and suitable for all levels but warns that current can be fairly strong (rated moderate to strong).

## Marine Life

Pure Diving Aruba lists hawksbill turtles, green turtles, moray eels, jacks, silversides, octopus, coral crabs and trumpetfish. Duikersgids names barracuda, eagle ray and turtle. DreamWrecks and Divernet describe the wreck as covered in gorgonians, orange cup coral and sponges.
