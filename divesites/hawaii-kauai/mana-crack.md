## Mana Crack

Mana Crack is an 11-mile sunken fringing reef off the Na Pali Coast — the furthest offshore dive site in the state of Hawaii — always dived as a live-boat drift due to strong currents and the absence of any moorings.

## Overview

Mana Crack runs along the Na Pali coastline as a lengthy submerged reef with many distinct drop points. Operators select different sections for the first and second dives. The first dive typically runs 60–100 feet (18–30m); the second dive is done shallower at 45–80 feet (14–24m) on a different drop point, with a 90-minute surface interval in between spent exploring the Na Pali coast's sea caves, waterfalls, and hidden beaches.

The wall itself features massive black coral stalks swaying in the current and ancient coral formations with deep crevices. There are no moorings anywhere along Mana Crack, so all dives are live-boat entries: divers drop into the water, drift with the current along the reef, and are picked up by the support vessel downcurrent. ScubaBoard contributors describe the site as "the 'Niihau of Kauai'" for its relative remoteness and concentration of large fish species compared to the south shore sites. Bubbles Below Scuba Charters, the specialist operator running this site, describes it in their own marketing copy as "the world's second fastest dead eroding finger coral reef" — a striking, specific claim that could not be independently corroborated elsewhere, so it's presented here as the operator's own characterization rather than a verified fact.

## Site Information

- **Location**: 11-mile sunken fringing reef along the Na Pali Coast, Kauai (west/northwest shore)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Depth Range**: First dive 60–100 feet (18–30m); second dive 45–80 feet (14–24m)
- **Water Temperature**: 24–26°C (75–79°F)

## Marine Life

Confirmed species from Explore Kauai Scuba's current site listing and ScubaBoard diver reports: large barracuda, spotted eagle rays, white-tip reef sharks, java moray eels, rainbow runners, striped-belly puffers, black surgeonfish, wringtail wrasse, and grey reef sharks.

Note: hammerhead sharks, giant trevally, ono (wahoo), grouper (mu), trevally (ulua), and the spotted knifejaw appeared in an earlier version of this description. On re-checking Explore Kauai Scuba's current site listing, hammerhead sharks, giant trevally, and ono are attributed specifically to a different site ("Speak Easy"), and the spotted knifejaw is attributed to Turtle Bluffs — not Mana Crack. These have been removed from this site's marine life list rather than carried forward from a source that, on closer reading, describes a neighboring site. Spinner dolphins and winter humpback whales, previously listed as regular sightings, were not reconfirmed for Mana Crack specifically in this pass and have been dropped pending a source that names them at this site.

## Tips

- A full-day charter (approximately 8 hours) is the standard format; budget accordingly
- Sea conditions can be rough on the open-water crossing; consider seasickness precautions
- Live boat diving requires practiced buoy/DSMB deployment for end-of-dive pickup
