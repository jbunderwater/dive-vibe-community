## General Store

General Store is a South Shore boat dive named for its historically "well-stocked" fish life, built around a horseshoe-shaped lava inlet that shelters the remains of the steam schooner Pele — one of Kauai's few accessible historic wrecks.

## Overview

The General Store site sits in a horseshoe inlet with small lava tubes on either side, providing structure for a large variety of fish species to shelter. Sources disagree on the exact depth range: Seasport Divers cites 65–90 feet, Fathom Five cites 65–100 feet, and Garden Isle Divers cites a wider 45–100 feet. Rather than pick one figure, this profile uses the deepest confirmed point (100 feet / 30m) as the maximum. The site is frequently dived as a current-influenced drift, though the inlet itself provides a refuge when surface currents pick up.

The wreck remains of the vessel Pele — which struck the sunken rock Kalanipuao on March 24, 1895, while carrying 200 tons of coal and general freight from Koloa to Makaweli — lie at this site. No lives were lost in the sinking. Historical newspaper accounts (The Garden Island, both a 2000 retrospective and a dedicated 2015 article) describe her specifically as a "steam schooner"; contemporary dive operators (Fathom Five, Garden Isle Divers) more often call her a "freighter." Both descriptions are consistent with a coastal steam-powered cargo vessel of the era — this is a terminology difference, not a conflict about the vessel's identity. No Wikipedia entry exists for this ship, and no source located — historical or maritime — gives a build date, hull length, or tonnage for the Pele herself; those details are not stated here rather than estimated. After more than 130 years underwater, very little of the vessel remains: the boiler, scattered hull plates, and a couple of anchors are the identifiable remnants. The name "General Store" predates common use of the wreck as the primary draw; the site is primarily dived for its abundant fish and macro life.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Poipu area, South Shore, Kauai
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Depth Range**: Reported 45–100 feet depending on source (14–30m); most operators describe the wreck itself as sitting around 65–90 feet
- **Water Temperature**: 24–27°C (75–81°F)

## Wreck Details

- **Vessel**: Pele — described as a "steam schooner" in historical newspaper accounts and as a "freighter" by current dive operators
- **Sank**: March 24, 1895
- **Cause**: Struck sunken rock Kalanipuao, approximately three-quarters of a mile from shore between Koloa and Eleele, during a night passage in heavy rain
- **Cargo**: 200 tons of coal and general freight; no lives lost
- **Remains**: Boiler, hull plates, anchors; minimal structural integrity after 130+ years
- **Unconfirmed**: Build date, hull length, and tonnage of the vessel are not given in any source found (no Wikipedia entry exists for the Pele). Not stated here rather than guessed.

Sources: The Garden Island newspaper (2000 historical ships list and 2015 dedicated article on the Pele) are the historical record for the vessel name, sinking date, and cause; Fathom Five and Garden Isle Divers independently confirm, from the dive-operations side, that a wreck identified as the Pele lies at this site.

## Marine Life

Confirmed on Fathom Five's current site listing: black coral, lemon butterflyfish, helmet conch, octopus, sea turtles, and dolphins passing through the outer water column. Confirmed on Explore Kauai Scuba's current listing: schooling black triggerfish, milletseed butterflyfish, pennant butterflyfish, reticulated butterflyfish (described as extremely rare on Kauai), white-tip reef sharks, spotted puffer, and giant porcupinefish. Lobsters and crabs occupy the small lava tubes on either side of the inlet.

Note: whiskered boarfish appeared in an earlier version of this description, attributed to Fathom Five. Fathom Five's current dive-sites listing for General Store no longer names this species, so the claim has been removed pending reconfirmation rather than carried forward on an outdated source snapshot.
