## Prince Kuhio Beach

Prince Kuhio Beach (also called Prince Kuhio Park) is a very shallow south shore shore dive best known for its reliable sea turtle encounters in 3–21 feet of water.

## Overview

The entry is a rocky lava shore — booties or reef shoes are essential. Divers step off the lava into approximately 2 feet of water, put on fins, and proceed to the reef. The main reef area is a long swim from the entry point. The site is very shallow throughout. Sources disagree slightly on the maximum: this profile previously used 3–21 feet, but The Scuba Directory and Zentacle (both re-checked 2026-08-06) now report a maximum closer to 25 feet (average around 12 feet), so the deeper figure is used here — most of the dive is still well under 10 feet. The Scuba Directory notes that the reef is not particularly fish-rich or coral-dense compared to nearby sites, but the site's appeal is primarily the reliable presence of Hawaiian green sea turtles resting and grazing in the shallows. Some reviewers describe the entry itself as rough, with poor footing in surge conditions, despite the easy underwater profile.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Koloa, South Shore, Kauai (approximately 4.8 miles south of Koloa town on Poipu Road west fork)
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 8 meters
- **Depth Range**: Sources report 21–25 feet maximum (6.4–7.6m); average around 12 feet
- **Water Temperature**: 25–28°C (77–82°F)

## Marine Life

Hawaiian green sea turtles are the primary draw and are frequently observed resting and feeding in the shallows, with reviewers reporting 10-20+ individuals at cleaning stations on a single visit. Whitemouth moray eels, trumpetfish, and Picasso triggerfish (humuhumunukunukuapua'a) are confirmed at this site. Re-checked 2026-08-06: The Scuba Directory and Zentacle additionally confirm 3-spot dascyllus juveniles, cleaner shrimp, starfish, spotted boxfish, and — as occasional sightings — spotted eagle rays, scorpionfish, lionfish, and gray reef/sandbar sharks. General reef fish are present, though fish density and coral coverage are described by The Scuba Directory as limited compared to nearby Koloa Landing.

## Tips

- Bring reef shoes or booties — the lava entry is rough, and some divers report difficult footing in surge
- The reef is a long surface swim from the entry; plan your air accordingly
- Suitable as a warm-up dive alongside a Koloa Landing trip rather than a primary destination
