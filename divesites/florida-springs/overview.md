## Florida Springs

Freshwater cavern and cave diving in north-central Florida's spring-fed karst system, famous for 100+ foot visibility, a constant ~68-72°F year-round, and some of the longest mapped underwater cave systems in the continental US.

## Description

North-central and north Florida (roughly the Gilchrist/Suwannee/Alachua/Marion/Levy/Lafayette/Madison county belt, extending into the Panhandle for a few outlying named sites) hosts one of the highest concentrations of freshwater cave and cavern diving sites in the world, fed by the Floridan Aquifer. More than 700 springs surface across the northern Florida peninsula, and the region is the historical home of U.S. cave-diving training organizations including the NACD (National Association for Cave Diving) and the NSS-CDS (National Speleological Society – Cave Diving Section).

**IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTE**: Full cave penetration beyond the cavern/daylight zone is one of the highest-risk activities in diving and requires specific full-cave certification (NACD, NSS-CDS, or equivalent) and specialized training and equipment. Cavern zones stay within the daylight zone and a short linear distance from open water; cave zones go beyond the light zone into a fully overhead environment. Access policy also varies materially by site — Troy Spring, for example, permits open-water diving only, with cave entry and dive lights explicitly prohibited.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Cavern Diving**: Sites like Devil's Den and Blue Grotto have wide, accessible cavern zones suitable for cavern-certified divers and training.
- **Cave Diving**: Ginnie Springs (Devil's Eye/Devil's Ear), Wes Skiles Peacock Springs State Park, and Madison Blue Spring connect to extensive mapped cave systems requiring full cave certification.
- **Shore Diving**: All sites in this region are shore-entry.

### Accessibility

- **Getting There**: Most springs cluster around High Springs, Branford, and Williston, roughly an hour from Gainesville Regional Airport (GNV).
- **Access**: Several springs are privately operated day-use recreation sites with posted entry fees; others are Florida State Parks with specific diving policies (verify cave-access rules per site before diving).

### Conditions

- **Water Type**: Freshwater, spring-fed.
- **Season**: Diveable year-round; water temperature stays constant regardless of season.
- **Visibility**: Famously excellent, commonly 100+ feet under normal flow conditions.
- **Temperature**: Constant ~68-72°F (20-22°C) depending on the spring.

## Additional Information

- **Currency**: US Dollar
- **Language**: English
- **Safety**: Full cave certification (NACD/NSS-CDS or equivalent) and continuous guideline use are required for any diving beyond the cavern/daylight zone. Access rules differ by site — some state parks restrict diving to open water only, and some sites are gated/members-only.
