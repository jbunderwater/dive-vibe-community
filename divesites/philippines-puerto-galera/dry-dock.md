# Dry Dock

A scuttled steel-and-plywood dry dock structure resting at up to 30 meters off Scandi Dive Resort, now encrusted with sponges and gorgonians and home to a variety of reef fish.

## Overview

The Dry Dock is a large steel-and-plywood floating dry dock structure, originally used for hoisting small boats, that was deliberately sunk off Scandi Dive Resort to create an artificial reef. PADI's site listing dates the scuttling to 1998 (single-source attribution -- no second source confirms the exact year). Sitting at up to 30 meters depth with a blue-water descent to around 25 meters, the structure has developed a healthy coating of sponges and gorgonian sea fans, and an adjacent coral reef ridge adds further habitat. An earlier version of this description mentioned nearby beer-bottle-crate artificial reef structures and regular turtle sightings on ascent; neither claim could be corroborated by additional sources and both have been removed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Scandi Dive Resort, Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate (PADI recommends this for advanced divers due to occasional currents and the blue-water descent)
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-20 meters
- **Current**: Generally mild, occasionally stronger
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

Sweetlips, batfish, surgeonfish, groupers, and snappers are reported around the structure, along with lionfish, pufferfish, and porcupinefish. Nudibranchs and flatworms graze on the sponge-covered surfaces. Octopus have been found on and around the wreck, and the adjacent coral reef ridge hosts pygmy seahorses and anemonefish colonies (per PADI's listing).

## Dive Profile

Descend to the dry dock structure at up to 30 meters and explore the exterior, checking sponge-covered surfaces for nudibranchs and flatworms and looking into gaps for octopus. As bottom time at depth is limited, plan the deeper exploration first, then work gradually shallower toward the adjacent reef ridge where pygmy seahorses and anemonefish have been reported. Finish with a safety stop in the shallows.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a torch to peer into the structure's recesses for octopus and reef fish
- Bottom time at 25-30 meters is limited on air; Nitrox is recommended
- Ask your guide about current conditions before descending, as they can pick up
