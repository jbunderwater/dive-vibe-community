# Dry Dock

A large steel-and-plywood structure, originally built to lift small boats out of the water, scuttled around 1998 to form an artificial reef now heavily encrusted with coral and sea fans.

## Overview

Dry Dock is a large steel-and-plywood construction, originally designed to lift small boats out of the water, that was sunk to create an artificial reef -- operators generally date the scuttling to around 1998. The structure now resembles a large table on the seabed and is completely covered in coral growth and sea fans. An earlier version of this page described a "floating dry dock" scuttled specifically off Scandi Dive Resort with additional beer-bottle-crate artificial reef structures nearby; neither the floating-dry-dock framing, the specific resort location, nor the beer-bottle-crate detail could be corroborated against other sources during this audit, so they have been removed rather than repeated on a single, unverified source. No source documents a vessel name, build date, or tonnage for this structure, since it was never itself a ship.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-20 meters
- **Current**: Can be strong; several sources recommend this site for advanced/Nitrox divers rather than beginners
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

The structure hosts larger reef fish including sweetlips, batfish, surgeonfish, groupers, and snappers. Lionfish are commonly found on top of the structure, and large pufferfish and porcupinefish shelter between its pylons. Moray eels and octopus are reported in the structure's gaps and recesses.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the dry dock structure and work along its coral-and-sea-fan-encrusted surfaces, checking for lionfish on top and pufferfish or porcupinefish tucked between the pylons. Peer into gaps for moray eels and octopus. Given the depth (up to 30 meters) and reports of strong current at this site, plan bottom time conservatively and ascend gradually, finishing with a safety stop.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This site is generally recommended for advanced divers, ideally on Nitrox, rather than beginners
- Bring a torch to peer into gaps for moray eels and octopus
- Check between the structure's pylons for pufferfish and porcupinefish
- Bottom time at 30 meters is limited on air
