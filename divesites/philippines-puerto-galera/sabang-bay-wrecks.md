# Sabang Bay Wrecks

Three deliberately sunk small vessels on a sandy bottom in Sabang Bay -- a mix of steel-hulled and wooden-hulled wrecks -- forming a beginner-friendly wreck and critter dive.

## Overview

Sabang Bay Wrecks (also listed as Sabang Wrecks) is a collection of three deliberately sunk small vessels resting on the sandy bottom of Sabang Bay at shallow depths. PADI and BADLADZ both corroborate the count of three wrecks, describing a mix of steel-hulled and wooden-hulled vessels rather than the single named "yacht" this description previously singled out. The shallow depth and calm bay conditions make this an accessible site for beginners, while the critter life rewards divers willing to look carefully.

An earlier version of this description named a resident "black giant anglerfish" on the yacht and a "peacock razorfish" in the surrounding sand. Neither claim could be corroborated by PADI's or BADLADZ's independent listings for this site, so both have been removed. Turtles are confirmed here by both PADI and this page's original source, but the added detail of turtles "with remoras" is unconfirmed and has been dropped.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Sabang Bay, Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 19 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-30 meters depending on source
- **Current**: Generally calm
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

Turtles are commonly seen here, confirmed by two independent sources. The wrecks and surrounding rubble support a rich variety of critters: damselfish, batfish, surgeonfish, scorpionfish, moray eels, lionfish, and frogfish (including juveniles) are reported by BADLADZ and PADI alike. Mantis shrimp, cleaner shrimp, and goby fish occupy the sand between wrecks, and PADI additionally notes skeleton shrimp, demon stingers, pufferfish, snake eel, and stargazer in the surrounding rubble zones. Octopus, squid, sea slugs, and hermit crabs have also been reported.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the wreck cluster and explore each of the three vessels in turn, checking recesses and encrusted surfaces for moray eels, frogfish, and nudibranchs. Check the sand between wrecks for mantis shrimp, skeleton shrimp, and demon stingers. Watch for turtles moving through the area. The shallow depth and calm conditions allow for relaxed, extended dives with plenty of time to hunt for critters.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Move slowly over sandy areas between wrecks for camouflaged critters like demon stingers and skeleton shrimp
- Excellent beginner wreck dive -- shallow depth and generally calm conditions
- A torch helps reveal colours and life in the wreck shadows
- Macro photography is well rewarded at this site given the critter density
