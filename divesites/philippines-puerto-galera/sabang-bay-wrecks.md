# Sabang Bay Wrecks

Three small scuttled boats -- a steel-hulled yacht and two wooden boats -- resting on a shallow sandy bottom in Sabang Bay, a beginner-friendly wreck-and-critter site popular for fish feeding.

## Overview

Sabang Bay Wrecks (also called Sabang Wrecks) is a cluster of three small, deliberately scuttled boats on the sandy bottom of Sabang Bay: one steel-hulled yacht and two wooden boats, described by different sources variously as fishing junks or general wooden vessels in different stages of decay. No source documents individual vessel names, build dates, or tonnage for these boats -- unlike the well-documented MV Alma Jane nearby, this is a case where the historical record genuinely isn't available, and this page states that rather than guessing. The shallow depth (maximum 19 meters here; other sources cite the broader wreck cluster at 18-23 meters) and generally calm bay conditions make this an accessible, popular site for beginners. An earlier version of this page named a specific "black giant anglerfish" resident on the yacht; other sources describe anglerfish more generally on the site's sponges rather than tied to that specific boat, so the claim has been softened accordingly.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Sabang Bay, Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 19 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10-15 meters, up to 30 meters reported in some seasons
- **Current**: Generally calm
- **Water Temperature**: 27-30°C (April-November), 19-24°C (December-mid March)

## Marine Life

Anglerfish have been reported in the area, particularly on the site's elephant-ear and barrel sponges. Frogfish of various sizes -- including juveniles not yet large enough to climb onto the wrecks -- are a signature find, along with seahorses and ghost pipefish. Turtles, often accompanied by remoras, are frequently sighted. Skeleton shrimp and demon stingers hide in sandy areas covered with sea pens, and mantis shrimp occupy burrows between the wrecks. Nudibranchs and scorpionfish are found on the wreck surfaces.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the wreck cluster and explore the steel-hulled yacht first, checking nearby sponges for anglerfish and frogfish. Move to the two wooden boats and examine their more heavily decayed structures for nudibranchs and scorpionfish. Check sandy areas between the wrecks, covered in sea pens, for skeleton shrimp and demon stingers, and watch for turtles moving through the site. The shallow depth and calm conditions allow for relaxed, extended dives.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Check sponges near the yacht for anglerfish and frogfish of various sizes
- Move slowly over sandy, sea-pen-covered areas for skeleton shrimp and demon stingers
- Excellent beginner wreck dive -- shallow depth and generally no current
- A torch helps reveal colours and life in the wreck shadows
- This site is popular for fish feeding, which affects fish behavior near divers
