# Sabang Point

A current-affected site on eastern Sabang Bay with sand and coral outcrops, reliable turtle sightings, and PADI-reported pygmy seahorses and blue ribbon eels.

## Overview

Sabang Point sits on the eastern side of Sabang Bay, where currents sweep around the headland and bring nutrients that support a varied community of marine life. The bottom is a mix of sand and coral outcrops, with a small wall dropping to a white sandy bottom around 20 meters (per PADI). Turtles are regularly encountered here, confirmed by both PADI and this page's original source. An earlier version of this description also named large pufferfish, porcupinefish, garden eels, and egg cowries; none of these could be corroborated by PADI's or Capt'n Gregg's independent listings for this site, so they have been removed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Eastern Sabang Bay, Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: ~15 meters
- **Current**: Variable, often present
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

Turtles are one of the most reliable sightings at Sabang Point, corroborated by two sources. PADI's listing reports pygmy seahorses on the site's fan corals and blue ribbon eels on sandy patches -- single-source, attributed to PADI. Parrotfish, snappers, surgeonfish, and triggerfish are also reported, along with cuttlefish, octopus, scorpionfish, frogfish, nudibranchs, and moray eels.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the sand and coral outcrops, working with any current present. Explore the coral outcrops and fan corals for pygmy seahorses, and check sandy patches for blue ribbon eels and octopus. Watch for turtles throughout the dive. Work gradually shallower through the coral outcrops for the second half of the dive.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your guide about recent pygmy seahorse and blue ribbon eel sightings
- Check coral outcrops carefully for octopus and nudibranchs
- Current can be variable -- be prepared with an SMB for drift situations
- Turtles are often seen mid-dive -- keep scanning ahead
