# Sabang Point

A current-affected site on eastern Sabang Bay with sand and coral outcrops, turtles, pygmy seahorses, garden eels, and three small wrecks nearby.

## Overview

Sabang Point sits on the eastern side of Sabang Bay, where currents sweep around the headland and bring nutrients that support a varied community of marine life. The bottom is a mix of sand and coral outcrops broken up by small walls and ridges at varying depths. Turtles are regularly encountered, along with pygmy seahorses -- confirmed independently by more than one operator source. Three small scuttled vessels (a steel-hulled yacht and two wooden boats) lie close to Sabang Point, per multiple sources, though they are treated here as a nearby, distinct feature rather than folded into the main reef/drift description of this site. Deeper sandy areas host colonies of garden eels, while coral outcrops harbour nudibranchs, egg cowries, and octopus.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Eastern Sabang Bay, Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 27 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: ~15 meters
- **Current**: Variable, often present
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

Turtles are one of the most reliable sightings at Sabang Point, often seen cruising between coral outcrops. Pygmy seahorses have been reported here, along with large pufferfish and porcupinefish patrolling the reef edges. At greater depth, garden eels extend from the sandy bottom, retracting as divers approach. Moray eels are very common. Nudibranchs, leaf scorpionfish, ornate ghost pipefish, and egg cowries are found across the coral outcrops, and octopus hide in crevices among them.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the sand and coral outcrops, working with any current present. Visit the deeper sandy areas to observe garden eels, approaching slowly to avoid spooking them back into their burrows. Explore the coral outcrops for pygmy seahorses, nudibranchs, egg cowries, and octopus. Watch for turtles, pufferfish, and porcupinefish throughout the dive. If time and route allow, the nearby small wreck cluster (steel yacht and two wooden boats) adds macro subjects sheltering in soft coral on their surfaces. Maximum depth reaches approximately 27-30 meters.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Approach garden eel colonies slowly and stay low to the bottom for better viewing
- Check coral outcrops carefully for pygmy seahorses, egg cowries, and octopus
- Current can be variable -- be prepared with an SMB for drift situations
- The mix of sand and coral habitats means both macro and wide-angle subjects are available
- Ask your guide whether the day's route includes the nearby small wreck cluster
