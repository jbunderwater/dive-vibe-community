# Fantasea

A drift and reef dive close to the El Galleon jetty, with coral bommies, swim-throughs, and reported gorgonian-dwelling pygmy seahorses.

## Overview

Fantasea (also listed as Fantasea Reef) lies close to Puerto Galera's El Galleon jetty and combines drift-dive and reef characteristics over coral bommies and swim-throughs. An earlier version of this description made a headline claim of a rare white Rhinopias frondosa (weedy scorpionfish) sighting at this site, along with scuttled aluminium boats, orangutan crabs, mantis shrimp, and banded sea snakes. None of these claims could be corroborated by PADI's or Zentacle's independent listings for this site, so they have been removed rather than repeated. Sources also disagree on depth, ranging from about 15 to 27 meters -- treat this as a moderate-depth site rather than a fixed number.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near El Galleon jetty, Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 23 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-20 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

One source (Zentacle) reports pygmy seahorses on the site's gorgonian sea fans at a relatively shallow, photography-friendly depth. PADI's listing describes schools of butterflyfish and angelfish, along with sea turtles, blue-spotted stingrays, lionfish, sharks, barracudas, and rays passing through. Regional diving characteristics (turtles, reef fish, and macro life typical of Puerto Galera) likely also apply here, but no additional site-specific reports were found beyond what is listed above.

## Dive Profile

This is a drift dive over coral bommies and swim-throughs near the El Galleon jetty. Scan gorgonian fans for seahorses and watch the sandy patches for blue-spotted stingrays. Keep an eye on the blue water for passing sharks, barracuda, and rays. Deploy an SMB for boat pickup at the end of the drift.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your guide about current pygmy seahorse sightings on the site's gorgonians
- Carry an SMB as this is a drift dive and you may surface away from the entry point
- A macro lens works well for the gorgonian fans; keep an eye on the blue water too for passing pelagics
