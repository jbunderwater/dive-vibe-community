# Montani

A shallow muck dive site (also listed as Montani Bay) over sand and sea grass, known for rare critters including wonderpus, blue-ringed octopus, and flamboyant cuttlefish.

## Overview

Montani, also listed as Montani Bay, is a shallow muck dive site that epitomises muck diving at its finest. The bottom is predominantly sand covered in sea grass. Sources conflict on entry and current conditions: Michael McFadyen's original notes describe a shore entry with a gentle drift, while PADI's listing describes boat entry into a bay "protected from currents." Both are presented here rather than resolved, since neither could be independently confirmed. An earlier version of this description included a specific claim of an 81-minute dive logged by a visiting diver and a submerged water pipe used as artificial habitat; neither detail could be corroborated by other sources and both have been removed. The shallow maximum depth does allow for long bottom times, and Montani is suitable for beginners as well as experienced divers as an introduction to muck diving.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Puerto Galera channel/bay area, Mindoro Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 14 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 5-15 meters
- **Current**: Sources disagree -- described as either a gentle drift or a current-protected bay
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

The macro life density at Montani is exceptional and corroborated across sources. Nudibranchs, frogfish, pipefish, and scorpionfish are found throughout the site. PADI and Casalay (a local dive operator) both report the occasional rare find of wonderpus, blue-ringed octopus, and flamboyant cuttlefish -- two independent sources agree on all three species. Seahorses, ghost pipefish, bobtail squid, mimic octopus, and cuttlefish have also been reported.

## Dive Profile

Enter as directed by your guide and move slowly over the sand and sea grass, scanning carefully for camouflaged critters. The shallow maximum depth allows for extended bottom times. The gentle conditions make this an ideal site for extended macro photography sessions.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a macro lens -- this is a dedicated critter site and wide-angle is largely wasted here
- Move very slowly and scan the bottom carefully; the best finds are camouflaged
- Ask your guide about recent wonderpus, blue-ringed octopus, or flamboyant cuttlefish sightings
- Confirm entry type and current conditions with your dive operator before the dive, as sources disagree
- Suitable for beginners, but the macro life rewards experienced eyes
