# The Canyons

Puerto Galera's signature Advanced dive -- a series of three canyons with strong currents, huge schools of jacks and trevally, an old Spanish anchor embedded in the rock, and reported sightings of manta rays, thresher sharks, and hammerhead sharks.

## Overview

The Canyons is widely considered Puerto Galera's premier dive site and carries an Advanced rating due to strong currents. The site comprises a series of three canyons with colourful walls, with reef tops around 22-24 meters and canyon floors reaching roughly 24, 27, and 30 meters at each successive canyon -- with a real risk of being swept to 40 meters or beyond in strong current, per two independent operator sources. Strong currents sweep through the canyons, bringing nutrients that support impressive marine life, most notably large schools of jacks, trevally, and batfish.

An old anchor sits embedded in the rock at the end of the deepest canyon. An earlier version of this description identified it as an "Admiralty anchor"; this has been corrected to "Spanish anchor," which is how it is independently described by both PADI's site listing and BADLADZ's dive site page -- two sources contradicting the prior single-source claim. This description previously also claimed occasional whale shark sightings; that specific claim was not corroborated by either new source. Instead, both PADI and BADLADZ independently report manta rays, thresher sharks, and hammerhead sharks as the large pelagics seen here, so the whale shark claim has been replaced with these corroborated sightings.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Puerto Galera, Mindoro Island, Philippines (northeast tip, Verde Island Passage)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Drift dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 29 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15-20 meters
- **Current**: Strong -- this is a current-driven site, best dived on a flood tide per BADLADZ
- **Water Temperature**: 28-29°C

## Marine Life

Schools of jacks, trevally, and king jacks are the headline attraction, corroborated across all sources, along with batfish and sweetlips. Manta rays, thresher sharks, and hammerhead sharks have been reported here, corroborated by two independent operator sources. Barracuda, tuna, and mackerel are also reported darting through the canyons. Barrel and basket sponges grow tall on the canyon walls, alongside giant gorgonian fans, whip corals, and hard green tree corals.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the canyon system, with reef tops around 22-24 meters and canyon floors progressively deeper at each of the three canyons (roughly 24, 27, and 30 meters). Work through the formations, using the canyon walls for shelter from the strongest current. The old Spanish anchor embedded in the rock at the deepest canyon is a distinctive landmark worth finding, and also serves as a regrouping point before ascent. Watch for schooling jacks and trevally in the water column. Strong currents require good buoyancy control and constant awareness of depth and position -- current can sweep divers beyond 40 meters if caution lapses. Gas management is critical; plan conservatively and begin ascending with ample reserves.

## Tips and Recommendations

- This is an Advanced-rated site for good reason -- strong currents are the norm, not the exception
- Stay close to your guide and group, as currents can separate divers quickly and pull you deeper than planned
- The schooling jacks and trevally are best appreciated with a wide-angle lens
- Look for the old Spanish anchor embedded in the rock at the deepest canyon
- Carry an SMB and be prepared for drift conditions
- Nitrox is recommended to extend limited bottom time
- If current is exceptionally strong, your guide may call the dive -- respect this decision
