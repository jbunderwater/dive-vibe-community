## Chicken Reef

Chicken Reef is a large reef complex in the eastern Dampier Strait, described by Papua Diving as divided into four distinct sections, each with its own character.

## Overview

Chicken Reef is a shallow reef complex that sources rate anywhere from Beginner-to-Intermediate (papua-diving.com) up to Intermediate-to-Advanced (rajaampatbiodiversity.com) — reflecting the site's variable conditions across its four sections. Water temperature averages 27–29°C.

**Correction (2026-08-27 initial validation):** previous content on this page ("named after local chicken farming," specific fish-community descriptions) was unsourced template text and has been replaced. Sources disagree on difficulty rating (Beginner-Intermediate vs. Intermediate-Advanced); Intermediate is used here as the reported middle ground, but divers should ask their guide which of the reef's four sections is planned, since conditions vary by section.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Eastern Dampier Strait, Raja Ampat, West Papua, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef (divided into four sections)
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate (sources range from Beginner-Intermediate to Intermediate-Advanced depending on section and current)
- **Maximum Depth**: Sources report up to 30 meters
- **Current**: Variable by section
- **Best Time**: October to April

## Marine Life

Sources confirmed at this specific site (papua-diving.com, rajaampatbiodiversity.com): pipefish, seahorses, nudibranchs, and wobbegong sharks. La Galigo Liveaboard's site index categorizes the location within its "seamount" grouping, though the two dedicated descriptions above both describe it as a reef complex rather than a true pinnacle.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from eastern Dampier Strait liveaboards or day-trip operators based near Mansuar or Arborek. Ask your guide which of the four sections is being dived, as conditions and difficulty vary between them.

## Safety Considerations

Conditions vary meaningfully across the reef's four sections — treat each as a distinct dive plan. Carry an SMB and confirm the planned section and its current with your guide beforehand.
