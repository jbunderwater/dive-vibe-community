## Farondi Cave

Farondi Cave and Wall is one of Misool's cave dive sites, on Daram Island in southern Raja Ampat — divers descend a vertical wall studded with overhangs before entering the cave chamber itself.

## Overview

The dive approaches via a vertical wall covered in numerous overhangs before reaching the cave entrance at roughly 5 metres depth. The cave is described as easy to enter and having a unique, atmospheric character, in contrast to more technical cave sites. Giant gorgonian sea fans are a feature both on the approach wall and inside the cave area. Note: an earlier version of this page described a "sea tunnel bisecting the island, exiting on a wall at 30 metres" — that specific claim could not be corroborated in this research round and has been replaced with the shallower, gorgonian-lined chamber described above. The site's overall depth range likely extends deeper along the approach wall, consistent with typical Misool wall depths in this area (roughly 5–30m), but this research did not find a source confirming a specific maximum beyond the ~5m cave chamber itself.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Daram Island, Misool, Raja Ampat, West Papua, Indonesia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

Sources confirmed at this specific site (notroublesjustbubbles.com): giant gorgonian sea fans hosting pygmy seahorses. The broader Sagof/Wagmab/Farondi area is described as having nudibranchs, flatworms, anemone crabs, and shrimp on its walls and overhangs, though those are area-level observations rather than confirmed sightings inside Farondi Cave specifically.

## Entry and Exit

Boat access from Misool liveaboards or Misool Eco Resort. Cave/overhang diving of this kind requires buoyancy control and comfort with overhead or semi-overhead environments; never attempt penetration beyond your training.

## Safety Considerations

Difficulty is classified Advanced in line with Raja Ampat's remote, liveaboard-access diving character and the site's overhead/cave elements. Any overhead-environment diving carries inherent risk even at shallow depth — this is not a routine or "completely safe" activity, and proper training and a guide familiar with the site are essential.
