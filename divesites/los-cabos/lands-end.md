## Land's End

The southernmost tip of the Baja Peninsula, where the Pacific Ocean meets the Sea of Cortez at the famous El Arco rock arch. A resident colony of California sea lions regularly approaches divers, making this one of the most entertaining dives in Cabo. The scattered remains of the cargo ship *Harry Lundeberg* lie across a football-field-sized area of seafloor near the point. Depth ranges from 8 to 22 meters across the site.

**Wreck history.** The vessel was launched in North Vancouver in late 1943 as *Kitsilano Park*, a "North Sands"-type Park Ship — Canada's wartime equivalent of the American Liberty ship — built for the Park Steamship Company of Montreal. She changed hands and names several times after the war: renamed *Lake Kootenay* in 1946, *Phopho* under Panamanian ownership in 1949, and finally *Harry Lundeberg* in 1950 after being acquired by Kaiser Gypsum-linked interests operating as Gypsum Carrier Inc. On February 8, 1954, while carrying gypsum plaster from Isla San Marcos in the Sea of Cortez, she struck a rock shelf near Land's End; Lloyd's Register logged her as "extensively damaged" and a total loss. Some local retellings describe a crew member deliberately steering the ship aground over gambling debts — this account is unconfirmed and should be treated as local lore rather than established fact. North Sands-type ships were built to a standard specification of roughly 7,130 gross tons; sources disagree on her length, citing figures from about 129 to 134 meters (424–441 ft). The wreck lay buried under shifting sand for decades until Hurricane Odile exposed sections of it in 2014. Sources disagree on its current state — some report the wreckage still surfaces intermittently as sand shifts, while others report it was reburied by a subsequent storm — so divers should not expect a guaranteed wreck viewing on any given trip.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Cabo San Lucas Marine Park, at the tip of the Baja Peninsula
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 22 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 10–25 meters
- **Current**: Can be strong — this is the meeting point of two bodies of water
- **Best Time**: September to November for best visibility and calmest conditions

## Marine Life

California sea lions are the star attraction and frequently approach divers directly. Schools of cow-nose rays, snappers, and baitfish are common. Moray eels, octopus, and pufferfish occupy rocky crevices. Whitetip reef sharks are also reported at the site. Mobula rays pass through seasonally, and whale sharks are occasionally sighted in the area.

## Dive Profile

The site surrounds the granite rock formations at the very tip of the peninsula. Surge and current are common due to the convergence of the Pacific and the Sea of Cortez. Start on the sheltered bay side and work toward the point as conditions allow. Wreck remains of the *Harry Lundeberg*, when exposed, lie scattered between roughly 12 and 30 meters — look for corroded hull sections and encrusted metal among the boulders, but be aware their visibility depends on recent sand movement (see Wreck History above). Strong surface current can develop quickly; carry an SMB.

## Safety Considerations

Currents and surge can intensify without warning, particularly on the Pacific side. Not recommended for inexperienced divers in rough conditions. Boat traffic is heavy in the area — deploy an SMB before surfacing.
