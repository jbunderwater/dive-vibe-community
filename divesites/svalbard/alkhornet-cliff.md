## Alkhornet Cliff

Alkhornet Cliff is a wall dive beneath a major Arctic seabird colony at the entrance to Isfjorden, Svalbard.

## Overview

Alkhornet is a 428-metre mountain on the northern side of the entrance to Isfjorden on Spitsbergen's west coast, next to the bay of Trygghamna. The cliffs are metamorphosed carbonate rock described as over a billion years old, and are a BirdLife International Important Bird Area. Sources disagree on the scale of the seabird colony: Wikipedia, citing IBA data, gives about 10,000 breeding pairs of seabirds, while the Norwegian Polar Institute's cruise handbook says Brünnich's guillemots number "in hundreds of thousands" and also reports a large kittiwake colony. No single bird count is given here.

Underwater, a 2026 Oceanwide Expeditions trip log describes a dive at Alkhornet (locally Sagaskjeret): many jellyfish and delicate comb jellies during the descent, a kelp forest below, and anemones, sea urchins, crabs and numerous other invertebrates on the rocky wall. This is a single operator report; the log gives no depth. No other site-specific marine-life source was found. The cruise handbook mentions seals in the water in neighbouring Trygghamna, not at the cliff itself, and does not mention diving.
