## Alkhornet Cliff

Alkhornet Cliff is a dive beneath a major Arctic seabird cliff at the entrance to Isfjorden, Svalbard.

## Overview

Alkhornet is a 428-metre mountain of metamorphosed carbonate rock more than a billion years old, on the northern side of the entrance to Isfjorden near the bay of Trygghamna. It is a BirdLife Important Bird Area. Sources disagree on the scale of the colony: Wikipedia gives about 10,000 breeding pairs of seabirds, while the Norwegian Polar Institute's cruise handbook says Brünnich's guillemots number in the hundreds of thousands, with a large kittiwake colony, glaucous gulls, nesting Arctic skuas and geese. No single bird count is given here.

Underwater, an Oceanwide Expeditions trip log (dive 7, Alkhornet "Sagaskjeret", 25 June 2026) describes passing through countless jellyfish and comb jellies to a flourishing kelp forest, with anemones, sea urchins, crabs and other invertebrates along the rocky wall. This is the only dive-specific source we found; it gives no depth or conditions, and the 22 m maximum depth and wall classification in our data rest on it alone. The Polar Institute handbook says seals may be seen in the water at neighbouring Trygghamna, not specifically at Alkhornet.

## Site Information
