## Alkhornet Cliff

Alkhornet Cliff takes its name from Alkhornet, the dramatic headland that guards the northern entrance to Isfjorden on Spitsbergen's west coast — a site better documented for its towering seabird cliffs than for its diving.

## Overview

Alkhornet is a 428-metre headland of metamorphosed carbonate rock, dated at over a billion years old, standing at the entrance to Isfjorden near the bay of Trygghamna. Above water it is one of the largest bird cliffs in western Spitsbergen, supporting more than 60,000 breeding kittiwakes and Brünnich's guillemots, along with fulmar, glaucous gull, little auk, and black guillemot; nutrient runoff from the colony feeds a patch of unusually lush tundra below, grazed by reindeer and used by nesting geese and denning Arctic foxes.

Alkhornet is named as a recognized diving area in at least one Spitsbergen liveaboard itinerary, alongside Kongsfjorden and Forlandsundet, confirming that dive trips do visit the site. However, no source found during this audit describes the underwater topography, exact depth profile, or marine life actually observed at Alkhornet specifically. The listed **wall** site type and 22 m depth are carried over from the existing record — plausible given the headland's steep above-water face, but not independently confirmed underwater, so treat them as provisional. Regional Arctic species (kelp, anemones, sea urchins, seals) are common elsewhere in Isfjorden and may well occur here, but no site-specific sighting report was found, so none are named below.
