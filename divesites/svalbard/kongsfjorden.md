## Kongsfjorden

Kongsfjorden is a glacial fjord on Spitsbergen's west coast that is a focus of Arctic kelp-forest diving research.

## Overview

Kongsfjorden is 26 km long and 6-14 km wide, with the Kronebreen and Kongsvegen glaciers at its head, and has been a Ramsar wetland since 24 July 1985 (Wikipedia). It is an open fjord without a sill, strongly influenced by inflow of warm Atlantic water from the West Spitsbergen Current (Hop and Wiencke, Kongsfjorden book introduction, Norwegian Polar Institute).

Scientific divers have monitored its kelp forests. Framforum reports large oarweed, winged kelp and sugar kelp as the dominant brown seaweeds, with kelp forests from the surface to 15-25 m and red algae persisting to at least 40 m. At the Hansneset site, shallow kelp biomass has risen 7-8 fold since the early survey; winged kelp now dominates to about 10 m while oarweed and sugar kelp have retreated to about 2.5 m. Hordes of green sea urchins were seen in the outer fjord in May 2024.

## Marine Life

Oceanwide Expeditions reports for the fjord: polar cod and capelin as the most common fish, the shrimp Pandalus borealis, ringed seals (50-100 pups born each March-April), bearded seals (about 25 pups a year), beluga whales (early spring through summer), minke whales in the outer fjord (late spring through summer) and fin whales foraging in the area. These are fjord-level reports rather than dive logs from a specific dive spot. Visibility, temperature and depth specifics for a recreational dive were not found; the 25 m maximum depth in our data is unverified.
