## Kongsfjorden

Kongsfjorden is one of the Arctic's most scientifically studied kelp-forest fjords, at 79 degrees north on Spitsbergen's west coast.

## Overview

Kongsfjorden is a 26 km long, 6-14 km wide glacial fjord on Spitsbergen's west coast, headed by the Kronebreen and Kongsvegen glaciers, and a Ramsar wetland (designated 24 July 1985). Long-term scientific diving surveys (the BlueARC project) describe kelp forests of large oarweed, winged kelp and sugar kelp from the surface to roughly 15-25 metres, with red algae persisting to at least 40 metres. At the shallow Hansneset study site, kelp biomass has risen about 7-8 fold since the early surveys, and large numbers of sea urchins were observed in the outer fjord in May 2024, raising concern about grazing pressure.

Wildlife reported for the fjord as a whole (not for a specific dive spot) includes ringed seals breeding here (roughly 50-100 pups born each March-April), bearded seals at low density, beluga whales from early spring through summer, minke whales in the outer fjord from late spring, and polar cod and capelin as the most common fish. Fin whales forage nearby but usually stay in coastal and offshore waters rather than inside the fjords. Visibility and conditions were not covered by the sources found and are not stated here.
