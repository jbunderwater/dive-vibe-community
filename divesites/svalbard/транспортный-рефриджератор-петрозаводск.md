## транспортный рефриджератор "Петрозаводск"

The Petrozavodsk refrigerator transport is a modern shipwreck dive site at Bear Island (Bjørnøya), Svalbard.

## Overview

The MS Petrozavodsk was a Russian refrigerated cargo vessel (a freezer ship serving fishing fleets) that ran aground on rocks below the cliffs near the southern tip of Bear Island (Bjørnøya), between Svalbard and mainland Norway, on 11 May 2009. All 12 crew were rescued. Rocks penetrated her port side.

## Wreck History

- **Built**: launched 4 September 1978 by Skala Skipasmidja, Faroe Islands, as Nordlandia (Wikipedia). Other sources consulted did not state a build date.
- **Names**: Nordlandia, Zapolyarye (2003), Petrozavodsk (2004) (Wikipedia only).
- **Size**: Wikipedia gives 67 m length, 12 m beam and 1,130 GT. No other source consulted states length or gross tonnage, so these figures rest on a single source. Deadweight is reported as 1,372 DWT (Wikipedia) and 1,287 DWT (gCaptain); the sources conflict.
- **Cause**: Sources agree the ship grounded; the captain and first officer were accused of being under the influence of alcohol and the first officer of falling asleep on watch. Spitsbergen-Svalbard.com reports sentences of 15 days (captain) and 40 days (first officer). The southern coast of Bjørnøya is closed to vessels over 40 feet from 15 May to 31 August. Wikipedia's article body does not discuss the sentences.
- **Pollution**: Wikipedia reports about 50 tons of diesel aboard, with the spilled diesel spreading over about 3 square km and largely evaporating; gCaptain reports only a small sheen that dissipated. Wikipedia also reports lead and cadmium contamination detected in sediment and marine life by 2011.
- **Current state**: Broken into two parts by 2011 (Wikipedia). gCaptain notes falling stones and landslide fears hampered salvage.

## Diving

No source found documents recreational or technical diving on this wreck, so depth, water temperature, conditions and entry details are not confirmed. The listed maximum depth of 25 m comes from the existing dataset and is unverified. The site is remote, exposed to cliff rockfall and in a seabird protection area; it should not be treated as an accessible dive without expedition-level planning. Wreck penetration, in particular, requires proper training and equipment. No site-specific marine-life reports were found.
