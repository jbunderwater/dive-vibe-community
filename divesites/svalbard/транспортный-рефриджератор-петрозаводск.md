## транспортный рефриджератор "Петрозаводск"

The Petrozavodsk was a Russian refrigerated transport ("freezing ship") that ran aground on Bear Island (Bjørnøya) in May 2009; its wreck lies at the foot of the island's cliffs.

## Overview

The Petrozavodsk grounded on 11 May 2009 on a reef under the cliffs near the southern tip of Bjørnøya, in a protected seabird area. All 12 crew were rescued. The vessel was working alongside Russian fishing ships in the Barents Sea.

## Wreck History and Source Conflicts

- **Cause**: Spitsbergen-Svalbard.com (citing the Svalbard Governor) reports that the captain and first officer had alcohol in their blood, that the first officer was on watch and probably asleep, and that the ship entered a protected zone closed to vessels over 40 feet from 15 May to 31 August. In June 2009 the North-Norwegian district court sentenced the captain to 15 days and the first officer to 40 days; the article describes these as accusations and the sentences, and both had already served 15 days under arrest. gCaptain (15 May 2009) said the cause was still under investigation and reported charges of drinking on duty, plus sleeping on duty for the mate. The Barents Observer reported the ship held a steady course toward the shore.
- **Tonnage**: Sources conflict: gCaptain gives 1,287 dwt, Wikipedia gives 1,372 DWT (summer deadweight). Wikipedia alone gives 67 m length, 12 m beam and 1,130 GT, and gives a launch date of 4 September 1978 as Nordlandia (renamed Zapolyarye 2003, Petrozavodsk 2004). A second independent source for build date, name history and dimensions was not found, so they are not treated as confirmed.
- **Fuel**: Wikipedia reports about 50 tonnes of diesel, spilled over roughly 3 km2 and largely evaporated; Spitsbergen-Svalbard.com reported about 53 tonnes on board and leaking; gCaptain reported only a small sheen that dissipated; the Barents Observer (June 2010) said the ship had been emptied of oil and fuel in 2009, while Wikipedia says about 690 litres of lubrication oil and some hydraulic oil remained. Sources do not agree on what remained aboard.
- **Condition**: The wreck was considered too dangerous to remove because of rockfall from the cliffs. The Barents Observer (2010) reports it had broken in two, with the front section tipped over; Wikipedia says it had split in two by 2011.

## Diving

No source found documents dives on this wreck, a wreck depth, or visibility. The OSM data indicates the wreck is visible at both high and low tide, so the 25 m figure below is unverified. Bear Island is remote, the wreck is deteriorating below rockfall-prone cliffs, and vessels over 40 feet are restricted in the surrounding protected waters at certain times of year. Any attempt would require full expedition planning, proper training and equipment; wreck penetration is not recommended.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Bear Island (Bjørnøya), Svalbard, Norway (Arctic)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
