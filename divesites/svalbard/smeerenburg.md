## Smeerenburg

Smeerenburg is a 17th-century Dutch whaling station on Amsterdamøya, Svalbard, associated with reported but unlocated shipwrecks.

## Overview

Smeerenburg ("Blubber Town") was the centre of Dutch whaling in the first half of the 17th century. Wikipedia gives Dutch occupation from 1614, a founding in 1619, use through 1657 and abandonment around 1660; the Norwegian Polar Institute says only that it began before 1620 and declined in the second half of the century. Source figures for the station differ: Wikipedia gives about 16-17 buildings in the 1630s, the Polar Institute about 19 at the peak; both give roughly 200 workers. The Polar Institute reports 101 graves of whalers from the 17th and 18th centuries (single source).

Two articles (Life in Norway and Norwegian SciTech News) report that in 1693 two French frigates fought 40 Dutch whalers in Sorgfjorden, two ships sank there, and seventeen captured Dutch ships were taken back to Smeerenburg fjord and sunk. Both quote marine archaeologist Øyvind Ødegård (NTNU) and appear to draw on the same account, so they are not fully independent. Other accounts found disagree on the details (two, three or four French frigates; 13 ships captured; Sorgfjorden vs Smeerenburgfjorden), so the count of seventeen wrecks should be treated as unconfirmed. As of the 2018 reporting the wrecks had not been located. Survey work uses sonar, hyperspectral imaging and ROV-mounted cameras rather than divers, since the articles note divers can work only to about 30 m and most wrecks lie deeper. No vessel names, dimensions or depths are given in any source found, and none documents a diveable wreck here. The 20 m depth and wreck classification below are unverified. The Arctic fauna of the region applies in general, but no site-specific marine-life reports were found for this site, so no species are named.
