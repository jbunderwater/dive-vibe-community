## Smeerenburg

Smeerenburg is a historic Arctic dive site at the ruins of a 17th-century Dutch whaling station on Amsterdamøya, Svalbard, whose surrounding fjord is popularly — but not consistently — associated with Dutch whaling ships said to have been lost in a 1693 naval clash with French forces.

## Overview

Smeerenburg ("Blubber Town") was the principal Dutch whaling station in Svalbard, active from around 1619/1620 until its abandonment near 1660 on Amsterdamøya, in the far northwest of Spitsbergen. At its peak in the 1630s the station held roughly 200 workers each summer season; archaeological excavations carried out in 1979–1981 confirmed this scale and uncovered the remains of blubber ovens and building foundations that can still be seen on site today.

Popular accounts — repeated across several tourism and history blogs — claim that in 1693, during the Nine Years' War, French frigates sent by Louis XIV attacked a fleet of Dutch whaling vessels, burning two on the spot and towing a further seventeen ships back to Smeerenburg fjord to be sunk there. **This specific version could not be corroborated for this re-audit.** Neither the Norwegian Polar Institute's own cruise handbook entries for Smeerenburg or for Sorgfjorden, nor the English Wikipedia article on Smeerenburg, mention any ships being sunk in Smeerenburg fjord. The more carefully sourced accounts of the actual 1693 engagement (Norwegian Polar Institute cruise handbook; PolarQuest) place the battle in Sorgfjorden, a separate fjord on Svalbard's northeast coast, and describe two to four French frigates intercepting roughly 40 Dutch whalers, with two Dutch vessels burned on site and 11–13 captured and sailed away as prizes — not sunk, and not at Smeerenburg. Louis XIV's role in dispatching the French warships to damage Dutch whaling is corroborated across sources, but the number of French ships (accounts vary from two to four) and the fate of the Dutch vessels are inconsistent between sources.

Notably, one of the two source chains that repeats the "seventeen ships sunk in Smeerenburg fjord" story is a 2018 science-journalism piece about underwater survey technology, which states plainly that the alleged wrecks had **not** been located or dived at the time of writing — a Norwegian researcher was still searching for them. No dive operator, forum, or expedition-cruise source reviewed for this audit confirms that divers have ever located or explored physical 17th-century shipwreck remains at Smeerenburg. Given this, the "seventeen ships / 1693 / French frigates / Louis XIV" narrative is flagged here as an unconfirmed, contested claim rather than presented as fact.

What is well established is that Smeerenburg is a regular shore-landing stop on Arctic expedition cruises, where visitors walk among the whaling station ruins and often see walrus, seals, and seabirds. Diving is offered as an optional activity on some of these itineraries, under the same general conditions as the rest of Svalbard — cold water, drysuit-only, minimum experience requirements — rather than as a dedicated, confirmed wreck dive. Divers should treat this as a historically evocative site rather than a verified, explorable shipwreck.
