## Hoi Siong No. 6 Wreck

Hoi Siong No. 6 Wreck is a wreck dive site in Mauritius, Africa.

## Overview

The Hoi Siong No. 6 is a former fishing vessel deliberately sunk in 2003 (africatouroperators.org) as an artificial reef off the southwest coast of Mauritius near Le Morne. Dive-industry sources describe it as a collaborative artificial-reef project, most commonly naming the Mauritius Marine Conservation Society and the Mauritius Scuba Diving Association, though this review could not independently confirm those organisation names from a directly-accessible source (see note below). The wreck sits at around 16–29 metres depth (wannadive.net) and has accumulated coral growth over the years, providing habitat for barracuda and tuna (tropicalwarehouse.co.uk, holidify.com) and lionfish and butterflyfish (holidify.com). The site is located in relatively sheltered waters near Île aux Bénitiers.

**Note on vessel details (wreck-history cross-check):** No Wikipedia article exists for this vessel, and its length could not be resolved from any directly-accessible source. This page previously cited "approximately 27 metres" without a working citation; other blog and dive-database pages indexed in search results (but returning 403/503 errors on direct access during this review) repeat a figure of approximately 45 metres. Both figures are noted here as an unresolved conflict rather than one being asserted as fact. The organisations most commonly credited with the sinking -- the Mauritius Marine Conservation Society, the Mauritius Scuba Diving Association, and sometimes the Mauritius Oceanographic Institute -- appear consistently in indexed search results but could not be confirmed via any page this review was able to load directly; treat as commonly cited but unverified. No tonnage or vessel origin information is available from any source.

**Marine life correction:** A previous version of this page listed "surgeon fish" and "schools of goatfish." Neither is supported by any source found in this review and both have been removed; barracuda, tuna, lionfish and butterflyfish remain, each confirmed by at least one source.

**Note on interior:** One source (mauritiusguidedtours.com) states the wheelhouse is intact and accessible, but this could not be verified against local operator sources. Wreck penetration requires appropriate training and equipment.
