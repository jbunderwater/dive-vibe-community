## Stella Maru Wreck

Stella Maru Wreck is a historic wreck dive in Mauritius, Africa.

## Overview

The Stella Maru is a Japanese fishing trawler, deliberately sunk in 1987 (prodivemauritius.com specifies December) by the Mauritius Conservation Society to create an artificial reef off the Trou aux Biches coast in the north of the island -- this vessel type, date and cause are corroborated by five independent sources (prodivemauritius.com, dansa.org, thediveglobe.com, divespiritmauritius.com, mauritiusattractions.com). She was originally sunk on her side; multiple dive-industry accounts report that a major cyclone in 2002 lifted and reset her upright onto the sandy bottom, and some (not independently verified via direct source access) identify it as Cyclone Dina in January 2002, a cyclone that is independently documented to have struck the Mauritius/Réunion area at that time. She now sits upright at 18–27 metres depth across the various source ranges, remarkably intact. The wreck is colonised with soft corals (mauritiusattractions.com) and is regularly reported to host giant moray eels -- sometimes two at once (divespiritmauritius.com) -- scorpionfish (dansa.org, divespiritmauritius.com as "flying scorpionfish"), triggerfish (divespiritmauritius.com), stonefish and large groupers (thediveglobe.com). Dansa.org's 2022 survey additionally recorded a hairy frogfish and a small gurnard. The wreck's distinctive profile makes it a popular subject for underwater photography.

**Note on vessel length:** Sources report the length as either 42 metres (mauritiusattractions.com) or 44 metres (thediveglobe.com); this could not be resolved to a single figure. Tonnage has not been confirmed in any available source and is not stated here.

**Correction:** A previous version of this page listed "porcupinefish" and described "a resident giant moray eel... habituated to divers." Neither claim is supported by any source found in this review -- porcupinefish has been removed, and the moray eel description has been corrected to reflect that operators report giant morays (sometimes multiple) without describing habituation to divers.
