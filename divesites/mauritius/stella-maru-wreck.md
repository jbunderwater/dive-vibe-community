## Stella Maru Wreck

Stella Maru Wreck is a historic wreck dive in Mauritius, Africa.

## Overview

The Stella Maru is a Japanese fishing trawler deliberately sunk in 1987 by the Mauritius Conservation Society to create an artificial reef off the Trou aux Biches coast in the north of the island. She was originally sunk on her side, but Cyclone Dina, which struck Mauritius in January 2002, generated swell strong enough at depth to lift and reset the hull upright on the sandy bottom, where she now sits largely intact. The wreck is colonised with soft corals and algae and is home to giant moray eels, scorpionfish, and triggerfish, with reef fish including red-toothed triggerfish and trevallies established around the hull. A resident giant moray eel is reported by multiple operators as habituated to divers. The wreck's distinctive profile makes it a popular subject for underwater photography.

**Note on vessel length:** Sources disagree substantially — mauritiusattractions.com and Pro Dive Mauritius cite 42 metres, thediveglobe.com cites 44 metres, and PADI's dive blog cites 52 metres. No single figure could be confirmed as authoritative, so a range of 42–52 metres is reported here rather than a single number. Tonnage and original build date have not been confirmed in any available source and are not stated.

**Note on depth:** Sources vary between an average of 18m/maximum 25m (thediveglobe.com) and an average of 23m/maximum 27m (mauritiusattractions.com). The 26m maximum recorded in this database falls within that reported range.
