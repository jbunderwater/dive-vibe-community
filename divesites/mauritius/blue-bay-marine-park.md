## Blue Bay Marine Park

Blue Bay Marine Park is a reef dive site in Mauritius, Africa.

## Overview

Blue Bay Marine Park is a 353-hectare protected lagoon on the southeast coast of Mauritius (Wikipedia), declared a national park in October 1997 and granted Marine Park status in June 2000 (Wikipedia; the park's 1997 Marine Park listing is separately corroborated by snorkeling-report.com). On 31 January 2008 it was designated a Wetland of International Importance under the Ramsar Convention (Wikipedia). Depths vary by zone: the shore-side lagoon is around 2 metres, while the coral garden area runs 5–6 metres (snorkeling-report.com), consistent with the 1–5m range used here. The park's signature feature is a brain coral reported by two independent sources (advantiko.com, wiotto.com) as roughly 1,000 years old and about 5 metres in diameter -- the largest in the Indian Ocean by these accounts. Coral species counts vary between sources: advantiko.com cites about 38 species, wiotto.com cites "more than 50" -- both figures are noted here rather than picking one. Fish species counts also vary slightly, from about 70 (advantiko.com) to 72 (wiotto.com). Reported fish life includes angelfish, clownfish, parrotfish, surgeonfish, damselfish, wrasse, triggerfish, several butterflyfish varieties, and Moorish idols, plus hawksbill and green sea turtles (snorkeling-report.com). The most pristine sections can only be accessed by guided boat tour, and strict no-touch rules protect the coral formations.

**Note on sources:** The Ramsar Sites Information Service page for this site (rsis.ramsar.org/ris/1744), cited in a previous version of this page, was unreachable (HTTP 502) throughout this review and has been replaced with two other sources that independently report the same brain-coral figures.
