## Tako Flats

Tako Flats is a shallow sand channel with scattered coral heads near the western tip of Molokini Crater, named for the Japanese/Hawaiian word for octopus (tako) and known as the crater's most reliable spot for octopus sightings.

## Overview

Tako Flats occupies a sand channel bordered by coral heads inside Molokini Crater, near the western tip close to Reef's End. The site is characterised by relatively calm water conditions — sources consistently describe it as one of the most sheltered spots within the crater — and a sandy bottom interspersed with coral rubble and discrete coral heads that give octopus plenty of places to den. Manta rays visit Tako Flats to feed on fish larvae. The site's calm conditions make it approachable for intermediate divers and a good complement to deeper crater dives. Sources disagree on maximum depth: one operator gives a shallow range of only a few feet to roughly 15 feet (4.5m) for the sand-channel portion, while another cites an 18-metre outer limit for the site as a whole; the 15m figure used here reflects the middle of that reported range rather than a single confirmed number.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Molokini Crater, Maui, Hawaii (western inner crater)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters

## Marine Life

Multiple independent sources confirm octopus, manta rays (feeding on fish larvae), moray eels, nudibranchs, and barracuda specifically at Tako Flats. Sources do not, however, break the octopus sightings down by species for this site specifically — the claim that day octopus, sand octopus, and white-striped octopus are all individually confirmed here comes from a general Molokini-wide octopus page, not from Tako Flats reporting. Molokini's regional octopus fauna does include those three species, and Tako Flats is where operators say divers are most likely to encounter them, but no site-specific report ties an individual sighting to a particular species at this location.

## Tips

- Go slow and scan the sand and rubble carefully — octopus at Molokini are masters of camouflage and are easily missed at normal swimming pace.
- Look for a slight mound of sand or a telltale pile of shells (a midden) at burrow entrances as a sign of an occupied den.
- Manta ray visits are most frequent during fish spawning periods; ask your operator about current activity before the dive.
