## Elba

The largest island of the Tuscan Archipelago, about 10 km off the Tuscan coast, with granite and iron-rich rocky coastline, offshore shoals (secche) capped with red gorgonians, the shallow Elviscot wreck at Pomonte and two aircraft wrecks.

## Description

Elba is Italy's third-largest island (223.5 km²), roughly 27 km from east to west and 18 km from north to south, separated from the mainland at Piombino by a channel about 10 km wide. Its geology is unusually varied: the western third is the granite massif of Monte Capanne (1,019 m), while the east is made up of iron-bearing rocks that were mined for centuries, notably at Monte Calamita near Capoliveri. Underwater this translates into granite boulder slopes and walls in the west, dark mineral sand and rock in the south-east, and a coastline indented by gulfs and headlands that gives sheltered diving in most wind directions.

Most dives are boat dives on rocky reefs, walls and secche — submerged rocks rising from 30–50 m to within a few metres of the surface — where red gorgonians (Paramuricea clavata), yellow cluster anemones (Parazoanthus axinellae) and sponges cover the deeper rock. Posidonia seagrass meadows fringe many sites. The dive sites listed here range from the 12 m Elviscot wreck and shallow islet dives at the Gemini to deep secche such as Secca del Semaforo and Secca di Capo Poro, whose tops lie at 30–40 m.

### Diving Opportunities

- **Boat Diving**: The large majority of Elba's sites, including the secche and headlands around Capoliveri (Picchi di Pablo, Capo Calvo, Scoglio del Remaiolo), the Gulf of Marina di Campo (Secca di Capo Fonza, Coralline, Scoglio della Triglia), the north coast (Scoglietto di Portoferraio, Scoglio della Nave, Punta della Madonna) and the Piombino channel (Secca del Frate, Isola di Cerboli)
- **Shore Diving**: Limited; the Elviscot wreck at Pomonte and the Cote Piane reef at Sant'Andrea can be dived from shore, as can Colle d'Orano from the Le Buche cove
- **Wreck Diving**: The Elviscot, a 62 m cargo ship that ran aground at Pomonte in January 1972 (12 m, suitable for beginners); the remains of a Junkers Ju 52 transport aircraft off Portoferraio (35–37 m, advanced); and a Britten-Norman Islander light aircraft off Capoliveri (12–16 m)
- **Pinnacle Diving**: Numerous secche from shallow (Secca di Santa Lucia, top at 6–8 m) to deep (Secca del Semaforo, top at 34–40 m), often with gorgonians and schooling fish
- **Current Diving**: The shoals in the Piombino channel (Secca del Frate, Isola di Cerboli) are regularly swept by strong currents and are best left to experienced divers

### Accessibility

- **Getting There**: Ferries sail from Piombino to Portoferraio (Moby, Toremar and Blu Navy, about 1 hour), Cavo (Moby and Toremar, about 30 minutes) and Rio Marina (Toremar, about 45 minutes). By train, travel to Piombino Marittima, the small station in front of the ferry terminal (some trains terminate at Campiglia Marittima, with a bus connection to the port). Elba's small airport at La Pila, about 2 km from Marina di Campo, has seasonal flights from Pisa, Florence, Milan and Rome
- **Dive Operators**: Dive centres are spread around the island, including at Capoliveri/Morcone (Aquanautic Elba), Marina di Campo (Marina di Campo Diving), Portoferraio, Biodola and Procchio (Diving in Elba), Sant'Andrea (Il Careno) and Cavo (Bolle d'Azoto). Most dives are organised as boat trips from these bases
- **Transportation**: A car or scooter is useful for reaching the dive centres and the few shore-dive sites

### Marine Life & Environment

- **Water Conditions**: Operators describe excellent visibility and, at most sites, easy navigation with currents only occasionally; exceptions are the channel sites off the north-east coast. One regional guide gives water temperatures of about 25 °C in summer and 17 °C in winter
- **Marine Biodiversity**: Operators report schools of small fish, barracuda, moray and conger eels, groupers and octopus as typical, with occasional sunfish and dolphins. Red gorgonian forests and yellow cluster anemones are characteristic of the deeper walls and secche; individual site pages list the species actually reported at each site
- **Conservation**: The Tuscan Archipelago National Park covers about 57% of Elba's land area, including islets such as the Gemini, Isola Corbella, Scoglio della Triglia, Palmaiola and Cerboli, but the park states that Elba's waters are not inside its perimeter. Around the Scoglietto di Portoferraio, a ministerial decree of 10 August 1971 created a biological protection zone between Punta Falcone and Capo Bianco, managed by the Comune di Portoferraio, in which fishing is prohibited

## Additional Information

- **Best Time to Visit**: May to October; many dive centres close over the winter
- **Currency**: Euro (EUR)
- **Language**: Italian; several dive centres also operate in German and English
- **Safety**: Many sites have deep sections beyond 30 m, and several secche have their tops at 30–40 m, so dives there are deep from the start; plan depth and gas accordingly. Channel sites can have strong currents and ferry traffic overhead — use a surface marker. Any wreck or cavern penetration requires appropriate training and equipment
