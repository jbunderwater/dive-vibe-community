## Scoglio della Nave

Wall dive around a large ship-shaped rock a few meters off Capo Enfola, with a shallow section for all levels and a deeper outer ridge for advanced divers.

## Overview

The Scoglio della Nave is a large rock a few meters off Capo Enfola, named because, seen from a certain angle, it resembles a ship. Operators describe two dives. The first, on the outer side of the rock, follows a wall that Elba CED and Diving in Elba describe as colorful and full of life from about 10 m, with yellow gorgonians, groupers and octopus; Elba CED gives its maximum as 25 m and Blu Elba as 27 m, with the boat anchored west of the rock on 18-20 m and a return through the 12 m channel between the rock and the shore rocks. The second, deeper route runs out to open water, where a wall drops almost vertically from 20 m to 40 m (Diving in Elba, DuecentoBar) or, along a ledge to the north, to 45 m (Elba CED). Blu Elba describes this outer ridge as an advanced-only dive of 25-40 m, with pinnacles, fissures and small yellow gorgonians.

Named for this site are groupers, octopus, moray and conger eels (some large), yellow gorgonians, spirographs, hermit crabs, nudibranchs, seahorses and pipefish (the last two "sometimes", near the surface), lobsters, large scorpionfish, "mustelle" (as named by the sources), sea bream and a few barracuda. A St. Peter's fish is reported in spring (Elba CED, DuecentoBar). Diving in Elba rates the site as easy with light to medium current, with groups split by level.

Elba CED and DuecentoBar both mention the remains of a World War II mine-carrying cart on the sand slightly offshore. The two pages use near-identical wording, so this is not treated as independently confirmed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Elba, Europe (off Capo Enfola, Portoferraio)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
