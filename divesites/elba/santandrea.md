## Sant'Andrea

Shore dive from Sant'Andrea beach on Elba's north-west coast, the "Le Cote Piane" house reef over boulder slopes running to 40 meters.

## Overview

The site starts from Sant'Andrea beach, where the Il Careno Diving Center runs its house reef "Le Cote Piane", which it describes as one of the most beautiful shore dives on the island. The dive centre's base is about 20 meters from the beach and embarkation point. According to Il Careno, the seabed slopes gently over the first 5 meters, and the dive follows boulder slopes ("franate") with the rocks on the left. At the tip of the first slope there are two routes. The simpler one, described as suitable for Open Water divers, keeps the rocky slope on the left, where a small family of groupers is often found. Heading west, the second slope ends beyond 30 m; the average depth is around 18 m, and a third slope reaches 30 m for advanced divers, ending at a large wall of Parazoanthus axinellae. The other route, for advanced divers, follows a long pipe to a first pinnacle with Parazoanthus, spirographs and, late in summer, scorpionfish, then heads north into open water down to 40 m. Schools of amberjack, dolphinfish and dentex are easy to spot there, and eagle rays are sometimes seen. A colony of seahorses is reported at about 5 m at the tip of the first slope.

Il Careno lists the site as 0-40 m, current "sometimes", suitable for all levels. Aquanautic Elba's dive map lists Sant'Andrea with a steep wall as its feature (the popup text is cut off), 0-40 m, easy navigation and possible current.

Il Careno warns divers to watch for remains of Roman-period amphorae hidden in the Posidonia. A hotel page mentions two Roman merchant-ship wrecks near Capo Sant'Andrea, one close to shore and one about 200 m out at about 50 m depth. No dates or ship details were found, so nothing more is stated here.

Other dives in the Sant'Andrea area, such as the Secca del Careno (listed at 30-60 m by Il Careno and 12-45 m by Diving in Elba, current sometimes strong) and the Formiche della Zanca (0-50 m, very difficult), are separate sites and are not described here.
