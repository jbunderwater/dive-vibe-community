## Isola di Cerboli

Dive around the small quarry-scarred limestone islet of Cerboli in the Piombino channel, with near-vertical walls, a shallow siphon and a slope reaching 45 m.

## Overview

Cerboli lies in the middle of the channel between Piombino and Elba, east of Elba's north-eastern tip (about 4 miles from Elba according to Infoelba), and belongs to the municipality of Rio. Elba CED says it is almost closer to the mainland than to Elba. Its profile was heavily altered by a limestone quarry opened in 1927 to supply the ILVA steelworks in Piombino (Wikipedia). Boats moor in the bay south of the islet on a 10 m bottom (Elba CED) or anchor in a cove at about 13 m on the south coast (Infoelba). From there the walls, almost vertical at first, are covered in parazoanthus and multicoloured sponges, and a gentle slope with scattered boulders descends to 45 m (Infoelba), where the first red gorgonians appear (Elba CED). Yellow gorgonians occur in the shallows and red ones deeper (Bolle d'Azoto). Elba CED also reports the iron skeletons of two small transport-ship wrecks lying close together on sand at 25 m in front of the old little harbour; no history for them was found.

The islet's best-known feature is the siphon or tunnel. Elba CED says a siphon a few metres deep, just behind the point, allows divers to enter and exit slightly deeper; Infoelba places the tunnel at about 15-16 m, with shingle and sand on the floor, light filtering in from another opening and the tunnel rising toward the surface. This is an overhead environment and requires suitable training, equipment and local guidance. Currents in the channel are always present and, according to Bolle d'Azoto, it can sometimes be hard to swim back; Infoelba says the site suits divers of a certain level. Bolle d'Azoto adds that the site is almost always practicable except in sirocco wind, and lists a minimum Open Water certification.

Reported marine life includes scorpionfish, lobsters, moray eels and octopus in the cracks (Infoelba, Bolle d'Azoto), crayfish (Infoelba), and dentex and other pelagic fish in the open water on the ascent (Bolle d'Azoto). The islet's land is protected by the Tuscan Archipelago National Park and is a Natura 2000 site together with Palmaiola; the park authority states that the waters of Elba are not inside the Park perimeter. No regulation specific to the sea around Cerboli was found in the sources read.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Elba, Europe (islet of Cerboli, Piombino channel, off Rio Marina and Cavo)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 45 meters
