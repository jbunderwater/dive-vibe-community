## Relitto di Pomonte

Relitto di Pomonte is the wreck of the cargo ship Elviscot, lying on sand at about 12 m beside the Ogliera rock, off Pomonte beach on Elba's south-west coast.

## Overview

The Elviscot was an Italian cargo ship of about 499 GRT and about 62 m long, built in 1960 in the Netherlands (Waterhuizen). It ran onto the Ogliera rock off Pomonte on 10 January 1972, and the crew was unharmed. Sources agree on the name, the date and the place but differ on details: the departure port was Taranto according to Dive Center Blu, Infoelba and Tirreno Elba News, and Naples according to Wikipedia, Marina di Campo Diving and others, with Marseille as destination. Most pages blame bad weather and poor visibility or a storm; Infoelba and Tirreno Elba News say the vessel was taking on water and the master steered for shore, hitting the rock. The bow stayed aground and the hull was partly recovered, and Tirreno Elba News reports it sank completely on 21 January after storm surges. Several pages reuse the same underlying text.

The wreck lies on a sandy bottom on the east side of the Ogliera rock (south side per Fetovaia.it), with a maximum depth of about 12 m; the top is reported at 2-3 m (Wikipedia) and the stern deck at 8 m (Tirreno Elba News). Distances from the beach range from a few tens of metres to nearly 200 m. It is one of few Elba dives that can start from the beach, and Taucher.Net and Fetovaia.it say it can also be reached by boat. Current is described as almost absent (Marina di Campo Diving) or low (Fetovaia.it). The hull lies on its side (starboard per Wikipedia, port per Tirreno Elba News), covered with algae, sponges and spirographs, and is in precarious condition. Named residents include conger eels, moray eels, octopus, bream, occhiate, brown meagre and groupers.

Wikipedia and Marina di Campo Diving say the bridge can be visited through a large opening in the deck, and that passing through the funnel to the engine room is recommended only for the most experienced; Fetovaia.it says beginners should stay out of the wreck and advanced divers may enter with caution; Aquanautic Elba likewise reserves the passage through the funnel into the engine room for experienced divers. Any penetration of a wreck requires proper training and equipment and is not safe for untrained divers. The Ogliera rock belongs to the Parco Nazionale Arcipelago Toscano's land territory, but the Park states that Elba's waters are not included in its perimeter (islepark.it).

## Site Information

- **Location**: Elba, Europe (Pomonte, Marciana, off the Ogliera rock)
- **Entry Type**: Shore or boat entry
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters
