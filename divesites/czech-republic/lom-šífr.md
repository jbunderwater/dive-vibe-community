## Lom Šífr

A long, narrow slate quarry near Svobodné Heřmanice with depths to 36 meters, a flooded diving caisson, and dramatic wall profiles.

## Overview

Lom Šífr is a flooded slate quarry located near the small village of Svobodné Heřmanice, near Opava in the Moravskoslezský region. Slate extraction likely began around 1775, paused during World War I, resumed at reduced capacity afterward, stopped again during World War II, then resumed after 1945 before gradually winding down as flooding increased. The quarry is approximately 500 meters long and 30-70 meters wide, reaching depths of up to 36 meters, and sits at an altitude of 450 meters above sea level. The narrow, elongated shape of the quarry creates dramatic wall diving along the slate cliffs.

The quarry has a notable place in Czechoslovak diving history: between 21 and 26 (sources disagree on the month — one source says September 1967, cs.wikipedia.org says November 1967) 1967, aquanauts Vilém Kocián and Vladimír Geist spent 100 hours at a depth of 25 meters inside a submersible habitat called "Permon," built by the Vítkovice steelworks — at the time the largest Czechoslovak experiment in prolonged human underwater habitation, and reportedly only the third of its kind in the world. The Permon caisson remains anchored in the quarry today, now sitting at about 17 meters depth, as a diveable memorial to the experiment.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Svobodné Heřmanice, near Opava, Moravskoslezský kraj
- **Entry Type**: Shore entry
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 36 meters
- **Current**: None
- **Best Time**: May to October

## Underwater Features

The main attraction is the historic "Permon" caisson at roughly 17 meters depth (see history above), along with a well structure and a telephone booth — quirky artifacts placed for diver interest. The slate walls create clean, angular underwater cliff faces that extend the full depth of the quarry. The narrow profile means wall diving is available from virtually any entry point.

## Marine Life

Limited freshwater life typical of high-altitude quarries. Some small fish and aquatic invertebrates.

## Dive Profile

The narrow quarry allows for wall diving along the entire length. Descend along the slate walls, which drop steeply to the 36-meter maximum. The caisson and well provide intermediate-depth points of interest. Due to the depth and cold water at altitude, this site requires advanced-level skills and proper gas management.

## Safety Considerations

The 36-meter depth combined with the 450-meter altitude requires careful dive planning. Altitude diving adjustments to decompression tables or computers are necessary. Cold water temperatures demand a dry suit and adequate thermal protection. The narrow quarry can feel confined at depth.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Adjust your dive computer for altitude diving — the quarry sits at 450 meters elevation
- The Permon caisson is the highlight — plan your dive to include adequate bottom time at its ~17-meter depth
- Bring lights for exploring the wall features and submerged structures
