## Orlík - Kostel Technice

The submerged ruins of a 14th-century Gothic church lying 45-60 meters below the surface of Orlík Reservoir — one of the Czech Republic's most iconic dives.

## Overview

When the Orlík Dam on the Vltava River was completed and the reservoir began filling in 1961, the village of Těchnice and its Church of St. Stephen (first documented in 1352, with major renovations around 1750) were submerged. The reservoir became the Czech Republic's largest-volume reservoir (716 million m³). The church was deconsecrated and its roof, ceiling, and timber framing removed before flooding — the vault above the presbytery survived intact. Divers first relocated the ruins in 2000 using old cadastral maps and geodetic instruments. Note: the village name is "Těchnice" (the site is sometimes anglicised as "Technice").

## Site Information

- **Location**: Orlík Reservoir, South/Central Bohemia (Středočeský / Jihočeský kraj)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 60 meters
- **Reservoir Maximum Depth**: 74 meters
- **Current**: None to light
- **Best Time**: Late summer/autumn when reservoir levels are lower

## Underwater Features

The church ruins retain substantial architectural detail despite decades of submersion. The Gothic gable, ribbed vaulting, and window openings are identifiable, and the far end contains intact vaulted chambers above the former altar. Cast-iron bannisters mark the former entrance. The surrounding area includes foundations and remnants of the flooded village of Těchnice. Depth reports vary by source and with reservoir water level: one detailed dive-site listing (stranypotapecske.cz) puts the roof at roughly 30 meters and the base around 45 meters, with typical dives to about 55-58 meters, while other sources describe the church sitting between 40-60 meters. Treat 45-60 meters as an approximate range rather than a fixed number.

## Marine Life

Freshwater reservoir species. Visibility and biological activity vary significantly with season and depth.

## Dive Profile

This is a deep dive requiring careful planning. Access is by boat only. The church depth varies with reservoir water level but typically sits between 45-60 meters. Decompression diving is likely required. Cold water at depth demands proper thermal protection and gas management.

## Safety Considerations

This is an advanced to expert dive due to depth, cold water, and limited visibility at depth (one source reports average visibility of only around 2 meters, though this varies with conditions). This is described as suitable only for technical divers, with trimix recommended for the bottom depth. The far end of the church contains intact overhead vaulted chambers with no direct ascent to open water — a serious overhead-environment hazard requiring a guideline. Dive with a qualified guide who knows the current depth and position of the ruins. Reservoir water levels change seasonally, affecting the dive profile.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Contact local dive operators for current water levels and church depth
- This dive may require technical certification depending on current depth
- Bring powerful lights — visibility at 45-60 meters in a reservoir can be very limited
- The Orlík area also offers shallower dive sites along the reservoir banks
