## Danglebens Pinnacles

Danglebens Pinnacles is a signature dive of the Soufriere-Scotts Head Marine Reserve, where five volcanic spires rise from depth along the northern rim of the submerged Soufriere crater and are carpeted in every imaginable form of sponge and coral.

## Overview

Located close to shore north of Scotts Head and accessed by boat, the five pinnacles range from about 40 feet (12 metres) at their shallowest to 80–98 feet (24–30 metres) at depth — sources vary slightly on the exact maximum — with table corals, brain corals, and barrel sponges covering the structures from base to tip. The site sits on the ancient crater rim of the Soufriere volcano, and the dramatic underwater topography reflects this geological origin. Mild currents typically run through the site, which can channel schooling fish, particularly during winter months when large aggregations of hunting fish are common. The site is generally manageable for intermediate divers but the current and depth put it above beginner level.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Dominica, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount

## Marine Life

Large moray eels shelter in the crevices of the pinnacles, and the Divers Alert Network specifically documents yellow frogfish here (earlier color variants — pink and brown — were unsupported padding and have been removed). Schooling bar jacks, horse-eye jacks, crevalle jacks, and barracuda hunt baitfish around the structures, particularly in winter. Southern stingrays rest on the sandy bottom between the spires, and giant basket starfish, porcupinefish, and angelfish are regularly observed. The shallower reaches have cleaning stations with Pederson shrimp. Only one source (Scuba Travel) reports a hammerhead shark sighting at this site, and it is explicitly noted as an anecdotal, one-off report rather than a typical encounter; Scuba Diver Life's dedicated Dominica article states directly that no sharks are documented at Dangleben's Pinnacles or any other named site it covers, so reef sharks are not listed here as a confirmed sighting.
