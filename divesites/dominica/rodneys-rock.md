## Rodney's Rock

Rodney's Rock is Dominica's most celebrated critter dive — a shallow rocky outcrop off the central west coast near Jimmit where the walls and overhangs shelter an extraordinary density of macro species, including the island's documented record of 11 seahorses sighted in a single dive.

## Overview

Located north of Jimmit on the central west coast, Rodney's Rock is a rocky formation with a maximum depth of 50 feet (15 metres), making it a relaxed and accessible dive suitable for all levels. The vertical faces and overhangs are carpeted with barrel sponges, gorgonians, and hydroids that shelter an exceptional variety of small creatures. Mini-caves and overhangs provide habitat for spiny and slipper lobsters, crabs, and moray eels. Sand and seagrass patches surrounding the main formation shelter additional critters including spotted snake eels. The site is also an excellent night dive when squid, lobsters, and crabs are out in full force.

## Marine Life

The seahorse record of 11 sighted in a single dive was originally documented on Discover Dominica's site page; that page now returns a 404 and the claim is retained here on the strength of independent corroboration from A Virtual Dominica, but flagged as needing a live replacement source. EWDR separately confirms seahorses, frogfish, lobsters, snake eels, sea cucumbers, and stingrays at this site. Other confirmed critters include longlure frogfish, shortnose batfish, bumblebee shrimp, leaf fish, ribbon eels, and spotted snake eels in sandy patches. Spiny and slipper lobsters inhabit the mini-caves. Night dives reliably produce squid, lobsters, and crabs (A Virtual Dominica: "squid, lobsters and crabs are out in full view"). The site is considered one of Dominica's premier macro and photography dives. Spotted eagle rays and barracuda, listed in a prior version as "occasionally reported," could not be traced to a source for this specific site during this re-audit and have been removed pending a source.
