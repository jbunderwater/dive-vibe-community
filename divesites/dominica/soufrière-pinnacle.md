## Soufrière Pinnacle

Soufrière Pinnacle is a cluster of three volcanic pinnacles rising from a 25-foot shelf in the sheltered bay directly in front of Soufrière village, known locally as "the Fish Bowl" for its dense aggregations of reef fish.

## Overview

Approached from the bay by boat — one dive report places the site roughly 300 feet offshore from the church in Soufrière village — the swim to the site passes over submerged hot springs that bubble through the volcanic sand, a reminder that Soufrière Bay occupies the flooded caldera of an ancient volcano. The three main pinnacles are sponge- and crinoid-encrusted, rising to within a few metres of the surface. Sources disagree on the depth of the surrounding seafloor: the Divers Alert Network describes the pinnacles rising from a 40-foot (12-metre) seafloor, while other operator listings place the shelf closer to 25 feet (8 metres); this description reflects that range rather than picking one figure. The gentle current in the sheltered bay and the minimal swell make this one of the easiest dives in the southern marine reserve, and it is frequently used as a second or warm-up dive alongside L'Abym or Danglebens Pinnacles.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Dominica, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters

## Marine Life

The sponge- and crinoid-covered pinnacles attract dense populations of reef fish including butterflyfish, moray eels, goatfish, and sergeant majors. Seahorses and frogfish are regularly found in the crevices, and lobsters inhabit deeper recesses. A first-hand dive report from ETB Travel Photography — one of the sources for this description — documents this specific dive in detail but does not report turtles here; that same source does document turtles at Champagne Reef and Danglebens, so a prior claim of "frequent" turtle sightings at Soufrière Pinnacle has been removed as unsupported. Squid are noted in the water column above the site.
