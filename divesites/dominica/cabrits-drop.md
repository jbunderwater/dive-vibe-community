## Cabrits Drop

Cabrits Drop is a steep volcanic wall off the Cabrits Peninsula in northern Dominica, within the Cabrits National Park Marine Reserve, where the reef drops sharply from shallow coral gardens to sandy bottoms at 155 feet and beyond.

## Overview

The Cabrits Marine Reserve encompasses several distinct sites grouped along the peninsula and Prince Rupert Bay — Cabrits Dive Center's own site list names them individually (Shark's Mouth, Volcano Site, Sunshine Reef, and others) rather than a single "Cabrits Drop"; this entry aggregates the deeper wall faces of that group, which other sources place at 155 feet (Cabrits South) and a third site further south dropping to over 185 feet. The reef is described by Cabrits Dive Center as exceptionally healthy, evidenced by the density of colourful sponges covering the wall from top to base. Large boulders along the shallower sections have created cave-like spaces and swim-throughs, and the headland provides good shelter from current on most days. The historic Cabrits Peninsula above water adds context — the peninsula hosts 18th-century British and French fort ruins. The site is a short boat ride from Portsmouth and Prince Rupert Bay.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Dominica, Caribbean
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

A Virtual Dominica and MaduroDive both describe this as a drift dive over barrel sponges with schools of creole fish (Paranthias furcifer) — a prior version of this description named "creole wrasse," a different species that was sourced from an unrelated southern-reserve site and has been corrected. Barrel sponges and encrusting corals provide habitat for moray eels and squirrelfish. Healthy hard and soft corals throughout the shallow sections support sergeant majors and reef fish populations. Barracuda and jacks are common in the open water. Hawksbill turtles and feather stars were listed in a prior version but could not be traced to a source confirming sightings specifically at this site (feather stars are documented at nearby Shark's Mouth, and turtles at a different site called Inbetween), so both have been removed here.
