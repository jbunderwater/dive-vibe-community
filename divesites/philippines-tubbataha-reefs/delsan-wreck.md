## Delsan Wreck

The Delsan Wreck, the only known shipwreck within Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, sits on the southeastern edge of the South Atoll and now serves as an artificial reef beside a dramatic reef crevasse known as "The Cut."

## Overview

Accounts of the Delsan's history conflict and cannot be resolved to a single confirmed narrative. One source describes it simply as a small freighter that ran aground on the reef flat, without giving a cause or date. Another - a liveaboard operator's dive log - describes it as an oil ship that suffered mechanical failure and was deliberately run onto the reef by its captain to save the vessel's oil, estimating the wreck at roughly 30 years old as of a 2023 report (which would place the incident in the 1990s). No source could independently confirm the vessel's name, length, or tonnage, and no Wikipedia article exists to cross-check either account, so neither version is presented here as settled fact. The wreck itself lies shallow on the reef flat, reported between about 12 and 18 meters, while the adjacent feature divers know as "The Cut" - a crevasse roughly 30 meters deep and 20 meters wide - is a separate, deeper highlight of the same dive and the likely source of some higher depth figures reported for this site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

The wreck and surrounding reef flat are reported to hold large schools of jacks, barracuda, fusiliers, snappers, and sweetlips, plus surgeonfish and other reef fish. At "The Cut," grey reef sharks and trevally are a consistent draw, hunting in the current, with whitetip sharks, hammerheads, and occasional tiger and reef manta ray sightings also reported at the site.

## Safety Considerations

Currents around "The Cut" can be strong, and depths there approach the edge of recreational limits; this is an Advanced-only dive, consistent with the destination-wide policy for remote Tubbataha sites.
