## Delsan Wreck

Delsan Wreck sits on Tubbataha's South Atoll, the broken remains of a small Filipino freighter that ran aground on the reef, now mostly reclaimed as an artificial reef dense with fish life.

## Overview

The vessel — referred to in sources as the Delsan or M/V Delsan, a small freighter — struck the reef and grounded; one source dates the grounding to a specific night in 1981, while other, less precisely sourced accounts place it more generally in the 1980s. In the weeks after grounding, salvagers reportedly stripped the vessel of fuel, oil, and other valuables before local authorities were alerted. No source gives the vessel's length or tonnage, so those details are omitted here. What remains today is mostly rusting steel ribs and hull plate broken up across the reef flat rather than an intact hull — more a landmark and navigational reference for the dive than a wreck to swim through. A sheltered crevasse near 23 meters sits close to the wreck site, with the surrounding wall dropping toward 33 meters.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 33 meters

## Marine Life

The site is known for a high density of reef fish: lionfish, and large groups (15-20) of batfish, along with snappers, groupers, and nurse sharks. Dogtooth tuna, great barracuda, giant trevally, and grey reef sharks are regularly reported, with less frequent sightings of whitetip, hammerhead, and tiger sharks. Yellowfin tuna, bigeye trevally, sea turtles, and manta rays have also been reported here, and the reef flat around the wreck supports staghorn coral habitat.
