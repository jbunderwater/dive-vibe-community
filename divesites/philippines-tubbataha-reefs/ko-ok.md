## Ko-ok

Ko-ok is one of Tubbataha's quieter gems — a calm, colourful, critter-rich coral garden on the northern part of South Atoll that contrasts with the park's adrenaline-heavy wall and current dives.

## Overview

Located near the northern tip of South Atoll, Ko-ok is composed of soft and hard coral gardens with sea fans, sponges, and crinoids, offering relaxed diving over a pristine coral plateau. It's a favourite for divers who love macro life and coral-garden diving rather than constant current and depth. That said, the northern tip of South Atoll is also an area where hammerhead sharks are known to pass by in the blue, so Ko-ok can deliver a pelagic surprise while divers wait among schools of circling jacks and fusiliers.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Atoll (northern part), Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-35 meters
- **Current**: Generally milder than Tubbataha's wall sites, but variable
- **Best Time**: March to June

## Marine Life

Ko-ok is best known for its macro life: frogfish, pygmy seahorses (especially tucked into gorgonian fans), nudibranchs, cleaner shrimp, and porcelain crabs hiding among the anemones. Schooling snappers and fusiliers move over the coral plateau, and schools of jacks circle overhead. Hammerhead sharks are occasionally seen passing in the blue off the northern tip of South Atoll.

## Entry and Exit

Liveaboard access with Tubbataha permits. Dinghy entry by giant stride; SMB return to dinghy. Park ranger protocols apply to all Tubbataha diving.

## Safety Considerations

Currents at Ko-ok are typically milder than at Tubbataha's wall sites, but conditions can change quickly in the open Sulu Sea. Always carry an SMB. Emergency evacuation from Tubbataha is a multi-hour operation.
