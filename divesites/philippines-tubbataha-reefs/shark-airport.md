## Shark Airport

Shark Airport takes its name from the sandy plateau where whitetip reef sharks lie at rest in numbers, resembling aircraft parked on a runway.

## Overview

Located on North Atoll in the Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Shark Airport is a shallow, sandy-bottomed plateau at around 15 meters that steps down to a shelf around 25-30 meters. Table coral formations mark the shallower ground, and the plateau is a dependable spot for resting whitetip reef sharks and cruising grey reef sharks. It's one of the park's more accessible sites for shark encounters, with reliable aggregations rather than chance sightings.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-40 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong
- **Best Time**: March to June

## Marine Life

Whitetip reef sharks are the defining feature, regularly seen resting on the sandy plateau, with grey reef sharks, nurse sharks, and silky sharks also reported and occasional whale shark and guitar shark sightings. Dogtooth tuna, moray eels, eagle rays, and triggerfish are seen working the plateau and shelf, alongside pyramid butterflyfish, moorish idols, and surgeonfish over the table coral. Turtles are a regular presence on the site.

## Dive Profile

Descend to the plateau around 15 meters, where resting sharks are typically found near the table coral formations. The shelf drops to around 25-30 meters for divers wanting more current-exposed water. Sharks here are generally undisturbed by divers holding position rather than approaching directly.

## Entry and Exit

Liveaboard access only. Dinghy entry by giant stride; SMB required for return to dinghy. Park ranger protocols apply to all Tubbataha diving.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Hold a stable position near the plateau rather than swimming toward resting sharks — they will move off if approached directly
- Currents can pick up on the shelf; monitor air and position relative to the dinghy pickup point
- Early dives tend to find the largest shark numbers on the plateau

## Safety Considerations

Depths to 30 meters and moderate-to-strong current require standard NDL and buoyancy discipline. Tubbataha's remote location means medical evacuation is a multi-hour operation; dive conservatively.

## Photography

The sandy plateau backdrop makes for clean wide-angle shark compositions, particularly with multiple resting whitetips in frame. Good ambient light in the shallow plateau reduces strobe dependence.
