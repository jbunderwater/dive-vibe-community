## Amos Rock

Amos Rock is a wall dive site in Philippines Tubbataha Reefs, Asia.

## Overview

Amos Rock is a North Atoll landmark — named for the midnight snappers that school around a distinctive rocky formation at 5 meters — where a coral-covered slope transitions into a wall densely packed with gorgonian fans, purple sea fans, and whip corals. Grey reef and whitetip sharks are reliably present when current is running, alongside dog-toothed tuna, eagle rays, napoleon wrasse, and both pickhandle and great barracuda patrolling the wall. Night dives reveal an entirely different world: lobsters, flatworms, nudibranchs, and pygmy seahorses tucked into the gorgonian fans make this an essential stop on any Tubbataha itinerary.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Philippines Tubbataha Reefs, Asia
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters

## Marine Life

Amos Rock is a standout shark site on the North Atoll, with grey reef and whitetip reef sharks reliably patrolling the current-swept wall alongside dog-toothed tuna, bluefin and giant trevally, mackerel, and barracuda. Grouper, snapper, and Napoleon wrasse are consistently present, and large schools of midnight snapper aggregate around the distinctive rock formation that gives the site its name. Manta rays, eagle rays, and sea turtles are also reported here. Night dives reveal a second life — lobsters, crabs, flatworms, nudibranchs, crinoids, and pygmy seahorses tucked into the gorgonian fans and whip corals become the focus after dark.
