## Amos Rock

Amos Rock (also called Southwest Rock) is a North Atoll wall dive named for a distinctive rock outcrop where midnight snapper school, its face carpeted in gorgonian fans below a shallow reef top.

## Overview

Amos Rock sits on the North Atoll, where a coral-covered slope beginning around 5 meters gives way to a sloping wall that continues well beyond recreational depths. Sea fans and soft corals cover the wall face, and the site holds a reputation as one of Tubbataha's most reliable spots for grey reef and whitetip reef sharks, especially when current is running. Schooling snapper, mackerel, grouper, and fusiliers move along the reef top, with Napoleon wrasse, angelfish, and butterflyfish adding color closer to the wall.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 20 meters

## Marine Life

Grey reef and whitetip reef sharks are reliably present along the wall, alongside dogtooth tuna, eagle rays, Napoleon wrasse, and barracuda. Schooling snapper, mackerel, and grouper move over the reef top, with fusiliers, angelfish, and butterflyfish among the smaller reef fish. Large schools of midnight snapper aggregate around the rock formation that gives the site its name. Night dives bring out a different set of residents — lobsters, flatworms, nudibranchs, crabs, and crinoids, with pygmy seahorses tucked into the gorgonian fans.
