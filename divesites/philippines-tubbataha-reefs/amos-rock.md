## Amos Rock

Amos Rock (also known locally as Southwest Rock) is a North Atoll wall dive named for the distinctive rock formation at 5 meters where midnight snapper school in numbers.

## Overview

Amos Rock sits on the south side of Tubbataha's North Atoll. Divers descend a gentle coral slope that steepens into a sheer wall, its face covered in whip corals and gorgonian sea fans; several sources report the wall continuing well beyond recreational limits, to roughly 70 meters, which is why the dive is worked in the 6-30 meter range and reserved for experienced, current-comfortable divers. Sightings of grey reef and whitetip reef sharks are common along the wall, and dive shops describe good macro life on night dives here, though no source specifies exact species seen at this particular site after dark.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

Sources consistently report mackerel, snapper (including the schooling midnight snapper that gives the site its name), grouper, fusiliers, surgeonfish, angelfish, and Moorish idols on the reef and wall. Larger animals include grey reef sharks, whitetip reef sharks, eagle rays, manta rays, dogtooth tuna, and barracuda patrolling in open water off the wall. Turtles and Napoleon wrasse are also reported. Species-level claims beyond this (e.g., a specific barracuda species) are not confirmed by available sources and have been left out.
