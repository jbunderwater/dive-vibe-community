## Black Rock

Black Rock sits at the northeast corner of Tubbataha's South Atoll, a gently sloping plateau known for reliable whitetip shark sightings and strong pelagic action.

## Overview

Black Rock is a plateau on the South Atoll that slopes gently from about 15 to 25 meters. Currents sweeping the site generate upwellings that concentrate nutrients, drawing in large dogtooth tuna and giant trevally to hunt mid-water. The site demands advanced experience and conservative depth management given the currents and depth temptations typical of Tubbataha's more exposed sites.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Atoll (northeast corner), Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-40 meters
- **Current**: Variable; can be strong across the plateau
- **Best Time**: March to June (only time the park is open to liveaboards)

## Marine Life

Whitetip sharks are the signature sighting at Black Rock, often seen resting on the seabed or cruising in the company of bluefin trevally. Grey reef sharks circle continuously over the plateau, and hammerhead or tiger sharks are occasionally reported. Large dogtooth tuna and giant trevally hunt mid-water in the upwellings, and reef manta rays pass through occasionally during plankton-rich conditions. Titan triggerfish are frequently encountered near the start of the dive.

## Entry and Exit

Access exclusively by liveaboard vessel with Tubbataha permits. Dinghy deployment from the liveaboard; entry by giant stride. SMB deployment for return to the dinghy. All diving in Tubbataha must follow park ranger protocols.

## Safety Considerations

Tubbataha's remote location means medical evacuation would take many hours, so conservative dive planning is essential. Current across the open plateau can be strong and unpredictable; watch depth carefully, as the pelagic activity here can tempt divers deeper than planned. Always carry an SMB.
