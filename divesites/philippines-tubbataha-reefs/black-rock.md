## Black Rock

Black Rock is a wall dive in the Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, its dark rock formation and sloping plateau attracting strong pelagic activity from upwelling currents.

## Overview

Black Rock sits within the UNESCO World Heritage-listed park in the Sulu Sea, accessible only by liveaboard during the March-to-June season. Described by dive operators as a sloping plateau leading into a wall (and by one source as a submerged pinnacle), the site's structure generates upwellings that draw in dogtooth tuna and giant trevally alongside a strong resident shark presence. The site's position within the fully protected park means minimal fishing pressure and correspondingly high fish density.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 20-40 meters
- **Current**: Variable; can be strong, driving the site's pelagic activity
- **Best Time**: March to June (only time the park is open to liveaboards)

## Marine Life

Black Rock is known for shark diversity: whitetip reef, grey reef, and hammerhead sharks are all reported, with occasional tiger shark sightings. Dogtooth tuna and giant trevally patrol the open water off the wall, and Spanish mackerel and barracuda are regularly seen. Green and hawksbill turtles are common, and the site hosts a manta ray cleaning station where mantas are seen year-round with a seasonal peak.

## Dive Profile

Descend along the rock/wall face, keeping it to one side as you work down through the 15-30 meter zone where sea fan and soft coral growth is densest. Watch the open water off the wall for tuna, trevally, and shark activity. Ascend along the shallower section for the safety stop.

## Entry and Exit

Access exclusively by liveaboard with Tubbataha permits. Dinghy deployment; entry by giant stride. SMB deployment required for return to the dinghy. All diving follows park ranger protocols.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Tubbataha liveaboards book out well in advance — plan accordingly
- The park's six-week accessible season (March to June) is fixed by conditions, not preference
- Watch the blue water off the wall for tuna and trevally activity in addition to the wall itself
- The manta cleaning station rewards patience — hold position rather than chasing

## Safety Considerations

Tubbataha's remote location (roughly 180 km from Puerto Princesa) means medical evacuation would take many hours. Current can strengthen quickly on this site; carry an SMB and stay close to your buddy/group.

## Photography

Wide-angle setups suit the wall structure and pelagic activity — tuna and trevally schools, shark silhouettes, and manta encounters at the cleaning station are the standout subjects. Visibility here typically supports large-scene compositions.
