## Black Rock

Black Rock sits at the northeast corner of Tubbataha's South Atoll, where a sloping reef gives way to a wall/pinnacle structure known for strong, current-driven pelagic action.

## Overview

Black Rock is described across sources as a sloping plateau that steepens into a drop-off, with one dive guide characterizing it as a submerged pinnacle generating upwellings that concentrate open-water species. Reported depths vary by source and by which face of the structure is being described: a reef top around 5 meters slopes to a crevasse near 23 meters before the wall continues to roughly 40 meters on one account, while another describes the drop continuing beyond 40 meters. Because Tubbataha operators name and describe Black Rock's faces somewhat differently, this entry uses 40 meters as a representative working depth.

## Site Information

- **Location**: South Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 40 meters

## Marine Life

Whitetip reef sharks are frequently seen resting on the seabed or cruising, and grey reef sharks patrol the deeper sections; scalloped hammerhead sharks are reported occasionally in the April-June window during early morning dives. Dogtooth tuna and giant trevally are drawn to the site's upwellings. Sweetlips, emperors, snappers, and chevron barracuda are common on the reef, and manta ray cleaning stations have been documented nearby. One source also reports occasional tiger sharks.

## Entry and Exit

Liveaboard access only, with entry by dinghy and giant stride. Current can be strong on exposed sections; guides typically brief divers on which face is being dived given the site's varying current exposure.
