## Bird Islet Wall

Bird Islet Wall drops off the north face of Tubbataha's North Atoll, named for the nearby islet that serves as a breeding ground for seven resident and endangered seabird species.

## Overview

The wall begins in fissured, crevice-riddled reef at 5-8 meters and plunges well beyond recreational limits, with visibility regularly reaching 30-40 meters in the UNESCO World Heritage park's Sulu Sea waters. This liveaboard-only site is accessible just three months a year, from mid-March to mid-June, when seas flatten and the park opens its gates.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Atoll (north face), Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wall dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters

## Marine Life

Nurse and leopard sharks are frequently found resting in the wall's fissures and crevices, alongside blacktip, whitetip, and grey reef sharks. Small and large manta rays, lagoon rays, and eagle rays have all been seen along the wall, together with flounders and turtles. The reef top supports snappers, surgeonfish, groupers, Moorish idols, and jacks, set against huge gorgonian fans, barrel sponges, and soft corals.
