## Malayan Wreck

The Malayan Wreck lies on the southwest edge of Tubbataha's North Atoll, adjacent to a wall that drops to around 60 meters - but its exact origin and history are disputed across sources.

## Overview

Independent sources give three different accounts of how the Malayan came to rest here, and none can be confirmed against a second, higher-authority source: one dive-travel source identifies it as the SS Malayan, a Philippine freighter said to have sunk during World War II at around 35 meters; a separate source instead describes it as a fishing vessel that ran aground on the shallow reef top during a typhoon in the 1970s; and a third describes it as having been deliberately sunk to create an artificial reef. No vessel length, tonnage, or build date is confirmed by any source, and no Wikipedia article exists for a ship by this name to help resolve the conflict. In keeping with the site's anti-hallucination policy, none of these three narratives is asserted as fact here - readers should treat the vessel's identity and history as unresolved. What is consistent across sources is the wreck's current condition: it still resembles an intact hull rather than scattered wreckage, sits mostly submerged with a large intact engine, and is now a coral-encrusted structure at the base of the adjacent wall.

## Site Information

- **Location**: North Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters

## Marine Life

Sources consistently report large sweetlips and impressive table coral growth on the wreck itself, with grey reef sharks, whitetip reef sharks, nurse sharks, and occasional hammerhead sharks along the adjacent wall. African pompano, rainbow runners, bumphead parrotfish, giant morays, batfish, and marble rays are also reported at or near the wreck, and the site is noted as good for macro subjects (nudibranchs, crabs, shrimp) as well as wide-angle wreck shots.

## Safety Considerations

As with any wreck, interior exploration should only be attempted with proper wreck-diving training and equipment; no source describes penetration here as routine or without risk.
