## Malayan Wreck

Malayan Wreck sits on the southwest tip of Tubbataha's North Atoll, a broken-down wreck now mostly reclaimed by coral and best known for the wall and reef life around it rather than the wreckage itself.

## Overview

Little of the original vessel remains intact — what's visible today is largely rusted hull plate and structural remnant fused into the reef, and the site is commonly used by liveaboards as a checkout dive to adjust weighting and buoyancy before deeper dives elsewhere in the park. The wreck's identity is not firmly established: it is sometimes referred to as the "SS Malayan," a Philippine freighter, but sources disagree on when it went down — one account places the sinking during World War II, another puts it as late as the 1970s or 1980s. No source documents the vessel's length, tonnage, or build date, so none of those specifics are stated here. What is well documented is the wall and reef surrounding the wreck: it drops to around 35 meters and is thick with gorgonian fans, drawing consistent shark activity.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Southwest tip of North Atoll, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 35 meters

## Marine Life

Grey reef and whitetip reef sharks, nurse sharks, and hammerheads are reported along the wall, with pygmy seahorses tucked into gorgonian fans. African pompano, leaf scorpionfish, rainbow runners, bumphead parrotfish, batfish, marble rays, and giant morays are all documented at the site, along with bannerfish, bluelined snapper, chevron barracuda, hawksbill turtles, and large schools of jacks. Groupers, lionfish, and nudibranchs are also reported around the wreck structure itself.
