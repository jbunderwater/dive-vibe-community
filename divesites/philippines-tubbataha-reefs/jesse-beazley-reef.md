## Jesse Beazley Reef

Jesse Beazley Reef is an isolated coral cay roughly 20 kilometers north of Tubbataha's main atolls - the smallest and most remote of the park's three reef structures, and often the least-visited given the extra transit time.

## Overview

The reef slopes from a shallow rim around 7-12 meters to a wall that drops past 50 meters, with visibility reported up to 40 meters. Its isolation from the main atolls means it sees fewer liveaboard visits than Amos Rock, Black Rock, or the other North/South Atoll sites, and not every Tubbataha itinerary includes it. Dive sites here are sometimes split into North End and South End depending on conditions.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Jesse Beazley Reef, Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park, Sulu Sea, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: liveaboard
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters

## Marine Life

Acropora and Porites corals support schools of barracuda, Spanish mackerel, unicornfish, and surgeonfish, with Napoleon wrasse and whitetip, blacktip, and grey reef sharks reported on the reef. In open water off the wall, sources report eagle rays, manta rays, hammerhead sharks, and - less commonly - mako and thresher sharks. Whale shark sightings and abundant sea turtles, both claimed in an earlier version of this page, are not confirmed by any source specific to this reef and have been removed; they are reported elsewhere in Tubbataha but not documented here specifically.
