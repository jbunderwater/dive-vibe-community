## Evo House Reef

Evolution Diving Resort's house reef is a muck-diving and mandarinfish site directly off their beachfront, described by the operator itself as one of the best muck dives in the country.

## Overview

Evo House Reef (Evolution House Reef) sits a few minutes' swim or boat ride from Evolution Diving Resort. The operator's own site describes it as a macro/muck specialist site, with a shallow patch of staghorn coral around 6 meters where mandarinfish gather to perform their dusk mating display, and a maximum depth of about 15 meters. Artificial structures are scattered through the sandy bottom, adding habitat for the site's macro residents.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Evolution Diving Resort, southern Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters

## Marine Life

Confirmed by the operator's own descriptions and independent guides: repeatedly-seen pairs of crinoid squat lobsters and imperial shrimp, dozens of bobtail squid at dusk, coconut octopus, robust ghost pipefish, several mantis shrimp species, seahorses, and — the signature draw — mandarinfish performing their courtship display in the staghorn coral at dusk.

## Dive Profile

Best dived at dusk for the mandarinfish display: descend onto the shallow coral patch, dim or shield torches, and watch quietly for around 20 minutes as pairs rise from the coral. Afterward, work the surrounding sandy bottom and artificial structures for the resort's other signature macro subjects.

## Entry and Exit

A short boat ride (a few minutes) from Evolution Diving Resort. Entry by giant stride; return to the boat by ladder.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Time your dive for dusk if the mandarinfish display is the priority
- Shield or switch off torches during mandarinfish observation
- Bring a macro lens — this is a muck site, not a wide-angle reef
- Ask about bobtail squid, which are reported regularly at dusk

## Safety Considerations

The shallow depth minimises decompression risk. Because the mandarinfish dive runs into dusk/dark conditions, treat it as a night dive: carry a primary and backup light and stay close to your buddy.
