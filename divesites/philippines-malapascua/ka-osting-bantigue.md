## Ka-Osting / Bantigue

Ka-Osting and Bantigue (also spelled Bantigi) are two adjacent muck-diving sites on Malapascua, regarded by local divers as among the best critter-hunting grounds in the area — some operators describe Bantigi as rivaling Lembeh for macro diversity.

## Overview

Bantigue starts as a shallow reef before transitioning to a sandy, rubble bottom at around 12 meters, where goby-and-shrimp pairs occupy holes across the sand and small rocks shelter mantis shrimp. Ka-Osting, immediately to the east, is a shallow, low-current muck site with a similar cast of critters. Because the two sites are adjacent and frequently dived together, they are often listed as a single combined site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: East/north of Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 8-15 meters
- **Current**: Generally light
- **Best Time**: Year-round

## Marine Life

Sources report fire urchins, mantis shrimp, zebra crabs, dwarf lionfish, cuttlefish, seamoths, snake eels, snowflake moray eels, nudibranchs, and a large resident carpet anemone with guarding anemonefish at Bantigue. Ka-Osting adds lobsters, porcelain crabs, and — with luck — hairy frogfish. Occasional mimic octopus and stargazer sightings are also reported. Sweetlips and surgeonfish cleaning-station activity mentioned in earlier drafts of this page was not confirmed by any source and has been removed.

## Dive Profile

Work the sandy/rubble bottom slowly, checking holes and small rock piles for shrimp and crab activity. This is a dive for a slow pace and close attention rather than covering distance.

## Entry and Exit

Short banca ride from Malapascua resorts. Entry by giant stride; minimal current makes for an easy return to the boat.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a macro lens — this is one of Malapascua's best muck sites for small critters
- Ask your guide to point out goby/shrimp pairs and mantis shrimp burrows
- Move slowly and avoid stirring up the sandy bottom

## Safety Considerations

Shallow depth and light current make this an easy, low-risk dive. Standard boat-diving precautions apply.

## Photography

A strong macro photography site — fire urchins, mantis shrimp, and (with luck) hairy frogfish are the standout subjects.
