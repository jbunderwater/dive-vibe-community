## Overview

Malapascua has at least three distinct, separately-documented WWII Japanese wrecks, and this entry's recorded depth (17-33m) and near-shore coordinates do not cleanly match any one of them:

- **Lighthouse Wreck** — a Japanese landing craft, hit by an aerial bomb before reaching the beach with a cargo of cement, now broken into two pieces at 3-7 meters (sources: PADI, Kokay's Malapascua Diving, Dumaguete Info). Its location, close to Malapascua's lighthouse point, is a reasonable geographic match for this OSM point, but its depth (3-7m) does not match the recorded 17-33m.
- **Tapilon Wreck** — an unidentified Japanese cargo vessel, torpedoed during WWII and now broken into several sections spread across roughly 22-32 meters (sources: Kokay's Malapascua Diving, Dumaguete Info, Ocean Vibration). Its depth is a reasonable match to this entry's recorded 17-33m, but operators consistently place it about 30 minutes offshore near the mainland town of Tapilon — much farther out than this near-shore OSM coordinate.
- **Pioneer / Mogami Maru** — a Japanese gunboat sunk by torpedo, lying at 42-54 meters (multiple sources) — too deep to match this entry at all.

No source gives a specific vessel name, build date, length, or tonnage for either plausible candidate (Lighthouse Wreck or Tapilon), so none is stated here. No sinking-cause detail is asserted beyond what is common to both candidates (WWII-era loss, most likely by aerial attack or torpedo) since which vessel this is remains unresolved.
