## Japanese Ship Wreck

Known locally as the Lighthouse Wreck, this is a shallow WWII-era Japanese vessel resting near Malapascua's old lighthouse — an accessible wreck often used for beginner wreck-diving experience.

## Overview

Sources agree this is a Japanese vessel from World War II, but describe it with two variations that are worth stating plainly rather than resolving by guesswork. Three independent sources (PADI, Malapascua's Thresher Shark Divers, and Dumaguete Info) describe it specifically as a **landing craft** carrying a large cement cargo — reportedly intended for a gun emplacement — that was hit by a bomb just before reaching the beach, and now lies broken into two pieces in shallow water; the nearby "rocks" are said to be cement bags from the cargo, with weave imprints from their sacking still visible. A fourth source (Kokay's Malapascua Diving) describes it more generally as a Japanese warship that was found hiding at the site and was subsequently bombed, without the landing-craft or cement detail. No source gives a vessel name, build date, length, or tonnage, so none is stated here.

Reported maximum depth also varies slightly: most sources cite an average of about 3 meters, while one source gives a maximum of 7 meters. The wreck's location is close to (within roughly 150-200 meters of) the "Lighthouse" dive site, and OSM's own data for this entry already flags the location as approximate — consistent with these being the same site.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near the lighthouse, Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 7 meters

## Marine Life

Sources consistently report yellow-tailed barracuda, hermit crabs, octopus, pipefish, juvenile harlequin sweetlips, and banded sea snakes at this wreck. One source also reports seahorses and coral pipefish performing mating displays, along with nudibranchs and colorful tunicates. Earlier claims of batfish schools and lionfish "in the cargo holds" (tied to a since-corrected 33m depth profile) were not supported by any source and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

A shallow, easy wreck dive with an average depth around 3 meters (maximum reported up to 7 meters). Its shallow depth and minimal current make it a common choice for introducing divers to wreck diving, including practicing reel use and line-running skills used in more advanced wreck penetration elsewhere on Malapascua (such as the Dona Marilyn wreck).

## Entry and Exit

Short boat ride (roughly 10 minutes) from Malapascua resorts; entry by giant stride given the shallow depth.

## Tips and Recommendations

- A good site for practicing basic wreck-diving skills (reel use, line running) given the shallow, low-current conditions
- Look closely at the "rocks" near the wreck — sources report these are cement bags from the original cargo, with weave imprints still visible
- Suitable for beginner divers, including as part of Open Water course dives

## Safety Considerations

Wreck penetration always carries risk regardless of depth and requires proper training and equipment — this shallow site is not a substitute for wreck-diving certification if any interior exploration is planned. The shallow depth minimizes decompression risk for open-water observation of the site.

## Photography

The shallow depth provides good natural light for wreck photography without needing heavy strobe work. Macro subjects (pipefish, hermit crabs, nudibranchs) and wreck-structure wide-angle shots are both productive.
