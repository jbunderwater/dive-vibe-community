## Japanese Ship Wreck

This wreck's depth and profile match the site local dive operators call the Tapilon Wreck — an unidentified WWII Japanese cargo carrier that was torpedoed and now lies broken into several sections on the seabed north of Malapascua.

## Overview

Multiple independent Malapascua dive-operator sources describe a WWII-era Japanese cargo carrier known locally as the Tapilon Wreck, lying at 22–29 meters off the northern side of the island (sources report a range of 22–28m to 29m maximum — depths vary slightly between operators, so plan for the deeper end). The vessel was hit by torpedoes during World War II and now lies broken into several sections, though it remains recognisable as a ship. No source found documents the vessel's proper name, its build date, length, or tonnage — these details are not fabricated here because they could not be verified. The wreck is heavily colonised by black coral and, according to one source, soft coral growth, giving the structure a dark, textured profile that divers explore with torches. This entry's exact GPS position is flagged by OpenStreetMap as approximate, and the match to the Tapilon Wreck described by operators is based on depth and vessel-type profile rather than confirmed coordinates or a documented name — treat the identification as likely but not certain.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern side of Malapascua Island, Philippines (position approximate)
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Wreck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 29 meters

## Marine Life

Marine life reported at this wreck by dive-shop sources includes marble rays, ornate ghost pipefish, and occasional flamboyant cuttlefish — confirmed independently by two separate operators. Additional species reported by individual sources include nudibranchs, flatworms, moray eels, cuttlefish, squid, scorpionfish, fire sea urchins with resident zebra crabs, squat lobsters, Coleman's shrimp, frogfish, and — per one source — schooling small barracuda in large numbers. Because these come from dive-shop descriptions of the site rather than a single confirmed survey, expect variation dive to dive.

## Safety Considerations

This is an Advanced-rated wreck dive. Penetration of the broken sections requires proper wreck-diving training and equipment — entering any part of the structure without that training carries real risk and should not be attempted casually. Depths of 22–29 meters limit no-decompression bottom time; dive with an operator familiar with the site's current conditions and layout.
