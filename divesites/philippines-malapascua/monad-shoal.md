## Monad Shoal

Monad Shoal is a submerged plateau that was, for two decades, the world's most reliable place to see pelagic thresher sharks — but since around September 2022 the sharks have largely relocated to nearby Kimud Shoal, and Monad is now better known for tiger and bull shark sightings.

## Overview

Monad Shoal sits roughly 8 km southeast of Malapascua Island, its plateau top at 15–25 meters before the surrounding seabed drops away to around 250 meters. From 2005 through the late 2010s, researchers documented consistent pelagic thresher shark (*Alopias pelagicus*) cleaning behaviour here — remote-camera studies recorded 97 cleaning events across 1,230 hours in 2005–2009 and 118 events in 68 hours of filming in January 2018, and divers came from around the world for near-daily encounters. That changed around September 2022: multiple dive operators and ScubaBoard trip reports describe tiger sharks (and other higher-order predators such as silvertips and oceanic blacktips) moving onto the shoal and displacing the threshers, which shifted their cleaning activity to Kimud Shoal, a smaller, shallower seamount about 45–60 minutes further from Malapascua. Monad Shoal is still actively dived — most operators now run it as a first dive of the day specifically for tiger shark encounters, reporting roughly one tiger shark sighting per week — but it should no longer be marketed or dived with the expectation of a daily, reliable thresher shark encounter. Divers set on thresher sharks are directed by local operators to Kimud Shoal instead.

## Site Information

- **Location**: ~8 km southeast of Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Pinnacle/seamount
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 30 meters
- **Typical Visibility**: 15–30 meters
- **Current**: Moderate to strong; varies with tidal phase
- **Best Time**: Year-round; tiger shark sightings reported roughly weekly

## Marine Life

Tiger sharks are now the primary large-predator sighting reported at Monad Shoal, along with occasional bull sharks. Pelagic thresher sharks (*Alopias pelagicus*) are still occasionally recorded here — the site has two decades of documented cleaning-station use — but sightings are no longer daily or dependable; divers wanting a consistent thresher shark encounter should look to Kimud Shoal instead. Devil rays and whitetip reef sharks are also reported at the shoal.

## Dive Profile

Depart from Malapascua before dawn for the boat crossing. Entry is typically a negative descent directly to the plateau at 20–25 meters without lingering at the surface. Position on the sand or rock terrace, maintaining neutral buoyancy without touching the substrate, and watch the blue water for passing sharks. Maximum recreational depth is around 30 meters. Begin ascent with sufficient air for a safety stop at 5 meters.

## Entry and Exit

Malapascua dive operators run Monad Shoal as a boat dive requiring a seaworthy banca. Entry is from the boat in open water — negative entry directly to depth. Return to the surface via SMB and ladder.

## Tips and Recommendations

- If your priority is thresher sharks, ask your operator about Kimud Shoal — as of this writing it is the more consistent site for them
- Perfect your neutral buoyancy before any shoal dive — touching the substrate during shark observation is not acceptable
- Stay still during shark encounters — moving toward sharks causes them to retreat
- A wide-angle lens on a compact camera captures blue-water shark silhouettes well; dedicated setups work best in the deeper, lower-light conditions

## Safety Considerations

This is an Advanced-level dive. Depths of 20–30 meters, open-ocean exposure, and pre-dawn conditions mean this dive should only be attempted with experienced local guides who know the site. Carry a dive light for the pre-dawn descent. The nearest hyperbaric facility is on mainland Cebu, a significant travel time from Malapascua.
