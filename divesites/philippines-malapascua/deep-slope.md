## Deep Slope

Deep Slope (also known locally as Pantaw Pantaw) is a sandy-slope muck site off Malapascua that is highly regarded for underwater photography, with a topography sources describe as "a sand dune that slowly turns into a small wall."

## Overview

The site averages around 21 meters with a maximum reported between 25 and 28 meters across sources. The sandy dune bottom is home to garden eels, flamboyant cuttlefish, and pygmy seahorses, while the small wall section it slopes into is covered in sponges and soft and hard coral. Multiple independent sources describe this as a muck/critter site rather than a conventional coral reef.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Off Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Muck dive
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters

## Marine Life

Sources report garden eels swaying from the sandy bottom, flamboyant cuttlefish, pygmy seahorses, nudibranchs, pipefish, frogfish, and cowfish, plus green and hawksbill turtles passing over the slope. No source found for this site confirms thresher shark sightings — an earlier draft of this page claimed thresher sharks "occasionally pass through," which was not corroborated by any research and has been removed.

## Dive Profile

Descend onto the sandy slope and work down toward the small wall section, watching the sand for garden eels and cuttlefish and the wall face for pygmy seahorses among the soft coral. A dive computer and conservative profile are recommended given the 21-28m working depth.

## Entry and Exit

Short banca ride from Malapascua resorts. Entry by giant stride; return to the banca by ladder.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Move slowly across the sand — garden eels retreat into their burrows at the slightest disturbance
- Check soft coral on the small wall section carefully for pygmy seahorses
- A macro lens is the right choice for this site

## Safety Considerations

The 25-28m depth range calls for attention to no-decompression limits. Deploy an SMB for ascent.

## Photography

One of Malapascua's better macro sites — garden eels, flamboyant cuttlefish, and pygmy seahorses are the signature subjects.
