## Deep Slope

Deep Slope is a sand-dune-shaped macro site on Malapascua's northern flank, best known for garden eels and pygmy seahorses across its sandy, gently undulating terrain.

## Overview

Deep Slope features distinctive sand-dune topography rather than a typical coral reef, with sources reporting a working depth of around 23 meters. Because of the depth and the amount of time critter-hunting rewards, nitrox is recommended by at least one operator for extended bottom time. Only two independent sources with site-specific detail were found for this exact site name during this research pass, so treat depth and terrain description as reasonably confident but not fully cross-verified to the deep-QA 3-source standard.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 23 meters

## Marine Life

Confirmed by two independent sources: garden eels, pygmy seahorses, flamboyant cuttlefish, nudibranchs, pipefish (including ghost and banded pipefish), frogfish, and cowfish. A previous version of this description claimed thresher sharks occasionally pass through Deep Slope — no source found supports that claim, and it has been removed. Thresher shark activity at Malapascua is documented specifically at Monad Shoal and (more reliably, as of 2022 onward) Kimud Shoal, not at Deep Slope.

## Dive Profile

The sand-dune terrain rewards a slow, methodical search for camouflaged macro subjects like garden eels and pygmy seahorses rather than a fast-paced tour. Nitrox is worth considering given the depth and the amount of time worth spending here.

## Entry and Exit

Boat dive from Malapascua resorts. Entry by giant stride; return to the boat by ladder.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Bring a macro lens and consider nitrox for extended bottom time at depth
- Move slowly across the sand dunes — garden eels retreat into their burrows at the first sign of a diver approaching directly

## Safety Considerations

The ~23-meter working depth limits no-decompression bottom time on air; nitrox is a reasonable option if certified. Standard boat-diving precautions apply.
