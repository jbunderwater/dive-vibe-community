## Lighthouse

Lighthouse (also called Lighthouse Reef) is one of Malapascua's signature dusk dives, a shallow coral garden near the island's old lighthouse where splendid mandarin fish gather to mate as the light fades.

## Overview

Multiple independent sources describe Lighthouse as a shallow coral-garden reef, with a top around 7 meters and a base around 10-12 meters — not the 30-meter wall described in earlier drafts of this page. Divers gather here in the late afternoon; as the sun sets, mandarin fish (which shelter in staghorn coral patches around 6 meters during the day) emerge to perform their mating display, in which a male and female rise together off the coral in a brief, twisting embrace to release eggs and sperm. Around a dozen dive boats from Malapascua's operators typically converge here at sunset, making it one of the busier dusk dives on the island.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near the old lighthouse, Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 12 meters

## Marine Life

The mandarin fish mating display is the headline event, well documented by multiple independent trip reports and operator sites. Other species reported at this specific site include tigertail seahorses (clinging to hard coral rather than their more typical seagrass/sponge perches), lionfish, cuttlefish, crabs, shrimp, nudibranchs, cowries, and volutes, plus bobtail squid. Claims of thresher sharks, hawksbill turtles, and grey reef sharks at this site from an earlier draft were not corroborated by any source and have been removed — those species are documented elsewhere on Malapascua (notably Monad Shoal for thresher sharks), not at Lighthouse.

## Dive Profile

Divers typically enter in late afternoon and settle near the staghorn coral patches around 6 meters as dusk approaches. Guides ask divers to keep torches off or shielded during the mandarin fish display to avoid disturbing the fish, illuminating only briefly and indirectly. The shallow depth (max ~12m) allows an extended dive as darkness falls.

## Entry and Exit

Short boat ride from Malapascua resorts, timed to arrive before sunset. Entry by giant stride.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Time your arrival for approximately 30-45 minutes before sunset for the best chance at the full mating display
- Keep torch light to a minimum and never point it directly at displaying mandarin fish
- Expect other dive boats at this popular sunset spot — position patiently rather than crowding the coral patches
- A macro lens with fast autofocus and good low-light performance is essential for photographing the brief mating rise

## Safety Considerations

Shallow depth (max ~12m) keeps decompression risk low, but this is functionally a night dive by the end — carry a primary and backup light, and stay close to your buddy and guide in fading/dark conditions.

## Photography

A premier muck/macro night-photography site. The mandarin fish mating rise happens quickly and unpredictably — pre-focus on active staghorn patches and be ready to shoot with minimal, indirect light.
