## Lighthouse

Lighthouse is Malapascua's signature mandarinfish dive — a shallow reef near the island's lighthouse where pairs of mandarinfish emerge from staghorn coral at dusk to perform their brief mating display, almost every night of the year.

## Overview

Lighthouse sits close to Malapascua's dive centres, a short boat ride away, and is dived at dusk specifically for its mandarinfish. The fish shelter in a patch of staghorn coral at around 6 meters, with the site's maximum depth around 15 meters. Divers descend as the light fades, switch off torches (guides use theirs sparingly, half-covered), and wait for pairs of mandarinfish to rise from the coral for their courtship display — sightings are reported as near-guaranteed most nights, with the most prolific activity around the full moon. After the mandarinfish display, roughly 20 minutes into the dive, divers typically move on to explore the rest of the shallow reef. Multiple independent sources are consistent on this description; none describe Lighthouse as a wall dive or associate it with wall-adjacent pelagic species.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Near the Malapascua lighthouse, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Beginner
- **Maximum Depth**: 15 meters

## Marine Life

Mandarinfish are the headline attraction, performing their mating dance in the staghorn coral at dusk. Sources also report seahorses of varying shapes and colours among the hard coral branches, flamboyant cuttlefish, blue-ringed octopus, and a variety of crabs. No source reviewed documents thresher shark, grey reef shark, or hawksbill turtle activity at this specific site.

## Dive Profile

Dives here are timed to start around sunset. The first ~20 minutes are spent quietly observing the mandarinfish in the staghorn coral patch with minimal light, then the group moves on to explore the surrounding shallow reef for the remainder of the dive.

## Entry and Exit

Short boat ride from Malapascua resorts, typically departing just before sunset. Entry by giant stride or back roll; return to the boat by ladder.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Turn off (or heavily shield) your torch during the mandarinfish observation period — bright light disrupts the display
- Full moon nights are reported as the most active for mandarinfish courtship, though sightings are reported most nights
- Bring a macro lens — this is a muck/macro-oriented dive, not a wide-angle site
- Because the dive starts at or after sunset, treat it as a night dive: carry a primary light and a backup

## Safety Considerations

The shallow depth (max ~15m) keeps decompression risk low. Because this is effectively a night dive, standard night-diving precautions apply: dive with a buddy you can see clearly, carry a backup light, and agree on signals in low visibility. Deploy an SMB or use a strobe/light for surface signalling given reduced visibility to the boat crew.
