## North Point

North Point, on the exposed northern tip of Malapascua, is described differently by different operators — some treat it as an accessible macro reef, others as a deeper, current-driven pelagic dive. Both descriptions are presented here since sources genuinely disagree.

## Overview

Two local dive operators (Kokay's Malapascua Diving and Malapascua's Thresher Shark Divers) describe North Point as a largely sandy-and-coral area between 8 and 24 meters, suitable for Open Water divers, with large coral heads to cruise around and current that "tends to change direction." A third source (Dive The World) describes a deeper 15-30 meter wall-and-slope site with overhangs, strong current, and "adrenaline-pumping drift dives" recommended only for experienced divers. Given this conflict, treat North Point as a site whose character depends on conditions and which operator is running the dive — confirm with your guide what profile to expect before entry.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Northern tip of Malapascua Island, Philippines
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 24 meters

## Marine Life

The reef-focused sources report frogfish of different colors, fire urchins, zebra crabs, candy crabs, and nudibranchs, with numerous crabs and shrimp visible on night dives. The wall/pelagic-focused source instead reports barracuda, tuna, trevally, Napoleon wrasse, bumphead parrotfish, reef sharks, and eagle rays. Neither source mentions hammerhead sharks or dogtooth tuna at this site — those claims from an earlier draft were not corroborated and have been removed.

## Dive Profile

If diving the shallower macro profile, expect to cruise around large coral heads at a leisurely pace, watching for current shifts. If diving the deeper wall/slope profile, expect a current-managed drift dive along overhangs down to 24-30 meters. Confirm which profile your operator plans before the dive.

## Entry and Exit

Boat entry from Malapascua resorts, roughly 10 minutes' travel.

## Tips and Recommendations

- Ask your guide in advance whether this will be run as a macro reef dive or a current/drift dive — the experience differs substantially
- Currents can shift direction around the coral formations; stay close to your guide
- A reef hook can help if the current/drift profile is running

## Safety Considerations

Current conditions vary significantly by report — from mild and shifting to genuinely strong drift conditions. Confirm current expectations with your operator, carry an SMB, and be prepared for a drift ascent if diving the more exposed profile.

## Photography

Macro gear suits the coral-head/critter profile; wide-angle suits the wall/pelagic profile. Confirm which is planned before choosing your setup.
