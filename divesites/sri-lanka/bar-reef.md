## Bar Reef

Bar Reef is Sri Lanka's largest coral reef and largest marine protected area, a shallow reef system roughly an hour by boat off the Kalpitiya peninsula.

## Overview

Bar Reef Marine Sanctuary, declared in 1992, covers 306.7 km² off the northern end of the Kalpitiya peninsula and is described as Sri Lanka's most biodiverse coral formation, with 156 coral species and 283 fish species recorded (Wikipedia). Eco Team describes the reef as about 3 nautical miles long and 1 nautical mile wide, reached by boat from Kalpitiya in under an hour. Diving is shallow: Eco Team gives 2-8 meters on the reef and up to 40 meters slightly further out, while Extreme Nomads cites an average depth of about 3 meters. This makes it suitable for beginners and snorkelers. The season is late November to early May; the southwest monsoon (May-November) makes diving impossible. Extreme Nomads, quoting Kalpitiya Diving Center, gives visibility of 10-25 meters. A ScubaBoard contributor describes "great shallow coral gardens." Condition varies: Extreme Nomads reports shallow sections damaged by crown-of-thorns starfish, dynamite fishing, coral mining and anchors, with bleaching in 1998 and 2016, and deeper coral in better condition; Wikipedia lists overfishing, purse seining, sedimentation and agricultural/prawn-farm pollution as threats.

## Marine Life

Reef sharks and manta rays are reported by Extreme Nomads and Eco Team, and ScubaBoard mentions "a few reef sharks" and decent fish life on the shallow reef. Eco Team and Extreme Nomads also mention turtles (Eco Team: "occasional"). Species-level identifications (shark and turtle species), groupers and dolphins appear in only one source or none of the pages checked, so they are not named here.
