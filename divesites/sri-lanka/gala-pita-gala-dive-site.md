## Gala pita gala dive site

"Gala Pita Gala" is a real, dive-shop-confirmed local Sinhala name (roughly "rock on top of rock") for a boulder reef complex near Unawatuna, reached by a short boat ride from local dive centers.

## Overview

Multiple independent Unawatuna dive operators use this exact name for a site of "rock on top of rock" boulder topography reaching a maximum depth of 20-25 meters (Pearl Divers cites 25 meters; Sun Diving's listing for the same complex cites 20 meters; Dive Journey, a fourth independent source, gives a 10-25 meter range). The terrain is described by the dive shops as medium-sized rock formations rather than a classic fringing coral reef — though Dive Journey instead labels it a "boat-run Unawatuna pinnacle," a different topographic characterization worth flagging even though it doesn't outweigh two shop descriptions of boulder terrain. A small natural cave feature is embedded in the complex — the separately-listed "Cave dive site" entry in this database, about 150 meters away, is very likely the same rock formation or an immediately adjacent part of it. Sources describe the site as boat-accessible (roughly 10-15 minutes from Unawatuna dive centers) rather than a shore entry, which conflicts with this database's current "shore entry" tag. The prior write-up's claim of frogfish at this specific site was not found in any source consulted and has been removed; likewise the listed maximum depth of 18 meters is shallower than what dive shops report for this site (20-25 meters).

## Site Information

- **Location**: Sri Lanka, Asia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Reef
- **Difficulty Level**: Intermediate
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters

## Marine Life

Dive shop listings for Gala Pita Gala describe nudibranchs and sea slugs, sea worms, large lobsters, stingrays, blue ring angelfish, and lionfish among the boulders, plus patches of black, soft, and fan coral and various sponges on the rock surfaces. One operator also mentions occasional manta ray sightings. Dive Journey's independent listing for this site additionally names green and hawksbill turtles, scorpionfish, and (among reef fish) angelfish, butterflyfish, jackfish, and triggerfish. Earlier claims of moray eels and schooling chromis/anthias at this specific site were not corroborated by any source found and have been removed rather than repeated unverified.
