## Cave dive site

Locally known to Unawatuna dive shops as "Galapita Gala," this is a small natural swim-through cave set within a boulder reef roughly 10-15 minutes by boat from Unawatuna.

## Overview

This site sits within the same boulder-and-rock formation that local dive shops call Galapita Gala ("rock on top of rock" in Sinhala), about 150 meters from the separately-mapped "Gala pita gala dive site" entry in this database — the two are very likely the same rock complex recorded from two GPS fixes, or immediately adjacent features of it. Two independent Unawatuna dive operators describe a natural cave here: Pearl Divers lists a rocky, boulder-strewn site reaching a maximum depth of 25 meters with a 3-meter-long swim-through cave, while Sun Diving (operating under the diveinsrilanka.com domain) describes the same 3-meter/9-foot cave passage at 15-20 meters, open to Open Water divers for the surrounding reef but restricted to Advanced-certified divers for the cave passage itself. Both operators describe the site as boat-accessible rather than a shore entry, which conflicts with this database's current "shore entry" tag and should be reviewed. No source found for this entry mentions strong tidal currents at a cave mouth, wreck material, or a 25-meter general cave chamber depth (as distinct from the boulder reef's own max depth) — those specific claims from the prior write-up could not be confirmed and have been removed.

## Site Information

- **Location**: Sri Lanka, Asia
- **Entry Type**: Boat dive
- **Site Type**: Cave/cavern
- **Difficulty Level**: Advanced
- **Maximum Depth**: 25 meters

## Marine Life

Dive shop descriptions of the Galapita Gala boulder/cave complex mention nudibranchs and sea slugs, sponges, sea worms, large lobsters, stingrays, blue ring angelfish, and lionfish, along with patches of black, soft, and fan coral on the rock faces. These sightings are attributed to the site as a whole (cave passage plus surrounding boulders) rather than confirmed specifically inside the cave chamber, so treat the cave interior itself as unconfirmed for any particular species. Earlier claims of glassfish, shrimp, moray eels, scorpionfish, and turtles at this exact site were not found in any source and have been removed.
