## Tosei Maru

Tosei Maru is a WWII Japanese wreck dive site in the Kao Bay area of North Halmahera, Indonesia.

## Overview

This wreck lies in Kao Bay and is locally identified as the Tosei Maru, a Japanese vessel reportedly lost during World War II. **Its identity and location history are, on re-audit, unconfirmed and in conflict across sources — this is flagged rather than resolved.**

A ship named Tosei Maru (5,484 GRT, a Japanese troop/cargo transport) was torpedoed and sunk by the US submarine USS Tuna (SS-203) on 12 December 1943 — this date and tonnage are confirmed independently by Wikipedia and uboat.net. However, neither source places that sinking near Kao Bay: Wikipedia describes the action as occurring during Tuna's ninth patrol in the Java Sea and Flores Sea, while uboat.net gives sinking coordinates of 02°44'N, 126°14'E — roughly 300 km northwest of Kao Bay. No source found states the vessel was towed to or came to rest in Kao Bay. Separately, a 2025 academic paper (Jurnal Pusaka, Universitas Khairun) documents a wreck locally believed by residents to be a Japanese warship near Sosol Beach, Malifut, North Halmahera (in the general Kao Bay area), but explicitly treats the vessel's identity as unconfirmed and calls for further nautical-archaeological investigation — it does not corroborate the "Tosei Maru" name.

In short: the name "Tosei Maru" for this dive site cannot currently be independently verified against the historical sinking record. The ship name may derive from local naming or an OSM contributor's identification rather than a confirmed source. The site lies at a maximum depth of 20 metres, accessible for intermediate divers, and the wreck has been colonised by coral growth and now functions as an artificial reef regardless of its precise identity. Halmahera's Kao Bay (also spelled Kau Bay) hosted significant Japanese naval and logistics operations during the war, which is consistent with wreckage of some kind being present in the bay.

**Note on date:** The OSM source tag originally recorded a sinking date of 1941-12-12. If this wreck is in fact the historical Tosei Maru, the correct date would be 12 December 1943 (confirmed by Wikipedia and uboat.net) — but per the identity conflict above, this cannot be stated as confirmed for this specific wreck.

## Dive Profile

The shallow maximum depth of 20 metres means long no-decompression bottom times are possible, allowing thorough wreck exploration within a single dive. The wreck is dived by boat in the Kao Bay area. Visibility in the bay environment is typically 10–20 metres, with better clarity on outgoing tides.

## Entry and Exit

Access is by boat from Tobelo or Jailolo — the main towns near Kao Bay in North Halmahera. The site is also accessible on liveaboard itineraries that include North Halmahera. Due to its shallow depth, the Tosei Maru can be combined with reef dives in the area on a single-day programme.

## Tips and Recommendations

The shallow depth suits relaxed, photography-focused wreck diving. A torch is recommended for illuminating the interior structure. Given the relatively benign conditions at 20 metres, this is a suitable site for Open Water certified divers who have completed a PADI Wreck Diver specialty.

## Safety Considerations

North Halmahera is remote, with limited medical infrastructure. The nearest recompression chamber is in Manado. Standard wreck diving safety applies: maintain external positions and do not enter enclosed spaces without proper training, gas supply, and penetration equipment. An SMB should be carried on every boat dive. Water temperature is 27–30°C year-round.
